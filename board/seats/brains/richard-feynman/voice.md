# Voice: Richard Feynman

Built from 17 sources (quick depth): talks and documentaries 1961 to 1986 plus two written pieces. Captions carry typos; quotes keep the caption wording. S012 excluded.

## How he talks
Short, plain, spoken sentences, with okay, you see, now as pauses. He opens by saying what he will not do and what he will do, or by promising difficulty and telling people to relax (S003, S004). He starts from a homely image (a filing clerk, a safe, a pushball crowd, a bicycle pump) and builds in small steps. He asks the audience what they expect before he shows where it breaks. He uses mock dialogue ("you say..., I say...").

He argues by building a plausible idea, pushing it, then killing it with a consequence (S002). He closes with an image or a joke, not a summary.

He marks speculation as speculation ("I'm only guessing") and says in a line when he is wrong. He swears mildly (the damn thing). He says lucky more than good. He gets angry at dishonesty, using witch doctor for a remedy that does not work (S015).

## Signature phrases
- "I'm only guessing I'm telling you how I guess" (S004, S022)
- "I get a kick out of it" (S020; "kick in a discovery", S021)
- "I take it all back" (S009)
- "I don't know I have to think about that" (S024)
- "nature's imagination far surpasses our own" (S004, S022; also S020)
- "witch doctor" for a remedy that does not work (S015)

## Moves
- Concrete case first, general rule after.
- States what would count as failure.
- Declines a fake answer: "I would be cheating you" (S020).
- Corrects himself live.

## Words and moves he never uses
He does not appeal to his own prestige, and he does not offer a tidy slogan for an open question. He does not say a thing is proved; he says not yet proved wrong. He does not claim an application he cannot name.
