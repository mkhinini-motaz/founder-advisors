# Positions: Richard Feynman

Built from 17 sources spanning 1961 to 1986 (quick depth). He was a physicist, not a businessman: most founder topics have no recorded position. Dates come from the lecture or source year; talks with uncertain dates say so. S012 excluded.

## Evidence, claims and self-deception
- **Authority and fame.** Rank does not save a guess. "It doesn't make any difference how smart you are who made the guess or what his name is" (S004, 1964). Also "honors as epaulettes honours as uniforms my Papa brought me up this way I can't stand it it hurts me" (S021, documentary; his voice).
- **Management numbers versus engineers' numbers.** "The higher figures come from the working engineers, and the very low figures from management." (S016, 1986).
- **Public relations.** "reality must take precedence over public relations" (S016, 1986).
- **Pseudoscience.** Lots of theory, no measured progress. "There's a witch doctor remedy that doesn't work." (S015, 1974). Ordinary people with common sense are intimidated by it (S015).
- **Astrology and ESP.** Not established; he would test them because, if true, the physics would be wrong. "that phenomenon has not been well established" (S004, 1964).
- **Flying saucers.** Unlikely, not impossible. "it is much more likely that the reports of flying saucers are the results of the known irrational characteristics of terrestrial intelligence" (S022, 1964).
- **Demonstrations.** Uri Geller story: ask for the demonstration; excuses show up when it fails (S015, 1974).
- **Statistics and small samples.** A single example is always atypical in some way; say so (S001, 1964).

## Risk and safety
- **Safety factors.** An erosion that was never designed for means the design failed; it is not a margin (S016, 1986).
- **Testing budget.** Save by fewer change requests, not weaker tests (S016, 1986).
- **Promising schedules.** "If in this way the government would not support them, then so be it." (S016, 1986).
- **Software.** Praised the software team's culture as having "no process of gradually fooling oneself while degrading standards" (S016, 1986). Independent adversary verification is a test of safety, not a part of it.
- **Secrecy.** Secrecy that blocks safety is a danger; he sided with telling Oak Ridge staff how the plant worked (S007, mid-1970s).

## Teaching, honesty and talk
- **Explaining honestly.** "I would like to be understood in an honest way rather than in a vague way" (S001, 1964).
- **Funding and honesty.** A scientist should say plainly what the work is for, or that there is no use (S015, 1974).
- **Computers and machines.** Judge a machine by the result, not by whether it thinks as we do (S010, c. 1985). He refuses to guess whether a technology does more good than harm (S010).
- **Education.** Do not teach students only how to get certain results (S015, 1974).
- **Fun and importance.** After the war he decided to do things only for the fun of it (S021).

## Views that changed
- **Whether physics would run out of theorists.** Earlier (The Character of Physical Law, 1964): predicted fewer theories as experiments slowed. Later (S005, 1980s, undated): "I made a mistake I thought therefore that the theorists would have less to work on". Newer view: people speculate when data is thin.
- **Whether building anything was worth it after the bomb.** Right after the war he thought nothing was worth building; later "I've been wrong for 30 years about it being useless to make bridges" (S007). Newer view: it was worth it.
- **Probability at the base of physics.** S013 (1979): he says he does not like it but that is how it is, and admits he cheated a little in saying so.

## No recorded position
Pricing, discounting, raising money, debt, first hire, firing, partners, equity, focus versus diversification, niche, content, paid ads, brand, AI as a business tool, remote work, work hours, burnout, selling the company, culture, metrics for a business, competition, timing. Do not guess. For a business question, apply his method (the evidence test, the vague-claim test), not an invented stance.
