# Frameworks: Peter Thiel

Built from 25 sources (2008 to 2026): interviews, talks, essays and one set of class notes. S011 is a student's class notes of his 2012 Stanford CS183 lectures (Blake Masters), not his own text. S051 is a host reading the book Zero to One aloud, so it is book text, not speech. S053 is a debate: only his turns are used. Scoring guidance below is labelled as guidance and is the builder's reading, not his rule.

## 1. Monopoly versus perfect competition (the two kinds of business)
- What: there are two kinds of business, monopolies that earn profit and competitive businesses that earn none. Little sits between. Monopolists hide it (they say they are in a huge market) and non-monopolists exaggerate their uniqueness.
- Parts: a monopolist describes its market as a union of markets; a non-monopolist as an intersection (the only British food restaurant in Palo Alto).
- Use when: any how big is the market and who competes question.
- Score (guidance): if you cannot name what makes you the only close option, score it as a commodity business.
- Quote: "there are exactly two kinds of businesses in this world there are businesses that are perfectly competitive and there are businesses that are monopolies" (S044)
- Used in: S011, S022, S044, S045, S050, S051, S052 (7 sources).

## 2. Capitalism and competition are antonyms
- What: competition competes profit away, so the capitalist goal (capital) is the opposite of competition. Competition also makes you better at the thing you compete on while costing you the bigger question of what is valuable.
- Quote: "I think people always say that capitalism and competition are synonyms. But I think they're really antonyms." (S010)
- Used in: S010, S011, S044, S050, S052 (5 sources).

## 3. Zero to one versus one to n (vertical versus horizontal progress)
- What: horizontal progress copies what works (globalization); vertical progress does something new (technology). Horizontal has a formula, vertical has none.
- Use when: judging if an idea is a copy of a known model or a new thing.
- Quote: "technology involves intensive, vertical, zero to one progress. Globalization involves horizontal, copying, one to end progress." (S010)
- Used in: S001, S003, S010, S011, S032, S045, S047, S052 (8 sources).

## 4. The contrarian question: a truth few agree with
- What: his interview question and his test for a company. A good answer has the form most people believe X, but the truth is the opposite of X. It must be true as well as unshared.
- Parts: ask it of a founder; ask what valuable company is nobody building?; ask what people in the business cannot say.
- Quote: "Tell me something that’s true that very few people agree with you on." (S001)
- Caveat from him: unshared is not enough. Quote: "I don’t think it’s sufficient." (S003)
- Used in: S001, S003, S010, S011, S049, S051, S052 (7 sources).

## 5. Conventions, mysteries and secrets
- What: things everybody knows, things impossibly hard, and in between the secrets: hard but knowable. A company is a group that holds a secret. Secrets are hidden by nature and by the people around you.
- Parts: secrets of nature and secrets about people; tell no one and it dies, tell everyone and rivals copy it; big secrets give you time.
- Quote: "I define a company as a group of people who have a secret" (S052)
- Quote (class notes): "The bigger the secret and the likelier it is that you alone have it, the more time you have to execute." (S011)
- Used in: S011, S051, S052 (3 sources).

## 6. Value created times value captured (X and Y)
- What: a company's worth is the value it creates (X) times the share it captures (Y). They are independent. Airlines make huge X and keep almost nothing; Google search is smaller and worth far more.
- Score (guidance): ask for Y before X. A big market with a tiny Y is a charity.
- Quote: "x and y are completely independent variables" (S044)
- Used in: S044, S050 (2 sources).

## 7. Last mover, durability over growth
- What: the winner makes the last great development in a market. Value sits far in the future, so durability counts as much as growth.
- Parts: PayPal's March 2001 model put about three quarters of its value in cash flows from 2011 on; growth is easy to measure and durability is not.
- Quote: "In practice, I think it’s more important to be the last mover than the first mover." (S003)
- Quote: "Durability is at least as important as growth." (S003)
- Used in: S003, S011, S044, S050, S051 (5 sources).

## 8. Start small, then expand in concentric circles
- What: pick a market so small that people ignore it, take all of it, then widen. Starting big and shrinking is the way to fail.
- Parts: Amazon (books), eBay (Pez dispensers), PayPal (about 20,000 eBay power sellers), Facebook (10,000 at Harvard).
- Quote: "you start with a really small market and you take over that whole market" (S044)
- Quote: "the single business idea that you hear most often is: the bigger the market, the better. That is utterly, totally wrong." (S011)
- Used in: S011, S044, S050, S051 (4 sources).

## 9. Four traits of a lasting monopoly
- What: proprietary technology, network effects, economies of scale, branding. Technology should be about ten times better than the next best thing (he calls this rule arbitrary). Brand is the weakest form. Each trait needs a time dimension: will it last.
- Quote: "you want to have a technology that's an order of magnitude better than the next best thing" (S050)
- Quote: "is the weakest form of Monopoly it's a slippery concept but it is real" (S045)
- Used in: S011, S044, S045, S050, S051 (5 sources).

## 10. Definite versus indefinite optimism (plan versus lottery ticket)
- What: determinate worlds reward a plan and conviction (calculus); indeterminate worlds reward process, diversification and A/B tests (statistics). He wants a definite plan, even a bad one.
- Quote: "a bad plan was better than no plan." (S003)
- Quote: "You are not a lottery ticket." (S002)
- Quote: "The problem is the search space is just way too big." (S032)
- Used in: S002, S003, S011, S032, S045, S051 (6 sources).

## 11. Stagnation: bits versus atoms
- What: since about 1970 progress was fast in computers and slow in energy, space, travel and medicine, because bits were lightly regulated and atoms heavily. He leans on culture (risk aversion, bureaucratic science), not only on low fruit picked.
- Quote: "we lived in a world in which bits were unregulated and atoms were regulated." (S001)
- Quote: "They promised us flying cars and all we got was 140 characters." (S003)
- Dated change: in 2026 he says AI may restart growth. Quote: "perhaps AI um is bigger than the internet and is at least has the potential to to reacelerate growth" (S049)
- Used in: S001, S003, S032, S036, S045, S046, S047, S048, S049, S053, S054, S055, S056 (13 sources).

## 12. Mimetic rivalry (Girard): choose your enemies well
- What: people copy what others want, and conflict is fiercest between near-identical parties. In companies it is Shakespeare, not Marx: rivals look alike and grow more alike. Colleagues fight when they want the same thing, so give each one a different job.
- Quote: "you have to always choose your enemies well, because you will soon be like them." (S005)
- Quote (class notes): "Focusing on your enemy is almost always the wrong thing to do." (S011)
- Used in: S002, S005, S011, S022, S037, S044, S051, S056 (8 sources).

## 13. Distribution is part of the product
- What: poor distribution is the main cause of failure. Match the channel to ticket size: ads and viral for cheap goods, sales teams for mid-priced, the CEO for huge deals. Sales works best hidden.
- Parts: customer lifetime value must beat acquisition cost; the missing middle (mid-priced products for small business) is hard; "it sells itself" is a sales pitch.
- Quote (class notes): "Poor distribution—not product—is the number one cause of failure." (S011)
- Quote: "If you've invented something new, but you haven't invented an effective way to sell it, you have a bad business" (S051)
- Used in: S011, S045, S051 (3 sources).

## 14. Power law
- What: outcomes are not normal. One market, one channel, one bet dominates. In venture, the best investment can equal all the rest, so ask if one stake could return the fund.
- Quote: "We do not live in a normal world. We live under a power law" (S051)
- Quote (class notes): "Is there a reasonable scenario where our stake in this company will be worth more than the whole fund?" (S011)
- Used in: S011, S051 (2 sources).

## 15. Founding is destiny: alignment and the foundation
- What: a startup messed up at its foundation cannot be fixed. Check how the founders met and how long they have worked together. Formal alignment (structure, roles) plus informal alignment (people who work well together).
- Quote (class notes): "A startup messed up at its foundation cannot be fixed." (S011)
- Quote: "it would be like getting married to the first person you met at the slot machines in Las Vegas." (S003)
- Used in: S001, S003, S011, S051, S052 (5 sources).

## 16. Optimism and pessimism both converge on doing nothing
- What: acceptance (it will be fine, or it is hopeless) and denial both excuse inaction. Muddling through is the default disaster.
- Quote: "They converge on doing nothing." (S001)
- Quote: "Whenever people think you can just muddle through, you’re probably set up for some kind of disaster." (S002)
- Used in: S001, S002, S003, S007, S010, S045, S054 (7 sources).

## 17. Scylla and Charybdis (two catastrophes)
- What: unconstrained science risks blowing things up, but stopping all science needs a policed global state. Fear both. People overweight the first.
- Quote: "I think we should be at least as scared of the Antichrist as of as of Armageddon" (S055)
- Used in: S002, S048, S055 (3 sources).

## 18. Technology has a valence (crypto libertarian, AI communist)
- What: tools are neutral in use, but some lean one way. Crypto decentralizes. AI centralizes data and returns to scale.
- Quote: "if we say that crypto is libertarian can we also say that AI is communist or something like this" (S056)
- Used in: S002, S007, S056 (3 sources).

## 19. Substitute or complement test
- What: ask if talk, analysis, school, therapy or a screen is a complement to action or a substitute for it. Same for computers: they complement people.
- Quote: "talk is often a substitute for action" (S056)
- Used in: S032, S045, S056 (3 sources).

## Books

### Books: Peter Thiel

Built from 25 sources. Never book text; thesis in plain words. S051 is a host reading Zero to One aloud, so its quotes are 2014 book words, not speech.

#### Zero to One: Notes on Startups, or How to Build the Future (2014, with Blake Masters)
- Thesis: progress is vertical (0 to 1) or horizontal (1 to n). Great companies are creative monopolies: nobody offers a close substitute. Competition destroys profit.
- Frameworks: contrarian question; secrets; four monopoly traits; start small; last mover; power law; distribution as part of the product; founders as insiders and outsiders; definite optimism; you are not a lottery ticket.
- Four old rules he reverses after the 2000 bust: make incremental advances, stay lean, improve on the competition, focus on product not sales. His opposites are in S051.
- Quote: "This book offers no formula for success" (S051). Taught in: S051, S045, S052, S022, S010, S050.
- Origin: the book grew out of Blake Masters's class notes of his 2012 Stanford course (S011).

#### Course notes: CS183 Startup (Stanford, Spring 2012)
- Not a book by him. S011 is a student's essay version of 19 lectures. It carries most of the practical rules on boards, pay, hiring, firing, distribution and secrets. Credit it as class notes.

#### Essays and series
- Stagnation talks (2007 to 2026). The same thesis from bits and atoms in 2014, to the 2026 view that AI may restart growth. (S003, S036, S049)

#### Books he cites
- Anna Karenina (Tolstoy): inverted so that happy companies differ and unhappy ones are alike (S044).
- The Hedgehog and the Fox (Berlin): in business be the hedgehog (S011).
- The American Challenge (Servan-Schreiber): a forecast of leisure that failed (S047).
- Things Hidden Since the Foundation of the World (Girard): source of his view on imitation (S037).
- The Sovereign Individual (Davidson, Rees-Mogg): influenced him in 1997; he later doubted it (S002).
- The Limits to Growth (Club of Rome): the zero-growth case he calls the strongest from the other side (S055, S056).
