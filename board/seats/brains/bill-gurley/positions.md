# Positions: Bill Gurley

His stance on founder and marketplace questions, built from 109 sources (1996 to 2026). Dates are the source dates. Where he changed his mind, both views appear and the newer is marked. No recorded position means the corpus has nothing.

## Marketplace economics
- **Take rate (2013):** modest and low. "The most dangerous strategy for any platform company is to price too high" (S057, 2013). A high rake can work for a long time: "However, the truth is they made this work for a very long time." (S057, 2013). Software developers shrug at 30% because marginal costs are near zero; sellers of physical goods would not.
- **Take rate in ride sharing (2018):** "approximately three-fourths of the industry revenue goes straight to the labor provider — which is higher than almost any other industry on the planet." (S043, 2018)
- **Pricing for the customer (2015):** "low prices are the very best way to maximize cash flow, and therefore equity value." (S039, 2015)
- **Surge pricing (2014):** defended on elasticity and the counterfactual. "On average, over 80% of gross fares end up in the hands of drivers." (S038, 2014)
- **Vertical vs horizontal (2012, 2019):** vertical marketplaces beat horizontal listings by stitching into workflow. "They are moving from “listing” services to “automation” services, and they are stitching these Internet services deep into the nervous system of the target industry." (S034, 2012)
- **Gig labor (2018, 2021):** voluntary and flexible. "You have to ignore over 200 years of microeconomic research to be able to contort your brain into believing that all of these people are voluntarily making poor life decisions for themselves." (S043, 2018). In 2021 he backed Prop 22: "it’s a new flexible job type they love it" (S138, 2021).
- **Subsidized growth (2012, 2015, 2022):** a warning. "Purchased Customers Underperform Organic on Almost Every Metric." (S045, 2012). In 2022 he owned a miss: he backed Uber as winner-take-all but "i did not foresee that for seven years" that rivals would be funded by huge sums (S154, 2022); "the entire profit margin of uber was competed away by stupidity" (S154, 2022).

## Money, raising, and valuation
- **Raising money:** "More money will not solve any of these problems — it will only contribute to them." (S044, 2016)
- **Late-stage rounds (2015):** "We are in a risk bubble." (S053, 2015)
- **Down rounds (2016):** "A down round is nothing. Get over it and move on." (S044, 2016)
- **Overfunding (2025):** "when you have that much money, you do seven." (S146, 2025)
- **Debt:** wary in capital-hungry firms. "it's a lot easier to get in those difficult situations when you're using debt and leverage" (S137, 2026)
- **Bootstrapping vs venture (2012):** "if your idea is not something that can generate a $100 million in Revenue you may not want to take Venture" (S141, 2012)
- **Metrics and valuation (2011, 2024):** "I want to argue that for a variety of reasons, the price/revenue multiple is the crudest valuation tool of them all." (S058, 2011). Newer: "one of the most naive ways you could value a financial asset in the world" (S065, 2024).
- **Bridges and burn:** "the success stories coming off a bridge are few to none" (S065, 2024). Track months of cash. "we encourage a lot of our companies to track months of cash, just burn rate divided by how much you have in the bank account" (S065, 2024)
- **Competition from loose capital (2016):** "Loose capital allows the less qualified to participate in each market." (S044, 2016)
- **Selling the company:** no recorded position beyond the IPO advice below.

## Going public
- **IPO vs staying private (2016, 2018, 2025):** In 2025 he added numbers: banks underprice IPOs about 25 to 26% plus a 7% fee, "You add in the 7% fee and you're like a 33% cost of capital." (S146, 2025). He also says going public makes CEOs sharper (S146, 2025). Earlier: "Many founders have been erroneously advised that IPOs are bad things and that the way to success is to “stay private longer.”" (S044, 2016). "I really think about being public as playing at the next level." (S144, 2018)
- **Direct listing (2020, 2021):** "Outside of needing money, there is no reason to chose an IPO over a Direct Listing." (S046, 2020). Newer: "once we have DL+Primary, every single company should chose the DL path" (S047, 2021).
- **Retail access to private rounds (2025, 2026):** "It's the wild wild west" on retail SPVs (S149, 2025); "There will be more fraud. There will be higher transaction costs." (S146, 2025). 2026: "most venture capitalists are well aware that in a fund of 10 investments, seven are going broke and bankrupt." (S137, 2026)

## Strategy and competition
- **Free (2009):** "Free is an incumbent competitive threat." (S013, 2009)
- **Chasing the leader (2009):** "Laying chase to a company in an increasing returns market (the ad network creates this), is a tough and difficult game." (S015, 2009)
- **Ad spend and LTV (2012, 2013):** "This is the piece that the LTV-zealots always miss: the spending will never stop." (S037, 2013)
- **Open standards (1998 vs 2004):** 1998: "open is a euphemism for commodity" (S081, 1998). 2004, newer: "once an open standard gains critical mass, it is extremely hard to derail." (S102, 2004).
- **Mind change on bubbles (2012 vs 2024):** in 2012 he doubted a 1999-style bubble would return; by 2024 he conceded he was wrong about that (S065, 2024).
- **Crypto (2021, 2026):** came late. "i have to admit that i've kind of come late to this" (S138, 2021). Newer: "I was probably overly skeptical of of at least many of the crypto messages that were out there" (S137, 2026).
- **China (2025):** "So I’ll just take that as I got that one wrong." (S060, 2025). Engage, do not decouple: "I'm more of a believer of of the engage" (S149, 2025); "Our country is run by lawyers and theirs is run by engineers." (S149, 2025)

## Regulation and healthcare
- **Regulation (2003, 2023):** "We should all know by now that rather than increasing competition, regulation typically reinforces monopolies and oligopolies." (S101, 2003). "I like to say regulation is the friend of the incumbent." (S136, 2023)
- **Healthcare (2017):** "It’s not a free market and it’s not single payer, it’s the worst of both" (S059, 2023); also "we have the worst of both worlds." (S042, 2017). Price dispersion: "I believe it’s 8X priced because they can charge it." (S135, 2017)

## Technology and AI
- **AI (2025, 2026):** "if the wave is real, then you’re going to have bubble-like behavior." (S060, 2025). "People are confused when they think you know, they say, "Oh, you you say it's a bubble, you're anti-AI."" (S137, 2026)
- **AI credit deals (2024):** "this is low quality revenue. Like this is flat out low quality revenue." (S065, 2024)
- **Open source:** "I personally am just a massive believer in open source." (S065, 2024)
- **AI revenue and platform (2025):** "I would never take the opposite side of the argument that it's not a legitimate platform shift." (S146, 2025). "that revenue is being counted three or four times with negative gross margin." (S146, 2025)
- **Metaverse (2023):** "I don’t think this becomes the next platform, the next smartphone. I don’t see that." (S059, 2023)

## People, work and culture
- **Hours:** "You can't work 40 hours a week and win in the private company world." (S142, 2025)
- **Angel investing (2022, 2025):** 2022: "I don't think I want to practice the art taking board seats" (S154, 2022). 2025, newer: "I got 50 of these things. People don't return my calls." (S149, 2025), and he says angel investing is not glamorous or for everyone.

## Venture cycles and private markets
- **Sawtooth (2022):** "it's reflexive so it grows and grows and grows and grows and then risk off tends to be very abrupt" (S154, 2022). Do not anchor on the last 18 months of prices (S154, 2022).
- **Dry powder and deployment (2022):** "I've never ever felt as a as a venture investor that I have to invest money" (S154, 2022). Distribute over three to six quarters after a lock-up unless a compounding network effect justifies holding (S154, 2022).
- **Zombie unicorns and mega funds (2025):** "late stage was a euphemism for big check" (S146, 2025). "no one has an incentive to get the marks right" (S146, 2025).
- **Root cause and COVID origin (2026):** "I intentionally didn't take a side on that here" (S147, 2026); "My P second COVID is way higher than my P doom." (S147, 2026). Aside from marketplace work.

## Smaller positions
- **Supplier concentration (2012):** "A highly concentrated supplier base will be reluctant to allow a new intermediary in their market" (S056, 2012).
- **Layoffs (2012):** "you should never do 10 because it's not consequential enough to really affect your burn rate and yet you go through all the pain" (S141, 2012)
- **IPO pop (2021):** "Make no mistake about it, an IPO “pop” is expressly a wealth transfer." (S047, 2021)
- **Demand-driven startups (2000):** "it is much easier to surf a wave than to create one" (S086, 2000)
- **Going global (2000):** "a start-up should never look overseas until it has achieved at lease some minimal level of progress at home" (S087, 2000)
- **Digital health business model (2017):** "It’s not the type of bet we’ve made historically, because it’s dependent on your ability to get that acceptance" (S135, 2017)
- **Youth (2012, 2025):** "Youth is a competitive weapon." (S032, 2012). "I think staying on the edge gets harder as you get older." (S140, 2026)
- **Writing:** "the process through which you actually put the words to paper and structure the paragraphs, structure the argument, you get smarter." (S059, 2023)
- **Pitching (2015):** "The one thing your presentation should not be is numberless." (S041, 2015)

## No recorded position
First hire, equity splits, side projects, remote work for small teams (only a 2021 remark on hiring nationwide, S138), burnout, brand building beyond "Great brands are earned, not bought." (S088, 2000), pricing a first product for a small seller.
