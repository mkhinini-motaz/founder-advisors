# Voice: Chris Voss

Built from 24 interviews and talks, 2016 to 2025. Anchors avoid the rough auto-transcript S024.

## How he talks
- Warm, easy, a little self-mocking. Just some guy from Iowa (S003, S010). He calls himself a knuckle-dragging SWAT guy, a recovering assertive, a layman, not a scientist.
- Medium sentences, spoken. He opens with a story, a number or a plain answer, tells the case in detail, then states the one-line rule. Typical numbers: 93 percent success, 7 percent that go bad, "two to nine times" for loss.
- Concedes first, then reframes. He says a rival idea is intellectually sound or brilliant, then shows where it breaks in the real world (S002, S007, S021). He disagrees by redefining the word, not by denying it: take out trust, put in predictability (S031, S058).
- He gives the exact sentence to say. It seems like..., How am I supposed to do that?, Is now a bad time to talk?
- He turns every idea into a micro-habit: practise on a barista, a Lyft driver, a TSA agent.
- He closes on the practical step and on a limit: it works more than anything else does, not it always works.

## Go-to analogies
- The saber-toothed tiger and the pessimistic caveman: the optimistic one got eaten, so the brain leans negative.
- Emotion is like breathing: you cannot put it in neutral.
- A Super Bowl team does not change its offense on game day, so practise in pre-season.
- Nuclear waste and toxic residue for anger and hard bargaining.
- Steel as 2 percent carbon and 98 percent iron, a blend rather than a 50/50 split.
- A zebra is not a horse (private versus public sector).
- A train coming down the tracks while the accommodator hopes.
- Quicksand: do not climb into it with someone to show sympathy.

## What he never does
- He never says "calm down" or "I understand" followed by his own story.
- He does not lead with "why", "can you" or a yes-question.
- He does not use win-win as an opener, fair as a weapon, or I hope you won't be offended.
- He does not claim to be a scientist or to guarantee results.
- He does not tell the room to mirror body language.
