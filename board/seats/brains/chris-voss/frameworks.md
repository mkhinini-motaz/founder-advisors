# Frameworks: Chris Voss

Built from 24 interviews and talks, 2016 to 2025 (see sources.md). Most-used first. Scoring lines are guidance written for this room, not his own thresholds.

## 1. Tactical empathy
- What: understand where the other side is coming from and say it out loud. It is information, not sympathy, liking or agreement.
- Steps: work out their world, state it, watch for relief or correction, then move.
- When: first move in every negotiation, including with people you dislike.
- Scoring (guidance): can you state their position so well they would sign it? If not, you are not ready to ask for anything.
- Quote: "just straight understanding where they're coming from and articulating it which requires no agreement whatsoever that becomes a very powerful tool" (S012)
- Sources: S003, S007, S010, S012, S013, S018, S021, S023, S024, S031, S058 (11).

## 2. Label
- What: a short observation of the other side's emotion or situation, opened with it seems like, it sounds like, it looks like, then silence. Avoid I.
- Steps: guess the feeling, say it as an observation, stop talking. If wrong, that's just the way it seems.
- When: to gather information, to deactivate a negative, to open a call instead of "how are you".
- Misfires: a label that is absurdly obvious, or ignores what they just said, shows you are not listening.
- Scoring (guidance): count labels versus questions in your plan. Labels first.
- Quote: "labeling is is hanging a label on an emotion or an affect and then just calling it up" (S012)
- Sources: S002, S011, S012, S013, S019, S021, S023, S024, S031, S036, S037 (11).

## 3. Calibrated "what" and "how" questions
- What: open questions starting with what or how, built to make the other side think about the problem, not to collect answers. Drop "why", which sounds like an accusation.
- Steps: ask, go quiet, judge how they respond before judging the answer.
- When: you need them to solve your problem for you, or you need to diagnose them.
- Scoring (guidance): a good question makes the other side explain a cost or a plan. A bad one can be answered yes or no.
- Quote: "On our level one, our basics are the how and what questions" (S007)
- Sources: S007, S012, S013, S018, S023, S024, S032, S033, S062 (9).

## 4. Go for no (the calibrated no)
- What: ask Do you disagree?, Are you against this?, Is now a bad time to talk?, Is this a ridiculous idea? so a no is safe and the real problems surface.
- When: a cold email, a close, an exhausted counterpart, a call opening.
- Scoring (guidance): if the question can only be answered yes, rewrite it.
- Quote: "We call this a calibrated no, and a calibrated no is worth at least five yeses." (S002)
- Sources: S002, S005, S007, S013, S019, S024, S031, S033, S036, S048 (10).

## 5. Three kinds of yes
- What: commitment, confirmation, counterfeit. Only commitment counts. A counterfeit yes is said to make you go away.
- When: any yes you hear. Test it with a "how" question.
- Scoring (guidance): a yes with no how attached counts as zero.
- Quote: "there's three kinds of yesses. There's commitment, confirmation, and counterfeit." (S007)
- Sources: S007, S010, S030, S048 (4).

## 6. That's right (not "you're right")
- What: summarize their position and feelings until they say "that's right". "You're right" means stop talking to me.
- Steps: summarize, pile on their grievances if needed, listen for relief or a new idea.
- Scoring (guidance): no "that's right" yet means keep summarizing before you ask.
- Quote: "that's right is what people say when they feel understood" (S018)
- Sources: S005, S007, S010, S011, S012, S013, S018, S019, S024, S030, S051 (11).

## 7. Accusation audit
- What: list every negative the other side may think about you, say it first, and then be silent.
- When: before a price rise, a hard ask, a first meeting with a hostile party.
- Scoring (guidance): write five to fifteen items; if you cannot name five, you do not know them yet.
- Quote: "the accusations audit is going after everything negative everything everything everything everything and being very proactive about it" (S030)
- Sources: S012, S013, S019, S024, S030, S031, S037, S058 (8).

## 8. Look for the loss
- What: people decide mostly to avoid loss. Find what they lost recently, often in the last 24 to 48 hours, and what they fear losing. Use it only through empathy.
- When: a stuck deal, a seller holding for too much, a refusal that does not add up.
- Misfires: used without empathy it is blunt force, and you become the hostage taker.
- Quote: "look for the loss, look for the loss, look for the loss" (S021)
- Sources: S003, S010, S011, S018, S019, S021, S033 (7).

## 9. Yes is nothing, how is everything (implementation)
- What: a deal is judged by whether it is carried out. Ask how they will do it; a liar or an unprepared party goes quiet.
- Quote: "We used to say yes is nothing without how and now we say yes is nothing. How is everything!" (S003)
- Scoring (guidance): no named steps, dates and owner means no deal yet.
- Sources: S003, S007, S012, S021, S023 (5).

## 10. Three types: assertive, analyst, accommodator
- What: fight, flight, make friends; about a third of people each. Assertives want to be heard, analysts want data and time, accommodators want the relationship. Each is essential and inadequate. Voss is a natural assertive.
- When: read the counterpart and your own default. Do not label people by category beyond this.
- Quote: "Now the most evolved negotiators learn the strengths of all three." (S005)
- Sources: S005, S011, S012, S013, S018, S019, S030, S037, S050 (9).

## 11. Black swans (unknown unknowns)
- What: each side holds back facts that would change the deal. Keep them talking until one surfaces.
- When: before anchoring on a goal; any time the other side seems irrational.
- Quote: "A black swan is a piece of information that neither side could predict, that if it gets out in the open, it's going to change everything." (S007)
- Sources: S007, S013, S019, S033, S036 (5).

## 12. Mirroring
- What: repeat the last one to three words, verbal only, with a rising tone, then wait.
- When: you need more detail, need to buy time, or need to test how firm a position is. He admits he does not mirror much himself.
- Quote: "We just mirror what someone has just said, and we tend to get tons of information downloaded towards us that we could use." (S002)
- Sources: S002, S007, S011, S012, S013, S018, S021, S023, S032, S036, S037, S062 (12).

## 13. Late night FM DJ voice
- What: slow, calm, downward-inflecting voice with a dropped chin. It calms them and you. Use it to hold a line: state it as a flat fact, then go silent.
- Quote: "you can force a system override if you're upset" (S011)
- Sources: S011, S013, S023, S030, S031, S032 (6).

## 14. Warn, then say the lousy part
- What: name the pain coming (this is going to sound harsh, I've got a lousy proposition for you), give the other side three seconds to brace, then say it plainly.
- Quote: "I've got a lousy proposition for you." (S007)
- Sources: S007, S013, S021, S023, S024 (5).

## 15. Favorite or fool (proof of life)
- What: when a buyer keeps asking for your price, you may be the favorite or the filler bid. Ask a what question, for example the percentage chance of getting the deal, and read the speed of the answer.
- Quote: "it's not so much that they answer what the answer is it's how quickly they change" (S030)
- Sources: S030, S036, S050 (3).

## 16. Ackerman bargaining model
- What: when you must bargain on price: set a target, open at 65 percent, raise in shrinking steps to 85, 95, 100 percent, add empathy and a "no" between rounds, end on a precise non-round number plus a non-money item.
- Quote: "if you're gonna bargain it's the most effective bargaining model out there nothing beats it" (S013)
- Sources: S013 (1). Single source: treat as a low-confidence recipe.

## 17. Overlapping ground and the blend
- What: look for overlap and high-value trades (cheap for you, valuable to them), not the middle.
- Quote: "let's look for overlapping ground you know versus middle ground" (S011)
- Sources: S011, S019, S031, S050 (4).

## 18. Dynamic silence
- What: ask, then stay quiet and read their face and voice. His son's rule, as Voss relays it: count thousands until you run out of numbers.
- Quote: "Dynamic silence is the opportunity for you to actually look at the other person and read what's going on on their face" (S030)
- Sources: S012, S030, S058 (3).

## 19. Guess their view and let them correct you
- What: open with your best guess of what they think; saying it slightly wrong on purpose draws out the truth.
- Quote: "So I'm going to start out by saying, Here's what I think you're thinking." (S023)
- Sources: S023, S024, S031, S048 (4).

## 20. ELF and HALF clients
- What: easy, lucrative, fun clients versus hard, annoying, lame, frustrating ones. His firm found HALFs cost two to five times the time and bring no repeat business.
- Quote: "stop doing business with the HALFs and do business with the ELFs." (S050)
- Sources: S050 (1). The "HALF" half is Joe Polish's term.

## Books

### Books: Chris Voss

Built from 24 interviews and talks, 2016 to 2025. Never reproduces book text.

#### Never Split the Difference: Negotiating As If Your Life Depended On It (2016)
- Co-written with Tahl Raz and with his son Brandon; the working title was Killer Deals (S013). He says it sold close to 2.5 million copies (S018).
- Thesis: negotiation is an emotional process, not an intellectual one, and people are predictable. Getting to Yes assumes a rational actor and breaks on emotion (S002, S021).
- Core in his words: tactical empathy, labels, mirrors, calibrated questions, no over yes, the accusation audit, that's right, black swans, the three voices. The FBI had eight skills; the book bundles them as nine tools, tools as opposed to nine steps (S032, S048).
- He left goal-setting out on purpose, because a goal makes you quit at the line (S032).
- Taught in: S002, S005, S007, S010, S011, S012, S013, S018, S019, S021, S030, S031, S032, S036, S037.

#### Other works he points to
- Getting to Yes (Fisher, Ury): intellectually sound, owned by everyone, read by few; it did not work on kidnappers (S002, S007, S013, S019). He concedes why Roger Fisher invented BATNA (S051).
- Start With No (Jim Camp, 2002): the ancestry of his no method; he says his own version evolved from it and is trickier, leakier but more effective (S007, S013, S024).
- Beyond Winning (Robert Mnookin): empathy and assertiveness as a sequencing issue, which he reads as: empathy first puts you in a position to assert (S012, S019, S036).
- Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman): loss aversion and why "how" questions trigger slow thinking (S013, S018, S023, S033).
- The Black Swan (Taleb): source of his firm's name (S013, S019). Antifragile is cited for curiosity (S024).
- Stalling for Time (Gary Noesner), his FBI mentor's book (S010).
- Ego, Authority and Failure (Derek Gaunt) on tactical empathy and leadership (S050).
- The Talent Code (Daniel Coyle), for everything is learned (S003, S012, S030, S033, S050).
- The Full Fee Agent, a coming or companion book for real estate agents, with Steve Shaw (S013, S030).

No other books of his own are recorded in the corpus. He also made a documentary called "Tactical Empathy" (S050).
