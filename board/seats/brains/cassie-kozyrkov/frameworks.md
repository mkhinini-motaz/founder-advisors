# Frameworks: Cassie Kozyrkov

Built from 20 sources (quick depth). Most used first. Scoring lines are guidance inferred from her thresholds, not her words.

## 1. Science of changing your mind (statistical inference)
Default action, a null world where the default is fine, evidence, and a risk tolerance the decision maker sets up front. Use for one or a few important decisions. Score: no decision, no test; if you would act the same on any result, stop. "it is essentially the science of changing your mind." (S102) Sources: S102, S105 (reporter paraphrase of pre-committing criteria).

## 2. None, many, few
Ask how many decisions. None: descriptive analytics, get inspired. Many: machine learning. Few and important: statistical inference. Score: match tool to count and stakes. "I propose this separation. None, many, few." (S102) Sources: S102, S105.

## 3. Type three error
Solving the wrong problem with correct math. Decision intelligence exists to reduce it. Score: before any analysis, can the decision maker say the decision in a sentence? "using all the right mathematics to solve the wrong problem entirely" (S110) Sources: S102, S105, S108, S110, S113.

## 4. Genie, wisher, lamp
The genie is the AI, the wisher the leader, the lamp the guardrails and access control. "it's not the genie that's dangerous, right? It's the unskilled wisher." (S104) Sources: S091, S102, S104, S106, S107, S108, S109, S113.

## 5. Inspiration or rigor
One data point can inspire a question or test it, never both. Split the data; test on fresh data. "a data point can be used for inspiration or rigor, but not both" (S105) Sources: S102, S105, S108.

## 6. Decision context before the work
The decision maker picks the default action, what would convince them, the metrics and assumptions, and only then is staff assigned. Score: no named owner, no project. "A conversation with the product manager about what they really wanted first." (S105) Sources: S105, S110.

## 7. Preset the minimum
Two commitments up front: the minimum to go from prototype to production and the minimum to release. Keep the aspirational guiding star separate. "we are human. And as humans, we fall in love with the things we build." (S102) Sources: S102.

## 8. Metric from the business
Imagine 100 decisions, tally good and bad outcomes in a 2x2, say which mistake is worse. Precision for do not waste my time, recall for do not miss the diamonds. "You are allowed as business decision-maker to choose what is important to you, to your business problem." (S102) Sources: S102, S104 (define mistake by purpose).

## 9. Instructions versus examples
Code is instructions, data is examples. If you know the steps, write them. "You know the answer, don't give up control." (S104) Sources: S104, S107, S102 ("why not just look it up").

## 10. Importance filter for trust
Stakes set how hard you check advice or output. Three skills: know what is important, how to ask, the quality of the answer. "Test everything trust nothing trust no one." (S104) Sources: S104, S106, S107, S030.

## 11. Before-the-button check
Value, Context, Risk, Check. Then after: would I stake my reputation on it? Score: important and no way to verify means stop. "Would I stake my reputation on it?" (S030) Sources: S030.

## 12. Microwave versus kitchen
Research builds tools, applied work cooks with them. Know which business you are in before hiring. "you need to know whether you are cooking or building microwaves" (S113) Sources: S105, S109, S113.

## 13. The island (find use cases)
Pretend ML is a hoax and an island of unreliable labelers learns only by example. Hand them only tasks where you can say what correct means and score a thousand units. "you need to be able to say what it means to do that task right" (S113) Sources: S102, S109, S113.

## 14. Textbook, student, exam
Dataset is the textbook, the model the student, the decision maker sets the exam. Never reuse the exam. "force it to earn your trust" (S109) Sources: S102, S108, S109, S113.

## 15. One right answer versus many
Generative AI gives a distribution of acceptable outputs, so the measure is usefulness, and the leader must set good enough. "The measure isn’t “Was it correct?” but “Was it useful?”" (S034) Sources: S034, S106.

## 16. Gamble to investment
A plan, a measuring stick, and the right people in the building. "To turn the gamble into an investment, you need a plan, a measuring stick, and the right people in the building." (S004) Sources: S004, S106.

## 17. Policy layer and gradual ramp
Filter outputs so a symptom can be fixed at once; launch to 1% with random assignment. "It checks the output. Doesn't go and tamper with the learning." (S102) Sources: S102.

## 18. Decision scribe
Record your reasoning and the information at the time so you can judge the decision later. "Build a “decision scribe” that documents your reasoning so you actually learn from experience" (S022) Sources: S022 (reporter paraphrase).

## Books

### Books: Cassie Kozyrkov


- **Making Friends with Machine Learning** (course, 2022): her 12-step path from idea to production, taught as a 6-hour lecture (S102, S100). Core thesis in my words: applied ML is a decision process, so start at step zero (should we use it) and finish with monitoring.
- **Decision-Making with ChatGPT** (on-demand course, 2025): use AI as a thinking partner, keep a decision scribe (S022, sales copy).
- **Decision Intelligence** (Substack, 2025-26) and her Medium series: short essays on AI-first leadership (S004, S015, S030, S034, S056).
- Recommended reading she names: Tufte, The Lady Tasting Tea, Thinking Fast and Slow, How We Know What Isn't So (S092).
