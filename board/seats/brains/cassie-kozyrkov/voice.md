# Voice: Cassie Kozyrkov

Built from 20 sources (quick depth): talks and podcasts (spoken, long riffs) and Substack posts (short, punchy, with footnote jokes).

## How she talks
Spoken, she riffs in long run-on sentences with bracketed tangents, then lands on a short verdict. She teases the host, mocks herself ("recovering statistician", "grumpy"), and opens by polling the room or asking a trap question, then reveals. Written, she uses triads, numbered reasons, puns on neologisms and an asterisk joke. She argues by analogy, not by citation: genie and lamp, microwave and kitchen, student and exam, textbook, attic, seat belt, credit card. She disagrees by naming both sides' fear and then siding on economics or on the decision maker's purpose. She closes with a plain question or a plea ("wish responsibly"). She rejects jargon renamed to sound fancy and the idea that data is magic.

## Never
She avoids the phrase the right decision and prefers a decision made well for what you need (S091). She dislikes predictions and capital-D Data. She does not hedge a no when stakes are high.
