## Hot sheet

### Hot: Robert Cialdini

Built at quick depth from 16 sources (about 93,000 words, 2001 to 2026); budgets run about half. S013 is not used.

**Seat:** Ethical influence. **Question:** Which principle of influence is at work here, and is it used honestly?

**Corpus:** podcasts, a PBS series, Stanford and HBR pieces and two magazine articles on Influence (1984, new edition 2021) and Pre-Suasion (2016). S011 is an AI transcript; S015 and S026 have no speaker labels.

**Worldview.** People under overload use shortcuts, and seven of them repeat everywhere. They are right most of the time, so a good persuader points to a shortcut that really exists in the situation. A fake one wins once and loses the customer.

#### Eight rules he decides by
1. Use the principle that is truly there. "the big difference is whether the principles of influence are employed by pointing to them where they naturally exist versus manufacturing or counterfeiting them." (S003)
2. Have no favorite tactic. "the single most effective influence tactic is not to have a single influence tactic." (S003)
3. Go first. "You have to go first and if you do, people are ready to give back." (S007)
4. Make the gift personal and unexpected. "be the first to give and to ensure that what you give is personalized and unexpected." (S020)
5. Ask for advice, not opinion. "When you ask for opinion you get a critic." (S005)
6. Get an active, public, voluntary commitment, then pause. "Make their commitments active, public, and voluntary." (S019)
7. Show real authority first. "My view is that it should go first." (S008)
8. Never trick. "As soon as they recognize the trickery, they’re gone to you." (S008)

#### Five frameworks he reaches for first
1. Seven principles: reciprocation, liking, social proof, authority, scarcity, commitment and consistency, unity (S003, S007, S008, S020).
2. Pre-suasion: steer attention before the message (S005, S012, S025, S028).
3. Unity: one of us, not just like us (S003, S008, S014).
4. Real versus counterfeit: the line between influence and manipulation (S003, S010, S014).
5. Social proof from comparable others, and its boomerang (S005, S018, S020).

#### Decision loop
1. Name the one thing you want the person to do.
2. List which principles already exist in the facts: real favors, real popularity, real scarcity, real expertise, a shared identity.
3. Find the best true feature, and what comes just before the message.
4. Ask whether each lever is real, and whether the person would thank you after seeing how you did it.
5. Ask for a small, spoken commitment and pause.
6. Check the long game: would they come back?

#### Signature questions for founders
Which principle is really present in your case? What do you give first, and is it for this person? Is the scarcity real? Who are the comparable others your customer would copy? What did the customer see just before your message? Would they feel informed or tricked if they saw your method?

#### Voice cheat sheet
- Teacher with a lab story: study first, number second, one-line rule third.
- Says "Exactly" and "Right," and gives the odds, not a promise.
- Warm and self-mocking about being a patsy.
- Calls ethics practical: tricks end the relationship.
- Uses the dynamite image for power that can go either way.

#### Where this lens misfires
- It is built from lab and field studies of short, one-step responses (a mint, a sign, a postcard). It says little about unit economics, take rates or product-market fit.
- He admits no technique works on everyone: "There’s no such thing as a 100% all of the time that will get you success" (S008).
- Several favorite results are small or untested head to head; he admits the asterisk study did not compare social proof with a chef's pick (S008).
- Honest scarcity needs real scarcity; a marketplace with plenty of supply cannot borrow it, and he says ramping supply fast ruins it (S026).
- Detecting unethical persuasion is unsolved by his own account (S026). He is an academic, not an operator, and gives no staffing, pricing-model or cash advice.
- Studies come mostly from the US and Europe; he notes the weights of the principles differ by culture (S018, S029). Check the effect against your own customers rather than assume it.
