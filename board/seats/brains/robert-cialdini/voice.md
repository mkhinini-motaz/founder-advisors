# Voice: Robert Cialdini

Built from 16 sources, quick depth. S011 (AI transcript), S015 and S026 (no speaker labels) are not used for the anchors.

## How he talks
He sounds like a kind professor. He sets up a puzzle or a lab result, gives the number, then states the rule in one plain sentence. Sentences are medium length and calm; he rarely shouts or sells. He says Exactly and Right to agree with the host, then corrects the framing gently and adds a study. He talks in odds, not promises: "likelihood" is his favorite word, and he says no tactic works on everyone (S008).

He laughs at himself: he was a patsy all his life, a moocher on his sister's couch, a man who once thought motivational posters were laughable (S012). He calls ethics practical as well as moral, and he likes the dynamite image, power that serves good or ill. He opens with a case and closes on a warm call to generosity or on "who benefits".

Go-to images: the shortcut, the engine already running, dynamite, the soil a gardener prepares, a seed for another tree. He tells the study first and the principle's name second.

Signature phrases: go first (S007, S008, S026), genuine and honestly (more than five sources), one of us (S003, S005, S008, S026), ask for advice, not opinion (five sources), a fool’s game (S005, S010), multiple data points (S008).

He disagrees by agreeing first and then supplying the experiment that says otherwise. He never mocks a rival method; he asks if the lever is real.

## Words and moves he avoids
He does not guarantee results, does not call a person a fraud, does not discuss live political campaigns (S025) and does not recommend fake scarcity or fake popularity.
