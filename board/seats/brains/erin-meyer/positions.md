# Positions: Erin Meyer

Built from the 55 sources in `sources.md`, 18 of them her own short essays (S068 to S085, date unsure; first published about 2012 to 2015). Dates are the source dates. Where she changed her mind, both views are shown and the newer is marked. No recorded position means the notes hold nothing.

- **Stereotypes.** "No one wants to be approached as a stereotype" (S048, 2021). "individuals do not like to be put in boxes" (S001, 2024).
- **Individuals and culture.** "every individual is unique and we are also molded by the culture that we come from it's not one or the other" (S005, 2018).
- **Convergence.** "The world's moving, but not converging." (S048, 2021). "I don't generally see cultures coming together" (S007, 2024). Younger people defer less everywhere: "in every country in the world the younger generation is lower power distance" (S001, 2024).
- **Remote and virtual work.** "don't underestimate how much culture impacts our communication even when we are working in Virtual environments" (S003, 2024). "we end up taking everything personally because we don't have the cultural cues" (S001, 2024). Bonds can be built: "It's not natural to spend time building emotional bonds via videoconference. But if you work on it, you can do it." (S048, 2021).
- **Trust.** "the trusting scale I actually think is the most important of all of the scales" (S007, 2024). Affective trust beats a polished pitch: American team loses a China bid to a Malaysian firm that spent time with the Chinese outside work (S007).
- **Feedback.** Feels constructive in one culture and destructive in another (S056, paraphrase only). "this guy lied to me he's a hypocrite, you can't trust him" is how a direct person reads softened feedback (S027, 2017).
- **Hiring.** "If you hire the people most similar to your own culture, you lose out on the advantage of diversity." (S051, 2014).
- **Diversity and speed.** "multinational teamwork is usually a lot less efficient than monocultural teamwork" (S051, 2014). "When we're with people who come from the same tribe, we understand faster." (S048, 2021): the case for mixing is innovation, not speed.
- **Firing and performance (Meyer relaying Netflix).** "it's irresponsible for a manager not to do this" (S008, 2021). She calls the severance rule very controversial: "i know it's very controversial if you dislike it i understand" (S016, 2020). Where firing is hard, a bigger package: "if you offer enough enough money you can you can ask that person to leave" (S064, 2021).
- **Pay.** Pay top of market: "they pay top of market" (S011, 2020). "i do believe that that idea of bonuses is totally outdated" (S063, 2021).
- **Rules and process.** "employee freedom breeds innovation right and process kills flexibility" (S016, 2020; Hastings's lesson as she tells it). Not for safety-critical work: "if you are running a manufacturing plant or you're working in a safety critical environment by all means follow the methods of the industrial era" (S063, 2021).
- **KPIs.** "kpis are horrible for organizational flexibility" (S022, 2021).
- **Culture statements.** "never have I come across an organization that said here we are all about corruption right there's just no good option to Integrity" (S059, 2024).
- **Changing a company's culture.** "you can't change your organizational culture if you don't change your own behavior" (S004, 2025). Start in your own team: "you have to act within your realm of control right" (S011, 2020).
- **Candor in relationship cultures: she changed her mind.** First view, told to Hastings: "reid you can't you can't do that candor in japan you can't do that in singapore it's gonna ruin the relationships" (S064, 2021). Later: "so i i was wrong i was wrong" and "i was right in that they had to be adapted they had to be adapted and they had to be home grown" (S064, 2021, the newer view).
- **Psychological safety: she changed her mind.** First puzzled: "I just couldn't figure out how a company that was innovative like Netflix was could be focusing not on psychological safety" (S061, 2026, telling the earlier story). Later: "I don't think that psychological safety means that everyone has to feel comfy in their job" (S008, 2021): candor must be safe, comfort need not be. Newer view.
- **Corporate culture as a topic.** Earlier: "i actually was not very interested in corporate cultural differences because i found corporate cultures generally to be kind of superficial" (S017, 2021). Later co-wrote No Rules Rules.
- **Experience abroad.** "the more you know the more you know you don't know" (S004, 2025). "There's nothing more disheartening than someone saying they've worked internationally for a long time, so they know all this." (S048, 2021).
- **Virtual meetings.** Facilitators can balance who talks with chat and electronic hands (S001, S002); she prefers them to in-person for quality (S001).
- **Remote work at Netflix (Meyer relaying Hastings).** "there's no benefits because we can't come face to face when we feel like it" (S011, 2020).
- **Gender and candor.** "this woman was fired because she was really direct and then people said well she's really aggressive" (S058, 2023).
- **Immigration.** She does not give political advice (S004, 2025).
- **Salary transparency (Netflix).** "it went from being like fairness to just creating anxiety or angst" (S064, 2021); put to a vote and dropped.
- **Pricing, raising money, debt, equity, side projects, selling the company, burnout, AI, competition, timing.** No recorded position.

Added from her written essays (S068 to S085; date unsure):
- **Decision-making style.** "Both consensual and unilateral styles of decision-making can be effective" (S069). Neither is better; clashes come from mismatched expectations.
- **Confrontation in teams.** Challenges the US business literature that treats conflict as always good: "But what if you come from a culture where confrontation is downright rude?" (S076).
- **Bi-cultural leaders.** Good for travel, creativity and relationships, not automatically for leading global teams: "Don’t assume, just because Cosimo speaks three languages at the breakfast table, that he is the best person to lead your global interactions." (S074).
- **Diversity as asset.** "Managed with care, cultural diversity can become your team’s greatest asset." (S078). "the more diverse the team, the greater the potential for innovation." (S068).
- **Human nature and culture.** "At a deep level, no matter where we come from, we are driven by common physiological and psychological needs and motivations." (S078). Culture changes what feels natural.
- **Hierarchy.** Every style has costs and gains: "every style has its advantages and disadvantages." (S084). A boss in a hierarchical culture owes protection and mentoring (S075).
- **Stereotypes.** "the facile stereotypes about loud Americans and shy Chinese, only obscures the steps we can take" (S085).
- **Feedback.** Do not mimic the more direct side: "don’t try to do it like them." (S072). Offence is often cultural: "in some cases, painful incidents are the result of cross-cultural misunderstandings." (S073). Real arrogance exists too: "Both arrogance and dishonesty do exist, of course." (S073).
- **Time and customer service.** "Understanding how your international clients think about time — and adjusting your expectations accordingly — is critical" (S082).
- **Presenting and persuading.** "what you say is important, but how you say it, how you structure your message, can make all the difference" (S080).
- **Authenticity (peach among coconuts).** "if you try to be someone you’re not, it never works." (S079).
- **Culture-blind leaders.** Those who adapt effortlessly may lack empathy for those who struggle (S074).
