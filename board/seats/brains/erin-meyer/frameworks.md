# Frameworks: Erin Meyer

Built from the 55 sources in `sources.md` (2014 to 2026), now including 18 of her short written essays (S068 to S085, date unsure). Scoring lines are guidance, labelled as such, not her thresholds. Netflix items are from No Rules Rules; where she relays Hastings it says so.

## 1. Culture Map, eight scales
Communicating, evaluating (feedback), persuading, leading, deciding, trusting, disagreeing, scheduling. Countries sit on each; you compare two. Built from about 180,000 interviews in about 64 countries per S001. Use when two groups must work, sell or decide together. Guidance for scoring: list the scales where the gap is widest and fix those first. Quote: "culture mapping is a methodology that I use to break culture down into eight behavioral scales" (S001). Sources: S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S025, S049, S064.

## 2. Cultural relativity
A culture is read through the gap to the viewer. France sits between the UK and India on scheduling, so the British call it disorganised and Indians call it rigid. Use before labelling any group. Guidance: always say "relative to whom". Quote: "cultural reactions are always relative to the gap between those cultures" (S005). Sources: S002, S004, S005, S006, S007, S019, S025, S029, S049, S052.

## 3. Cognitive vs affective trust (trusting scale)
Cognitive trust comes from reliable work; affective trust from an emotional bond. UK, US, Germany, Scandinavia separate them; emerging markets weave them together at work (S005, S006). Use for any first deal or hard message. Guidance: score the bond before the ask; low bond means meals and personal talk first. Quote: "the most foundational of all of the dimensions" (S003). A second-hand line from a blogger's summary: "Once we get the trust right, then other issues become less important." (S066, second hand). Sources: S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S066.

## 4. Evaluating scale: direct vs indirect negative feedback
Separate from context. Direct cultures use upgraders, indirect ones downgraders. Americans give three positives per negative (S025); Dutch and Israelis are the most direct (S001). Use when giving criticism across cultures. Guidance: place both sides on this scale, not on general directness. Quote: "In more indirect cultures we use more down graders, like, you might possibly think about doing this a little differently, maybe." (S027). Sources: S001, S002, S003, S006, S007, S025, S027, S052.

## 5. Context scale and feedback scale cross
Low context is explicit, high context implicit; the US is the lowest context culture but indirect on negatives, France high context but direct. A culture at the same place on both scales reads as cultural; a switcher reads as one rude person. Quote: "people are much more likely to think that individual is inappropriate instead of recognizing that there's a cultural component to it" (S027). Sources: S001, S006, S025, S027, S052.

## 6. Leading and deciding are two scales
Egalitarian does not mean consensual. Denmark is egalitarian, Sweden and Japan consensual, the US egalitarian with a boss who decides; Germany more hierarchical yet consensual (S003, S007). Guidance: consensual fits costly mistakes, top-down fits speed. Quote: "I have two separate dimensions for those" (S003). Sources: S003, S007, S064.

## 7. Persuading: principles-first vs applications-first
Spain, Italy, France, Germany open with theory; the US and UK with the practical point (S002, S007). Use in pitches and presentations. Quote: "application-first versus principle-first cultures" (S007). Sources: S002, S007.

## 8. Silence and turn-taking
Overlap, ping pong and pause patterns. Americans get uncomfortable at about two and a half seconds of silence, Chinese go to seven or eight (S025). Use to run meetings and calls. Quote: "the third group loses because they're waiting for that moment to speak that never comes" (S025). Sources: S002, S025, S048, S051, S052.

## 9. Authentic flexibility
One foot rooted in your home culture, the other adapting to the person you must influence. Quote: "it's most important to be both" (S005). Sources: S005.

## 10. Cultural or personal diagnostic (unnamed)
If cultural, help everyone see it; if personal, coach the person. Quote: "In every international situation, some things are cultural, and some things are personal." (S051). Sources: S001, S005, S006, S051, S052.

## 11. Peach and coconut cultures
Peach cultures (US, Brazil) are soft and friendly outside; coconut cultures (Netherlands, Russia) hard outside, warm once the bond exists (S007). Use for first meetings. Quote: "a coconut culture, like the Netherlands, but even more so Russia" (S007). Sources: S007.

## 12. Same values, local behaviours
Keep the principle, let each country grow the technique. In Japan, spontaneous feedback failed and structured dinners worked (S063, S058). Quote: "you don't have to change your value systems, you don't have to change the underpinning reasons for why you are who you are, but you do need to let each country focus on behaviors that work in that environment" (S048). Sources: S048, S058, S063, S064.

## 13. Adapt or set a team culture
Adapt to a few, but with a whole global team set a strong team norm. Quote: "trying to adapt to every member of a global team just leads to chaos and inefficiency" (S051). Sources: S051, S002, S003.

## 14. Netflix sequence: talent density, candor, then freedom (Meyer relaying Hastings)
Hire fewer, better people; make candid feedback routine; then remove rules and controls. Order matters. Quote: "we have to do it in that order" (S063). Sources: S008, S009, S011, S015, S016, S058, S061, S062, S063, S064, S065.

## 15. Keeper test (Netflix, relayed)
Imagine the person resigns: devastated means keep, relieved means ask whether you coached them first. Quote: "the principle is called the keeper test" (S009). Sources: S008, S009, S016, S017, S021, S023, S058, S062, S063, S064.

## 16. Pyramid vs tree, lead with context not control (Netflix, relayed)
In a pyramid the leader is the bottleneck; in a tree the leader sets context at the roots and lower managers decide. Quote: "lead with context not control" (S009). Sources: S003, S004, S008, S009, S016, S022, S062, S063.

## 17. Four A's of feedback (No Rules Rules)
Aim to assist, actionable, appreciate, accept or decline. Quote: "i call them the four a's" (S015). Sources: S003, S008, S015, S017, S061, S065.

## 18. Dilemmas, not absolute positives
Write culture as the real tension, because nobody picks the opposite of integrity. Quote: "speak in dilemmas not in absolute positives" (S016). Sources: S013, S015, S016, S059, S063.

## 19. Industrial-era hangover
Rules and process fit error prevention; freedom fits innovation; split by the work. Quote: "of course when we are working towards safety critical goals rules and process are our friends" (S013). Sources: S004, S008, S009, S013, S017, S059, S063.

## Added from her written essays (S068 to S085)
These are her own written words (blog essays, first on INSEAD Knowledge or HBR, date unsure).

## 20. Decision with a lowercase d vs a capital D
Unilateral cultures (US) decide fast, early, by one person, and the decision stays open to revision, so implementation is slow. Consensual cultures (Sweden, Japan) decide slowly, then implement fast and the decision is fixed. Japanese nemawashi (informal root-binding talks) and ringi sit behind it. Guidance: on a mixed team, agree the method early: vote or boss, whether 100 percent agreement is needed, how open to later change. Quote: "A decision with a capital D, one might say." (S069). Sources: S069, S076, S077, S078.

## 21. Specific vs holistic thinking
Western, Northern European thinkers pull an item from its setting and analyse it alone; East Asians attend to background and links (Nisbett and Masuda fish and photo studies). Instruct specific thinkers with segmented tasks and deadlines; persuade holistic thinkers with the big picture. Quote: "one person sees a fish and another sees an aquarium" (S068). Sources: S068.

## 22. Power distance and Confucian reciprocity
Hofstede's term, with her question set: deference, going through the chain, what gives the boss an aura. In hierarchical cultures the higher person owes protection and mentoring. Egalitarian and hierarchical behaviours listed in S084 (disagree in public, act without approval, seating by rank, matching levels). Guidance: lead up and down the scale. Quote: "You need to develop the flexibility to manage up and down the cultural scale." (S075). Sources: S075, S084, S001, S003.

## 23. Upgraders and downgraders, with deliberate understatement
Direct cultures strengthen criticism (absolutely, totally); indirect cultures soften it (kind of, sort of, a little bit) or understate (not quite there yet). Decode by ignoring the downgraders; never copy a more direct culture, you will overshoot. The Anglo-Dutch translation guide is a joke table she uses. Quote: "There is one rule for working with cultures that are more direct than yours: don’t try to do it like them." (S072). Sources: S072, S073, S083 (with the older S027).

## 24. Depersonalised disagreement and pre-meetings
Separate idea from person: collect ideas via a third party without names, or put them anonymously on a board; hold one-on-one pre-meetings (nemawashi); if you are the boss, consider skipping the meeting. Mianzi (face) vs Sachlichkeit (objectivity) is the cultural reason. Quote: "participants can confront each idea during the meeting — without confronting the person associated with it." (S076). Sources: S076, S083.

## 25. Match cultural traits to tasks on a team
Use the map to find strengths as well as gaps: linear-time people for tight deadlines, flexible-time people for a client who changes his mind, indirect-feedback pros for a sensitive client. Fixes: team dinners, mixed sites, rotation, bi-cultural hires. Quote: "Managed with care, cultural diversity can become your team’s greatest asset." (S078). Sources: S077, S078.

## 26. Structure vs flexibility on time
Germany, Netherlands, UK, Denmark, Australia, US value structure; Brazil, India, Indonesia, Russia, Nigeria value flexibility, where stressing punctuality signals inability to adapt. Quote: "“Adaptability over punctuality” I remind myself." (S082). Sources: S082.

## 27. Culture Map used as a diagnosis on a real team
Plot the cultures on the scales and join the points; two cultures that cluster on evaluating and disagreeing can split on deciding and scheduling (Japan near Germany, China near France). Quote: "Start addressing your problem by creating a simple culture map using the eight scales." (S077). Sources: S077, S078.

Also backed by the essays: cognitive and affective trust (S070 adds: trust is an insurance policy bought up front); principles-first vs applications-first (S080, S081, with Aristotle, Bacon, Descartes, Hegel as roots and tips for each side); peach and coconut (S079, with Trompenaars and Hampden-Turner credited; ask no personal questions until they bring it up); silence and turn-taking (S071, S085, with her meeting rules); cultural vs personal (S073, S085).

## Books

### Books: Erin Meyer

Built from the 55 sources in `sources.md`. Thesis lines are in my words, not hers; no book text reproduced.

#### The Culture Map (2014)
Thesis: culture changes how people communicate, evaluate, persuade, lead, decide, trust, disagree and schedule, and the useful reading is the gap between two cultures. Key ideas: eight scales with countries placed on each, cultural relativity, cognitive vs affective trust, the negative-feedback scale separate from context. She says it started from notes on expat families in Paris (S004) and would gain a chapter on virtual work (S001). Taught in: S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S019, S025, S028, S029, S049, S051, S052.

#### No Rules Rules (2020, with Reed Hastings)
Thesis: Netflix grew by raising talent density, then candor, then removing controls, which produced freedom and flexibility. Her side: 200 interviews over three years, she reports what Hastings states and where it failed (S009, S059). Key ideas: keeper test, four A's of feedback, tree not pyramid, dilemmas not absolute positives, 360 dinners. A late chapter takes candor international (S007). Rules in it are Hastings's and Netflix's, relayed by her. Taught in: S002, S004, S007, S008, S009, S010, S011, S013, S015, S016, S017, S022, S058, S059, S061, S062, S063, S064, S065.
