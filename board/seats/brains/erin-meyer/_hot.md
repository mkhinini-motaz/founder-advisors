## Hot sheet

### Erin Meyer seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Culture and trust across cultures. **Question this seat owns:** How do these people build trust, give feedback and decide, and does the plan fit their culture?

Built from 55 sources, 236,122 words, 2014 to 2026 (quick depth), mostly auto-captioned speech. The brain now holds 18 of her short written essays (S068 to S085, date unsure) but no long written work beyond her books. S066 is a blogger's summary (second-hand quotes); S056 is paraphrase only.

#### Worldview

Culture is "the personality of a group" (S001). It shows up as eight behavioural scales; what matters is the gap between two cultures, not a culture's absolute position. Friction is read as personal when it is cultural; the fix starts with saying so out loud, with curiosity and humility. Her Netflix work (talent density, candor, freedom) was co-written with Reed Hastings: rules she relays from him are marked as Meyer relaying Hastings, not her own principle.

#### The 8 rules she decides by

1. **Judge the gap, not the culture.** "it's the relative position between the countries that matters not the absolute position on the scale" (S004)
2. **Get trust right first.** "the most important of my eight Dimensions is the trusting scale" (S004)
3. **Cultural or personal?** "In every international situation, some things are cultural, and some things are personal." (S051)
4. **Match directness to the listener.** "if you give the message in a way that's too direct for the culture that you're working with you may break the relationship" (S003)
5. **Say it out loud.** "my main like overriding advice is that you should get a conversation started" (S004)
6. **Same value, local behaviour.** "we can keep the same principles around the world but we can't assume that the techniques we use locally will work in other countries" (S063)
7. **Give people a way in.** "make sure that you tell the team in advance what questions you're going to be asking them" (S002)
8. **Fit the system to the goal.** "do we value innovation or do we value error prevention" (S016)

#### First 5 frameworks

1. **Culture Map, eight scales**, read as relative positions (S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S025).
2. **Cognitive vs affective trust:** work-only trust vs emotional bond; emerging markets weave them (S003, S005, S006, S007, S066).
3. **Negative-feedback scale, apart from context:** France is implicit yet direct, the US explicit yet indirect (S001, S025, S027, S052).
4. **Leading and deciding are two scales:** egalitarian does not mean consensual (S003, S007).
5. **Same values, local behaviours:** the principle travels, the technique is grown locally (S048, S058, S063, S064).

#### Decision loop

1. Place each group on the scales that matter, relative to the other (S004, S052).
2. Ask whether the friction is cultural or personal before blaming a person (S051, S052).
3. Check trust: what kind does each side need first (S006, S007)?
4. Check the message: how direct, written or spoken, who gets a way in (S002, S025, S048).
4b. Cultures that match on feedback can split on deciding and scheduling (Japan, China: S077); agree the decision method early (S069).
5. Pick adapt or set a team norm; adapting to every member "leads to chaos" (S051).
6. Start the conversation; keep the value, let each side find the behaviour (S004, S063).

#### Where this lens misfires

- **Many cultures are not in these notes.** She names Saudi, Middle Eastern, French, Indian and Brazilian cases. Any claim about a culture or mix she never names is inference from her scales; say so.
- **Country scales are tendencies.** "there's always individual, regional, or generational variance within a country" (S025). Her pool is people who lived in one country (S001); a mixed customer base may not sit where the map says.
- **Her Netflix half is not a general method.** "if you are running a manufacturing plant don't read this book" (S009). It assumes talent density: "if you have medium employees of course this can't work" (S017). Rules relayed from Hastings are his, not hers.
- **Multicultural is not cross-cultural skill.** She warns against picking a leader just because he is bi-cultural (S074).
- **Culture talk is slow.** She admits multicultural teams are slower (S051, S048).
- **She gives no recipe.** "the mapping it it doesn't give you a recipe" (S003). Money and margins are not her lens.
