# Frameworks: Brian Chesky

Built from 16 interviews, talks and podcast transcripts, 2014 to 2024. Most-used first. Source counts come from the extraction notes. "Score" lines are guidance, not his words.

## 1. System of trust (design trust for strangers)
- **What:** trust is a design problem. Combine profiles, two-way reviews, ID verification, online payment, messaging, 24/7 support and a damage guarantee so a stranger becomes a specific person.
- **Steps:** make the stranger specific (profile); make behavior visible (reviews on both sides); verify people before content; back it with money (guarantee); staff a human line.
- **Use when:** any marketplace where people give each other access to homes, money, time or goods.
- **Score (guidance):** for each interaction, ask if each side can see who the other is, what they did before, and what happens if it goes wrong. Any "no" is a gap.
- **Quote:** "we just need to build a system of trust to take the strange out of the stranger." (S019)
- **Sources (8):** S014, S019, S021, S027, S034, S073, S077, S091.

## 2. Principles over business decisions (crisis)
- **What:** when data is gone, write the principles first, then decide against them. Ask how you want to be remembered. Agree the principles with the board so you can make about a thousand fast decisions and elevate only one-way doors.
- **Steps:** write 4 to 6 principles (act fast, preserve cash, all stakeholders in mind, do not be villains, win the next season); check each decision against them; communicate two or three times as much.
- **Score (guidance):** a move that breaks a written principle is a no, whatever the spreadsheet says.
- **Quote:** "in a crisis, I don’t think you make business decisions as much as you make principle decisions." (S004)
- **Sources (8):** S004, S014, S016, S019, S034, S039, S073, S080.

## 3. Hundred people who love you
- **What:** perfect the experience for one person, then ten, then a hundred, before chasing a million. Those who love it become the marketing department. Credited to Paul Graham.
- **Use when:** a product has users who are "fine" but nobody who tells everyone.
- **Score (guidance):** ask if you can name 100 people who truly love it. If not, do not scale.
- **Quote:** "It's better to have 100 people love you than a million people that sort of like you." (S017)
- **Sources (4):** S004, S017, S039, S074.

## 4. Do things that don't scale, then automate once it hurts
- **What:** live with the first users, do the work by hand, and only when the hand work is painful build the tool. Photos, checks and spreadsheets went by hand first.
- **Steps:** meet users in their homes; do it yourself; hire one person; spreadsheet; then software.
- **Quote:** "you do everything unscalable before you scale it." (S019)
- **Sources (4):** S019, S055, S074, S077.

## 5. Seven-star (five to eleven-star) experience
- **What:** five stars mostly means nothing went wrong. Imagine a sixth, a seventh, then absurd stars (the Beatles check in, a trip to space), then walk back to the sweet spot you can build by hand and industrialize.
- **Use when:** "good enough" is the standard and nobody tells friends.
- **Score (guidance):** write the 5, 6, 7 and 10 star version of the key moment. If your plan equals the 5, it is forgettable.
- **Quote:** "a five-star mostly means like nothing bad happened" (S004)
- **Sources (2):** S004, S074.

## 6. Storyboard the whole experience (Snow White, service design)
- **What:** draw the journey frame by frame like a film, from booking to check-in and beyond, then design one frame at a time. Most of the trip was not in the home.
- **Quote:** "We had this process I named after the movie “Snow White.”" (S004)
- **Sources (3):** S004, S039, S074.

## 7. Design first, scale second (two brains)
- **What:** designing an experience is intuitive and empathetic; scaling is analytical and operational. Do the first with full attention, then hand to the second.
- **Quote:** "The designing of experience is a different part of your brain than the scaling your experience." (S074)
- **Sources (2):** S074, S019.

## 8. Functional organization, CEO as chief product officer
- **What:** drop the divisions. Group by expertise (design, engineering, product marketing, marketing, sales, operations), one roadmap, leaders expert in what they lead. Ten divisions became one.
- **When not to:** companies doing truly disparate things; he calls dogma the error.
- **Quote:** "I don’t think all companies should be functional, but I think they should be as functional as they can get away with." (S034)
- **Sources (7):** S004, S019, S021, S034, S039, S080, S091.

## 9. Founder mode (presence, not absence)
- **What:** the leader is in the details, partners with experts, skips levels, co-hires VPs, sets pace and standards. Start in the details, loosen as muscle memory forms. It is not micromanagement.
- **Quote:** "great leadership is presence, not absence. It’s about a leader being in the details." (S034)
- **Sources (4):** S019, S034, S091, S004.

## 10. Pull decisions in (concentric circles)
- **What:** the top 30 to 70 people work on everything together; the CEO reviews all work, so people can predict his answer. A solar system, not a pyramid.
- **Quote:** "I don’t push decision-making down the organization. I pull decision-making in." (S021)
- **Sources (5):** S004, S019, S021, S034, S091.

## 11. Rolling roadmap, review cadence, two launches a year
- **What:** a rolling two-year plan, next month fixed; every project reviewed at 1, 2, 4, 8, 12 weeks; two big releases a year as milestones and lulls; each release told as a story.
- **Quote:** "So the way we align the companies, we put everything on these deadlines twice a year." (S073)
- **Sources (4):** S019, S021, S073, S091.

## 12. Assets versus capabilities
- **What:** a brand and traffic are assets; filling airline seats is a capability. Extend only inside your capabilities, or build the new one first. Flights and transportation failed this test.
- **Quote:** "a mistake commonly people do is they confuse their assets with their capabilities." (S021)
- **Sources (2):** S021, S014 (hotels refused).

## 13. Stakeholder promises (design your company)
- **What:** list every stakeholder (employees, shareholders, guests, hosts or partners, society), write two or three promises per stakeholder as principles, measure them in the roadmap, fix the structure before VCs join the board.
- **Quote:** "You should design your company or your company will be designed for you." (S017)
- **Sources (4):** S016, S017, S021, S080.

## 14. Marketplace quality ladder
- **What:** reviews are necessary but not sufficient. Move from reviews to vetting, certifying, and apply-to-list, stopping short of owning inventory. Verify people before you moderate content.
- **Quote:** "It turns out that is necessary, but not sufficient." (S034)
- **Sources (2):** S034, S091 (Guest Favorites).

## 15. Few, unique core values, hired and fired on
- **What:** three to six values that make you you, written early, interviewed separately by people outside the function. Changed later: lists help only a little (see principles).
- **Quote:** "there have to be like three, five, six things that are unique to you" (S055)
- **Sources (2):** S055, S039 (the later correction).

## 16. Missionaries versus mercenaries
- **What:** use it to refuse a clone you could buy. Fight, localize and outlast.
- **Quote:** "In a war between missionaries and mercenaries, the missionaries always win." (S014)
- **Sources (2):** S014, S055.

## 17. Cut deep, cut once, be generous
- **What:** one deep layoff gives closure; a series creates permanent fear. Then give severance, healthcare, an alumni directory, and recruiter help.
- **Quote:** "you have to move quickly and you have to cut deep enough and cut once." (S073)
- **Sources (4):** S016, S073, S080, S018.

## 18. Explain it or do not do it (letter as forcing function)
- **What:** write the letter or keynote first; if the explanation does not sound right, the move is not right. Product managers also do marketing for the same reason.
- **Quote:** "If I can’t explain it, we can’t do it, and if it does not sound right, then it’s probably not right." (S080)
- **Sources (2):** S073, S080 (and S091 on product marketing).

## 19. Add a zero, and 80 percent passes
- **What:** imagine the goal ten times bigger to force first principles; spend 80 percent on big leaps and 20 percent on conversion optimization.
- **Quote:** "Add a zero at the end, which is to imagine something you order, imagine it bigger." (S091)
- **Sources (1):** S091.

## 20. Go to the source, then first principles
- **What:** when you have ten hours, find the defining source and interview people who did it; learn branches, not leaves.
- **Quote:** "don't learn about the tree leaves, learn about the fundamental branches." (S017)
- **Sources (2):** S017, S018.

## 21. Guests are customers, hosts are partners
- **What:** the supply side gets partner treatment: advisory board, endowment, listening tours, previews. Great guest experience starts with great host tools.
- **Quote:** "The guests are the customers, and our hosts are our partners." (S021)
- **Sources (4):** S021, S055, S080, S091.

## 22. Look to orthogonal industries
- **What:** to reinvent travel, borrow from cinema, not from hotels.
- **Quote:** "to reinvent an industry, you do not take inspiration directly from that industry, you need to look at orthogonal industries." (S074)
- **Sources (1):** S074.

## 23. Why do you deserve to exist?
- **What:** the question he puts to founders and to Airbnb itself, and the basis of the founders' letter (what would we most miss if it were gone tomorrow).
- **Quote:** "Why do you deserve to exist?" (S019)
- **Sources (3):** S014, S019, S080.

## Books

### Books: Brian Chesky

Built from 16 interviews and talks, 2014 to 2024. Brian Chesky has not written a book in these sources. The list below is the books and essays he cites, in his reading. No book text is reproduced.

- **High Output Management (Andy Grove, 1983).** Source of first principles of management and of the line that great companies are defined by a crisis. Taught in S016, S017, S019.
- **Walt Disney: The Triumph of the American Imagination (Neal Gabler, 2006).** The Go-getter chapter showed him he could build a smaller Disney; the Folly chapter on Snow White's storyboard inspired storyboarding the trip. Taught in S039.
- **Amp It Up (Frank Slootman, 2022).** The CEO sets the vision and, more, the pace and the standards. Taught in S034.
- **The Score Takes Care of Itself (Bill Walsh, 2009).** Do everything perfectly and winning follows; expect the best of your people. Taught in S017.
- **The Second Half of the Chessboard (parable, applied by Ray Kurzweil).** Exponential growth lands late, so design the company to outlive you. Taught in S017.
- **The Hero with a Thousand Faces (Joseph Campbell, 1949).** The hero's journey as the shape of a trip and of leaving a job. Taught in S016, S074.
- **Paul Graham's essays.** make something people want, do things that do not scale, a hundred people who love you. Taught in S004, S019, S055, S074.
- **Jeff Bezos's 1997 shareholder letter.** Model for the IPO letter that made shareholders partners. Taught in S080.
- **Wall-E (film).** Warning about efficiency that designs out human connection. Taught in S018.
