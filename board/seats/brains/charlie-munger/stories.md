# Stories: Charlie Munger

Built from notes on 19 sources (quick depth). Retold in short; no long quotes.

1. **Federal Express night shift.** Pay by the hour and the shift would not finish; pay by the shift and they went home when the planes were loaded. Lesson: get the incentives right. (S031, S033)
2. **Xerox commissions.** Salesmen's commissions pushed the inferior machine until the perverse incentive was found. (S031)
3. **The surgeon and the gall bladders.** He truly believed the organ caused all evil, so incentive-caused bias hid the fraud from himself. (S031)
4. **Westinghouse and Kidder Peabody.** Accounting showed instant profit on 95 percent construction loans to hotel builders; billions were lost. Lesson: bad accounting makes bad incentives. (S031)
5. **Patterson and the cash register.** Staff stole from the store; a register made it profitable, and National Cash Register followed. Lesson: block the temptation. (S031)
6. **Belridge Oil.** He bought 300 shares at $115, declined 1,500 more, and it sold to Shell near $3,700 a share. Lesson: his own deprival reaction. (S031)
7. **CBS under Paley.** Hostility to bad news left the boss in a cocoon (the Persian Messenger problem). (S031, S033)
8. **McDonnell Douglas evacuation test.** Several tendencies stacked up; they repeated a dangerous test after the first failed. Lesson: use the checklist. (S031)
9. **New Coke and the Coca-Cola exercise.** Deprival, conditioning and social proof explain both the giant success and the New Coke folly. (S070)
10. **Scandinavian canoeists.** They tried the rapids because the whirlpools were big, and all died. Lesson: stay away from big dangers. (S004, S010)
11. **Carl Braun.** He fired people who left out the why. Lesson: give the reason with every order. (S031, S033)
12. **Buffett's new loom.** In a commodity business the savings go to customers. Lesson: ask who keeps the saving. (S033)
13. **Diversified retailing.** He bought a Baltimore department store chain, saw the mistake as the ink dried, and reversed it. Lesson: leave an error quickly. (S011)
14. **The Wooden effect.** A coach put 90 percent of playing time into seven players, as Berkshire concentrates capital. (S004, S009)
15. **GM careerists.** Executives thinking like climbers, not owners, denied a hopeless position until bankruptcy. (S046)
16. **Peter Hopper.** A gifted man who gambled what he had and needed was wiped out twice. Lesson: do not risk what you need. (S048)
17. **William Miller Instruments.** His first investment was overtaken by magnetic tape and nearly lost him everything. Lesson: technology kills as well as helps. (S012)
