# Stories: Michael E. Gerber

Built from 21 sources (quick depth). Figures are his own and unchecked unless noted.

1. McDonald's and Ray Kroc. He went in for a hamburger and met a business system. Kroc never made burgers; he worked on the prototype and scaled it to 40,000 stores. Lesson: the system is the product. Sources: S001, S002, S004, S011, S015, S024.
2. Bob, Ace and the first client. At about 40 his brother-in-law Ace sent him to a client who could not turn leads into sales. Gerber knew nothing of the business, asked questions, and found each salesperson sold differently. Lesson: seeing a business as a system is the skill. Sources: S001, S007, S021, S024.
3. Encyclopedia sales script. He memorised a 15-minute script, said it the same way twice, and sold. Lesson: a script, a system, works. Sources: S001, S002, S011, S024.
4. Michael Thomas Corporation, 1977. Two partners and no firm built a turnkey program a novice delivered for about $700 a month instead of an expert at $800 a day. Lesson: the system is the asset. Sources: S004, S015, S026.
5. Infusionsoft. Founders at about $5 million revenue came to fix their business, found their dream in the Dreaming Room, and (as he reports) grew past $100 million. Lesson: the dream was missing from the company. Sources: S002, S004, S005, S015.
6. 1-800-Got-Junk. A student driving a junk truck read The E-Myth Revisited and built a national franchise (revenue as he relays it). Lesson: a new way of seeing changes what the owner does. Sources: S015.
7. Ray Kroc versus Murray. Murray kept his office in his restaurant; Kroc put corporate down the street. Lesson: where the owner's attention sits decides scale. Sources: S015.
8. Saxophone teacher. Merle Johnston set hard rules: you do not make music, music finds you when you practise. Lesson: master a script before you improvise. Sources: S011, S020, S024.
9. Sarah the baker. The first E-Myth had no stories; adding client stories made Revisited sell. Lesson: stories carry ideas. Sources: S001, S022.
10. Navy SEALs. Ninety percent drop out before they become SEALs. Lesson: hire through a hard selection system. Sources: S007, S025.
11. Walt Disney and the imagineers. Walt sent them back to go back and do the real work, and told them to bring something never seen. Lesson: New Co is invention, not repair. Sources: S020, S024.
12. Chabad rabbis. About 76 coached over three years; he called them his worst clients and quit. Lesson: a Chabad House is a McDonald's, but not every client can be moved. Sources: S002.
13. Eightieth birthday. A surprise event with 220 in the room and a reported 83,000 online launched Beyond the E-Myth; he treats the E-Myth as Old Co. Sources: S004, S020.
14. Dreaming Rooms. He led 59 himself before certified facilitators took over. Lesson: prototype the rollout yourself first. Sources: S005, S011.
