# Frameworks: Michael E. Gerber

Built from 21 sources (quick depth). S015, S019, S026 are one interview and S001, S022 are one; each group counts once in "sources". Scoring lines are guidance built from his thresholds, not his words.

## 1. Franchise prototype (the practice, three-legged stool)
- What: the unit of business that can be copied: lead generation, lead conversion and client fulfillment working together as one written system. Kroc did not make burgers; he built a prototype so it could scale.
- Parts: lead generation, lead conversion, client fulfillment, all turnkey.
- Use when: judging whether a unit, shop, city or product is ready to be repeated.
- Score (guidance): 0 if it works only because one person is there; 1 if fulfillment is written but acquisition is not; 2 if all three legs are documented and a novice has run them.
- Quote: "The Practice is the three-legged stool. The Practice is lead generation, lead conversion [and] client fulfillment, that's your franchise prototype." (S020)
- Sources: 6 (S005, S014, S020, S021, S024, S011). Inferred from S011 for the Kroc point.

## 2. Turnkey system
- What: define the process, so the content can be defined, so someone who knows nothing about the field delivers the same result. "Any way you want" is not a system.
- Use when: someone says only they can do it, or every case is different.
- Score: can a stranger follow step one, two, three and get the outcome?
- Quote: "the opportunity was to create a turnkey consulting system that I could hand off to somebody who didn't know anything about business." (S015)
- Sources: 8 (S004, S005, S011, S015, S020, S021, S024, S026).

## 3. Eightfold path (hierarchy of growth)
- What: dream, vision, purpose, mission (the Dreaming Room, the strategic half), then job, practice, business, enterprise (the tactical half). Do them in order.
- Use when: someone wants to grow before the base is built.
- Score: name the step the company is at; anything above it is premature.
- Quote: "the Eightfold Path is the process for growing a company of one to a company of 1000" (S007)
- Sources: 8 (S004, S005, S007, S011, S020, S021, S024, S025).

## 4. Dream, vision, purpose, mission
- What: dream is the great result; vision is the form the company takes; purpose is the consumer; mission is the turnkey system. Four personalities carry them: dreamer, thinker, storyteller, leader.
- Use when: a team cannot say what the company is for.
- Quote: "The dream is the great result you're going to produce, the vision is the form the business is going to take to produce it. The purpose is your consumer." (S015)
- Sources: 7 (S002, S004, S005, S011, S015, S020, S024).

## 5. Technician, manager, entrepreneur
- What: three roles in every company. Most owners are technicians suffering from an entrepreneurial seizure: they turn a skill into a job and become their own boss.
- Use when: an owner is the best doer and says there is no time.
- Quote: "The heart of every small business is a technician suffering from an entrepreneurial seizure." (S008)
- Sources: 7 (S001, S008, S014, S022, S023, S024). Counting S001/S022 once: 5.

## 6. Company of one
- What: any company that would not work if the owner were not there, whatever the headcount. The test is dependence.
- Quote: "You could have fifty contractors. But if you have to be there, you've got a company of one." (S020)
- Sources: 4 (S014, S015, S020, S004).

## 7. Old Co / New Co
- What: do not fix the broken business. Keep Old Co running to pay bills, design New Co on paper with beginner's mind, as a separate entity (a skunk works), then merge and let Old Co fade.
- Use when: a team wants to patch a messy operation.
- Score: is there a separate, written design, with a named share of attention?
- Quote: "We're not here to fix what we've got, we're here to create what we don't." (S015)
- Sources: 6 (S004, S005, S007, S015, S020, S025).

## 8. Design, build, launch and grow
- What: the cycle applied to every system and every step of the ladder.
- Quote: "We're going to design it, build it, launch it and grow it and turnkey it." (S004)
- Sources: 4 (S004, S009, S011, S025).

## 9. Innovation, quantification, orchestration
- What: continuous improvement of the turnkey system: improve it, count it, orchestrate it. Metrics are set at the start, funnel step by step.
- Quote: "the game I taught you in E-Myth is innovation, quantification and orchestration. It's called continuous improvement." (S004)
- Sources: 3 (S004, S005, S023).

## 10. Business and enterprise ladder (7 x 7)
- What: a business is up to seven turnkey practices plus a turnkey management system; an enterprise is up to seven turnkey businesses plus a turnkey leadership system. The business is a district; the enterprise a region.
- Use when: planning the second and third unit or location.
- Quote: "A business is nothing other than up to seven turnkey practices, plus a turnkey management system." (S024)
- Sources: 5 (S004, S011, S020, S021, S024).

## 11. Order of build
- What: client fulfillment first, validated even if given away; then client acquisition fills the technicians to capacity; then add units.
- Score: never fund marketing while fulfillment is not turnkey.
- Quote: "Now we have a turn key client acquisition system can fill to capacity technician number 1, technician number 2, technician number 3. Now you're ready to grow." (S020)
- Sources: 3 (S005, S011, S020).

## 12. Franchise as distribution
- What: a franchise is exactly like the prototype, not roughly. A franchise is a model for expanding reach. Entrepreneurs invent franchises; buyers are technicians.
- Quote: "Franchising is simply a model for expanding the reach of your company, it's a distribution model." (S005)
- Sources: 4 (S005, S014, S020, S024).

## 13. Seven centers of management attention
- What: leadership, marketing, finance, management, then lead generation, lead conversion, client fulfillment.
- Quote: "it is leadership first, it's marketing second, it's finance third, it's management fourth, it's lead-generation/lead-conversion and client fulfillment" (S023)
- Sources: 2 (S015 named only, S023).

## 14. Five essential functions
- What: inspiration, education, application, implementation, continuous improvement; keeps a client on track.
- Quote: "That's called system of five essential functions – inspiration, education, application, implementation [and] continuous improvement." (S020)
- Sources: 1.

## 15. Innovation versus creativity
- What: innovation alters what is; creativity makes what is not. Seed question: what is missing in this picture?
- Quote: "Innovation is really the alteration of what is. Creativity is the creation of what isn't." (S020)
- Sources: 2 (S020, S024 for the question).

## 16. Apprenticeship, craftsmanship, mastery
- What: how a person becomes a master of the system.
- Quote: "Anybody can do it who has been taken through apprenticeship, craftsmanship and mastery." (S020)
- Sources: 2 (S020, S011).

## Books

### Books: Michael E. Gerber

Built from 21 sources (quick depth). Thesis lines are in my own words; he says 32 E-Myth books exist (S006).

#### The E-Myth (first edition, 1986)
Thesis: small businesses are started by technicians, not entrepreneurs. Frameworks: technician, manager, entrepreneur. He now calls it "the expression of a problem" and Old Co (S020). It had no stories (S022). Sources: S020, S022.

#### The E-Myth Revisited (1995)
Thesis: the same idea told through client stories, notably Sarah the baker. He says it sold over 2 million copies (S001) and defining what "right" means for a business (S006). His one book to read (S002, S011). Frameworks: working on the business, the franchise prototype, the McDonald's model. Taught in S001, S002, S004, S006, S011, S015.

#### Beyond the E-Myth (about 2016)
Thesis: how to grow from a company of one to a company of 1,000, in under 113 pages by his count (S015). Frameworks: the eightfold path, Dreaming Room, turnkey ladder, Old Co / New Co. He says it takes them beyond everything he wrote before (S026). Taught in S004, S005, S007, S015, S020, S025.

#### Awakening the Entrepreneur Within (2008)
Thesis: the entrepreneur as creator; the four personalities and the scale of the dream. Taught in S002, S014, S023.

#### E-Myth vertical books
About 22 industry books with co-authors (S024), including attorney, accountant, chiropractor, pest control (S006, S021). They apply the model to one trade. Taught in S006, S021, S024.

#### E-Myth Enterprise and E-Myth Mastery
Forthcoming at the time of S023: seven centers of management and seven disciplines. Taught in S023.

#### Making It on Your Own in America
A free book that is the curriculum of Radical U (S002).

#### Books he recommends
Banker to the Poor (Muhammad Yunus) (S001, S022); Marketing for Business Growth (Theodore Levitt) (S011); Principles (Ray Dalio) (S021); Marty Sklar on Disney (S024). He says he does not read business books (S011, S020).
