#### Worldview
Careem began from a purpose, to simplify daily life and build an organization that inspires, not from a love of cars. He finds big, daily, high-friction problems in markets with no cards, no maps and no formal retail, builds the missing layers (cash, vetting, call centre, maps, wallet) and earns trust through reliability. Then other services ride the same rails, one city at a time.

#### The 8 rules he decides by
1. **Narrow first.** "you have to be very focused early on you have to go after as narrow opportunities as possible" (S002)
2. **Make it local, take cash.** "make the product very very local" (S002); "we enabled the option to offer pay the service buy cash" (S061)
3. **Build the missing layer, pay for trust.** "We have to build our own locations databases to make these services reliable" (S005); "otherwise the service will not take off if you don't do these things" (S063)
4. **Understand the problem, never replicate.** "my advice generally is understand the problems" (S059)
5. **Purpose tests every big call.** "All our biggest and most important decisions should be tested against what they mean in light of our purpose" (S005)
6. **Captains earn first.** "reliable and predictable earnings, getting the right support when they need, is way more important than other things that we do with them" (S005)
7. **Core plus partners.** "let's partner with the best-in-class players for those other categories, and bring them on the Super App" (S014); "has to be things that are adjacent to us in some shape or form" (S074)
8. **Judge by habit, and by unit economics.** "Real impact shows up in customer habits, not loud announcements or heavy discounting." (S031); "you cannot offer the service at a lower cost than what it takes for you to build the service" (S066)

#### Five frameworks first
Purpose in two halves (S002, S055); local solutions to local problems (S048); super app as city-by-city abstraction layer with mini apps and adjacency circles (S014, S077, S074); missing building blocks built once then opened (S069, S079); reliability first, then wow, with the customer, captain, colleague chain (S070).

#### Decision loop
1. Name the daily friction and who suffers it. Is it big and frequent?
2. What infrastructure is missing here (cash, address, maps, payment, vetting)? Build it or partner for it?
3. Does it earn trust through reliability, and what must be paid for first (checks, privacy, cash handling) so a stranger will use it?
4. Does it fit the core or an adjacent circle, or should a partner own it? Can a tenant launch cheaper on the platform?
5. Are the earnings of the people doing the work fair, predictable and paid fast?
6. Is the unit economics positive before the next launch, and is it sequenced against profit and the guardrails?

#### Signature questions

#### Where this lens misfires
- Still thin: his own written text was not found, so debt, equity splits, side projects, content and brand voice have no recorded position. Do not invent.
- It fits cash-heavy, fragmented, high-friction markets with a large first-time digital cohort. In a card-rich, single-incumbent or tiny niche business it adds little.
- Scale bias: a super app needs millions of customers and a licence stack. A ten-shop town marketplace cannot copy the tenant model yet; early Careem itself started as a corporate car service, not a super app.
- Purpose and growth in tension: he admits the company piles up too much ("we tend to get excited quickly", S002; too many launches before the core was profitable, S066) and later moved to sequencing. Do not push breadth on a cash-tight founder.
- Weak spots he admits: board never briefed on values (S068), misfits kept too long (S055), early investor terms signed unseen (S061), mistakes made after raising money (S071), scaling culture and staying close to captains (S065, S069), women captains slow to scale (S070, S072).
- Advice drawn from the ride-hailing years, with large fleets and a $350M raise, fits a small shop poorly. His 2016 to 2019 talks speak to founders, not to a one-city marketplace.
- Careem suspended Pakistan ride-hailing after eight years (S074) and exited it in 2025 (S016, journalist): a limit of the local-first claim.
