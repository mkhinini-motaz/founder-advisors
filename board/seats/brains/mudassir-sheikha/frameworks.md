# Frameworks: Mudassir Sheikha

Built from 26 sources (2019 to 2026), topped up with batches 03 to 05 to 44 ok sources (2016 to 2026), quick depth. Most-used first. Scoring guidance is labeled as guidance and is the builder's reading, not his thresholds, unless a number is quoted.

## 1. Purpose in two halves (unnamed)
Free people's time from daily hassles, then build an organization that inspires them to use it on big things. Purpose is set once and never changes; strategy changes yearly.
- Steps: state the functional half, state the inspirational half, test every big decision against both.
- Use when: choosing a business, a hire, an exit, or a new service.
- Scoring (guidance): a decision that fails the purpose test is out, however good the return.
- Quote: "simplify the lives of people and build an awesome organization that inspires" (S002)
- Sources: S002, S005, S014, S018, S029, S052, S055, S056, S057 (9)
- Top-up: same two lines in S064, S070, S071 (+3, total 12). "simplify the lives of people and build an awesome organization that inspires" (S064)

## 2. Every problem is an opportunity (unnamed)
Look for daily, high-friction, under-served problems. The bigger the problem, the bigger the opportunity.
- Use when: picking what to build next.
- Scoring (guidance): rank candidates by how often the friction hits and how many people suffer it; ignore passion for the product.
- Quote: "The bigger the problem, the bigger the opportunity." (S016)
- Sources: S016, S031, S055, S029, S059 (5)
- Top-up: "you have to find a problem to and the bigger the problem the bigger the opportunity" (S071); "every problem is an opportunity the bigger the problem the bigger the opportunity" (S076, about 2018). Also S060, S075 (+4, total 9).

## 3. Local solutions to local problems (unnamed)
Make the product very local: accept cash, run a call centre, build your own maps, build wallet rails. Use foreign ideas as inspiration, never obsession.
- Use when: entering a market where cards, addresses or infrastructure are weak.
- Scoring (guidance): count the pieces of the experience that depend on infrastructure the market lacks; each one is something to build or partner for.
- Quote: "We find local solutions to local problems. This is what we built Careem on" (S048)
- Sources: S002, S005, S048, S050, S055, S059 (6)
- Top-up: "feel free to get inspired by what's happening in the rest of the world but the focus and the obsession needs to be with local problems" (S078, 2017). Also "if you are obsessed with while others are doing you're going to miss the boat" (S061, 2016). Also S063, S064, S067, S070 (+5, total 12).

## 4. Void to fill, not an incumbent to displace
In a market with no public transport, maps or card payments, you build the layers beneath the service.
- Quote: "There is a void that had to be filled versus something that had to be displaced" (S005)
- Sources: S005, S056 (2)
- Top-up: "we are filling a void that existed" (S069, 2017); "it's a big white space it's a big void" (S076, about 2018). Also S070, S079 (+4, total 6).

## 5. Super app as abstraction layer and city-by-city shelf
The platform carries licences, payments, SMS and customers, so a third party builds once and runs in many cities. It is won one city at a time, not by region or country.
- Parts: shared login, payment and address; delivery rails; tenant partners.
- Use when: judging whether a service should live on the platform or outside it.
- Scoring (guidance): can a partner launch cheaper on the platform than alone, and does the city already have the customer base?
- Quote: "The Super App is really a city-by-city game" (S014)
- Quote: "we're trying to create an abstraction layer where people can build their businesses" (S002)
- Sources: S002, S012, S014, S048, S056 (5)
- Top-up, the platform as he built it later: "the super app is a is a platform" (S074, 2025); "most services over time will be provided by partners" (S066, 2022); "anyone that wants to target our customers can build their application on the korean super app" (S064, 2022, transcript spelling). Also S067, S071, S072, S077 (+6, total 12).

## 6. Core plus best-in-class partners
Do only what is close to your core (logistics, some payments). Bring best-in-class tenants for the rest.
- Quote: "let's partner with the best-in-class players for those other categories, and bring them on the Super App" (S014)
- Sources: S014, S031, S051 (3)
- Top-up: "has to be things that are adjacent to us in some shape or form" (S074, 2025); "we want to be a platform and not get into more things directly than we need" (S067, 2023). Also S064 (+3, total 6).

## 7. Acquire once, monetize many (supply and demand synergies)
A customer who rides also orders food. A captain who moves people at peak hours delivers food in quiet hours. Multi-service customers are reported as staying longer and transacting more (journalist's figures, 1.5x and 3x, inferred from S014).
- Quote: "we have supply synergies as well" (S004)
- Sources: S002, S004, S014, S050 (4)
- Top-up: "we have proven the super app model in dubai now we're seeing people using multiple services ending up engaging on the platform way deeper than we've ever seen before" (S065, 2022); "instead of downloading another app people were more comfortable in the same app" (S077, 2020). Also S066 (Careem Plus subscription) (+3, total 7).

## 8. Impact first, profit as byproduct, with guardrails
Solve for impact toward the purpose and let profit follow. Guardrails stop the slippery slope: no buy-now-pay-later, no stock trading.
- Scoring (guidance): a revenue line that profits from customers living above their means fails.
- Quote: "you focus on driving impact and the byproduct is profits" (S005)
- Quote: "we are not going to sell dope on the super app" (S005)
- Sources: S005 (1)
- Top-up: "is that if you do the right thing other things follow." (S067, 2023); "we should always support the right thing and let the Business and Economics figure it way out" (S070, 2019). Also S063, S069 (+3, total 4).

## 9. Cash to digital rails
Cash makes each transaction cost more. Accept cash first because captains and customers lack cards and bank accounts, then build wallets, bills and remittances around them.
- Quote: "if you keep relying on cash, the ultimate cost of any transaction increases, because cash is not convenient" (S048)
- Sources: S002, S004, S019, S048, S050, S051, S055 (7)
- Top-up, how cash was handled in practice: "Literally every day we had bags of cash from captains and people were going to bank accounts to deposit that cash." (S067, 2023); "we enabled the option to offer pay the service buy cash" (S061, 2016); "a lot of the business in the region happens on cash" (S072, about 2021). Also S064, S070, S077 (+5, total 12).

## 10. Five-dollars-to-one rule (unnamed)
When out-funded, each dollar must do the work of five: creative stunts, a local product, quality hires.
- Quote: "every dollar that we spend has to match the five dollars that our competition is spending in our Market" (S002)
- Sources: S002, S055 (2)
- Top-up: "for every one dollar that we spend they were able to spend three or four dollars" (S071, 2019); "we have been competing against 10 times our balance sheet." (S069, 2017). Also S065 (+3, total 5).

## 11. Values as a filter, with a weekly failure review
Purpose, then values tested by five questions each, then hiring, promotion and firing by those values. Half a day every Thursday goes to bad trips and ratings, why they happened, and the product change.
- Quote: "these values are a filter to hiring promoting and firing of people" (S055)
- Quote: "we blocked half a day on Thursday which was sort of the the learning time right" (S055)
- Sources: S055, S014, S057 (3)
- Top-up: "on day one of green before we did anything else we wrote down the first version of our values" (S065, 2022); "every week every sort of Thursday which is the Friday in Dubai we would make a list of all the failures that we have had in the week" (S071, 2019). Also S061, S074 (+4, total 7).

## 12. Product-market fit as growth under constraint
Fit means growing fast on constrained resources, because money alone can buy growth.
- Scoring (guidance): growth that stops when the discount stops is not fit.
- Quote: "my definition of a broad market fit is you are growing rapidly within constrained resources" (S055)
- Sources: S055, S031, S002 (3)
- Top-up: "that's when you need the money to grow" (S076, about 2018, after product-market fit); "let's just launch it and we see what the market tells us" (S061, 2016). Also S078 (+3, total 6).

## 13. Genuine customer dependence test
Judge by habit, not announcements or discounts.
- Quote: "Real impact shows up in customer habits, not loud announcements or heavy discounting." (S031)
- Quote: "Are people quietly weaving your product into their weekly routines because it authentically simplifies things for them?" (S031)
- Sources: S031, S002 (2)

## 14. Plan to the most conservative case; cut once, keep investing
In a shock, make one deep cut on a pessimistic projection, move fast, and keep funding what matters.
- Quote: "we basically asked ourselves what's the most conservative projection that we can make" (S058)
- Quote: "The people that survive are the ones that move the fastest, not the biggest." (S052)
- Sources: S058, S052, S019, S012 (4)
- Top-up: "we are pretty much going back to the basics and these are not the wrong basics" (S066, 2022). Also S067 (+2, total 6).

## 15. Growth and profit in sequence
After 2021 he moved to profit before pace: make one product in a few markets profitable before the next. Split the profitable core from the capital-hungry bet.
- Quote: "But we just cannot do them simultaneously anymore" (S012)
- Quote: "You cannot have this or that- you have to find the right balance" (S014)
- Sources: S012, S014, S019, S052 (4)
- Top-up: "Let's grow sequentially. Let's make the right bets with the profits that we make." (S067, 2023); "our current focus is to make sure that we go deeper in our existing countries" (S072, about 2021). Also S066, S077 (+4, total 8).

## 16. People, things, money
The three services of the super app, in his words at launch: moving people, moving things, moving money. Payments are the most critical bottleneck.
- Quote: "the mobility of people, the mobility of things and the mobility of money" (S049)
- Sources: S019, S048, S049, S050 (4)
- Top-up: S072 names the same three businesses (ride-hailing, deliveries, payments) and says the recovery from COVID ran 10x, 4x and 2x (paraphrase). Also S066, S077 (+3, total 7).

## Added by the top-up (batches 03 to 05)

## 17. Smallest segment first, perfect it, then the adjacent one (unnamed)
A big vision is reached in waves. Careem began with a corporate transport service for consulting firms, then airport trips, then women in Saudi. Each wave is a segment that looks like the last.
- Use when: choosing the first customer for a cash-market service.
- Scoring (guidance): can you name the smallest group whose problem you can get perfect? If the first wave is broad, it is wrong.
- Quote: "you have to build something for the smallest segment of customers uh possible and then you have to get it right get it perfect" (S060)
- Quote: "every time we've done it this way we have succeeded every time we've forgotten this way we have failed" (S060)
- Sources: S060, S061, S064, S065, S067 (5)

## 18. Enabler, not disruptor (unnamed)
Start as a technology layer on a licensed, traditional operator (limousine firms, then Dubai taxis), earn trust, then co-build deeper. Where the old system is weak, enable things for the first time.
- Use when: entering a regulated market where incumbents hold the licences.
- Scoring (guidance): does the incumbent gain from your layer on day one? If not, you are disrupting, not enabling.
- Quote: "so our model basically is let's find the right technology layer on the top initially" (S070)
- Quote: "we really see ourselves as an enabler in the region" (S079)
- Sources: S070, S079, S069, S063 (4)

## 19. Missing building blocks, built once then opened (unnamed)
Cards, payments, last mile, messaging and maps are missing or thin in the region, so building any service costs more. Careem built them to make its own service work, then offered them to others.
- Parts: location database and maps, payment stack with cash, call centre in local languages, vetting, dispatch.
- Use when: judging how much of a service you must build yourself in a low-infrastructure market.
- Scoring (guidance): list the blocks the service needs; each missing block is a build or a partner, and the full list sets the real launch cost.
- Quote: "You need credit cards. You need payments. You need last mile delivery. You need messaging infrastructure. You need good maps." (S069)
- Quote: "we've had to build our own location database, our own maps, our own payment systems" (S079)
- Sources: S061, S063, S064, S069, S070, S071, S072, S076, S079 (9)

## 20. Reliability, then wow, then proactive; customer, captain, colleague chain (unnamed)
Three pillars of customer experience in order, and a chain: the captain shapes the customer's experience, the customer and staff shape the captain's, the company shapes the staff's.
- Use when: deciding where to put service effort.
- Scoring (guidance): if a car (or order) does not reliably arrive, nothing above it counts. Test the rebound on a failure, not the average day.
- Quote: "it really all starts with reliability if you are not able to deliver a reliable service then you're not building trust with people" (S070)
- Quote: "if you have an unhappy captain, you have an unhappy customer" (S072)
- Sources: S070, S072, S077, S063 (4)

## 21. Pay for trust where trust is the barrier (unnamed)
Where strangers must trust each other, buy the trust: a background-check firm (the one used by the US consulate), GPS pins of city and village home, police and neighbour checks in Pakistan, hidden phone numbers for women, becoming the regulator.
- Use when: the first customers fear a stranger in the car, the house or the money.
- Scoring (guidance): name the fear in the customer's mother's words; if the product does not answer it before launch, the service will not take off.
- Quote: "that's what you need to do in a place like Pakistan to make sure that you're able to offer a safe service that people will trust" (S063)
- Quote: "we are effectively becoming the regulator because we need to now ensure that our cars are safe" (S063)
- Sources: S061, S063, S070, S076 (4)

## 22. Super app architecture and mini apps (unnamed)
One sign-on, one loyalty programme, one payments layer (cards, bank transfers, wallet), many services. Outside developers publish a mini app through Careem's APIs and skip their own app, payment gateway, delivery fleet and marketing.
- Use when: judging whether a service belongs on the shared layer.
- Quote: "they can build a mini app through the apis that we publish" (S077)
- Quote: "a common payments layer" (S077)
- Sources: S077, S064, S066, S071, S074 (5)

## 23. Concentric circles of adjacency (unnamed)
Run the core services yourself (mobility, food, grocery, payments); open the platform to partners only in adjacent services (car rental, home services). Each new partner opens the next circle. A partner that outgrows you is success.
- Scoring (guidance): would a customer think of the app first for this service? If not, it is too far out.
- Quote: "co-entric circles that we can think of to expand into new services" (S074, transcript spelling)
- Quote: "we consider that to be our success" (S074)
- Sources: S074, S067 (2)

## 24. Extract the payments stack from the cash business (unnamed)
Careem built a cash-plus-digital payments stack because nothing else existed, then branded it Careem Pay and offered it to merchants. Daily or instant payouts to captains were used to shift the cash habit.
- Quote: "we have extracted the stack, we have branded this thing" (S072)
- Quote: "what we're calling daily payouts" (S077)
- Sources: S072, S077, S064, S067, S060 (5)

## 25. Fixed subscription instead of commission (unnamed)
Restaurants pay a fixed fee for a place on the super app plus technology plus access to its customers (48 million signed up), against a 20 to 30 percent aggregator commission.
- Scoring (guidance): used only in one source and one market (UAE food); treat as a stance, not a proven rule.
- Quote: "forget about commission, forget about a high commission — pay a fixed subscription" (S072)
- Sources: S072 (1)

## 26. Frugality from dollar-denominated costs (the 70-cent trip) (unnamed)
A 70 cent motorbike trip in Pakistan, a 20 percent take is 14 cents; hosting 5, maps 3, other systems 2, SMS 1, leaving about 4 cents before support. Costs are in dollars, incomes are not.
- Scoring (guidance): do the per-trip arithmetic in local prices before approving any cost; price at cost plus a bit, never below the cost to build and deliver.
- Quote: "if we're not frugal we're not going to be able to serve the masses in our region sustainably" (S065)
- Quote: "you cannot offer the service at a lower cost than what it takes for you to build the service" (S066)
- Sources: S065, S066, S078, S079 (4)

## 27. Marketplace chicken-and-egg: supply first (unnamed)
Start on the captain side in the slow season; later guarantee supply a daily revenue so cars are there before demand.
- Quote: "you have to invest some money on one side of the equation to get the supply up and then you focus on the demand" (S061)
- Sources: S061, S078 (2)

## 28. Big and meaningful, the 2x2 (unnamed)
A founding screen: every idea must be very big and meaningful. He and Magnus plotted sectors on two axes; education and health scored on both but nothing clicked, so transport. Later also challenging and fun.
- Quote: "you build a 2x2 matrix" (S074)
- Quote: "Whatever we end up doing should have huge potential, should have the opportunity or the ability to become very, very big." (S069)
- Sources: S061, S069, S071, S074, S075 (5)

## 29. Five local lessons and growth cadence (unnamed)
Solve local problems, iterate, hire awesome people whatever it takes, find local allies, have a purpose. Track growth every 15 minutes; 30 percent monthly needs about 7 percent weekly.
- Quote: "solve local problems be quick and iterate hire awesome people whatever it takes get local allies on board and discover your purpose" (S078)
- Sources: S061, S078 (2)

## 30. Power law of talent, equity for everyone (unnamed)
A top performer is worth far more than an average one; use equity, acqui-hires and persistence to get them, and give equity to everyone.
- Quote: "a top performer is 20 times more productive and more valuable than an average performer" (S078)
- Quote: "talent is worth a lot more than you think and use equity as much as possible" (S061)
- Sources: S061, S078, S076, S065 (4)

## 31. Platformise what you learned once (unnamed)
Dispatch and captains built for ride-hailing are reused by food, grocery and order anything; the core cash generator limits how many new businesses can be funded.
- Quote: "that capability doesn't have to be built in every vertical again and again so that capability has been platformitized" (S066)
- Quote: "the amount of new business and expansion that you can do will be limited by the cash that you're generating from the core business" (S066)
- Sources: S066, S067, S064 (3)

## 32. Four phases and three chapters of Careem (unnamed)
Phases: scrappy product, regional expansion, many products in many markets, business lines under their own CEOs. Chapters: underdog, Uber years, everything app. He uses the frame to say what the fight is now.
- Quote: "I would say there have been maybe four phases" (S066)
- Quote: "the fight for survival has ended." (S067)
- Sources: S066, S067 (2)

#### Added by the top-up (batches 03 to 05)
Still no book written by him. Books and essays he cites:
- **The Lean Startup** (Eric Ries, 2011). Core thesis: ship a minimum viable product and learn from customers. He read it as a first-year founder and found the market wanted different features than planned. Taught in S061, S075, S078.
- **The Hard Thing About Hard Things** (Ben Horowitz, 2014). He calls it therapeutic for a scaling founder; the line he takes is that there is no right CEO, only the right person for the right role at the right time. Taught in S071, S075.
- **Made in America** (Sam Walton). Walton shared hotel rooms with executives so savings reached customers; he uses it for frugality. S065.
- **Paul Graham, "do things that don't scale"** (essay). S075.
- **Masters of Scale** (podcast, Reid Hoffman), the Tel Aviv ambition episode. S071.
- Steve Jobs's Stanford commencement ("connect the dots") and Elon Musk's working-backwards habit, in passing. S060, S067.
