# Stories: Fadi Ghandour

Built from 44 ok sources. Facts as the notes record them; quotes are from the speaker.

1. **Founding Aramex, 1982.** With Bill Kingson, after seeing FedEx while studying in the US, he set out to build a Middle East courier firm. Quote: "We simply said let's give it a shot." (S010). Lesson: ignorance helped. Also "I was never afraid of failure because I was a bit stupid" (S060). Sources: S010, S014, S055, S060, S065.
2. **Airborne refuses 50 percent.** He offered Airborne a large stake for $50,000 to $100,000 and was refused; Airborne referred him to its London head, and later took 9 percent for $2 million (S014, S055, S059). Lesson: the big carrier became the first partner and capital source.
3. **Wholesale model.** Aramex delivered inbound for carriers with no regional office, learning tracking and systems from them. Quote: "everything I know in my business is seven years of delivering packages for Federal Express" (S046). Sources: S010, S011, S046, S055.
4. **Finding partners city by city.** Months on the road signing small local partners, Beirut after 1982, and partners abroad such as Beijing paid a few dollars a package (S010, S062). Lesson: global from day one forced alliances.
5. **Lebanon during the civil war.** Drivers swapped packages at a midday sniper cease-fire; a hub in Larnaca when Beirut airport closed; Shop and Ship started when Lebanese wanted a US or UK address (S064). Lesson: sticking around built trust that lasted years.
6. **First Gulf War.** A bank officer asked him to move backup tapes to Dubai every night, and FedEx left Kuwait and Bahrain, which gave Aramex more deliveries (S064, S059). Lesson: reliability in chaos earns the account.
7. **The NASDAQ listing, 1997.** A private placement in the Arab world failed, so they went public in the US; Bill proposed it and he said yes in ten minutes (S055). Raised more than target (S007); stock stayed microcap; bought back with Abraaj in 2002 and relisted in Dubai in 2005 . Lesson: public markets gave discipline, not cash.
8. **The Saudi licence.** Two founding partners said it was impossible; it took 14 years and the business grew sharply afterwards (S060, S062). Lesson: keep knocking.
9. **2009 downturn.** Revenue fell about 10 percent, net income rose 25 percent, no staff fired; airlines came begging (S045, S062, S049). Lesson: asset-light and debt-free means you buy in a slow market.
10. **DHL buys Airborne, 2003.** Aramex celebrated, built its own shipment system in Amman, and its web tracking replaced Airborne's, leading to a global alliance of 40 providers (S059, S032). Lesson: a rival's move can free you.
11. **Liquidity crisis and the stolen cash.** First years: payroll first, little sleep, a large cash shipment stolen, years of legal case (S007). First 2 to 3 years hard, 14 years to first serious capital (S059).
12. **Maktoob.** He backed the Jordanian portal around 2000; Yahoo bought it in 2009 (about $165 to $175 million), and its alumni started about fifteen companies (S049, S060, S066). Lesson: one visible exit lifts a whole ecosystem.
13. **Ruwwad in Jabal al-Nadhif.** The first community meeting read out a 1967 letter promising a police station; Ruwwad fixed the school in six months, then a clinic, post office, library, centre (S063). Lesson: trust comes from delivering what you promise.
14. **Jamalon.** A Ruwwad scholar from East Amman who sat beside him from age 16 and asked for $1,000 for a website, then raised $400,000 and built a company; first staff his mother and brother (S063, S066). Lesson: be ready when the founder is.
15. **Careem.** He passed on early rounds because Uber looked too strong, then joined once Careem localised with cash payments (S007). Lesson: local adaptation can beat a giant, and he admits his first misjudgment.
16. **His son and Theeb.** He pushed his son toward engineering; the son became a film worker and the film Theeb went to the Oscars (S062, S066). Lesson: his own pressure was wrong.
17. **Leaving the CEO job.** He planned for years, stayed through the 2008 crisis, and the stock did not move when he left (S062, S064). Lesson: the best result is that the firm does better after you.
18. **Roger Bannister.** The four-minute mile was broken again soon after; a model of why role models matter (S048, S050).

## Added in the top-up (S067 to S091). Facts as the notes record them; where he tells a figure two ways, both are given with ids.
19. **Silver Streak, Kingston, and the pivot from competing to being the foreign majors' Arab arm.** Age about 23, with Bill Kingston of Silver Streak (a small courier in four north-eastern US states); they met in Paris. The first plan was a FedEx for the Arab world; within months they saw that Aramex could deliver for the foreign carriers that had parcels for the Arab world and no way to deliver them. Quote: "لنكون ذراعهم في التوصيل في العالم العربي" (S088) [to be their arm for delivery in the Arab world]. Lesson: the first plan (compete) was not the plan that worked (partner). Sources: S088, S089; S090 (host's telling, unverified).
20. **First client, first capital, first salary.** First client the Housing Bank in Jordan, October 1982, a letter to New York at about 25 dinars, collected by himself. First capital about half a million dollars, spent on a small office in each city, rented vans and staff. His salary stayed about 600 dollars for a long time. A first bank sale meant explaining what a courier was; the bank said it used the post. He went by taxi to Jeddah in a suit. Quote: "25 دينار يعني حوالي 35 دولار" (S089) [25 dinars, about 35 dollars]. Quote: "اول راس مال يمكن نص مليون دولار" (S089) [first capital maybe half a million dollars]. Lesson: the founder does everything. Sources: S088, S089.
21. **Naming Aramex.** Registered in Jordan as Arab American Express; telex was charged per letter, so they shortened it. Quote: "كنت تدفع على الحرف" (S088) [you paid by the letter]. Source: S088.
22. **Dubai and the licences.** To avoid DHL's grip on Bahrain the firm moved to Dubai in the 1980s and rode the Gulf customs union. The Saudi licence came after 14 years of knocking, in the same year as the Airborne cheque (S088); Egypt asked 250,000 dollars (S088). Another telling: Aramex got its licence in 1996, 15 years in (S070). Quote: "فنحن ل 14 سنه نطرق الباب 14 سنه نطرق الباب بدنا ترخيص بدنا ترخيص بدنا ترخيص" (S088) [for 14 years we kept knocking on the door, we want a licence, we want a licence, we want a licence]. Lesson: licences are won by staying. Sources: S070, S088, S089.
23. **Failed Arab fundraising, then the Nasdaq road.** About 5 million dollars sought in the Arab world in the early 1990s (company worth 35 to 40 million, his figure) and refused (S088). He wrote a Harvard Business Review piece on handling a failure that leads to a bigger success (S088). Then the US: he met at least 90 investors in a month and a half (S088); another telling is about 50 meetings with 50 investors in a month, San Francisco to Denver (S089). Decided in August, public in January (S083). Quote: "قعدت شهر ونص في امريكا زرت ما لا يقل عن 90 مستثمر" (S088) [I spent a month and a half in America, I visited no fewer than 90 investors]. Sources: S070, S083, S088, S089.
24. **Airborne's 1996 cheque.** After about ten years of persuasion Airborne bought a stake of about 10 percent for two million dollars, which closed old debts so that suppliers saw liquidity. Quote: "مليونين دولار في سنه 96 انقذت حياتنا" (S088) [two million dollars in 1996 saved our lives]. Quote: "كان عننا ديون كتيره سكرناها" (S089) [we had many debts, we closed them]. Sources: S088, S089; see the differences list below.
25. **The stolen cheques.** A car carrying several large shipments of bank cheques for New York was stolen when he was 25; a lawsuit lasted seven years and he won it (S089). Existing entry 11 has the same event as a cash shipment (S007); the sources differ on cheques or cash. Quote: "البيروقراطيه في العالم العربي لها فوائد كمان" (S089) [bureaucracy in the Arab world has benefits too].
26. **Lebanon, 1990 and the Gulf rerouting.** In Beirut snipers paused for part of the day and customers on both sides exchanged parcels through Aramex; with the Kuwait office closed and airspace shut, freight went Gulf to Jordan to Syria to the Mediterranean. Quote: "كان في فتره من النهار القناصه توقف عن القنص" (S089) [there was a period of the day when the snipers stopped sniping]. Quote: "ما وقفناش يوم" (S089) [we did not stop a day]. Lesson: staying is the brand. Sources: S089 (adds to entries 5 and 6).
27. **A succession that failed.** He wanted two strong people as chair and COO; they could not work together and one left after about 22 years. Quote: "فشلت انا بالنسبه لي فشل لانه اثنيناتهم كانوا ممتازين" (S089) [I failed, for me it was a failure because both of them were excellent]. His other handover, eased in over two to three years, worked (S077, S088, S089). Lesson: even a planned bench can fail at one seat.
28. **Careem, in more detail.** Turned down their first approach ("كيف بدهم ينافسوا اوبر" (S089) [how will they compete with Uber]); they won with a cash button and a pre-booking button that Uber took two years to match (S078) and services for women and children (S088). Saudi rules pushed drivers to be Saudis, and 70 to 80 thousand drove (S078). A 500 million dollar round, as much as the whole regional VC pocket (S082). More than 400 companies came out of Careem alumni (S089). Adds to entry 15.
29. **Jamalon, as a failure.** A Ruwwad scholar's online bookstore, the Arab Amazon in the plan; books failed online because shipping was a large share of a low-price item. Quote: "الامازون تاعت العربي حتطلع من خلال جملهم وفشلنا" (S089) [the Arab Amazon will come out through Jamalon, and we failed]. Entry 14 tells the start; this is the end (S089).
30. **Twiga and the logistics copy.** In Nairobi Twiga buys from the farmer and delivers to the retailer with a basic phone and M-Pesa; he says copy it for Cairo. Quote: "go and copy it and do it in other place" (S078). Source: S078.
31. **eFishery.** A board of respected names, audited, a BCG due diligence one month before the collapse. Lesson: keep your antennas up even then. Source: S070 (2025).
32. **Ruwwad in more places.** Cairo (Ezbat Khairallah): a computer and a language centre; a Jordanian arriving alone would have been laughed at, so he found a local figure (S077, S080). Budrus, Palestine: scholarships for every student who passed the national exam, and the pass rate rose within a month; 500 to 600 scholarships a semester and 35 to 38 thousand volunteer hours (S078). Tripoli: sects that compete outside find common ground in the centre (S080). By 2011: 540 scholarships, five schools, a clinic, a post office (S091). A fund is named for a friend killed, Musab Khorma (S091). Sources: S077, S078, S080, S091.
33. **The basketball club.** Aramex owned a not-for-profit basketball club for 30 to 35 years with youth teams from age six; it reintroduced women's basketball in Jordan and was its first social act (S077, S081).
34. **Jordan University and the research lab.** The dean made Aramex a research lab for industrial-engineering projects; graduates then applied to Aramex. Aramex never advertised, and called its sales force knocking on doors (S076). The Nasdaq team was graduates of the University of Jordan, auditors too (S091).
35. **Maktoob, told five ways.** See the differences list; the arc is the same: invested early, sold to Yahoo in 2009, the alumni seeded the next companies (S067, S074, S075, S078, S082, S083, S088, S089).
