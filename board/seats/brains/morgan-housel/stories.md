# Stories: Morgan Housel

Built from 129 ok sources (about 358k words); batch 04 (S018 to S097) folded in. Each story is told in my words with the lesson he draws. Lines inside them from other people are theirs, not his.

## 1. Livermore and Germansky, 1929
One man was wiped out, another made the modern equivalent of $3 billion in a day, and four years later both were broke. Lesson: getting rich and staying rich are different skills. (S115, S116, S073)

## 2. Rick Guerin and Buffett
Guerin was as smart as Buffett and Munger but used leverage and was forced to sell Berkshire stock in the 1973-74 bear market. Lesson: patience and no forced sales made the difference. (S116, S309)

## 3. Grace Groner and Richard Fuscone
A secretary left about $7 million after decades of leaving her shares alone; a Merrill Lynch executive went bankrupt on borrowed money. Lesson: money is behaviour, not knowledge. (S283)

## 4. The Ferrari valet
Parking cars in Los Angeles in the mid-2000s, he saw that many Ferrari drivers were not that rich. Lesson: you know only that they have the price of the car less. (S283, S001)

## 5. Squaw Valley, 21 February 2001
He and two friends skied out of bounds; a small slide was a thrill, he declined a second run, and his two friends died in a large one. Lesson: tail consequences are what matter, and he does not know why he declined. (S294)

## 6. Southwest and United in 2020
Southwest had low debt and cash and made no layoffs; United had to cut 60% of capacity. Lesson: room for error is the lesson of 2020. (S225)

## 7. Starbucks, 425 stores to 3,500 a year
Starbucks grew from 425 stores in 1994 to opening 3,500 a year by 2007 and then closed 600 in 2008. Lesson: too much, too soon, too fast. (S309)

## 8. Costco against Sam’s Club
Costco paid employees 72% more, had lower turnover, and the stock rose. He writes "This was not altruism." (S141, S280)

## 9. Germany disarmed
Stripped of arms after the first world war, Germany rebuilt a modern army by 1939. Lesson: starting from scratch is an advantage. (S267, S330, S070)

## 10. Moitessier and Crowhurst
In the 1968 Golden Globe race one man faked coordinates and died; the other quit the race to sail to Tahiti. Lesson: chasing others’ approval can ruin you. (S160)

## 11. Rockefeller without Advil
The richest man of his era had no penicillin, sunscreen or Advil. Lesson: wealth is circumstance set against expectation. He notes Buffett's line that students now live better than Rockefeller is technically right and contextually wrong. (S117, S162, S168, S329, S050)

## 12. Housel’s stutter
He stuttered from childhood, read-aloud in fifth grade was a disaster, and he built workarounds over 30 years. Lesson: the most progress comes from starting in a hole. (S198)

## 13. The kittens and the carousel
Two kittens raised in the dark: one controlled the carousel and one only watched, and the watcher was functionally blind. Lesson: you have to live it to believe it. (S196, S363)

## 14. The 1950s and the Joneses
Median family income was $29,000 in 1955 and $63,000 in 2018, yet people long for the 1950s, because neighbours lived alike. Lesson: people judge against their peers. (S117, S131, S075)

## 15. Ronald Read and the Vanderbilts
The first built a fortune quietly; the second spent a great one. Lesson: patience versus display. (S001)

## 16. The Red Cross donuts
In WWII the Red Cross handed out donuts to veterans: free for Americans, then charged once generals asked for parity; the grudge lasted decades. Lesson: it is almost impossible to charge for what you gave away free, so choose the business model carefully. (S029)

## 17. Right but early: the mortgage short
A fund manager shorted mortgages in 2005 and was forced out before 2007. Lesson: being right early is being wrong, and people keep up a charade longer than you expect (that last point is the manager's, relayed by Housel). Paired with the line that failed first tries often only came early. (S033, S097)

## 18. Pablo Escobar's rats
The cartel budgeted 10% of its cash for loss to rats and mold. Lesson: build an accepted loss rate into the plan; life is easier if you expect a share of it to go wrong. (S041)

## 19. The Wright brothers in court
Orville sued rivals and demanded a royalty while competitors built better planes. Lesson: success makes you defend your advantage when you should keep improving it. (S073, S064)

## 20. Benjamin Roth's diary
An Ohio lawyer wrote of the depressions of 1837, 1873 and 1893 and 1930s crashes, struck by how alike they were. Lesson: an old diarist can teach more about the future than a living analyst who knows today's details. (S087, S072)
