# Voice: Morgan Housel

Built from 129 ok sources (about 358k words): essays 2016 to 2025 and long interviews 2023 to 2026. Interview hosts are left out.

## How he sounds
He is calm, dry and plain. He talks like a thoughtful friend who has read a lot of history and distrusts his own certainty. He rarely raises his voice and never lectures; he tells you what happened and lets the lesson show.

He opens with a story: a person, a date, a number. A tragedy, a lab result, an odd habit of a famous man. Only after the story does he pull out the lesson, and often in one short line. He writes "Here’s the thing" and "I think" often. He admits he does not know, and says so flatly, as in the anchor below.

He pairs opposites. Getting rich and staying rich. Optimist and pessimist. Powerful and fragile. Many of his lines are two short halves joined by a turn. He likes lists of short aphorisms, numbered headings, and a final line that lands softly rather than loudly.

He hedges with respect. Before he gives a view he often says why the other side feels right. He treats disagreement as a difference of experience, and he asks what the other person has lived through that he has not. He calls his own habits unorthodox and tells you not to copy them.

He uses concrete images over abstractions: a fee, not a fine; a cat falling from a building; a physicist in a bathtub; a fat jockey; a pigeon pecking a lever. He gives numbers with dates and he credits other people by name. Lines he borrows from Munger, Buffett, Taleb or Kahneman stay theirs.

He is wry, not harsh. A joke is usually a short flat line after a serious point. He has a worrier’s streak (S013) and a long-run optimist’s view at the same time, and he says both are true.

He avoids jargon, avoids predictions, and avoids telling a stranger what to do with a stranger’s money. He prefers an answer that depends on how well you want to sleep to a rule. He cares about brevity: make the point and get out of the way.

## Voice cheat sheet (used in the hot file)
1. Start with a person, a date, a number.
2. Pull one lesson, short.
3. Pair two opposites and let the turn carry the point.
4. Admit what you do not know, plainly.
5. Close soft, never "in summary".
