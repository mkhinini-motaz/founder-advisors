# Positions: Morgan Housel

Built from 129 ok sources (about 358k words), dated by source; batch 04 (S018 to S097) folded in. Where he moved, both views are given with dates. A topic with no evidence says so.

## Optimism and pessimism
"Pessimism always sounds smarter than optimism because optimism sounds like a sales pitch while pessimism sounds like someone trying to help you." (S162, 2025-08-13). Same stance in 2020 and 2023: "So the reasonable optimist expects the world to break all the time." (S285, 2020-12-03). Also: "The past wasn’t as good as you remember. The present isn’t as bad as you think. The future will be better than you anticipate." (S040, 2024-10-14).

## Forecasting
"Most forecasts are terrible, but making forecasts is reasonable." (S210, 2018-12-11). In 2020: "But I suspect we have no idea what happens next." (S324, 2020-11-19). He also warns: "But assuming no one can predict anything is almost as dangerous as assuming they can predict anything." (S284, 2019-12-20). Later: "No one knows anything, even when it looks obvious." (S029, 2024-08-06). On date predictions: "How could anyone know what kind of mood a bunch of strangers will be in a year from now? I don’t know what kind of mood I’ll be in tonight." (S058, 2022-06-01).

## Tariffs and trade
"I think the tariffs are a terrible idea. Not just a terrible idea, but a horrendous idea." (S181, 2025-04-09). He says this departs from his habit of avoiding news: "this is so against what I normally do." (S181).

## Government debt
"We won’t ever pay it off." (S347, 2020-04-17). Also: "History never repeats. We can’t automatically assume we’ll be able to grow out of Covid-19 debt like we did after World War II." (S347).

## Inequality
"But it doesn’t matter whether you think rising inequality is right or wrong, good or bad, or what we should do about it." (S339, 2020-04-09). He describes it and avoids judging it.

## Bubbles
"It’s only a bubble if return prospects don’t improve after prices fall." (S260, 2016-09-27). Later: "About once a decade people forget that bubbles form and burst about once a decade." (S162, 2025-08-13). On why they form: "A big problem with bubbles is the reflexive association between wealth and wisdom, so a bunch of crazy ideas are taken seriously because a temporarily rich person said it." (S028, 2023-10-15).

## Index funds and stock picking
"my investing strategy is to own index funds for as long as I possibly can to be average for an above average period of time" (S016, 2024-05-28). He is "not a passive zealot the slightest" (S016) and "I could never be a stock picker" (S016).

## Cash and his own portfolio
"I’m an optimist who holds an amount of cash consistent with impending catastrophe." (S210, 2018-12-11). "My own money is barbelled. I take risks with one portion and am a terrified turtle with the other." (S283, 2018-06-01).

## Paying off a mortgage
"the worst financial decision we've ever made but it's the best money decision we've ever made" (S016, 2024-05-28). He says it is not advice for other people.

## Housing
"Housing is often a liability masquerading as a safe asset." (S162, 2025-08-13). On his own house: emotion overrode the math (S255, 2023-01-12).

## Leverage
"Leverage is the devil here." (S283, 2018-06-01). "To the leveraged investor a small setback is a huge risk because of how they’re forced to deal with decline" (S225, 2020-07-29).

## A little debt (a reversal he admits)
"A little debt can keep you away from distraction." (S070, 2019-03-13). He adds: "This hurts admitting because I’m so against debt." (S070). His reason: "There’s no way to learn the value of money without feeling the power of its scarcity, and a little debt is a constant reminder that you can’t get too comfortable." (S070). Set beside the 2018 and 2020 leverage lines above; the difference is a small, survivable amount against one that can force a sale.

## Happiness and wealth
"happiness is always for everybody a fiveminute emotion." (S002, 2025-10-13). "I think most wealthier people are not necessarily happier than less wealthy people, but I think they have fewer bad days." (S002). In 2023: "Happiness is complicated, but if you simplify it into things like a loving family, health, friendship, eight hours of sleep, well-balanced children, and being part of something bigger than yourself, you realize how limited money’s role can be." (S028, 2023-10-15). In 2019: "There is a correlation between money and happiness, but it diminishes with each additional dollar gained." (S075, 2019-10-05).

## Wealth's downsides and inheritance
"I think what many people really want from money is the ability to stop thinking about money." (S028, 2023-10-15). "Quick wealth is fragile wealth." (S028). On leaving money to children: "Those are the two options for the rich: Ruin their ambition with inheritance, or risk some form of strife by denying them an easy life." (S028). He calls Munger's view on it "probably 80% true" (S028) and offers no clean answer.

## Expectations
"But your happiness completely relies on expectations." (S093, 2022-10-24). "You want to feel a gap between what you expected and what actually happened." (S093). On why low expectations are hard: "I think it’s often hard to distinguish high expectations from motivation. Low expectations feels like giving up and minimizing your potential." (S093). His answer is to accept losing often (S093).

## Independence as the goal
"Independence is the best financial goal for most people." (S208, 2025-02-27).

## Saving
"The solution to 90% of financial problems is “save more money and be more patient.”" (S149, 2021-03-09). In 2025 he frames saving as purchasing independence (S002).

## Status and spending
"no one is as impressed with your stuff as much as you are." (S255, 2023-01-12). "Wealth, in fact, is what you don’t see." (S283, 2018-06-01). Earlier: "Acting like you’re rich is the fastest way to cease being rich." (S062, 2017-04-20).

## Experts and credentials
"It’s amazing what happens when you become open to the best ideas, rather than the most credentialed voices." (S263, 2024-03-03). Also: "There is a big difference between an expert, whose talent should be celebrated, and a guru, whose bad ideas should never be questioned." (S303, 2023-04-26).

## Experience that expires, and being early
"So it will always be the case that those with the most experience – and the good, smart, accurate wisdom that comes from it – will be the least willing to adapt their views as the world evolves." (S097, 2021-11-10). On failed first tries: "It usually means other parts of the system have evolved in a way that allows what was once impossible to now become practical." (S097). A short seller of mortgages in 2005 was forced out before 2007: right but early is wrong (S033, 2025-04-24, inferred).

## Conviction
"Most of what people call “conviction” is a willful disregard for new information that might make you change your mind." (S162, 2025-08-13).

## Growth and scale
"Revenue might scale but disappointed customers scale faster." (S309, 2021-03-18). "Quality falling with quantity is visible in almost every industry." (S276, 2017-02-09).

## Pricing and giving things away
"It’s almost impossible to charge for something once you’ve given it away for free, so choose your business model carefully." (S029, 2024-08-06). "People are extremely sensitive to even reasonable price changes, which is why inflation is always an emotional issue." (S029).

## Competition
"Business competitors destroy each other; businesses without competitors destroy themselves." (S070, 2019-03-13). "Your competitors can probably innovate and execute as well as you can." (S033, 2025-04-24). On first movers: "The first-mover advantage idea has largely been shown to be a myth, with three times as many counterexamples." (S070).

## Treating employees and customers
"Trust, both from employees and customers, doesn’t offer many second chances." (S280, 2016-10-25). Costco: "This was not altruism." (S141, 2018-05-09). On bosses: "If you, as a boss, expect that learning stops at graduation and employees are merely meant to produce work, you will get the kind of employees you deserve." (S042, 2024-01-02).

## Marketing
"Good marketing wins in the short run and good products win in the long run." (S303, 2023-04-26). In 2024: "The best marketing is a good product." (S029, 2024-08-06). In 2025: "There is too much marketing (waving your arms) and not enough branding (building trust)." (S162).

## Metrics and quantification
"A belief that rational decisions can be made with quantitative measures alone, when in fact the things you can’t measure are often the most consequential." (S018, 2020-02-20). "So now you have a useless prediction metric, but you’re highly confident in it – more confident than you’ve ever been – because you’re using math." (S078, 2019-05-09).

## Communication and writing
"When in doubt, people will assume your poor communication is intentional." (S046, 2018-04-09). "And long-term capital is one of the most lucrative edges in the business." (S046). He argues the habit of writing reasons down exposes emotion (S046).

## Luck and skill
"In most cases of extreme success or failure, the line between bold and reckless was thin." (S058, 2022-06-01). "Knowing you’re part of a group but thinking your talents directly lead to your results is cognitive dissonance on steroids." (S080, 2020-01-28).

## Work hours, busyness and burnout
"Busy is the waste that occurs when you don’t know what to do with the free time that’s fallen into your lap, so you squander it on something unproductive and disappointing." (S061, 2017-03-16). "When you try to do 10 things at once, none of them get done very well, and take longer to finish." (S061). On stress: "Stress can quickly go from motivating to debilitating." (S062, 2017-04-20). He admits "I feel swamped most days." (S061).

## Learning and reading
"Don’t contain your learning to your own profession or major. Read and learn as broadly as possible." (S042, 2024-01-02). "If you find something that is true in more than one field, you’ve probably uncovered something particularly important." (S042).

## Using history
"To me, the point of paying attention to history is not the specific details of certain events, which are always random and never repeat; it’s the big-picture behaviors that reoccur in different eras, generations, and societies." (S087, 2022-09-27). In 2020: "Big events grow big because they’re complex, and complexity never repeats itself in its exact form." (S063, 2020-03-19). On nostalgia: "We don’t remember how we actually felt in the past; We remember how we think we should have felt, given what we know today." (S040, 2024-10-14).

## Information and the news
"Permanent information is what teaches you what to do with expiring information." (S081, 2019-03-27). "The trick – a hard one – is knowing when information is merely entertainment and shouldn’t influence your actions." (S081).

## Contrarianism
In 2020: "But none – not a single one – is always a contrarian." (S121, 2020-07-22). In 2018: "Most attempts at contrarianism is just irrational cynicism in disguise." (S283).

## Technology adoption
"It usually takes more time to convince people that your technology has changed the world than it does to invent a world-changing technology." (S252, 2019-10-16). On railroads: "It’s only a small exaggeration to say the number of railroad investors who prospered during this time rounds to zero." (S064, 2019-11-11). Also: "Change eventually comes, but agonizingly slower than you might assume." (S033, 2025-04-24).

## Politics and investing
"you are not gonna make good investing decisions if you are doing it through the lens of your tribal beliefs." (S181, 2025-04-09).

## Changing his mind on other people's money choices
He said in October 2026 that he has changed his mind about criticising other people’s money decisions (S010, 2026-10-09). Earlier he held: "A lot of financial mistakes come from decisions that would be right for someone else but wrong for you." (S208, 2025-02-27).

## Stock-market timing
"Patience will be rewarded, stubbornness will be penalized, and we’ll never be able to tell which is which." (S183, 2017-11-07). In 2017 he adds he is "more open-minded than I’ve ever been as an investor." (S183).
