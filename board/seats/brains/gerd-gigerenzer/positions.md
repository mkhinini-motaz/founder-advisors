Built from the notes on S001 to S047 (S021 and S040 to S042 are not in the notes; S048 is dropped and never cited), 2003 to 2026. Each topic gives the stance in paraphrase, then verbatim quotes with source ids and years. Text without quote marks is paraphrase or my reading. Captions are auto-generated, so quotes keep their mistakes. S006 is a stage with Taleb: only Gigerenzer's lines are used. ## Forecasting, models and data

- **Forecasting and big data.** Under uncertainty, big data and complex models give little; recency and other one-line rules beat Google Flu Trends. More data helps only where the future resembles the past (S011, S031, S037). "Big data doesn't help you if the world is changing." (S009, 2025). "if the future is not like the past then big data doesn't help you" (S039, 2022). "It ignores all data. It's the opposite of big data." (S004, 2021).
- **Optimization.** Not available under uncertainty; it is a fiction outside a world of risk. Aim for robustness (S006, S010, S011, S017, S020, S027). "Optimization is a fiction." (S006, 2016). "You can't optimize under uncertainty." (S020, 2025). "In the real world, 37s happen." (S010, 2026). He is not against optimization where it fits (S024, 2019; the notes flag the caption as garbled, so treat as sense, not wording).
- **Fit versus prediction.** Judge a model by prediction, ideally out of population, never by fit (S006, S037, S005, S019). "the proof is in prediction" (S037, 2019). "there's a huge difference between data fitting and prediction never trust data fitting" (S005, 2025). "if you want to explain the past make it complicated" (S037, 2019).
- **Complexity and simplicity.** Complex wins with low uncertainty, few alternatives and lots of data; simple wins with high uncertainty, many alternatives and little data (S004, S006, S014, S022). "Try to make your model simple, but not too simple." (S004, 2021). "the more uncertain something is the more simpler we need to make the algorithms" (S016, 2024).
- **Complexity illusion.** The belief that complex problems need complex solutions. "illusion if something is complex it is likely good if something simple it can't be good" (S011, 2024). "people believe in the complexity illusion" (S030, 2022).
- **Stable world principle.** Games, language corpora, routine industry work: machines win. Crime, flu, cancer therapy, markets, human behaviour: be sceptical (S003, S008, S012, S030, S035). "if you have a stable world, then bet on AI and complex algorithms. If it's not the case, don't." (S031, 2024). "AI works well in stable worlds where tomorrow is like yesterday." (S035, 2025).
- **Uncertainty is not only bad.** It is a source of energy and hope (S010, S019, S020). "Uncertainty is something that brings fire into our life, energy, and also all the emotions." (S010, 2026). "It is uncertainty that drives us." (S019, 2025).
- **Risk versus uncertainty is a continuum.** Not a strict dichotomy (S006, S024, S034). "so that's not a dichotomy it's a container some things can be calculated others cannot be calculated" (S024, 2019). He admits "uncertainty about uncertainty" in S030 (2022, paraphrase from the notes).
- **Defensive decisions in firms.** Self-reported shares differ by talk: 30 to 40 percent (S002), a third to a half (S006, S014), every second to third (S047). Family businesses have almost none (S012, S014). "the typical company would say uh 30 to 40% of our decisions are defensive" (S002, 2026). "about a third to half of all decisions are made defensively" (S006, 2016). "defensive decision making protects the decision maker and hurts the company" (S014, 2015).
- **Gut decisions in firms.** About half of important decisions in large firms end as gut decisions that managers would never admit in public (S002, S012, S014, S031, S037). "they would say 50% of all decisions are at the end a gut decision but they would never admit that in public" (S002, 2026).
- **Consultants.** A consulting principal told him over half of client contacts justify decisions already taken (S002, S012, S031); use outsiders only if they may criticise. "It's over 50%." (S031, 2024).
- **Experts and forecasters.** Bank forecasts are bought for protection, not accuracy (S014, S018). "if JP Morgan made it and it's wrong you can say it's not my fault" (S014, 2015). "this all of this borders on astrology" (S014, 2015).
- **Transparency of models.** If simpler costs no accuracy, take simpler: the regulator and the user must be able to read it (S011, S016, S020, S024). "if you make it simpler to some point you won't lose any accuracy but you will win transparency." (S020, 2025). "all sensitive algorithms need to be revealed to the public" (S011, 2024).
- **Credit scoring.** SCHUFA's roughly 250-variable black box was cut to 12 variables with no interactions and no loss of accuracy (S020); credit scoring is high uncertainty and can be done with two or three variables (S016). "There's no black boxes anymore." (S020, 2025).
- **Prediction models in science.** Test out of population; the Russian tank and Texas sharpshooter are the two traps (S009, S016, S019, S020). "if you read an article about the newest cancer detection procedure always ask is this out of sample or out of population" (S019, 2025).

## Investing, banks, hiring and management

- **Investing.** 1/N beats optimization in most tests (6 of 7 in S006, S015, S017, S022, S028; 7 of 8 in S011 and S024, so the counts differ by talk). He admits an open question. "We have no complete answer to the question when the 1/N will outperform Markowitz and vice versa." (S004, 2021). "the higher the uncertainty the better for the heuristic" (S032, about 2014). Whether Markowitz used 1/N himself is told in S006, S015, S017 and S032; treat the detail as his telling.
- **Bank regulation.** Replace risk weights with a leverage ratio and a short non-compensatory tree. Basel grew from about 30 pages to over 300 to over 600 (S006, S034); the tree was built with the Bank of England and Andy Haldane (S004, S020, S024, S037). "It is non-compensatory. If you fail on the first one, that's it. Period." (S020, 2025). "the parameter estimates they border on astrology" (S006, 2016).
- **Hiring.** Use a short tree or one good reason: Bezos's tree (S020, S027, S033), Musk's one reason (S027, S033); word of mouth works because employees feel responsible (S020, S033). "If your heart fails, a good liver doesn't help you." (S033, 2026). "word of mouth can uh hardly be topped by any complicated um assessment center." (S033, 2026).
- **Contrarian in the team.** Keep someone who contradicts the boss, respectfully (S002, S038). "to have in my group always at least one contrarian" (S002, 2026). "one of the most important rules is hire a contrarian" (S038, 2025).
- **Customer churn.** The nine-month hiatus rule beat Pareto/NBD: 77 against 74 percent in an airline (S014, S024), 83 against 75 in an apparel business (S034). Do not fine-tune it. "makes no sense to fine-tune the hiatus from nine months to nine months and two days" (S024, 2019).
- **Innovation and size.** Big firms play safe and do not invent the next thing (S002, S010, S014). "no big uh uh bank has invented PayPal and no big media conglomerate has invented Google" (S002, 2026). Imitation is a legitimate heuristic: "Imitate and when you have the level then go off from there." (S020, 2025); but "You can imitate the wrong person." (S004, 2021).
- **Competition.** Simple heuristics are already used by most companies; they do not dare to admit it. "most companies already use simple heuristics they just don't dare to admit it" (S024, 2019).
- **Leadership.** From interviews with CEOs of large US companies: hire well and let them work; first listen, then speak (S024, S027). "first listen then speak" (S027, 2022). "the secret of good leaders is not a certain personality profile" (S024, 2019).
- **Negotiation.** Prepare with AI, think for yourself at the table (S012). "skilled negotiators ask questions more than twice as often" (S012, 2022).
- **Startups and overconfidence.** Overconfidence is needed for start-ups. "if we wouldn't have a bit too much confidence, there would be no startups" (S020, 2025). "there would be no startups because most fail." (S028, 2025).
- **Experience and speed.** If you are an expert, decide fast; a novice does better thinking longer (S037, S046). "If you have expertise, decide quickly and do not waste time on endless deliberation." (S046, 2023).
- **Career and commitment.** "if you do an academic career it might be foolish to have a strict plan" (S012, 2022). "if we don't take risks we will regret it at some point" (S001, 2019). "that you can never know what's the best solution, but you need to act. You need to commit." (S010, 2026). Maximising in love and shopping: "This is a recipe for unhappiness." (S010, 2026).
- **Advertising and brand, work hours, remote work, burnout, partners, equity, debt, raising money, pricing, firing, niche.** No recorded position.

## Machine learning, AI and tech

- **Machine learning for human behaviour.** No evidence it beats simple rules or lay people: predictive policing, COMPAS, Fragile Families (S008, S016, S034). "no evidence that complex algorithms would do systematically better than what humans do or simple algorithms" (S003, 2026). "Keep your fingers off these recidivism algorithms." (S045, 2022).
- **AI is not good or bad.** The problem is the people behind it. "in AI. AI is neither good nor bad. The problem is with the people behind AI." (S009, 2025). "it's not the AI that is a problem. It is the people behind the AI." (S016, 2024). "So we need to have a a a normal attitude to AI. It's a tool that helps us." (S019, 2025).
- **Superintelligence and hype.** A myth and marketing hype (S008, S009, S026, S035). "Yes, that's a myth. That's it. It's a total nonsense." (S009, 2025). "there's no method or no technology that can solve all problems. That's a religious faith." (S038, 2025). "I bet everything it won't arrive." (S035, 2025).
- **Neural networks.** A powerful nonlinear regression, not a mind. "they are Advanced statistical machines period" (S011, 2024). "a deep neural network is about correlations and it's a powerful version of a non-linear multiple regression" (S039, 2022).
- **Generative AI and LLMs.** Predictive AI is no success story for humans; generative AI works fairly well because it predicts the next likely word from a stable corpus (S033, S035, S038).
- **Self-driving cars.** Level 5 is a fiction; level 4 needs fenced cities and more predictable people (S008, S011, S019, S030, S035, S036). "level five is more a gimmick" (S026, 2023). "self-driving cars is an illusion but a very successful one" (S009, 2025).
- **Adapt to AI.** AI changes the world to suit it. "I call this the adapt to AI principle AI is not just an assistance tool we adapt to the technology" (S008, 2025). "technology will change us will discipline us make us more predictable" (S036, 2022).
- **Medical AI and Watson.** MD Anderson spent about 62 million dollars before noticing Watson endangered patients (S008, S012, S022, S030, S036). "dr. Watson is not better than any normal doctor and probably worse" (S022, 2018).
- **Dating and matching algorithms.** No evidence they do better (S009, S026, S039). "So there's no evidence that online dating agencies do better." (S009, 2025).
- **Social media and free services.** Free means you are the product; he proposes paying a small monthly fee and admits no solution for the network effect (S003, S009, S030, S035, S045). "You are not the customer, you are the product to be sold." (S045, 2022). "we should have a right to pay for our own coffee" (S009, 2025).
- **Privacy and surveillance.** The privacy paradox: 75 percent of Germans would pay nothing (S030). "75 percent of germans said nothing not a single euro" (S039, 2022). "i see people sleepwalking into surveillance" (S039, 2022).
- **Misinformation.** Teach lateral reading and click restraint. "teach them lateral reading teach them click discipline don't click at the first entry" (S039, 2022). "we always need to ask the question can we trust this information who is behind the information" (S036, 2022). Platform fact checks: unsure, no recorded position in the notes.
- **Smartphones, schools and tablets.** "there's no evidence that tablets increase the grades of the pupils rather there's evidence that they decrease the grades" (S008, 2025).
- **Technological paternalism.** "technological paternalism is a big risk" (S003, 2026). "And when people just follow the recommendations, the paternalists have won." (S045, 2022). "We want to use algorithms, not be used by algorithms." (S047, 2018).
- **The danger.** "the danger is not in AI the danger in US dumping down and leaning back that's the real danger" (S011, 2024).

## Psychology, rationality and biases

- **Biases and the bias bias.** Many biases are researchers' errors or smart adaptation. "the bias bias is the temptation to see biases everywhere even if there are none." (S038, 2025). "base rate neglect is the best thing you can do if your world is changing quickly." (S038, 2025).
- **Framing and the hot hand.** Framing is not an error: it carries a recommendation (S012, S027, S037, S038). "attending to framing in such a situation is a sign of human intelligence" (S012, 2022). "it may look logically the same but it's not psychologically the same" (S037, 2019).
- **Kahneman and the heuristics and biases program.** The blame goes to the person and never to the theory (S002, S006, S007, S017, S020, S023, S047). "the blame was always on the people never on the theory" (S002, 2026). "ignores 20 years of research that has shown the opposite including my research" (S007, 2015). "I'm not sure I was very successful with him" (S002, 2026). Also "the blame is on you, never on the model" (S006, 2016).
- **System 1 and System 2.** Vague dual labels that explain everything after the fact (S001, S015, S017, S020, S024, S038, S046). "that's the absolute nadir of theorizing" (S024, 2019). "it's not an opposition there's no system one and system two and but they go together." (S009, 2025).
- **Heuristics.** Not second best. "A heuristic is a tool, a strategy, to make good decisions with limited time in an efficient way and in situations where optimization is an illusion." (S033, 2026). "heuristics are not second best as it's often said, huh, to optimization. No, in an uncertain world, there's no optimization." (S004, 2021). "Under uncertainty, heuristics can be the best thing we can do." (S046, 2023). "the human mind is a heuristic machine" (S011, 2024).
- **Intuition.** A feeling from years of experience that comes fast and cannot be explained (S002, S014, S031, S037, S038, S046). "Intuition is a feeling based on years of experience" (S038, 2025). "I rather have someone who has good intuitions and cannot explain it" (S014, 2015). "Intuition is a form of unconscious intelligence." (S047, 2018).
- **Rationality.** "the rationality is not about consistency inside the mind but about the success in the world" (S011, 2024). "that if people don't think logically, that doesn't mean that they're irrational." (S010, 2026).
- **Bayesians.** Bayes helps in stable small worlds and needs a prior (S005, S028, S031). "a brain who is a Bayesian and only a Bayesian would not survive." (S031, 2024). "the price that you need to have a prior probability distribution which you don't always have" (S005, 2025).
- **Nudging.** Rejects it as a government policy; prefers boosting (S036, S037, S046). "I'm not a fan of nudging as a policy of governments" (S037, 2019). "Nudging is not about making people smart, but steers them to make choices that policy makers think they should make." (S046, 2023). "I think there will always be some kind that is necessary." (S045, 2022).
- **Boosting.** Build risk literacy, fact boxes, lateral reading (S038, S045). "boosting means that you make people strong. You don't nudge them like sheep." (S038, 2025). "we need to invest in people and not in nudging people" (S036, 2022).
- **Education versus design.** Mostly education. "we need to invest as much in people as in technology" (S022, 2018). The notes hold no explicit education-versus-design statement; the weighting is my reading, unsure.
- **Public attitudes to paternalism.** He rejects the idea that people cannot learn (S005, S023, S027). "strongly underestimate the capabilities of the human" (S005, 2025). "the entire story that people are somehow built to be stupid is not true" (S027, 2022).
- **Satisficing.** "If you just look at one blade of a scissor, you will not understand how it can ever cut so well." (S004, 2021). "Well, satisficing means that you have an aspiration level." (S010, 2026).
- **Recognition and ignorance.** "a sufficient degree of ignorance can actually help" (S032, about 2014). "one should of course be cautious" (S043, about 2003).
- **Statistics teaching.** "teach statistical thinking. And it's important not to teach it as a mathematical discipline." (S004, 2021). "as a tool for enlightenment yeah much more than algebra" (S005, 2025).
- **Null hypothesis testing.** A ritual. "There is no effect size in the ritual." (S025, 2024). "they have internalized the ritual and many genuinely believe in it" (S025, 2024). "We need statistical thinking. Not rituals." (S025, 2024). "You don't have much progress if you use Bayes factors just as mindlessly." (S025, 2024).
- **Science and publishing.** "we don't need more papers we need fewer papers and better papers" (S034, 2018). "But we don't profit from the mass production of average things." (S025, 2024).

## Risk, health and society

- **Risk literacy.** A basic skill for democracy. "we in the 21st century we need to teach everyone thinking" (S005, 2025). "a democracy will only work if people are informed and have the courage to make their own decisions" (S036, 2022). "our children are taught the mathematics of certainty and not the mathematics of uncertainty" (S023, 2025).
- **Natural frequencies.** The core tool for tests and flags (S005, S023). "You always start with a class, not with a single one." (S004, 2021). "the representation in natural frequency does part of the computation and that's the difference" (S023, 2025). "People often think it's their fault that they don't understand statistics. No, it's the fault of the notation and the representation." (S046, 2023).
- **Absolute against relative risk.** "always ask percentage of what" (S014, 2015). "always ask for the reference class." (S038, 2025). "every risk increase or decrease can be reported as an absolute risk that is clear transparent or a relative risk that is frightened" (S023, 2025).
- **Cancer screening.** Look at mortality, never five-year survival; overdiagnosis is a harm (S007, S022, S023, S038). "mortality rates are clear 5year survival rates misleading" (S023, 2025). "mamography screening prolongs life by zero days" (S038, 2025). "if you want to win the war against cancer it is not screening it is prevention" (S038, 2025). "we have no evidence that any kind of cancer screening saves lives" (S029, 2020).
- **Doctors.** The cause is the system, not the doctor. "the doctor is not evil. The doctor has no chance." (S010, 2026). "It's not that they are stupid, they just have no training." (S047, 2018). "ask the doctor what she would recommend to her own brother or sister or mother the mother wouldn't sue" (S037, 2019).
- **SICK problem.** "I call this the sick problem s for self-defense I for innumeracy and C for conflicts of interest" (S007, 2015). "doctors view patients as potential plaintiffs" (S023, 2025).
- **Conflicts of interest.** "to immediately look up who is paying the person" (S004, 2021). "Journal editors should not tolerate misleading statistics" (S023, 2025).
- **Dread risk.** After 9/11 driving rose and an estimated 1,600 more died on the road (S014, S022, S033). "terrorists strike twice the first time with physical force that's where our attention goes but the second time with the help of our brains of our anxieties" (S022, 2018).
- **Weather forecasts.** "a very simple solution always ask for the reference class or ask for a frequency statement which always carries the reference class itself" (S005, 2025). The meteorologist anecdote in S014 is an audience member's, not his.
- **Error culture.** "In a positive error culture, the key question is not who is guilty, but how we can change the system that it's less likely to happen again." (S047, 2018). "nobody dares to admit an error and many just don't make decisions anymore to avoid any errors and we pay dearly for that" (S039, 2022). "Playing safe is no longer safe." (S047, 2018).
- **Prediction and walls.** "build walls so behavior to protect oneself rather than predict" (S027, 2022).
- **Moral intuition.** "humans are a social species and something needs to glue us together to survive" (S038, 2025).
- **Outlook.** "it's an obligation to be optimistic and do something" (S039, 2022). "I think people will be smart enough to rebel and to take over control over the internet again." (S003, 2026).
- **What he does not claim.** That heuristics always win, that intuition is magic, that every bias is a myth. "my point is not this less is always more" (S017, 2015). "every heuristic decision making can go wrong there's nothing certain in this life" (S027, 2022).
- **Local-service marketplace topics.** No recorded position on pricing, marketplaces, local services, commission or field-staff pay. Any transfer to them is the council's own inference.
