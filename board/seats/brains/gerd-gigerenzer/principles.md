# Principles: Gerd Gigerenzer

Built from 42 sources, 2015 to 2026. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Most-used first.

1. **First ask whether you are in risk or uncertainty; only in risk do you calculate.** "the first step is to distinguish between situations of risk and uncertainty in situations of risk then do your calculations" (S037). Sources: 25 or more (S001, S002, S004, S006, S010, S014, S017, S019, S020, S024, S027, S037 and more). Confidence: high.
2. **Under uncertainty, ignore most information and keep the few robust cues.** "in uncertain worlds you need to simplify you don't win if you made it more complex" (S008). Also "It's the art of ignoring" (S002). Sources: 14 (S002, S006, S008, S009, S011, S015, S016, S024, S031, S032, S037, S047, S017, S046). Confidence: high.
3. **When a complex model fails, make it simpler, not more complex.** "And in this world, if a complex algorithm doesn't work, make it simple." (S004). Sources: 8 (S004, S011, S026, S030, S035, S037, S036, S031). Confidence: high.
4. **Judge a model by prediction, never by fit.** "the proof is in prediction" (S037). Also "that's not science that's hindsight" (S015). Sources: 12 (S004, S005, S006, S015, S016, S017, S019, S020, S024, S034, S037, S028). Confidence: high.
5. **Test your favourite model against the best simple competitor.** "don't test your own favorite model test it against the best competitors" (S024). Also "test your complex models competitively against a simple heuristic to learn how how they are doing." (S006). Sources: 6 (S004, S006, S020, S024, S034, S026). Confidence: high.
6. **Ask whether a claim is in sample or out of population.** "if you read an article about the newest cancer detection procedure always ask is this out of sample or out of population" (S019). Sources: 5 (S009, S019, S020, S016, S017). Confidence: high.
7. **Do not optimize under uncertainty; bet on robustness.** "you need to bet on robustness not on optimization because optimizing in a uncertain world makes the system fragile" (S011). Also "Optimization is a fiction." (S006, S028). Sources: 10 (S004, S006, S010, S011, S017, S020, S027, S028, S019, S034). Confidence: high.
8. **Ask percentage of what for every probability.** "always ask percentage of what" (S014). Also "always ask for the reference class." (S038). Sources: 6 (S014, S022, S027, S038, S005, S023). Confidence: high.
9. **Ask for the absolute risk, not the relative change.** "We need to make people risk savvy so that they immediately ask, yeah? What is the absolute risk, yeah? Do you want to mislead me?" (S004). Sources: 10 (S002, S004, S007, S014, S022, S023, S029, S038, S047, S005). Confidence: high.
10. **Ask who is behind the information and who pays them.** "we always need to ask the question can we trust this information who is behind the information" (S036). Also "to immediately look up who is paying the person" (S004). Sources: 8 (S004, S007, S008, S009, S027, S030, S035, S036). Confidence: high.
11. **Make people competent instead of nudging them.** "we need to invest in people and not in nudging people" (S036). Also "we need to make people strong so they can decide themselves rather than um guiding them like sheeps" (S027). Sources: 12 (S001, S022, S027, S029, S031, S033, S036, S037, S038, S045, S046, S030). Confidence: high.
12. **Before you call a deviation a bias, ask what the world rewards.** "base rate neglect is the best thing you can do if your world is changing quickly." (S038). Also "attending to framing in such a situation is a sign of human intelligence" (S012). Sources: 7 (S001, S012, S017, S028, S033, S038, S037). Confidence: high.
13. **Use intuition to raise the alarm and analysis to check it, and trust it only with experience.** "So in that example the intuition inspired the diagnostic." (S038). Also "If you have expertise, decide quickly and do not waste time on endless deliberation." (S046). Sources: 10 (S002, S009, S024, S026, S031, S037, S038, S046, S047, S012). Confidence: high.
14. **Keep a contrarian in the group, and hire people who criticise your work.** "So always have a contrarian." (S002). Also "one of the most important rules is hire a contrarian" (S038). Sources: 2 (S002, S038). Confidence: medium.
15. **Do not fine-tune a heuristic's parameter.** "makes no sense to fine-tune the hiatus from nine months to nine months and two days" (S024). Sources: 2 (S024, S006). Confidence: medium.
16. **Keep your hands off predictive tools for human behaviour unless the real-world test is shown.** "Keep your fingers off these recidivism algorithms." (S045). Also "is it for something uh which is not of this kind like cancer therapy then keep your fingers off it's likely marketing hype" (S008). Sources: 10 (S008, S009, S016, S019, S020, S022, S026, S039, S045, S003). Confidence: high.
17. **Ask an advisor what he would do himself.** "ask the doctor what she would recommend to her own brother or sister or mother the mother wouldn't sue" (S037). Also "ask the waiter what he or she would eat this evening." (S033). Sources: 5 (S006, S024, S032, S033, S037). Confidence: high.
18. **Make a black box transparent if simplicity costs no accuracy, and publish sensitive algorithms.** "if you make it simpler to some point you won't lose any accuracy but you will win transparency." (S020). Also "all sensitive algorithms need to be revealed to the public" (S011). Sources: 6 (S011, S016, S020, S024, S030, S037). Confidence: high.
19. **Check the false alarms on the real population before buying a test or a tool.** "that means roughly on a basis of 12 million twelve thousand" (S026). Inferred from S026, S005, S004: a good hit rate with a small false alarm rate still means many wrong flags when the base rate is low. Sources: 4 (S004, S005, S026, S037). Confidence: high.
20. **If you do not pay for the service, ask who the customer is.** "If you don't pay for something, you should be alarmed." (S030). Sources: 6 (S003, S009, S016, S030, S039, S045). Confidence: high.
21. **Teach statistical thinking in a real problem, not as mathematics.** "teach statistical thinking. And it's important not to teach it as a mathematical discipline." (S004). Sources: 10 (S004, S005, S014, S022, S023, S027, S036, S039, S043, S029). Confidence: high.
22. **Under uncertainty you cannot know the best option; commit.** "that you can never know what's the best solution, but you need to act. You need to commit." (S010). Also "You don't need more information. You need better information, reliable information" (S010). Sources: 3 (S010, S018, S027). Confidence: high.
23. **Take away the shame, not the error.** "Playing safe is no longer safe." (S047). Also "Learn from this error as long as you attribute it to yourself and not to others." (S010). Sources: 5 (S004, S010, S022, S039, S047). Confidence: high.
24. **A significant result alone is useless; report effect size and the real error.** "a significant result by itself is useless. You need to minimize the real error." (S025). Also "There is no effect size in the ritual." (S025). Sources: 3 (S020, S025, S005). Confidence: high.
25. **Do the arithmetic on a marketing claim.** Inferred from S039, S026: "every 11 minutes a single falls in love" becomes about 5 percent a year for a million customers. Quote: "i see people sleepwalking into surveillance" (S039) is his reason for teaching it. Sources: 3 (S026, S039, S009). Confidence: high.
26. **Ask what a regulator, a doctor or a busy person can read.** "if you reduce this to a few variables then the regulator has a chance" (S024). Also "You need sufficiently simple models to have a robust solution." (S006). Sources: 8 (S006, S014, S017, S018, S020, S024, S037, S047). Confidence: high.
