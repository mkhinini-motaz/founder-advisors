# Stories: Gerd Gigerenzer

Built from the notes on S001 to S047 (S048 dropped, never cited). Facts as he tells them; numbers are his, not independently checked, and differ between talks. Quotes are verbatim from the notes; lines without quote marks are paraphrase. The meteorologist anecdote in S014 is an audience member's, so it is excluded. S006 is a stage with Taleb: only Gigerenzer's lines count.

1. **Markowitz and 1/N.** Markowitz won a Nobel prize for mean-variance optimization. Told in S006, S015, S017 and S032 as putting his own retirement money equally into funds; treat the detail as his telling. In the tests the 1/N rule won most (6 of 7 in S006, 7 of 8 in S011 and S024). "We have no complete answer to the question when the 1/N will outperform Markowitz and vice versa." (S004, 2021). Lesson: the optimizer's author did not trust the optimizer under uncertainty.
2. **Google Flu Trends.** Google fitted search terms to flu data, missed the out-of-season swine flu and added more complexity. A rule that predicts this week from last week beat it. Numbers differ: 50 million terms and 160 variables in S004 and S026, 550 million and 260 in S037. "It ignores all data. It's the opposite of big data." (S004, 2021). Lesson: when a model fails, make it simpler.
3. **The Hudson pilots.** They kept a fixed angle to the tower, the gaze heuristic, and landed on the river (S006, S017, S031). Lesson: expert action follows one simple cue.
4. **The dog and the Frisbee.** A dog catching a Frisbee acts as if solving differential equations but keeps the angle constant (S006, S020). Lesson: the process is simple though the as-if model is hard.
5. **The bank leverage ratio.** At the Bank of England a leverage ratio and a short tree predicted bank failure better than complex weighted capital rules (S004, S024). "It is non-compensatory. If you fail on the first one, that's it. Period." (S020, 2025). Lesson: a regulator can read a tree, not hundreds of pages.
6. **Chest-pain triage.** A three-question tree beat a regression card out of sample (S004, S005, S033). Lesson: non-compensatory rules can beat weighted sums.
7. **The airline and the nine months.** A nine-month hiatus rule beat Pareto/NBD, 77 against 74 percent (S006, S024). "If a customer hasn't bought for 9 months classify as inactive, otherwise active." (S006, 2016). Lesson: the simplest rule can pass an honest competition.
8. **Giuliani and prostate cancer.** Survival of 82 percent in the US and 44 in the UK, with mortality about the same (S007, S022, S023). "mortality rates are clear 5year survival rates misleading" (S023, 2025). Lesson: ask for mortality.
9. **The pill scare.** A UK warning that a pill doubled thrombosis risk (1 in 7,000 to 2 in 7,000) led women to stop it, and an estimated 13,000 extra abortions (S004, S014, S038). "always ask percentage of what" (S014, 2015). Lesson: absolute risk saves harm.
10. **After 9/11.** Many Americans drove instead of flying; an estimated 1,600 more died on the road (S004, S014). "terrorists strike twice the first time with physical force that's where our attention goes but the second time with the help of our brains of our anxieties" (S022, 2018). Lesson: dread risk misleads.
11. **German doctors learn natural frequencies.** A short session lifted gynecologists to 87 percent correct on a mammography problem (S004, S007, S023). "the representation in natural frequency does part of the computation and that's the difference" (S023, 2025). Lesson: change the representation, not the person.
12. **The Russian tank.** A neural net seemed to find tanks and had learned the sky or an x-ray machine (S019, S020, S024). "if you read an article about the newest cancer detection procedure always ask is this out of sample or out of population" (S019, 2025). Lesson: test out of population.
13. **The London temperature.** Polynomials of rising degree fit the past better and predicted the future worse (S006, S015, S037). "the proof is in prediction" (S037, 2019). Lesson: fit is not prediction.
14. **The turkey.** Fed every day, his confidence in the farmer grows, until the day before Thanksgiving (S004, S014, S020). Lesson: calm periods say nothing about the next shock.
15. **Fragile Families.** Teams with big data tried to predict life outcomes and did hardly better than simple models (S008, S045). "Keep your fingers off these recidivism algorithms." (S045, 2022). Lesson: human lives are an uncertain world.
16. **The Bezos hiring tree.** Exceptional ability, do I admire the person, will they raise the team's average (S020, S027, S033; the earlier file cited S002, S024, S038, which do not hold it). "If your heart fails, a good liver doesn't help you." (S033, 2026). Lesson: three questions can be enough.
17. **The merger and the gut.** A senior manager felt something wrong about a merger partner and a check found the problem (S024, S038; unsure, the notes only support the general point). "Intuition is a feeling based on years of experience" (S038, 2025). Lesson: intuition raises the alarm, analysis tests it.
18. **The checkpoint tree.** A short tree for soldiers at a checkpoint cut civilian deaths (S024). Lesson: rules help where fear and rush bring errors.
19. **MD Anderson and Watson.** About 62 million dollars spent before the hospital noticed Watson endangered patients (S008, S012, S022, S030, S036). "dr. Watson is not better than any normal doctor and probably worse" (S022, 2018). Lesson: a stable-world win does not transfer.
20. **SCHUFA.** A roughly 250-variable credit score cut to 12 variables with no loss of accuracy (S020). "There's no black boxes anymore." (S020, 2025). Lesson: simplicity buys transparency.
21. **The soccer penalty.** Lehmann's sheet of paper before the 2006 penalties; do not make an expert think about what he is doing (S012). Lesson: experts decide fast.
22. **The Benetton ad.** It raises recognition and gives no product information (S043). Lesson: recognition is a cue, advertising attacks it.
23. **The dating agency claim.** "out of 100 people who pay for the services within a year 95 have nothing" (S009, 2025). Lesson: ask what the evidence is.
24. **The coffee house.** "The salespeople are the customers in this coffee house, not you." (S009, 2025). Lesson: free means you are the product.
25. **The Linda problem.** "and" is polysemous; frequencies make the conjunction error largely vanish (S033, S037). Lesson: the question shapes the answer.
