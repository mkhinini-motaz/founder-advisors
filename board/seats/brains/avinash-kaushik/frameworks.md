# Frameworks: Avinash Kaushik

Built from 55 of his own essays on kaushik.net (2007 to 2026). Reader comments are not his; only his post text and signed replies count. Scoring lines are labeled guidance, inferred from his thresholds. Most-used first.

## 1. Three Layers of So What
Ask "so what" of any metric three times. Each answer must raise the next question. If the third gives no action, the metric is wrong. From his first book, Web Analytics: An Hour A Day.
- Use: before any metric goes on a report or dashboard.
- Score (guidance): third answer names an action and an owner = keep; otherwise kill.
- Quote: "If at the third “so what” you don’t get a recommendation for an action you should take, you have the wrong metric. Kill it." (S066)
- Sources: S066, S041, S051, S107, S098 (5).

## 2. KPI, Diagnostic Metric, Influencing Variable
A KPI is a metric tied directly to the bottom line, with a pre-set target. Diagnostic metrics (4 to 6 per KPI) explain the why. Influencing variables (25 to 35) explain part of a metric. A business has four KPIs at most; a CxO dashboard holds only KPIs. Questioned yearly (KPIs), every six months (metrics).
- Use: cutting a dashboard down to what a leader can act on.
- Score (guidance): no target, no line of sight to profit = it is a metric, not a KPI.
- Quote: "A Key Performance Indicator (KPI), is a metric whose movement directly impacts the company’s bottom line." (S060)
- Sources: S060, S097, S098, S093, S101, S109, S113, S114 (8).

## 3. Insights, Actions, Business Impact (IABI)
A real dashboard or analysis has three text sections beside a little data: what is happening and why, what to do (ranked, with owners), and the expected money impact. Data pukes are not dashboards.
- Use: any output going to an executive.
- Score (guidance): half the page in plain words is a good sign; more than three metrics in a table is a warning.
- Quote: "Your dashboard should have some data, but what it really needs are three sections: Insights, Actions, Business Impact. IABI." (S044)
- Sources: S044, S041, S098, S114, S027, S060 (6).

## 4. Digital Marketing and Measurement Model (DMMM)
Five steps done with stakeholders before touching any tool: why the business exists online, what to focus on, what level of performance means success or failure, what dimensions to analyse. Gives the critical few metrics and a shared view of objectives.
- Use: starting with a business you do not know, or when people disagree on what success is.
- Quote: "No touching Google Analytics. No going to web analytics conferences. No tweeting for help." (S023)
- Sources: S023, S004, S033, S035, S044, S059 (6). In S059 he says the Impact Matrix suits larger firms and the DMMM is the simpler route.

## 5. Macro and micro conversions with Economic Value
Find the single biggest reason a site exists (macro), then every smaller valuable action (micro), and give each a money value. Economic Value is revenue plus the value of all of them.
- Use: any business where most visitors never buy today.
- Quote: "Economic Value is the sum of Revenue plus the Business Value created by the macro- plus micro-conversions on your website." (S079)
- Sources: S004, S023, S043, S045, S079, S127, S112 (7).

## 6. Real business profitability point
Trace the whole funnel to the step where the company actually makes money (kept sales net of returns, closed deals, course completion) and judge channels there.
- Use: when a channel looks great on leads or revenue.
- Quote: "obsess about identifying the real point of business profitability." (S018)
- Sources: S018, S109, S101, S080 (4).

## 7. Money In-Out Continuum and the profit ladder
KPIs are judged by distance from money in the bank: Conversion Rate (far), ROAS (ok), Gross Profit (a KPI), Net Profit (the Most Important KPI), then Customer Lifetime Value. Priority order for ads: POI, POAS, ROI, and last, ROAS.
- Use: choosing the number a founder watches.
- Score (guidance): if you can compute net profit, stop at stage 4; do not insist on CLV before it.
- Quote: "Most Important KPIs are those that are tied to money going into your bank account." (S109)
- Sources: S109, S101, S080, S076, S079 (5).

## 8. Attribution is not incrementality
Attribution shares credit among touchpoints. Incrementality counts the conversions that would not have happened without the marketing. Large-company incrementality tends to be 5% to 25%. Last-click for in-flight optimization, data-driven attribution for monthly budget, incrementality for CFO talks.
- Use: any claim about what a channel adds.
- Quote: "Attribution is simply the science (sometimes, wrongly, art) of distributing credit for Conversions." (S073)
- Sources: S073, S032, S043, S075, S080, S092, S105 (7).

## 9. Controlled experiment (test vs control)
Choose like-type groups, leave the control alone, change the mix only in test, measure revenue, profit, expense and net. Judge by net, not revenue. Act at once, then retest.
- Use: the hard question of what a channel adds, for a firm with enough volume.
- Quote: "So you test, learn, rinse, repeat, become awesomer." (S032)
- Sources: S032, S043, S073, S092, S105, S112 (6).

## 10. Methodology over metric
The metric name can be identical while the method makes it good or bad. Ask for the method behind any number; choose the simpler one. His Methodologies Matrix rates measurement quality against input quality.
- Use: vendor, agency or platform numbers.
- Quote: "Always, always, always ask for the Methodology." (S075)
- Sources: S075, S096, S009, S077, S056 (5).

## 11. Replication Risk and the 90% floor
Weak evidence can fail twice: the sign may not survive a repeat and the size is probably overstated. For consequential spend he uses 90% certainty as the floor and pushes for 95% when spend is large.
- Use: reading a platform's lift study before moving real money.
- Quote: "for consequential business decisions I use 90% Certainty as the floor." (S056)
- Sources: S056, S016, S035 (3). Treat the worked numbers as his illustration.

## 12. N-A-C-R and out-of-sights
Every finding must be Novel, Actionable, Credible and Relative (in context) before it counts. An insight is what the audience can already see; an out-of-sight is what they cannot.
- Use: the weekly self-audit of an analyst's output.
- Score (guidance): count the share of this week's findings that pass all four.
- Quote: "Every finding from your rigorous data analysis has to meet the above-mentioned four attributes – N-A-C-R –, before it can be called an out-of-sight." (S130)
- Sources: S130, S004 (2).

## 13. Reporting Squirrels vs Analysis Ninjas, and the twelve signs
Judge by the work: squirrels spend 75% or more on producing data; ninjas 75% or more on analysis that ends in an action. The twelve signs of a data-driven culture include no aggregate data, custom reports only, and effort split 15% capture, 20% reporting, 65% analysis. 10/90 rule: $10 on tools, $90 on people (reversed to $10 on strategists and $90 on AI in 2026).
- Quote: "Analysis Ninjas spend 75% or more of their time in analysis that delivers actionable insights." (S033)
- Sources: S033, S072, S045, S041, S027 (5). The 2026 reversal is the newer view (S027).

## 14. Effectiveness plus efficiency, two North Stars
Pick one effectiveness metric and one efficiency metric. Put a floor on effectiveness when cutting cost, and let efficiency stretch when effectiveness rises.
- Use: setting a target for a team or an agency.
- Quote: "Don’t pick one true north metric. Pick two." (S076)
- Sources: S076, S079, S101 (3).

## 15. See-Think-Do-Care (STDC)
Four audience intent clusters, not funnel stages. Content first, marketing second, measurement always. Give each cluster its own metric and horizon. Intent beats demographics.
- Use: when a team judges all media by conversion rate.
- Quote: "you should have a content strategy first (and content investment first)." (S094)
- Sources: S094, S046, S097, S112, S113 (5).

## 16. Win before you spend
Creative and media weight together decide the result. Pre-test the creative with a vendor that predicts lift on your own KPI, then check media sufficiency before spending. Passed creative plus sufficient media is almost guaranteed.
- Use: large paid campaigns only; skip for small budgets.
- Quote: "Creative is responsible for 60 – 70% of the success of your Marketing." (S078)
- Sources: S078, S077, S016, S005 (4). Conflict: he is Chief Strategy Officer at a pre-testing vendor (S077).

## 17. Impact Matrix and the ladders of awesomeness
Place metrics by business impact and time-to-useful. Each employee moves one step up and right. Ladders: find your step, max it out in people, process and structure, climb exactly one.
- Quote: "Don’t jump two steps! Just one step forward." (S043)
- Sources: S059, S043, S105, S109 (4).

## Books

### Books: Avinash Kaushik

Built from the book mentions in his own essays (S004, S023, S032, S033, S035, S041, S044, S046, S051, S059). Books other people recommend in comments are not his and are left out.

#### Web Analytics: An Hour A Day (2007)
His first book. Source of the three layers of So What and the outcomes trinity (S051). Proceeds go to charity (S046).
- Quote: "If at the third “so what” you don’t get a recommendation for an action you should take, you have the wrong metric. Kill it." (S066)

#### Web Analytics 2.0 (2009)
Subtitle "The Art of Online Accountability & Science of Customer Centricity" (S127). Holds the multiplicity idea (what, why, what else), the time-series best practice (S035), controlled experiment examples in Chapter 7 (S032), centralized decentralization in Chapter 14 (S033) and a CD of sample dashboards with sparklines. He now says that dashboard lacks the right-hand actions column he insists on (S044).
- He sent a free copy to the contestant who analysed Texas (S004).

#### Occam's Razor (blog) and his newsletter
Not a book, but his main body of work: 0.8 million words by him and 1.7 million in comments (S009). The paid newsletter editions, TMAI Premium, are cited by number on data visualization (S038).

#### Gaps
- No recorded position on other authors' books as his own picks. Readers recommend Krug, Few, Tufte and Zelazny; those are readers' picks, not his (S041, S044, S035).
- Charity: $350,000 donated by 2015 from book proceeds (S046).
