# Stories: Katy Milkman

Built from 30 sources (2021 to 2026). Stories she tells herself, with the lesson she draws. Stories told by a host are not hers and are left out: BJ Fogg's popcorn maker and the host's water fast (S046), the host's treadmill novels and 48-hour reply rule (S020), the host's ice cream and Netflix (S044), the Gandhi and Prussian prince tale (S002), the host's "cycle to the Moon" goal (S030). Where a study size differs between tellings, both are given.

1. **The Google question.** A Google HR leader around 2012 asked whether there is an ideal time to nudge change. The literature had no answer, and it started the fresh start research with Hengchen Dai and Jason Riis. Years later Google told her it had built a "moments engine" (S001, S002, S006, S007, S008, S013, S016, S018, S023, S024, S030, S036). Lesson: timing is a lever and a good question can turn a research agenda.
2. **Grad school and the audiobooks.** Tired after classes, she could not make herself go to the gym or do homework, so she allowed Harry Potter and other audiobooks only at the gym and began craving it. It became temptation bundling and her first study (S001, S007, S017, S028, S034, S036). Lesson: fix your own problem, then test it on others ("me-search", S020).
3. **Agassi and Brad Gilbert.** Agassi, ranked around 30, had a dinner with coach Brad Gilbert, who told him to stop hitting his own game and play to the opponent's weakness; he won the US Open and went on to number one (from Agassi's book Open). She uses it to open How to Change (S008, S013, S018, S024). Lesson: know the opponent, here the barrier.
4. **The Philippines savings account.** About 30 percent took a locked account and savers saved about 80 percent more over the year (Ashraf, Karlan, Yin; she was an admirer, not part of it, S013). Lesson: people will tie their own hands, and higher take-up would help more (S002, S006, S007, S016).
5. **The smoking deposit study.** Smokers offered a forfeitable deposit quit about 30 percent more, even those who did not use it (Dean Karlan and Zinman; S001, S013, S016, S024, S027, S036, S044, S046). In S028 she puts it at about 10 versus 13 percent. Lesson: having the stick available is enough.
6. **Baseball trades.** Players traded across leagues had their stats reset; good performers got worse, weak ones improved (Hengchen Dai; S002, S008, S011, S013). Lesson: do not disrupt a roll.
7. **Air Force Academy roommates.** A random studious roommate lifts your grades; engineered pairing of top and bottom students backfired two years running as the groups split into cliques (Scott Carrell; S001, S002, S026, S028, S032). Lesson: peers must be within reach.
8. **Eskreis-Winkler's advice study.** Struggling people had insight but nobody asked. High-school students who gave advice to younger ones improved their own grades; about 2,000 students and 10 minutes in S007, S032 and S035, about 1,000 in S016, eight minutes in S001 and S046. Lesson: ask, do not advise; the effect is small (S024).
9. **Hotel housekeepers.** Told their work counted as exercise, they lost weight and lowered blood pressure (Langer and Crum; S001, S026, S036). Lesson: belief changes outcomes.
10. **The 24 Hour Fitness megastudy.** About 63,000 members, 30 scientists, 53 treatments (54 in S018), 28 days. About 45 percent beat placebo; 9 percent beat the best-practice arm. Winner: a 9-cent bonus for coming back after one missed visit, plus 27 percent (S014, S040, S045). Lesson: known best practice is hard to beat and experts cannot predict.
11. **The joke text that flopped.** She bet on a cat-and-dog joke message for flu shots; the formal "reserved for you" wording won (S014, S040, S045). Lesson: her own predictions failed too.
12. **Her 401k norm test.** "Everyone is saving" lowered enrolment at an organisation; she does not know why (S013). Lesson: test even a proven idea.
13. **The elastic habits experiment.** She and John Beshears rewarded Google employees for exercising on a rigid or flexible schedule; the rigid group did worse after rewards stopped. Size: about 200 employees in S024, about 2,500 in S027 and S016. Lesson: she was wrong, and said so (S027).
14. **Nick Winter's stakes.** He staked $14,000 of his savings on goals and finished them (S028, S030). Lesson: money you would hate to lose works.
15. **Her father's cigarettes.** She threw his cigarettes in the toilet as a child; he then put the cigarette money in a jar with a penalty for slips, and a year of savings paid for a family trip to New York (S010). Lesson: positive plus negative incentive plus accountability.
16. **The Philadelphia vaccine sweepstakes.** A preregistered randomised lottery did not raise vaccination, which moved her toward mandates (S035).
17. **The 40 percent slide.** At a Penn medical school seminar a pie chart showed about 40 percent of premature deaths come from changeable behaviour, and her research turned from curiosity to mission (S007, S008, S010, S018, S029, S030, S032, S035, S046).
18. **The no club and the advice club.** She was often the only woman in senior faculty votes and built a small email group of women at other schools to ask for advice on what to say no to (S011, S023, S026, S028). Lesson: build the community you lack; giving advice helps the giver.
19. **Facebook voting.** An A/B test with about a million users showing friends who had voted raised turnout, and close friends mattered more (S023). Lesson: social norms work in the right place.
20. **Thaler's advice on the book.** Around 2016 Richard Thaler told her over drinks it was too early to write a book; she waited and says he was right (S019).
