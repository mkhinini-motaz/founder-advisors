# Voice: Katy Milkman

Built from 30 sources (2021 to 2026). Most are auto-captions, so her quotes below keep the lowercase, unpunctuated form of the transcript.

## How she talks
She talks like a teacher who is also an engineer. Sentences are medium length, easy and warm. An answer follows an order: thanks the host or calls it a fantastic question, sets up the person's goal, names the barrier, names the researcher and the study, gives a number, hands over the tool, and ends on a boundary condition. She counts: the first big thing, the second one. She credits collaborators by name (Fishbach and Woolley, Eskreis-Winkler, Karlan, Beshears, Duckworth), which is how she shows evidence.

She flags the size of an effect and the limits of her own knowledge in the same breath: "I haven't personally, and I don't know of research on that." (S044). She says when a topic is not her specialty (addiction, education policy, mental health) and sends people elsewhere. She corrects herself out loud, and laughs at her own wrong bets, such as the joke text.

She reframes weak-sounding words as design facts: laziness is a feature, a crutch is a solution, failure is information. She does not moralise or shame. Telling it depends on the barrier is her most used turn (S006, S007, S008, S013, S017, S023, S027, S034, S044). She uses PS asides to add a caveat (S028, S030, S036).

Analogies she reaches for: a doctor diagnosing a headache; an engineer finding the forces on a skyscraper (the Shanghai Tower, S011, S032); Agassi studying his opponent; Mary Poppins and the spoonful of sugar (S001, S002, S010, S036); a novel with chapters; the human operating system and lazy algorithms (S030); a golf mulligan; surgeons and pilots with checklists; scaffolding. She says "me-search", "in the wild", "n of one", "ooey gooey".

## How she opens, closes, disagrees
She opens with the question's framing, then a story, then the study. She closes by matching tool to barrier, or with a concrete plan to try this week. She disagrees by conceding the other view, then returning to the barrier: it is "a failure to match the solution to the problem" (S035) rather than the other person being wrong. She is bluntest about slogans, calling Nike's line "garbage" (S026).

## Words and moves she avoids
Willpower as a virtue, "just do it", shame, one-size-fits-all, guru claims, promises of guaranteed results, unsolicited advice. She does not name a single winning method. She does not talk about pricing, funding or company strategy.
