# Positions: Hamdi Ulukaya

Built from 46 ok sources, 2012 to 2026. Where a view changed, both are shown with dates. Topics not listed: no recorded position.

## Raising money and investors
- Early (2012 to 2014): no investors. "we are still 100% independent" (S069, 2012-10-31); "we end up making sales almost close to a billion dollars in that factory without raising one penny of capital" (S061, 2022-04-21).
- Fear was control, not money. "will they force me to do things that I don’t want to do?" (S003, 2022-04-04).
- Newer (2022-04-21): welcomes outside shareholders after the critical tests. "this would be a perfect moment for us to be able to have um others to come and join" (S061).
- Newer (2023): unsure about going public. "I have heartburns on how this would affect the company" (S060, 2023-03-29); "So this is not an exit." (S025, 2023-05-01). The 2022 listing was withdrawn (S025).

## Debt
- Took one SBA small business loan to buy the plant (S003). In 2011 turned down a 250 million credit line after doing the sum in minutes (S058, 2018-04-14). No view on debt as a principle: no recorded position.

## Wages and benefits
- "If you make $7 or $8 or $9 an hour, you can’t have a house." (S024, 2018-08-30)
- Minimum wage plus benefits, never in place of them. "why we did that is because math doesn't work" (S057, 2021-04-12).
- Parental leave for every parent, the CEO included (S058). On the minimum wage in 2014: "if we can we need to make life better for everyone" (S070, 2014-02-12).

## Employee ownership
- 2016 shares, about 10 percent of the company. "We used to work together. Now we are partners." (S006, 2016-04-28)
- Shares were slow because he would not promise what the company could not keep (S058). "I hope that this becomes a very normal practice, not that you brag about it." (S059)

## Hiring and refugees
- "The minute they get a job that’s the minute they stop being a refugee." (S041, 2017-04-09)
- Business case: "refugees are staying in the job 30 to 40 percent longer than anybody else." (S003) Later caption wording gives a higher figure: "refugees often three to five times more likely stay in the job longer than anybody else" (S056, 2024-03-28). The two do not agree.
- He began it as community work and named it only around 2015 or 2016 (S066).

## Firing and turnover
- "every year 10% of the people will leave let go or fired, whatever that is just function of business." (S059) He calls separating from people "one of the hardest decisions to make" (S061).

## Pricing and food access
- "I want to make yogurt for everybody." (S043, 2023-06-20) "we found a way to make $1 a yogurt" (S059). Food makers must be aware people struggle to afford food (S025). No recorded position on discounting tactics.

## Focus versus diversification
- He lived in the factory for two years and spent his time on manufacturing: "90% of the time I spent, its factories, supply chain, these boring details that nobody wants to talk about." (S059). No recorded position on side projects.

## Niche and copying
- "I didn’t come from 2,000 miles away to make exactly what we were making back home." (S024) Yet his aim was his mother's yogurt for all (S022).

## Competition
- "I love innovation, I love competing. I hate my competitors." (S041) Big food "never make innovations. They don’t have to." (S003)

## Brand and marketing
- Product first. "buy my product, not because I do good in the world, buy my product because it's the best damn product there is." (S002) He answered customers himself: "the 1-800 number that was in the cup was my personal number." (S014)

## Partners and board
- "So for me, the board was me, myself, and I." (S002)

## Culture
- "Funny thing about culture is it’s either real, or it’s just not there, or it’s just in the walls and it’s in the papers." (S003) Built in hard times: "those are culture building times" (S060).
- No patience for disrespect: "One thing that I have no patience for is disrespect" (S003). Anger is fine when "it’s not personal" (S003).

## First hire and early team
- Hired four of the 55 workers let go from the Kraft plant first (S058, S055). The lesson he draws is that people who were given up on built it back.

## Metrics
- Judge by how, not size: "it's not how much you sell it's not how big you become" (S069). He asked whether the same shoppers buy again (S068). "Spreadsheets are lazy." (S055)

## Selling the company
- Refused a forced offer to walk away. "I told myself, if I took that offer, I’d die a little bit every day for the rest of my life." (S013, 2025-06-13)

## AI
- "what matters most is the hand that wields it and the heart that guides it." (S029, 2025)

## Remote work, work hours, burnout
- He worked in the plant without leaving for years: "I did not leave that factory for seven years, more than three days" (S059). He offered staff freedom to stay home when unwell (S057). No recorded position on remote work.

## Timing and crises
- "we don’t want crisis and challenges, but we don’t want to waste it either."  "i said let's transform from this let's not just survive" (S057).

## Business and social problems
- "I think if business act right like truly right is the most powerful platform to solve most alarming problems that we're facing today" (S060). Yet "That idea hasn't been proven yet." for stakeholder firms in public markets (S002).


## Greed and the rich
- Early distrust: "I didn’t even like it I didn’t like the rich" (S065). His fix: "how do i build a place where i don't become a person that i grew up hating" (S061).

## Giving
- "Since day one, we have given 10% of our profits to charity through the Chobani Foundation" (S047, 2015-05-28). Most of his wealth pledged to refugees (S047).

## Food waste
- Saw fruit left in the field because it looked wrong and agreed to invest (S054).

## Government
- Business as the faster actor, with local governments as partners: "Can we remove the bureaucracy?" (S008, 2026-06-23). "we should get an example of Idaho how a government and a business can work together" (S070).
