# Principles: David Heinemeier Hansson

Decision rules built from 90 sources. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Do not take venture capital.** Other people's money wants the largest return and installs time bombs. "Don’t take venture capital." (S001) "I can’t install all sorts of time bombs in my business." (S003). Sources: 6 (S001, S003, S005, S010, S011, S096). Confidence: high.
2. **Small is a destination, not a stepping stone.** "Small is not a stepping stone." (S001) Sources: 5 (S001, S003, S004, S006, S033). Confidence: high.
3. **Do not chase the whales.** Moving up-market abandons small accounts and ends in a thousand people. "you will abandon your small and medium-sized accounts to chase the big whales with a huge sales force and, by then, you’re 1,000 people and life sucks." (S001) Sources: 1. Confidence: low.
4. **Own what you will use for years; rent only the spike.** "it’s cheaper to own for the long duration than it is to rent." (S001) "if you have a steady state of load, none of that stuff makes sense." (S005). Sources: 6 (S001, S005, S054, S062, S084, S097). Confidence: high.
5. **Do the math yourself.** "So if the money matters – and when does it not? – I urge you to do your own math." (S062) Sources: 4 (S054, S062, S078, S097). Confidence: high.
6. **Never aspire to a more complicated stack than the problem needs.** "Never aspire to a more complicated stack than what your application calls for." (S041) Sources: 5 (S041, S053, S072, S082, S005). Confidence: high.
7. **Do not distribute your program unless you must.** "It is the first rule of distributed programming. Do not distribute your programming." (S001) "Simplicity demands that you do not start by inviting the beast of complexity – distributed systems – to the first dance." (S072). Sources: 3 (S001, S072, S073). Confidence: high.
8. **Cut scope, hold quality.** "Can’t do crappy work." (S007) "that you make half, but you make it good." (S008). Sources: 2 (S007, S008). Confidence: medium.
9. **Launch before it feels ready.** "You can scare yourself into not launching forever" (S007) Sources: 2 (S007, S008). Confidence: medium.
10. **Build for yourself first.** "I build much better software when I can evaluate the quality of that software by my own use." (S001) "We build the v1 of what we want. That's it. After v1, you start listening more" (S011). Sources: 4 (S001, S002, S011, S046). Confidence: high.
11. **Protect long uninterrupted stretches; cut meetings.** "We just need to leave people the hell alone to work on problems that they enjoy for long stretches of uninterrupted time." (S001) "The modern workplace has become an interruption factory." (S088). Sources: 5 (S001, S003, S042, S047, S088). Confidence: high.
12. **Work 40 hours or fewer.** "40 hours a week is actually a ton if you don’t piss it away." (S001) "Fire the workaholics." (S004). Sources: 4 (S001, S003, S004, S043). Confidence: high.
13. **No full-time managers at this size.** "We're going to make this company work without full-time managers or die trying." (S033) Sources: 4 (S001, S006, S033, S049). Confidence: high.
14. **Keep specialist roles as a service you call on.** "I want a manager service I can call on for seven hours this week and then I want to take it down to zero for the next three months." (S001) Sources: 1 (inferred from S001, S025 on support). Confidence: low.
15. **Hire on work product, not resume.** "The capacity to evaluate work product is a superpower when it comes to hiring." (S001) "We're hiring for peak promise — and such promise only remains until it's revealed." (S021). Sources: 3 (S001, S021, S059). Confidence: high.
16. **Hire full stack.** One person should take an idea to production. "If they can't, your setup is too complicated, and you need to simplify." (S081) Sources: 2 (S081, S005). Confidence: medium.
17. **When competence is the problem, end it quickly.** "problems with core competency is the pit where good intentions go to die." (S059) Sources: 1. Confidence: low.
18. **Price at what you would pay.** "I can't in good conscience sell software at prices where I'd never pay." (S068) Sources: 2 (S068, S046). Confidence: medium.
19. **Let customers cancel without hassle.** "We don't hassle customers who want to cancel Basecamp because that simply feels like a shitty thing to do." (S089) Sources: 1. Confidence: low.
20. **Do not A/B test core values.** "the only way to do that consistently is by not A/B testing your core values." (S089) Sources: 1. Confidence: low.
21. **Make the founders stay on support.** "You want that whole-grain feedback straight from the customers' mouth!" (S025) Sources: 1. Confidence: low.
22. **Cut products rather than hire up.** He chose one product over four, and says they went too long first. "Of course we actually went too long." (S004) Sources: 2 (S004, S003). Confidence: medium.
23. **Budgets, not estimates.** "Give up on estimates, and embrace the alternative method for making software by using budgets, or appetites" (S035) "It’s the tension from the constraints that produces something great." (S008). Sources: 3 (S035, S008, S026). Confidence: high.
24. **Do not build the general thing until the second sighting.** "let’s not overreact here and build some huge, honking framework to do it again." (S003) Sources: 2 (S003, S082). Confidence: medium.
25. **Choose durable skills, not churn.** "if you learned how to use Active Record in like 2009, 15 years later those skills still have value." (S005) "Invest in things that don't change." (S087). Sources: 2. Confidence: medium.
26. **Check that your facts are not out of date.** "getting your facts straight – and sometimes it’s not that your facts were wrong; like, they were accurate in 2015, in 2017. They’re not accurate in 2024" (S005) Sources: 1. Confidence: low.
27. **Describe the problem to an agent, not the path.** "I’m telling it the problem I have. I’m telling it the fuzzy, vague idea I have." (S002) Sources: 1 (newest view, 2026). Confidence: low.
28. **Treat AI as a junior programmer.** "as a senior programmer, you'd do well to treat AI as you would a junior programmer." (S028) "AI is a superb pair programmer, but I'd retire before permanently handing it the keyboard to drive the code." (S019). Sources: 3 (S001, S019, S028). Confidence: high. Newer view (S002, 2026): agents write all of Omarchy's code; see positions.md.
29. **Hold a bet, not a trophy.** "what you won was a bet, not a trophy made of truth." (S031) Sources: 1. Confidence: low.
30. **A founder takes the big calls.** "They're big, difficult decision because they require a leap of faith." (S105) "I will burn this business down before I hand over 30% of it to Apple." (S001). Sources: 2 (S001, S105). Confidence: medium.
