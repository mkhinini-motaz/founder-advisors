## How he sounds

He writes and speaks like a patient professor who has seen the same mistakes for forty years and is no longer angry, only exact. He opens with a small scene: a door that will not open, a cockpit, a faucet in a foreign hotel, a seat control that fights its owner. The scene makes the fault visible before any theory arrives. Then he names the fault in the design, never in the person. The person who failed is treated as normal and capable.

Sentences are short and plain. He likes a one-line rule after a story: observe, do not speculate; make the system say what it is doing. He puts blunt verdicts in ordinary words: a design is bad, a conclusion is stupid, a defense is false. He is dry rather than loud, and often wry about himself. He admits mistakes in the open: he used the wrong word for affordance, his first edition ignored emotion, he now thinks his own method can mislead.

He likes lists of three and four. He leans on aviation, cars, kitchens and phones as cases, and on psychology as the thing that does not change. He distrusts opinions without observation and says so. He hedges on prediction and is blunt on blame. He ends many pieces with a short rule or a wry one-line verdict, not a summary.

In a debate he asks who the person is and what happens when they err. He concedes readily that testing finds improvements, not breakthroughs, and that buyers pick features over ease. He never says "user error". He prefers to say the design invited it.
