# Stories: Don Norman

Built from the 91 sources in `sources.md`. Told in my own words, with short verbatim lines where noted.

1. **The Macintosh power switch (S007).** The hard power switch let people cut power mid-save and lose work. The team hid the switch and made a software shut down easy to reach. Lesson: hide the destructive control, show the safe path. "Experience indicated that we should hide the power switch, while making software shut down as readily available and visible as possible." (S007)

2. **The L1011 O-rings (S017).** A mechanic fitted a part in a way the design allowed, and a rare combination of events followed. He asks who is at fault and answers: the system. "Who is at fault here? ... The system? Yes." (S017)

3. **Phobos 1 and the cost of a typo (S002, S065).** A command error ended a space mission. The lesson he draws is that a system sensitive to a single slip is badly designed. "there is no excuse for equipment and procedures that are so sensitive to human error" (S002)

4. **The Royal Majesty (S089).** A ship's navigation system lost its feed and kept showing a position; the crew trusted it. Lesson: a system must say what it is assuming. "You want a system that is basically always telling you what it's thinking, what its assumptions are, what it's about to do" (S089)

5. **Norman doors and the English taps (S043, S045, S090).** Doors that need a sign to say push or pull, and taps where hot and cold are guesswork, show that bad design is invisible only when it works. Lesson: the fault is the door.

6. **The Lufthansa seat (S069).** A seat control shaped like the seat, correct in theory, still failed in use and came with a long manual. "Oops, 14 pages? That should be warning enough." (S069)

7. **The brick in the Google auditorium door (S014).** A propped door defeated the security it was meant to serve. Lesson: when security is a nuisance, well-meaning people defeat it.

8. **The Unix lost-work story (S043).** A command wiped work with no way to recover. Lesson: make it easy to back out; undo matters.

9. **Domino's pizza tracker (S078).** A company that treated the whole experience, including the waiting, as part of the product. Lesson: think of the service from intention to reflection.

10. **The iDrive and its 700 settings (S024).** A car interface that hid everyday controls in menus. "But the solution is better design, not hiding them away in a complex menu structure." (S024)

11. **The Dutch traffic engineer (S039).** Removing signs and rules made drivers pay attention. Lesson: sometimes the safer system gives people more to read, not less.

12. **Apple's early digital camera (S091).** He predicted it would change the world; it failed three times and took 15 to 20 years. "The world wasn't ready for it." (S091)

13. **The commencement speech edit (S091).** He asked Word's assistant to shorten his draft, saw it cut things he valued, and finished by hand. Lesson: the system drafts, the person judges.
