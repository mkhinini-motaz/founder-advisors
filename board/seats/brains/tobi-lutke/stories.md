# Stories: Tobi Lütke

Built from 11 sources, 2011 to 2026.

1. Snowdevil. He and a friend sold snowboards online, hated the software, built their own on Ruby on Rails and launched Shopify in 2006. He closed the store in 2005 because he was prouder of the software. Lesson: build the tool you need. (S001, S002, S004, S012, S017, S031)
2. The first model was wrong. A flat percentage taxed the best merchants; the 2007 subscription change was "the true launch". Lesson: price so your best customers stay. (S004)
3. The 40,000 stores. In 2008 a big VC passed on a market of 40,000 online stores. Years later he thought they may have been right about the market as it stood. Lesson: you can grow a market, but it can also be small. (S002, S018, S031)
4. Customers' overnight feature. A user complained of a missing feature; he built it overnight and replied that it was already there. Lesson: speed to the customer. (S001, S002)
5. The Build a Business prize, 2009. Shopify was short of cash, so the last $100,000 became a competition with Tim Ferriss; it made the NYT and brought DODOcase. Lesson: do what people do not expect. (S001, S004, S012)
6. DODOcase. An iPad case maker found the last book binders in San Francisco and now employs dozens. Lesson: a merchant's success can revive a trade. (S001, S012)
7. TheCHIVE t-shirt drops, about 2010. They crashed Shopify; he rebuilt for the peak, and later did the same for Supreme and Kylie Jenner. Lesson: the hardest customer is the gym. (S031)
8. Point of sale. Merchants asked for inventory sync; the real answer was a POS on the same database as the store. Lesson: right problem, wrong solution. (S018)
9. Unicorn. A peer thank-you system with 1% of revenue; a new hire who looked weak at three months was the second-highest recipient. Lesson: the company knows more than its managers. (S004, S012)
10. Post-IPO drift. He copied how a public-company CEO should act, delegated, and found boondoggles. COVID forced a review: about 60% of projects cancelled, every executive turned over in a year. Lesson: stay in the details. (S002, S033, S035)
11. Stock fell 80%, 2021 to 2022. He rebuilt pay around a number and sliders. Lesson: keep the stock out of the work. (S033, S035)
12. The father-in-law and the bedroom. He started in his wife's childhood bedroom, no salary for about four years, payroll covered by his father-in-law. Lesson: keep costs low. (S001, S002, S033)
13. Shipping container. Malcolm McLean cared about moving goods, not trucks; cost per unit fell from about $6 to $0.16. Lesson: solve the underlying problem. (S001)
14. Cargo cults. Islanders kept running the airfield to bring the cargo back. Lesson: copying behaviour does not copy outcomes. (S002)
15. Apprenticeship in Germany. His meister said code freezes after about two years; Shopify's old code is a delight because understanding accumulates. Lesson: get the problem domain right early. (S001, S003, S031)
16. Gymshark and Kylie. A founder ran five stores while delivering pizza before it hit; a small team ran a $900 million invoice. Lesson: small teams reach far on the platform. (S002, S018)
