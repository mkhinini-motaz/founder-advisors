# Principles: Tobi Lütke

Built from 11 sources, 2011 to 2026. Confidence: high is 3 or more sources, medium 2, low 1.

1. Make the merchant more successful and growth follows. "what I need to do is make my customers more successful." (S002) Sources: S001, S002, S003, S017, S018 (5). High.
2. Do not chase revenue as the aim. "We don't build our business to make revenue, basically." (S018) "Money is sort of a side effect of this critical success." (S012) Sources: S012, S017, S018 (3). High.
3. Remove the step where people give up. "every single time we made something significantly simpler, it actually caused more success." (S003) Sources: S001, S002, S003, S018 (4). High.
4. Stay close to the front line and take support yourself. "the distance to front line becoming bigger has probably killed more companies" (S001) Sources: S001, S002, S004, S031 (4). High.
5. Keep one product and one sentence to say no with. "Shopify is going to be our only product though, and it will remain so." (S004) Sources: S004, S012, S031 (3). High.
6. Subtract on purpose. "The best thing founders can do is subtraction." (S035) Sources: S003, S035 (2). Medium.
7. Take risk by being different. "There’s exactly one thing that doesn’t take risk, which is do the same thing everyone else does." (S035) Sources: S002, S033, S035 (3). High.
8. Do not copy the surface of successful players. "It’s basically just putting flame stickers on the side of a car to make it go faster, really." (S002) Sources: S002, S033 (2). Medium.
9. Do not build only what others like. "Thinking we have to build products that other people like. I think this is the silent killer." (S003) Sources: S002, S003, S004 (3). High.
10. Give one person with taste a veto. "He has complete veto rights over every feature going in." (S004) Sources: S004, S033 (2). Medium.
11. Sweat the details; users can tell. "The way you do anything is how you do everything." (S033) Sources: S002, S004, S033 (3). High.
12. Do not fire the customer who breaks you; build for the peak. "We are using this as a gym and we’re going to use them." (S031) Source: S031 (1). Low.
13. Never let a feature lower checkout conversion. "we might not want to reduce conversion rates ever." (S031) Source: S031 (1). Low.
14. Tell customers when the software is wrong for them. "I’d rather not anyone use the software if it’s bad for them" (S031) Source: S031 (1). Low.
15. Make high expectations clear early. "It’s actually an unkind lie to obscure this." (S002) Sources: S001, S002 (2). Medium.
16. Give raw feedback, then say it kindly. "Is this really the best way we can do this?" (S001) Sources: S001, S002 (2). Medium. He later softened "This is shit" to this wording.
17. Treat a costly mistake as tuition, not grounds to fire. "See it as your tuition." (S002) Source: S002 (1). Low.
18. Change your mind on better information and discount sunk cost. "just change your opinion every time you get better information." (S033) Sources: S033, S035 (2). Medium.
19. Say publicly when you are wrong. Inferred from S002 (he wrote openly about closing a logistics department) and S031. Medium.
20. Keep costs low early; do not build monuments. "when companies build monuments to themselves, they usually decline right after." (S033) Sources: S033, S012 (2). Medium.
21. Do not use money to drive the right behaviour. "the coin-operated view of the world is also one of those things that the business community needs to bury" (S018) Sources: S012, S018, S033 (3). High.
22. Do not be the bottleneck. "I really just don't want to be the bottleneck of my business." (S018) Sources: S001, S018 (2). Medium.
23. Replace toil, not people. "What we should replace is toil and drudgery and bullshit." (S035) Sources: S035, S017 (2). Medium.
24. Prove AI cannot do it before asking for headcount. "Before asking for more Headcount and resources, teams must demonstrate why they cannot get what they want done using AI." (S017) Sources: S017, S031, S033 (3). High (dated April 2025).
