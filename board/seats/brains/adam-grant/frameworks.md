# Frameworks: Adam Grant

Built from 58 usable public sources (essays, newsletters, talks, podcasts) 2013 to 2026. Ordered most-used first.

## 1. Preacher, prosecutor, politician vs scientist
- What: three mindsets that block rethinking (from Tetlock). Preacher defends a sacred belief, prosecutor attacks to win, politician pleases an audience. The alternative is the scientist, who treats an opinion as a hypothesis.
- Steps: notice the mode; drop it; ask questions to learn; run a small test.
- Use when: a team, a founder or a customer is stuck defending a view.
- Scoring guidance (ours): if the decision is argued only by conviction, with no test and no stated change condition, treat it as unrethought.
- Quote: "good scientists are as motivated to disprove their hypotheses as they are to support them." (S139)
- Sources: S087, S126, S132, S139, S155 (5).

## 2. Givers, takers, matchers
- What: three reciprocity styles. Givers help without strings, takers try to come out ahead, matchers trade evenly. Givers sit at both the bottom and the top of performance.
- Steps: classify the people and the culture; find who the givers are and protect them; screen out takers.
- Use when: hiring, partnerships, team culture, a founder's own habits.
- Scoring guidance (ours): a taker in a senior seat is a red flag; a team of selfless givers with no boundaries is a burnout risk.
- Quote: "there are not two, but three kinds of people in the world. I call them takers, givers and matchers." (S013)
- Sources: S013, S121, S130, S141, S151 (5).

## 3. Failed vs successful givers (who, when, how)
- What: successful givers put others first most of the time but help in ways not at odds with their own interests: five-minute favors, check history, block time for own goals, specialise.
- Use when: a founder says yes to everyone.
- Quote: "it fundamentally comes down to the choices we make every day about who we help, when we help, and how we help." (S130)
- Sources: S013, S121, S130 (3).

## 4. Challenge network
- What: critics who attack your logic because they believe in you, beside a support network of cheerleaders. Pure challenge without support is abusive.
- Steps: pick people who respect your thinking; ask for advice, not praise; model openness first.
- Quote: "you need not just a support network of people who encourage you and cheer lead for you, but also a challenge network of thoughtful critics, coming in good faith to poke holes in your thinking" (S155)
- Sources: S117, S126, S138, S141, S155 (5).

## 5. Advice, not feedback
- What: feedback looks backward and breeds cheerleaders and critics; advice looks forward and creates coaches. Ask many people, then separate quality from taste.
- Rule of thumb: if five people independently raise one point, act on it; one strong view is taste.
- Quote: "it's actually more helpful to ask for advice than feedback because advice is forward looking." (S138)
- Sources: S117, S121, S126, S138, S141 (5).

## 6. List what would change your mind
- What: before committing, write the conditions under which you would revise. Ask others the same.
- Scoring guidance (ours): a plan with no stated kill or revise condition is not ready.
- Quote: "make a list of the circumstances when you would change your mind." (S126)
- Sources: S099, S126, S132, S139, S155 (5).

## 7. Aim for a nine, not a ten (calibrated targets)
- What: set the target score by how much the task matters, with an acceptable range, not perfection.
- Quote: "A 10 is for excellence, not for perfection." (S138)
- Also: "Healthy goals include two targets: an aspirational result and an acceptable outcome. If you fall anywhere between them, you haven’t failed." (S111)
- Also: "instead of aiming for perfection it's healthier and more effective to strive for excellence" (S186); excellence is high standards of quality, perfectionism is a need to be flawless that makes setbacks a threat.
- Sources: S111, S121, S138, S141, S186 (5).

## 8. Hidden potential: trajectory over starting point
- What: judge people by how far they travel, weight recent performance, bet on the most improved.
- Quote: "potential is not about where you start, it’s about how far you can travel." (S121)
- Sources: S121, S138, S141 (3).

## 9. Demand, control, support (burnout)
- What: burnout is structural. Cut demands, add support, or add control. The Cleveland Clinic started by cutting demands such as electronic overload; a small say over how or where you work helps for jobs as different as assistant, police officer or retail associate.
- Quote: "burnout can happen to anyone in any line of work but it doesn't have to" (S185)
- Sources: S084, S132, S185 (3).

## 10. Why-to, not how-to
- What: when behaviour will not change, people often lack a reason, not a method. Show the person the work serves.
- Quote: "This is not a how-to problem. People know how to wash their hands. It's a why-to problem." (S130)
- Sources: S130 (1; related evidence in S132 on culture and mission).

## 13. Escalation of commitment
- What: doubling down on a losing course. Fix: rethink in foresight, count sunk cost as sunk.
- Quote: "I’d rather admit now that you wasted two years, than see you waste the next 20." (S126)
- Also in the TED talk: a Panama volcano climb and a diving career he outgrew. "The ultimate goal is to make it down the mountain, not just to reach the top." (S178) Gritty people overplay their hands in casinos, and the most tenacious mountaineers die more often (S178).
- Sources: S121, S126, S178 (3).

## 14. Fear and futility behind silence
- What: people stay silent because of fear and futility. Leaders must say they want candid input and criticise themselves aloud.
- Sources: S117, S138 (2).

## 15. Strengths in the right situation
- What: overused strengths turn into weaknesses; know when to turn one off.
- Sources: S117 (1).

## 16. Identity foreclosure
- What: settling early on who you are and closing your mind to other selves. Fix: try on a range of possible selves; ask what values (grow, excel, contribute) a new role could still serve.
- Quote: "Foreclosing on one identity is like following a GPS that gives you the right directions to the wrong destination." (S178)
- Sources: S178 (1).

## 17. Peak flow: mastery, mindfulness, mattering
- What: flow needs small wins of progress, full attention, and a sense that your effort matters to someone. Bingeing is passive escape; active play with other people works better. Languishing is the neglected middle between depression and flourishing.
- Quote: "mastery, mindfulness and mattering" (S177)
- Use when: a team or founder is flat but not burned out.
- Sources: S177, S156 (mattering and the need to give) (2).

## 18. Originals: quick to start, slow to finish
- What: moderate procrastination raised idea creativity by 16 percent in his experiments; improvers beat first movers (failure 47 percent against 8 percent); doubt the idea, not yourself.
- Quote: "Procrastinating is a vice when it comes to productivity, but it can be a virtue for creativity." (S179)
- Scoring guidance (ours): do not mark a team down for starting small and delaying the final form; mark down the team with no tested idea.
- Sources: S179, S172 (2).

## 19. Task conflict vs relationship conflict
- What: task conflict (the problem, solution or decision) can be productive; relationship conflict (personalities, values) is usually destructive. Work out which you are in before you argue.
- Quote: "task conflict is disagreeing about the problem the solution or the decision" (S188)
- Sources: S188, S087 (2).

## 20. Pitch the problem first
- What: do not open with your bold vision. First show what is wrong with the present, then your solution; less selling is more.
- Quote: "no one cares what you'll create in the future until you convince them there's something wrong with the present" (S189)
- Sources: S189 (1).

## 21. Taking vs receiving
- What: taking is using someone for gain; receiving is accepting help that benefits you more than it costs the giver. Say thanks, then pay it forward, not "I owe you".
- Quote: "Taking is using someone for personal gain." (S164)
- Sources: S164, S013 (2).

## 22. Self-promotion that works
- What: self-promotion only paid off when the audience was distracted enough to remember the information but forget the source; admit limits first, then show motivation and skill, and use humor.
- Quote: "Humor signals competence without arrogance." (S162)
- Sources: S162 (1).

## 23. Diagnose before you change
- What: before a change program, find the real cause; copying a famous brand is not a diagnosis.
- Quote: "the surgeons didn't just misdiagnose the problem they failed to diagnose it at all" (S183)
- Sources: S183, S130 (2).

## Books

### Books: Adam Grant

Built from 66 usable public sources. Quick depth. Summaries are in my own words.

- **Give and Take (2013)**: people are givers, takers or matchers. Givers fill both the top and the bottom of performance; successful givers set boundaries and give where cost is low and benefit high. Taught in S013, S130, S141, S151.
- **Originals (2016)**: how non-conformists generate and champion new ideas; creativity as a different kind of potential. Mentioned in S130, S141, S155; his TED talk on original thinkers is S179, and the Warby Parker story is told there.
- **Option B (2017, with Sheryl Sandberg)**: resilience after adversity. Named only as a book on resilience (S141). Little else in this corpus.
- **Think Again (2021)**: rethinking as a skill. Think like a scientist, not a preacher, prosecutor or politician; build a challenge network; know what would change your mind. Taught in S126, S132, S139, S155.
- **Hidden Potential (2023)**: potential is built, not found. Character as skill, deliberate play, scaffolding, trajectory over starting point, aiming for a nine. Taught in S121, S138, S141, S182 (the Wharton talk with Angela Duckworth).
- **VIBE (2026)**: sparking, strengthening and sustaining bonds; closeness depends on depth, not frequency. Only the newsletter and one essay appear (S097, S115).
