# Principles: Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Built from 14 sources. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Lines credited to "the paper" are co-authored (S034, S035, S048).

1. Survive first, then take risks. "Take all the risks you want, but make sure you're there tomorrow." (S012). Also S032, S034, S045. High.
2. Never accept a chance of ruin because it is small. "we must consider any genuine risk of total ruin as if it were inevitable." (S034, the paper). Also S017, S035. High.
3. Remove the single exposure that can blow you up. "So that tiny, tiny, tiny tail option is what cost the banking system." (S011). Also S016, S017. High.
4. Change the exposure, not the forecast. "truncating payoffs blunt the effects of the inadequacy of the metrics." (S017). Also S016, S034. High.
5. Judge a bet by the size of the loss when it fails. "One can be right 99% of the time, but it does not matter at all since with some skewed distribution, the consequence on the expectation of the 1% error can be too large." (S017). Also S045. Medium.
6. Ask what the adviser holds. "do not tell people what you think, tell them what you have in your portfolio" (S035, the paper). Also S010, S012, S032, S046. High.
7. Do not give advice you do not follow. "you should never tell people what they should be doing. You should tell them what you're doing" (S012). Also S046. Medium.
8. Steady income is a warning sign. "Companies that have steady income are short an option somewhere." (S011). Also S016, S035. High.
9. If a risk may explode, act early. "If you must panic, panic early." (S011). Low.
10. Keep spare capacity. "redundancy is like an option. One certainly pay for it, but it may be necessary for survival." (S017). Also S011, S016. High.
11. Prefer what has lasted. "Things that have worked for a long time are preferable" (S016). Also S017, S046, S012. High.
12. Subtract before you add. "It is often better to remove structure or allow natural variation to take place rather than to add something additional to the system." (S034, the paper). Also S011, S016, S045. High.
13. Eliminate fragility before chasing antifragility. "The first thing to be antifragile is first, you have to eliminate fragilities." (S011). Low.
14. Run many cheap trials with a capped downside. "A large exposure to a single trial has lower expected return than a portfolio of small trials." (S041). Also S034, S032. High.
15. Plan short and keep exits. "plans need to 1) stay flexible with frequent ways out, and, counter to intuition 2) be very short term, in order to properly capture the long term." (S041). Low.
16. Fund the business with real cash flow, not a sale story. "I come from old finance, old-school finance where you haven't really succeeded until the company gets a strong cash flow base." (S045). Also S011. Medium.
17. Stay in your own trade. "unless you’re a trader, don’t trade." (S011). Also S032. Medium.
18. Do not copy the crowd. "If you imitate others, you're going to be late to the game, and you will also -- of course -- be late in exiting." (S032). Low.
19. Know where to stop. "Artisans typically have the instinct to stop." (S032). Low.
20. Never attach certainty to an estimate. "Taking all estimates and risks as an objective truth (i.e., attaching certitude to the estimate) is the greatest mistake a decision maker can make" (S048, the paper). Also S017, S034. High.
21. Demand evidence of safety, not of danger. "You need evidence of safety—not evidence of lack of safety" (S016). Also S034. Medium.
22. Use data to debunk, not to confirm. "Big data can tell us what's wrong, not what's right." (S052). Low.
23. Care about the right people's approval. "If people you respect approve of your action, not if the general public does." (S011). Also S045. Medium.
24. Keep a Plan B you can use. "So you write the resignation letter and you don’t date it." (S011). Low.
25. Where you cannot enforce skin in the game, shrink the cost of errors. "in situations in which we cannot enforce skin-in-the-game we should change the system to lower the consequences of errors." (S035, the paper). Low.
