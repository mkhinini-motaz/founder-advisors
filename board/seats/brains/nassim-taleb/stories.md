# Stories: Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Built from 14 sources. Stories from S034 and S035 are told by the paper (co-authored). Lessons are my reading.

1. **The turkey**: fed for 1,000 days, a surprise on day 1,001. A calm record hides the tail. (S016, S017 as "so you do not become a turkey")
2. **Plaza Accord, September 1985**: a huge move on tiny risk convinced him that prices have fat tails; he built a career on tail options, and the 1987 crash paid. Look at the payoff shape. (S011, S045)
3. **Fannie Mae**: small rate moves meant small losses, large ones much larger. He said so in the Black Swan and to the Times in 2003; it blew up about a decade later. Convex loss can be seen before the event. (S011, S032)
4. **Competitor who mixed hedges**: sold German puts to pay for US puts and went out of business when both fell together. Mitigating a strategy created a hidden dependence. (S011)
5. **Green lumber trader**: the man who knew every fact about lumber lost to a pit trader who thought it was lumber painted green. Practice teaches what theory misses. (S012)
6. **Butcher versus Hollywood surgeon**: same record, pick the butcher. Polish is a cost. (S012)
7. **Robert Rubin at Citibank and the Babuban trade**: bonuses taken, losses left for the public. Heads they win, tails someone else loses. (S012, S046)
8. **Selling volatility (Niederhoffer)**: sold options for pennies, bought them back at forty dollars. A steady small income hides a tail short. (S011)
9. **Long Term Capital Management, 1998**: Nobel-crowned people blew it up; the lesson was not learned. (S016, S055)
10. **Subprime sale**: a friend was sold leveraged subprime on thirty years of data showing it was safe. The past sample shows no tail. (S016)
11. **Morgan Stanley**: predicted the credit crisis and nearly collapsed because it missed the size of the damage. Right on direction is not enough. (S017)
12. **The Lindy restaurant**: a restaurant went bankrupt between his manuscript and publication; actors picked plays with 200 days behind them for 200 more. (S012, S046)
13. **Mao's sparrows**: killing sparrows led to insect plagues and famine. Disrupting a system you do not understand. (S045)
14. **Potato famine and monoculture**: one disease became a national disaster. (S034, the paper)
15. **The artisan and the consultant**: Jobs as the largest-scale artisan who stopped at a product line. (S032)
16. **Hammurabi's builder rule**: if the house falls and kills the owner, the builder dies. The best risk rule (S035, the paper).
17. **Domagk and the first antimicrobials**: hundreds of systematic trials of dyes, no theory, then a big win. Optionality. (S041)
