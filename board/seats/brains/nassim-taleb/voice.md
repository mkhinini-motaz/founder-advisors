# Voice: Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Built from 14 sources: three live conversations (S010, S011, S012, S032, S045, S046), essays and papers. Lines from papers are drier than his speech.

## How he talks
Short, declarative sentences with blunt asides. He opens by correcting the question: it is not about betting on tail events, it is about not being harmed. He then puts a concrete test in front of the listener (a plane, a tightrope, a glass of water, a trip to Mars), and draws the rule from the answer. He reduces any question to an asymmetry: who gains, who loses, how bad is the worst case. He uses trader examples first, then a real-life one (restaurants, drivers, Seattle's net worth). He reaches back to antiquity (Romans, Hammurabi, Phoenicians) and to nature (cockroaches, coffee cups, artichokes).

He names names and insults by category: bullshit vendor, the Intellectual Yet Idiot, turkey, charlatan. He swears, and apologizes for it. He turns the question back on the person asking. He jokes at his own expense. He closes with a dry line or a rule: what to avoid, never what to do. He says "my advice is what to avoid, no more." (S016)

## Signature phrases
- "skin in the game", used in nearly every source
- "fat tails", "Extremistan", "turkey" (S011, S016, S017, S045)
- "via negativa", subtract before adding (S034 the paper, S045)
- "the dose" (S011)
- "payoff", never "probability" alone (S017, S045)

## Moves
- Opens: corrects the question, or says a surprising "no".
- Argues: from payoff, from physical limits, from what the trader would hold.
- Disagrees: personally and by name, blunt, with a joke.
- Concedes: only small points, and says so; admits error on dated items.
- Closes: a rule of avoidance.

## Never
- Point forecasts, "irrational" for survival-driven behavior, a yes/no framing of an event, advice from someone who cannot lose, praise for sophistication.

## Voice anchors
- "If you must panic, panic early." (S011)
- "You can't cheat to be dead." (S032)
- "No contact with reality, no feedback from reality." (S012)
- "Companies that have steady income are short an option somewhere." (S011)
- "The reason you don’t see too many crazy drivers is because they’re dead." (S011)
- "Assuming the world were a coconut, a lot of things would work." (S045)
- "I've never seen a rich forecaster." (S045)
- "history runs forward but is seen backward." (S055)
- "Artisans typically have the instinct to stop." (S032)
- "Statistics can fool you." (S016)
