# Frameworks: Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Built from 14 sources (S010 to S055, 2004 to 2024): talks, interviews, essays and papers. Quick depth, so about half the usual entries. S034, S035 and S048 are co-authored, so their lines are credited to "the paper". Scoring guidance is labeled as guidance, not his words.

## 1. Fat tails versus thin tails (Mediocristan and Extremistan)
- What: in thin-tailed things (weight, height) no single case moves the total. In fat-tailed things (wealth, prices, pandemics, company outcomes) one case dominates.
- Test: can you diversify or insure it? If not, treat it as fat-tailed. Assume fat tails unless a physical limit rules the extreme out.
- Score (guidance): if the worst case is unbounded, drop every average-based argument and ask what the worst case does to you.
- Quote: "Fat tails means that you have the greatest contribution comes from smallest number of events." (S011)
- Quote: "Add the heaviest person on the planet to a sample of 1000. The total weight would barely change." (S016)
- Used in: S011, S016, S017, S034, S045, S055 (6 sources).

## 2. Ruin and survival first
- What: a risk that can end the game is not priced like other risks. Repeat it enough times and it happens. Rationality is staying alive, not winning one bet.
- Steps: ask if the loss is recoverable; if not, remove it, however small the odds.
- Score (guidance): any real path to total loss is a no, whatever the expected return.
- Quote: "Take all the risks you want, but make sure you're there tomorrow." (S012)
- Quote: "No matter how small the probability, in time, something bound to hit the ruin barrier is about guaranteed to hit it." (S034, the paper)
- Quote: "You've got to look at the activity, not an episode." (S045)
- Used in: S012, S032, S034, S045, S046 (5 sources).

## 3. Fragile, robust, antifragile; convexity
- What: fragile means harm grows faster than the dose (concave); antifragile means you gain more from variation than you lose (convex). Fragile things hate disorder.
- Steps: list what a shock does to you. If a big shock hurts more than many small ones, you are fragile.
- Score (guidance): count the exposures that are concave. Fix those before looking for upside.
- Quote: "convexity means you like volatility. Concavity, you don’t." (S011)
- Quote: "A coffee cup is fragile because it wants tranquility and a low volatility environment" (S041)
- Used in: S011, S041, S034, S052 (4 sources).

## 4. Skin in the game
- What: three jobs. An incentive, a disincentive (you pay when wrong), and a filter that removes bad risk takers. Also a test of honesty: does the adviser bear the cost of the advice?
- Steps: ask what the adviser holds, who takes the upside, who takes the downside.
- Score (guidance): advice from someone who cannot lose counts for little.
- Quote: "you cannot walk away from risks you've created for others. You should own your risks." (S012)
- Quote: "do not tell people what you think, tell them what you have in your portfolio" (S035, the paper)
- Used in: S010, S011, S012, S032, S035, S046, S052 (7 sources).

## 5. The Fourth Quadrant map
- What: cross two things. Is the payoff simple (yes/no) or complex (size matters)? Is the randomness mild or wild? The dangerous cell is complex payoffs under wild randomness.
- Fix: you cannot change the world, so change the exposure. Cap the loss and move to a safer cell.
- Quote: "So the idea is to exit the fourth quadrant." (S017)
- Quote: "my advice is what to avoid, no more." (S016)
- Used in: S016, S017, S034 (3 sources).

## 6. Barbell and many small bets (1/n)
- What: most money safe, a small part in high-variance bets, nothing in the middle. In research or ventures, many cheap trials with capped cost.
- Quote: "Or to do what I call the barbell: to have a smaller amount allocated to the most volatile things, rather than a larger amount allocated to medium-volatile things." (S032)
- Quote: "A large exposure to a single trial has lower expected return than a portfolio of small trials." (S041)
- Used in: S017, S032, S034, S041 (4 sources).

## 7. Redundancy against optimization
- What: spare capacity is a paid option. Cutting slack improves the numbers and sells an unseen option.
- Quote: "redundancy is like an option. One certainly pay for it, but it may be necessary for survival." (S017)
- Quote: "Avoid Optimization, Learn to Love Redundancy." (S016)
- Used in: S011, S016, S017 (3 sources).

## 8. Payoff space over frequency space
- What: you are paid in money, not in how often you are right. Someone right 98 percent of the time can still be ruined.
- Quote: "in the real world you're not paid in frequency, you're paid in dollars and cents." (S045)
- Quote: "In frequency space, I'm bullish, and in pay-off space I'm bearish." (S045)
- Used in: S017, S045 (2 sources).

## 9. Lindy effect
- What: for non-perishable things (ideas, books, technologies, firms), the longer they have lasted, the longer they should last. Keep what has survived, doubt what is new.
- Limit: statistical, not certain, and he says it does not work for every domain.
- Quote: "what is recent will be replaced by something more recent." (S012)
- Quote: "the only expert around is time." (S046)
- Used in: S010, S012, S046 (3 sources).

## 10. Turkey problem and the silent past
- What: a long calm record says little about the tail. The turkey is fed for 1,000 days.
- Quote: "On the 1001st day, the turkey has a surprise." (S016)
- Quote: "Do not confuse absence of volatility with absence of risks." (S016)
- Used in: S011, S016, S034 (the paper), S035 (the paper) (4 sources).

## 11. Non-naive precautionary principle
- What: precaution is for harm that is irreversible, can spread, and has large error. Local, bounded harm gets ordinary risk management. Burden of proof falls on whoever adds the systemic risk.
- Quote: "a non-naive view of the precautionary principle is one in which it is only invoked when necessary" (S034, the paper)
- Quote: "We're against some classes of engineering that have irreversible effect and with a huge standard error." (S045)
- Used in: S011, S034, S045 (3 sources).

## 12. Errors on errors
- What: every estimate has an error, which has its own error. Layering these fattens the tails. The future is fatter-tailed than the past.
- Quote: "The future is fatter tailed than the past." (S048, the paper)
- Used in: S048, S034 (the paper) (2 sources).

## 13. Artisan versus businessman
- What: the artisan knows where to stop. Expansion for its own sake, pushed by consultants, ends in bankruptcy.
- Quote: "The typical artisan knows where to stop." (S032)
- Used in: S032 (1 source).

## 14. Cemetery of failures
- What: before crediting a winner's traits, look at the losers who share them.
- Quote: "go take a look at the less visible cemetery" (S055)
- Used in: S055, S035 (the paper) (2 sources).

## Books

### Books: Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Built from 14 sources. The books form one set, the Incerto. Summaries are in my own words, never book text. Years from the sources where given.

- **Dynamic Hedging**: his first book, on options trading. He says it shaped how he thinks of life as a series of steps. Taught in S032.
- **Fooled by Randomness**: luck mistaken for skill, survivorship, the cemetery of failures. He says it is the one people like most (S011), and that he now thinks he overstated mass hysteria in it (S032). The first edition had no references; he added them later (S045). Taught in S011, S032, S045, S055.
- **The Black Swan (2007)**: large rare events, hindsight, the turkey, Extremistan. He says it is the book people slight most (S011). Taught in S011, S016, S017, S032, S045, S055.
- **Antifragile (2012)**: gaining from disorder, convexity, optionality, the barbell, via negativa. He says it is the most misused: people read it as "what doesn't kill you" (S011). Taught in S011, S032, S035, S041, S046, S052.
- **Skin in the Game (2018)**: symmetry of risk, scale, the filter of selection, minority rule, rationality as survival. He says it cannot be summarized (S012, S046). Taught in S010, S011, S012, S032, S046.
- **The Technical Incerto**: the 70 to 80 papers behind the books; Silent Risk, Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails. Math is not used in this brain. Mentioned in S011, S034, S045, S048.
- **In progress**: a Latin-style book of questions on convexity, scalability and time (S011); a next book on time scale and probability (S045).

Essays and papers in the corpus: The Fourth Quadrant (S016, S017), the precautionary principle paper (S034, co-authored), the skin in the game heuristic paper (S035, with Sandis), errors on errors (S048, with Cirillo), Understanding Is a Poor Substitute for Convexity (S041), Beware the Big Errors of Big Data (S052), Learning to Expect the Unexpected (S055).
