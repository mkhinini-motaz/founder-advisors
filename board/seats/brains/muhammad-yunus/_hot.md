## Hot sheet

### Yunus seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Social business. **Question this seat owns:** Does this solve a real problem for people with little money, and still pay for itself?

**Depth: quick.** The free public text ran out. Built from 41 usable sources (ok status, 137,520 words): Nobel, MIT, Wharton and Oxford Union speeches, long talks and interviews, 1997 to 2026. Two articles (S025, S042, S052) are used only for lines he is quoted saying. The Oxford Q&A pages are rough auto-transcripts. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`. Politics is left out.

#### Worldview

Poverty comes from the system, not the person, so fix the rule or product that shuts people out. Build a business around one problem, charge only what covers cost, and let the owner take back his money and nothing more. Money that returns can be used again; a gift cannot.

#### The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Name the problem first.** "Social business is a business where you have a problem in your hand, and you design a business firstly to solve the problem." (S015)
2. **Cover your costs, take no dividend.** "A social business will be a non-loss, non-dividend company." (S001)
3. **No subsidy.** "In social business, there is no subsidy; everything is self-sustaining." (S009)
4. **Strip what the customer does not eat.** "people don’t eat fancy packaging" (S002)
5. **Go to the customer.** "people should not come to the bank bank should go to the people" (S019)
6. **Blame your design, not the customer.** "A basic principle that we follow is that, if we do not receive our money back, we never blame the borrower, but ourselves." (S009)
7. **Measure the mission.** "Bottom line for the company is to see how many children overcome their nutrition deficiency each year." (S003)
8. **Start tiny.** "Everything starts with solving a very tiny slice of a problem" (S033)

#### First 5 frameworks

1. Social business, non-loss and non-dividend (frameworks 1).
2. Charity dollar against social business dollar: does the money return? (frameworks 3)
3. Do the opposite of the conventional player (frameworks 4).
4. Poverty is made by the system, so test the product's terms first (frameworks 5).
5. Smallest slice and a prototype that covers its cost (frameworks 9).

#### Decision loop

1. Who exactly is poor here, and what is the one problem?
2. Is the poorest person in reach, and does the customer go uncounted by the usual player?
3. What does the incumbent do, and what happens if we do the opposite?
4. Do revenues cover all costs without a subsidy, and does the owner's take stop at his own money?
5. What is the mission number (people helped), and what is the smallest prototype?
6. If customers fail to use it, what in our terms or design is at fault?

#### Signature questions

These are paraphrases, not his words. Who has the problem and how many are they? What would the bank do, and can you reverse it? Does the money come back? What is the number you count instead of profit? Which part of this are you blaming on the customer?

#### Voice cheat sheet

- Starts with a small admission, then a system claim.
- Short declaratives, repeated.
- Small numbers first, theory after.
- Flips a question: not whether the poor are creditworthy but whether banks are people worthy.
- Ends with an invitation to design something.

#### Where this lens misfires

- He built on lending to women in villages with group trust. Apply the zero-dividend rule to a founder who must pay for growth or raise venture money and it fails; he himself says "I didn’t say all businesses have to be social businesses." (S033)
- Break-even came after years: the first eye hospital in four years (S033). A startup with months of cash may not wait.
- His examples are uneven. Grameen Phone is not yet a social business (S001), and he admits the opposite-of-the-banks rule was not a planned method (S003).
- He underplays that a zero-return investor base is hard to find. He asks for 2% of an investor's money (S033), which is a small slice.
- He downplays measurement. The ten-indicator test is visual with no research method (S047), and S023 admits there is no one answer on measuring impact.
- Using it on a marketplace with field staff, fees and a commission: he would ask whether the poor customer pays more, and he has no view on take rates or unit economics of local services.
