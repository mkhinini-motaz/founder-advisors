# Frameworks: Andrew Chen

Built from the notes on 120 sources (andrewchen.com 2007 to 2018, Substack 2024 to 2026, a16z and Reforge talks, podcasts). Scoring guidance is labeled as guidance. Book frameworks come only from what Chen says about the book in his own essays and interviews (S012, S058, S060, S061, S110); the book text is not in the corpus.

## 1. Atomic network and the five stages of cold start
- What: the smallest stable network that can retain and engage users on its own and grow. Every product has its own size: Zoom two or three people, Slack five or ten, Tinder, Airbnb or Uber hundreds in one place. Build one, then a second and a third. Five stages: Cold Start Problem, Tipping Point, Escape Velocity, Hitting the Ceiling, The Moat (his book's frame, as he describes it in S058, S110).
- Parts: pick the network structure (global peer, cross-side marketplace, team, ephemeral), find the size, launch one network by hand, then launch adjacent ones like dominoes.
- Use when: any product that is worthless with few users.
- Score (guidance): can you name the number of people in one place that makes it work? Does that network show a retention curve of its own? Tipping Point readiness in his words: a few hundred or thousand users returning, D30 above 20% or projected M12 above 30% (S110).
- Quote: "the smallest possible stable network that is stable and can grow on its own" (S110)
- Sources: S058, S060, S061, S110, S115 (5).

## 2. Minimum social group, critical mass and density
- What: the smallest group of friends in the right network before a tool is useful (Skype 2, mailing list 4-5, forum 10, social network 10-20). Density beats total users. Fragmentation is a fail state.
- Use when: choosing the first market, the first campus, the first city, the first team.
- Score (guidance): count connections per active user, not users. Under about 10 for a social product is empty.
- Quote: "you’d much rather have 100,000 users with a density of 30 connections/person, than 1,000,000 who have a density of 2 connections." (S115)
- Sources: S031, S041, S058, S115, S127 (5).

## 3. Three network effects: acquisition, engagement, economic
- What: a network effect is not one thing. Acquisition effect (users bring users, lower CAC), engagement effect (more users, higher retention and frequency, dormant users reactivated), economic effect (more users, higher conversion and ARPU). More users must mean network density, not scale.
- Use when: someone claims network effects. Ask which of the three, and show the curve.
- Score (guidance): can you point to a KPI for each pillar that rises with density? A claim with none is a scale effect.
- Quote: "not one singular effect, but rather, 3 distinct, underlying forces" (S110)
- Sources: S012, S064, S079, S110 (4).

## 4. Cohort retention, the leaky bucket and the smile curve
- What: group users by the week they joined and read each cohort's curve. Good curves fall then flatten; great ones swing back up (the smile) when the network fills in. A curve that goes to zero is a leaky bucket. Aggregates hide the problem.
- Use when: asking whether anyone loves the product. He asks for the curve first when meeting a startup (S068).
- Score (guidance): the number the curve converges to is the key metric (S047). Split by market density or team size to see the network effect as a natural A/B test (S054).
- Quote: "Whatever the D1, it drops by 50% on D7. Whatever the D7, it drops by another 50% at D30." (S068)
- Sources: S009, S010, S044, S047, S054, S068, S109 (7).

## 5. Retention benchmarks and the ten magic metrics
- What: for a scaled consumer product, signals of fit: cohort curves that flatten, actives/registered above 25%, a power user smile, viral factor above 0.5, DAU/MAU above 50%, D1/D7/D30 above 60/30/15, per-user expansion, over 60% organic, over 65% annual subscription retention, over 4x annual growth.
- Use when: judging fit after a launch. Early fit (his older bar): DAU/MAU above 25%, D1 of at least 30%, hundreds to thousands of organic signups a day (S053).
- Score (guidance): count how many of the ten hold. Benchmarks are rough and category-bound (S109, S132).
- Quote: "D1/D7/D30 that exceeds 60/30/15 (daily frequency)" (S080)
- Sources: S053, S066, S080, S109, S132, S137 (6).

## 6. Viral factor, multiplier and retention-weighted virality
- What: viral factor is a ratio over a time-bound cohort. Multiplier is 1/(1-v): 0.5 gives 2x, 0.75 gives 4x. With retention, factor is the sum over sessions. Virality amplifies other channels; above 1 is rare and short-lived. A viral loop is the steps from entering to inviting the next set of users.
- Use when: a founder talks about going viral. Ask the ratio, the cohort and the retention.
- Score (guidance): below 0.1 you cannot see the effect; 0.2 still lowers CAC; 0.5 or more is felt (S066).
- Quote: "Low retention apps need to be spammy. Sticky apps do not." (S066)
- Sources: S012, S037, S038, S039, S040, S042, S066, S102, S119 (9).

## 7. Law of Shitty Clickthroughs and fresh powder
- What: every successful channel decays through competition, platform clampdowns and user habituation. First banner above 70% clickthrough, about 0.05% now. The answer is to keep hunting new, frontier channels. By the time a case study is on Medium, it is probably done.
- Use when: someone proposes to scale one channel.
- Score (guidance): model CAC rising and LTV falling together (a 30% move each way can double payback, S033).
- Quote: "inevitably whatever worked in the past will no longer work" (S059)
- Sources: S018, S033, S035, S056, S057, S059, S120 (as a summary), S135 (7).

## 8. Big channels versus little channels
- What: big channels (SEO, influencers, PR, email, viral, ads, referral) scale with money but decay. Little channels (a mini event, a Facebook group, one college or town, emailing friends) do not scale and are early. Early on, trade off scalability.
- Quote: "Don’t focus on Big Channels, focus on Little Channels." (S057)
- Sources: S057, S061, S104 (3).

## 9. Hyperlocal marketplace: density, supply side first
- What: a ride business is hundreds of hyperlocal marketplaces. Virtuous cycle: more drivers, shorter pickups, more riders, higher earnings. Density has three drivers (pickup time, coverage, utilization). Network effect is a curve with diminishing returns, not a yes/no. Supply is the hard side because of professionalization.
- Use when: any two-sided local market. This is the closest match to a local, physical service.
- Score (guidance): can supply be kept busy 40 hours a week, and when can subsidies stop? (S006)
- Quote: "Because all these marketplaces tend towards supply constrained, you should evaluate each opportunity/company from the POV of the supply side." (S006)
- Sources: S004, S006, S007, S015, S054, S056, S059, S106 (8).

## 10. Nature versus nurture of frequency
- What: the category sets a natural DAU/MAU and retention range; execution moves you only within it. Compare only inside your category. Low-cadence products pick another metric.
- Quote: "It's just hard to fight nature. You can only do so much." (S068)
- Sources: S014, S026, S054, S068, S135 (5).

## 11. Golden Cohort, Looky-Loos and Durable-Scalable-Valuable
- What: the earliest, organic, highest-intent users are the best you will get; every later channel dilutes. Spikes bring low-intent Looky-Loos. Judge traction on three lenses: durable (D1/7/30, DAU/MAU), scalable (% organic, paid payback), valuable (conversion, ARPU).
- Quote: "Is it durable? Is it scalable? Is it valuable?" (S080)
- Sources: S068, S080, S127, S119 (4).

## 12. Meerkat curve, Metcalfe's Law and the death spiral
- What: network value is an S curve, not Metcalfe's square, because of critical mass and overcrowding. Reverse Metcalfe (his "Eflactem's Law"): as users leave, value falls and more leave. If one of three feedback loops fails (post, consume, connect), collapse follows.
- Quote: "As you lose users, the value of your network is decreases exponentially (doh!)" (S045)
- Sources: S045, S047, S061, S113 (4).

## 13. Growth stall diagnosis
- What: a stall is churn catching up with acquisition. Order: retention baseline, admit if people do not want it, activation and density, top of funnel and loops, then polish signup, invite and payment. Make acquisition a function of user base size (a loop).
- Quote: "At its core, a product stalls when its churn catches up with its customer acquisition." (S109)
- Sources: S109, S119, S127 (3).

## 14. Paid Marketing Local Max and per-channel CAC
- What: launch spike fades, spend rises, payback stretches from 9 to 12 months, layoffs, pivot. Blended CAC flatters by 2-5X. Cap paid at 30-40% of top of funnel; use paid to bootstrap a network after an activation point.
- Quote: "The first mistake is to start by thinking of everything as Blended CAC" (S011)
- Sources: S011, S054, S061, S119, S122 (5).

## 15. Cold start moves for marketplaces
- What: pay the supply side first (Uber guaranteed drivers $30 an hour recruited on Craigslist); bundle (AppSumo conference bundles sharing an email list); build tools for supply (Convoy); give single-user value that also connects (Dropbox); the coupon, which stimulates supply and demand at once (S133). Instacart built shoppers first, then approached grocers.
- Score (guidance): pick the playbook your resources allow, money or hustle (S060).
- Quote: "this was a way to stimulate both supply and demand simultaneously" (S133)
- Sources: S060, S104, S115, S133 (4).

## 16. Four cold-start approaches for social products
- What: single-user utility; publish into a pre-existing network; small network requirements; local network saturation. Baseline before growth work: 10,000+ active users organically gaining hundreds per day at 20%+ DAU/MAU.
- Quote: "you focus on getting 1,000 users who are densely connected already." (S115)
- Sources: S115, S041, S058 (3).

## 17. Fit before growth, and the 20% twist
- What: product/market fit first, then a growth team. Take a known category and change about 20% of the core, visible in the first 30 to 60 seconds. Time to fit must fit inside the runway (TTPMF). Minimum Desirable Product tests user value, not viability.
- Quote: "startups need product/market fit, not growth." (S019)
- Sources: S019, S020, S022, S050, S053, S068, S074, S085 (8).

## 18. Reach: concentric circles of users
- What: Upside is Reach times Impact. Core users are 5-25% of actives; projects touching non-core users have 4-20x more reach. Circle every roadmap item that touches non-users.
- Quote: "you might have 4-20X more reach! Wow." (S112)
- Sources: S008, S112 (2).

## 19. Strategy first, then metrics; time sinks and money sinks
- What: pick the loop or strategy, then the metrics that validate it. Time sinks live on engagement; money sinks (episodic, job-to-be-done) live on CAC/LTV and % organic. Business model shapes design.
- Quote: "You should pick your product strategy, then pick the metrics that validate that your strategy is working." (S132)
- Sources: S059, S061, S107, S132 (4).

## 20. Ladder of engagement and growth accounting
- What: users arrive for something valuable but infrequent and are moved up (Uber riders from airport trips to weekend dinners to commuting). Growth accounting: MAU change is New plus Reactivated minus Inactive; it is lagging, like a P&L.
- Quote: "Almost every product I’ve seen has a “ladder of engagement”" (S119)
- Sources: S054, S110, S119 (3).

## 21. Early and late S-curve, novelty effect
- What: early in a platform wave, building is hard and growth is easy, fast-following works, and novelty brings high growth with high churn. Late, building is easy and growth is hard; create new categories.
- Quote: "Late S-curve startups are better off trying to create new categories, rather than fast-following" (S138)
- Sources: S127, S135, S137, S138 (4).

## 22. Idea Maze and Growth Maze
- What: the founder's mastery of past paths and dead ends; go-to-market is also path dependent. Early in a technology the Idea Maze dominates; later distribution.
- Quote: "If you can’t see the maze, how can you possibly solve it?" (S078)
- Sources: S078, S074 (2).

## 23. Managed marketplace and market network
- What: the platform vets supply and creates trust, setting price at a higher take rate; a market network adds SaaS tools around a transaction for stickiness by utility. Marketplace eras: listings, unbundled Craigslist, Uber for X, managed. The essay S111 is co-written with Li Jin.
- Quote: "these marketplaces take on the work of actually creating trust." (S111)
- Sources: S004, S015, S111 (3).

## 24. Anti-Pitch and Simple-to-WTF
- What: the skeptic's one-liner; respond by ignoring, accepting then addressing, or denying, and prefer judo over denial. Describe the product against something familiar.
- Quote: "The optimist has the Pitch, and the skeptic has the Anti-Pitch." (S067)
- Sources: S067, S082 (2).

## Books

### Books: Andrew Chen

Built from the notes on 120 sources. The corpus has no text of The Cold Start Problem beyond what Chen quotes or describes himself (S012, S058, S060, S061, S110). Nothing below reconstructs the book.

#### The Cold Start Problem (his own)
- Thesis, as he describes it in S058, S060, S110: network effects are engineered in stages. The stages are the cold start problem, the tipping point, escape velocity, hitting the ceiling and the moat. The core move is building atomic networks, one at a time. He wrote it partly to hand to founders at the tipping point stage (S060). It took three years (S061) and he recorded the audiobook himself (S121, a short post with no content summary).
- Frameworks he names in the essays (his own words, see frameworks.md): atomic network, three network effects (acquisition, engagement, economic), tipping point as dominoes, hitting the ceiling as problems to manage, David versus Goliath network competition.
- Taught by: S012 (excerpt from the Acquisition Effect chapter, "PayPal"), S058, S060, S061, S110 (an excerpt of the five-stage frame), S079 and S133 and S127 (passing references only).
- Left out: web3, because it changes too fast (S058).

#### Other books he points to (not his)
- The Innovator's Dilemma (Christensen): why copying an incumbent plus features loses (S028), and why big companies forget how to solve cold start (S060).
- My Life in Advertising and Scientific Advertising (Claude Hopkins): inventor of coupons and stunt marketing; named as a favourite (S056, S059, S061).
- Different (Youngme Moon): finding a wedge in an existing market, antagonistic positioning (S053, S082).
- Crossing the Chasm (Geoffrey Moore): early adopters versus the early majority (S061).
- The Signal and the Noise (Nate Silver): foxes and hedgehogs, calibration (S030).
- The Lean Startup (Eric Ries): he lists lessons from a decade of MVPs (S074).
- Blue Ocean Strategy: ideas in his fit deck (S053).
- Others read, no argument: Startup Nation, Chip War, The Managerial Revolution (S069), To Infinity and Beyond (Pixar, S027).

#### Major essays and series (he has no other book)
- "Growth Hacker is the new VP Marketing" (April 2012, S018) and the reversal "You don't need a growth hacker" (September 2012, S019).
- "The Law of Shitty Clickthroughs" (2012, S033).
- Viral loop series (2007-2008, S037, S038, S040, S042).
- "Why retention is so hard for new tech products" (2025-09-08, S068) and "Braindump on viral loops" (2025-11-05, S066).
- Decks: "Zero to Product/Market Fit" (S053), "How to build a growth team" (c. 2018, S112), "The red flags and magic numbers that investors look for" (2018, S119).
