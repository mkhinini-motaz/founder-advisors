# Positions: Andrew Chen

Built from the notes on 120 sources. Dates come from URLs or the source (blank in the corpus for many old posts, so "undated" is stated). Where he changed his mind, both views are shown and the newer is marked. No recorded position means the corpus has none; nothing is guessed.

## Network effects and cold start
- Cold start: "if you build a product and none of your users are using it, then the product is not valuable at all, even if you have the right product features" (S060, undated).
- Launch style: no big bang. "Big bangs are hard to repeat." (S127, 2024-07-22). Older echo: big unfocused launches fail because they raise user count without density (S115, c. 2014, paraphrase).
- Network effects as a moat: "Well, it turns out they have network effects too" (S058, 2021-12-16). Newer (2026-10-06): "Agents don’t inherently have network effects." (S064).
- Critical mass: "The value of the network rises exponentially, which is hard to comprehend." (S031, 2013). Newer (S061, 2021-12-02): value is an S curve with a meerkat model, Metcalfe's Law ignores overcrowding.
- Evaluating new networks: favour products that already have an engaged network, even small (S031, 2013, paraphrase).

## Marketplaces and delivery
- Food delivery: "This is one of the reasons why I’m bearish on food delivery as a stand-alone business in the long run." (S006, c. 2016). Reason: Uber can top up drivers with food earnings.
- Unit economics: "Rideshare networks are fickle and require a long period of being unit economic negative before they can break even, with enough scale/density." (S006).
- Supply side: "the supply side is often the trickiest, hardest side" (S059, 2018-05-10).
- Supply cost: "Acquiring a broad base of labor for driving is expensive, often $300+." (S006).
- Subsidy: marketplaces subsidize for years to get the market to choose them, then price (S058, paraphrase).
- Managed marketplaces for services: the essay (co-written with Li Jin) says "We fully expect more Airbnb- and rideshare-sized outcomes in the service economy." (S111, 2018).
- Local start: DoorDash v1 was a static page with 8 PDF menus and a phone number, and he prefers existing markets (S074, 2025-03-24).

## Pricing and discounting
- Coupons: "Do promos actually help you attract new customers?" (S133, 2024-05-20); measure cost per incremental trip, target them, watch stacking.
- Go free to fix indifference: "Funny enough, I've never seen this work." (S085, 2024-09-18). Also: "more products should probably be paid/premium products, and should charge more for what they do" (S085).
- Subscription for LLMs: "People want free." (S133).
- Referral: suits the supply side; demand-side referrals bring slightly lower-quality customers (S059, paraphrase). Newer (S057, 2025-04-09): referral is as expensive as paid marketing, with fraud.

## Raising money and funding
- Money buys time: "Money buys time, and time buys product iterations." (S020, 2012).
- Runway: "If your plan for TTPMF exceeds your funding runway, you’re already dead." (S022, 2013).
- Business models: 2012: "Business models are a commodity now" (S032). Newer (S125, 2024-09-09): "it's the quality of users (and their ARPPU) that matters, not just the scale of users." He moved from audience-first to monetization quality.
- Venture as growth capital, not risk capital, if tiny teams are profitable from day one (S069, 2025-09-03, paraphrase).
- Portfolio: 20 or more companies, a top fund loses on about half (S061, paraphrase).

## Debt, partners, equity, first hire, firing
- Cofounders: "Expect to fight with your cofounders." (S020, 2012).
- First hire / Head of Growth: hands-on until 100 employees; "A hard requirement is that they’ve stood up a new growth channel from scratch before." (S110, c. 2022).
- Team that makes excuses: "As the TFE approaches zero, this is when you know the team probably needs to get replaced/rebooted." (S070, 2025-07-03, half joke).
- Debt, equity split, firing: no recorded position.

## Focus, niche, and diversification
- Zoom in: "Zooming in generally beats zooming out, because your value prop is that much more specific" (S085).
- Focus: "A lack of focus means that the startup is perpetually finding the next quick growth hack" (S110).
- Niche starts: Yammer inside a company, Yelp in San Francisco, Facebook at Harvard, Snapchat in SoCal high schools (S115, paraphrase).
- Side projects: no recorded position.

## Content, brand and marketing
- Brand: "Brand marketing is mostly useless for consumer startups." (S103, undated). Newer view (2024-25): "Quantity beats quality. Brand ubiquity beats brand dilution" (S071); "Drum beat not big bang." (S083). The change is voice and cadence, not paid brand.
- Press: "Journalists want to critique tech, not advocate for it." (S083, 2024-11-05). Earlier (2008): press is "fool’s gold" (S052).
- Content: "Write evergreen content that people want to read now, but possibly a year from now" (S029, 2013).
- Social media hires: "That’s an auto-reject for me." for people with no personal following (S071, 2025-06-30).
- Writing: "Writing is so valuable" (S061, 2021-12-02, short quote only).

## Paid ads
- "Scale effects mostly work against you in paid marketing." (S011, undated).
- "The largest sites ultimately cannot be bought through online ads." (S038, 2007).
- Fine as a tactic to bootstrap a network, dangerous as the main engine (S054, paraphrase). Paying for users: "and I hate it." (S055).
- Over 50% of acquisition from paid is a warning (S054, paraphrase).

## Viral growth
- 2007-2012 view: viral is engineerable. "There was a period of time that I was convinced that you could engineer a viral product into existence." (S012). Newer (2020-21): "the most powerful force in the tech industry isn’t viral growth, as I once believed. It’s actually retention, and strong product/market fit." (S012).
- 2025 view: "it’s basically impossible to create loops where the viral factor is >1 in the first session" (S066, 2025-11-05).

## Growth hacking and A/B testing
- 2012-04: "growth hackers are engineers leading teams of engineers." (S018). 2012-09 reversal: "startups need product/market fit, not growth." (S019). Newer (2024-05-13): "you can’t A/B test your way to product/market fit." (S135) and "Rather than get disrupted by the growth discipline, as I had predicted, instead we saw the other functional disciplines absorb it." (S135).
- Small tests: "Make big, bold moves, not little landing page changes" (S135). Yet in 2018: bias to low-effort projects early (S112).

## Metrics
- "I’ve never met an entrepreneur who’s happy with their metrics." (S009).
- Vanity: be careful with registered users and monthly actives (S061, paraphrase); "Be careful about focusing on vanity metrics like daily installs" (S107, a third-party note, paraphrase only).
- Benchmarks: DAU/MAU of 20%+ good, 50%+ world class (S014); but "Not everything has to be daily use to be valuable." (S014).
- Data: "Later on, I learned to be data-informed, still having intuition but overlaying data on top." (S131, 2024-05-28).
- Startups: "Startups aren’t spreadsheets." (S119, 2018).

## Retention
- Newest: "average product retention will collapse" (S065, 2026-09-08).
- "Great retention is magic." (S068, 2025-09-08). Earlier note (S109): retention is hardest to move, with two exceptions, activation and network-effect products.
- Churn: "Churn is asymmetric. It’s far easier to lose a user forever than to re-win them back" (S068).

## Competition
- 2013: do not clone fully. "Thus, I don’t think you ever want to do a full clone." (S022). Newer (S109, undated): "The easiest way to find a repeatable strategy is by simply fast-following other companies in your space."
- 2017: "It used to be that startups could count on their competitors to be big, dumb, and slow. Not anymore." (S108).
- No competition: "If you’re in a market by yourself with no competition, then perhaps it’s not that great of a market after all?" (S138, 2024-04-29).

## Timing
- Platform waves: "Late S-curve startups are better off trying to create new categories, rather than fast-following" (S138). Early: "good artists copy, great artists steal" (S138).
- Trough: 12 to 24 months of struggle is common; sometimes 3 years (S020, paraphrase).
- Moving first: first movers rarely define the outcome; "it took 5+ years for others, like Uber and Doordash, that used the technology in novel ways, to define the deca-billion dollar outcomes" (S079, 2025-02-04).

## AI
- Defensibility: "Any software product that AI can build within a few years will be arbitraged away." (S069, 2025-09-03).
- When building is cheap, distribution and networks decide (S075, 2025-03-10).
- Agents: "those are not network effects" for better models, UX or memory (S064, 2026-10-06).
- Marketing: "First order effects of inventions are easy to predict but secondary, tertiary, and n+1 effects are very hard." (S134, 2024-05-16).

## Remote work
- Remote work weakens city network effects (S061, paraphrase). Otherwise no recorded position.

## Work hours and burnout
- "I don’t advocate working even more hours." (S129, 2024-06-17); "The difference between a grinder and a lazy person is only 3x" (S129).
- Burnout: no recorded position beyond that.

## Selling the company, culture
- Bureaucracy: "If winners hire winners, and losers hire losers, what do bureaucrats hire? More bureaucrats of course." (S084, 2024-09-23).
- Selling the company: no recorded position.

## Pivots and quitting
- "Quitting is your decision." (S020). Restart test: "If you deleted all your code, fired your whole team, and started over with a fresh idea, what would that idea be?" (S085).
- Consumer to B2B pivots go better than the reverse (S085, paraphrase).

## Cheap MVPs
- 2009: Minimum Desirable Product, user value first (S050). Newer (2025-03-24): "I've grown increasingly skeptical of certain product validation approaches I previously championed" (S074) and "MVPs are often okay but not great products, and okay product lose in mature markets." (S074).

## Customers and indifference
- "our target audience who really doesn’t give a shit about how much effort we put in." (S009).
- Fun: "your product has to be fun." (S056).

## Prediction and humility
- "Be the fox, not the hedgehog." (S030, 2013). Miss: "I was off by 200-300x on pageviews/month! Total fail." (S034, 2012).
