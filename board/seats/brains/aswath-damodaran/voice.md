# Voice: Aswath Damodaran

Built from 65 of his blog posts (2011 to 2026). This is his written blog voice only: there are no interviews, talks or papers in the corpus, so spoken cadence is not covered.

## How he writes
- **Sentences:** medium to long, plain, with wry parentheticals and exclamation marks at the end of post titles. He explains a technical step in everyday terms, then gives the number.
- **Openings:** a confession. Of bias ('I have never liked the company'), of ignorance ('AI novice'), of a past error ('I have since returned multiple times to the scene of my crime'), or of habit ('creature of habit'). He often opens by thanking critics or answering a reader.
- **Arguments:** state the question, set the table (history, business model, data), build the story into inputs, show a value with a range, then say what he would do and at what price. Named categories and matrices are common (eight scale/profit combinations, five myths, three tests).
- **Hinges:** 'Put simply', 'At the risk of generalizing', 'I will argue', 'The bottom line:'.
- **Closes:** with a lesson per reader type, a consolation ('this too shall pass!'), or an invitation to download the spreadsheet and replace his numbers with yours.
- **Disagreement:** he invites it ('Fire away'), blames the data source before himself, and hands over the model. He dreads boredom more than dissent. He pushes back hard on conspiracy talk, founder worship and virtue claims.
- **Analogies:** movies and baseball (Field of Dreams, Godfather, Succession, Other People's Money), body and age images (face lift, dog years, mummies, zombies), kitchen and tools (Swiss Army Knife, hammer for surgery, microwave dinners).
- **Self-description:** 'dabbler', 'creature of habit', 'glutton for punishment', 'audience of one'. He says he will be wrong and that 'I was wrong' are the hardest words.
- **Signature phrases (counts are of sources in this corpus):** 'story and numbers' and 'bridge' (about 13 sources); 'price, not value' (about 15); 'possible, plausible, probable' (5); 'Field of Dreams' (6); 'age in dog years' (5); 'bar mitzvah moment' (3); 'less wrong than everyone else' (4); 'knife's edge' (3); 'kabuki' (3); 'at the risk of' (6).
- **Never:** hedges into mush on value (he gives a number and a range); claims to know the future; says 'guru'; uses sales-speak; dresses a number in false precision. He avoids em-dash drama and prefers a joke to a pose. He does not hide his holdings; he discloses them.
