# Frameworks: Aswath Damodaran

Built from 65 of his "Musings on Markets" blog posts (2011 to 2026). No books, interviews or papers were read, so these are the frameworks as he states them on the blog. Most-used first. Scoring guidance is labeled as guidance: it is drawn from his own thresholds, not a formula he published as such.

## 1. Valuation as a bridge between stories and numbers
- **What:** every story becomes a number, and every number has a story behind it. Story alone is a fairy tale; numbers alone are false precision.
- **Parts:** (1) develop a narrative; (2) test it against history, experience and common sense; (3) turn it into drivers (market size, share, revenue slice, margin, reinvestment, risk); (4) run a DCF; (5) keep the feedback loop open by listening to those who disagree most.
- **Use when:** valuing anything young or uncertain; reviewing a pitch.
- **Scoring guidance:** a pitch with no number tied to its story, or numbers with no story, scores low. Big gaps between two valuations usually mean different stories, not different inputs.
- **Quote:** "I think of valuation as a bridge between stories and numbers, where every story becomes a number in the valuation and every number in a valuation has a story behind it." (S039)
- **Sources (13):** S002, S004, S011, S018, S022, S031, S033, S039, S044, S047, S052, S057, S059.

## 2. The 3P test: possible, plausible, probable
- **What:** a story must pass three filters. Possible is the weakest, plausible is stronger, probable is the acid test.
- **Use when:** a founder or analyst gives a best case, a total addressable market or a margin target.
- **Scoring guidance:** best and worst cases are not stories, because choices tie together (a bigger market means lower margins and more reinvestment).
- **Quote:** "you evaluate whether it is possible, plausible and probable" (S018)
- **Sources (5):** S002, S018, S019, S022, S030.

## 3. Value versus price (the pricing game and the value game)
- **What:** value rests on cash flows, growth and risk. Price rests on what others pay for similar assets, mood and momentum. VCs, bankers and traders price; investors value. They are different processes and can give different numbers.
- **Parts:** pricing has three steps: standardize the price, find a peer group, control for differences in growth, risk and return. The choice of peers sets the answer.
- **Use when:** an IPO, a funding round, a "comparable" multiple, or a headline private valuation.
- **Scoring guidance:** say which game you are in before you decide. If you are an investor, decide on value; if you trade, value is a minor factor.
- **Quote:** "You can value an asset, based upon its fundamentals (cash flows, growth and risk) or price it, based upon what others are paying for similar assets, and the two can yield different numbers." (S042)
- **Sources (15):** S003, S011, S014, S019, S023, S024, S030, S031, S032, S033, S034, S038, S042, S051, S060.

## 4. The corporate life cycle
- **What:** companies age like people: idea business, young growth, high growth, mature growth, mature stable, decline. The task, the right CEO, the financing mix, the valuation method and the investing tool all change by stage. Tech companies run the cycle in compressed time.
- **Parts:** place a firm by operating metrics first (growth, margins, cash flow), then age or sector. Young: story drives value. Mature: numbers drive value. Decline: cash return, divestiture, liquidation.
- **Use when:** judging management fit, capital structure, buy-and-hold claims, or which valuation tool applies.
- **Scoring guidance:** if revenue growth is single digits, margins are stable and free cash flow is positive, treat it as mature however it describes itself.
- **Quote:** "tech companies age in dog years, with a 20-year tech company often resembling a hundred-year old manufacturing company" (S020)
- **Sources (11):** S003, S011, S013, S020, S029, S034, S040, S048, S049, S050, S054.

## 5. Cost of capital as an opportunity cost, checked against the market
- **What:** a hurdle rate is the return you can earn elsewhere on an investment of equivalent risk. It is constrained by what the market offers, so it is not a place for hopes and fears.
- **Parts:** three reality checks on a cost of equity: history, the market-implied expected return, and the PE the number implies. Match equity returns to cost of equity and capital returns to cost of capital.
- **Use when:** someone argues a 12 to 20 percent required return, or spends a meeting on the discount rate.
- **Scoring guidance:** about 80 percent of US companies fall between roughly 5.3 and 9.9 percent cost of capital; a US valuation at 14 percent goes on his suspect list. Rank the risk, then move to the inputs that matter.
- **Quote:** "hurdle rates are opportunity costs, reflecting returns that investors (businesses) can earn in the market on investments of equivalent risk." (S008)
- **Sources (5):** S001, S002, S008, S012, S015.

## 6. Failure risk as a separate probability
- **What:** the discount rate measures going-concern operating risk. Survival risk (a young, cash-burning company that may not make it) goes in an explicit probability of failure and a liquidation value, not in a higher discount rate.
- **Use when:** a company keeps losing money and depends on outside capital; extreme leverage.
- **Scoring guidance:** his own choices run 5 percent (Uber, 2017), 10 percent (Lyft, Zomato, Anthropic), 20 percent (WeWork, extreme leverage).
- **Quote:** "the cost of capital, a going concern measure of risk, is incapable of capturing the risk of failure embedded in the business model" (S031)
- **Sources (9):** S001, S002, S031, S033, S038, S043, S047, S051, S060.

## 7. Growth equals reinvestment times return, and only excess returns create value
- **What:** growth is not free. Growth rate = reinvestment rate times return on invested capital. It adds value only when ROIC beats the cost of capital; at equal returns it is neutral; below, it destroys value.
- **Parts:** two sources of growth: efficiency growth (short-lived, always adds) and new-investment growth (lasting, only if returns clear the hurdle). In stable growth, reinvestment must match the growth rate and never be zero.
- **Scoring guidance:** at a 2.5 percent margin, more revenue lowers value; the breakeven margin where growth starts to create value in his Amazon grid was about 4 percent.
- **Quote:** "It is not the growth rate per se, but the excess returns (the difference between return on invested capital and the cost of capital) that drives value." (S041)
- **Sources (5):** S007, S041, S058, S064, S040.

## 8. Country risk and the implied equity risk premium
- **What:** expected return = risk-free rate plus a risk premium. The mature-market premium is the implied premium of the S&P 500 (solve for the discount rate that justifies the index). Add a country premium: sovereign default spread scaled up for equity risk. Weight by where a company operates, not where it is incorporated.
- **Parts:** stay in one currency and convert with the inflation differential; do not double count a default spread already in the bond rate; use ratings and CDS together (ratings are slow, CDS are moody).
- **Quote:** "model agnostic and reflects what investors are demanding in the market, rather than making a judgment on whether the premium is right" (S009)
- **Sources (7):** S006, S009, S012, S015, S016, S017, S063.

## 9. Value as a distribution (Monte Carlo)
- **What:** put distributions, not point estimates, on the two or three inputs you are shakiest about (market size, share, margin), link variables that move together, and read the spread of value. Keep the model parsimonious; more uncertainty means a simpler model.
- **Scoring guidance:** buy and sell bands from deciles. In Airbnb (2020): under 28 billion a bargain, 28 to 33 a solid buy, 33 to 38 fair, 38 to 44 too rich, over 44 overvalued. Set the bands before the offering.
- **Quote:** "my value itself is a distribution, not a number" (S036)
- **Sources (6):** S019, S021, S023, S024, S036, S045.

## 10. Margin of safety as a type 1 versus type 2 trade-off
- **What:** a bigger margin cuts the chance you buy something expensive that looked cheap, and raises the chance you pass on a bargain that looked dear. Size it by valuation uncertainty, portfolio concentration, market liquidity and catalysts.
- **Parts:** five myths: it is costless; it excuses sloppy valuation; one margin fits all; portfolio margin equals single-stock margin; it substitutes for risk measures. Do not stack it on an already conservative value.
- **Quote:** "Increasing your MOS will reduce your type 1 errors but will increase your type 2 errors." (S046)
- **Sources (4):** S001, S021, S045, S046.

## 11. User and subscriber economics
- **What:** value of a user-based company = existing users + new users net of acquisition cost minus corporate drag. Get existing users to buy more before buying new ones.
- **Parts:** stickiness, profit per user, growth in profit per user, lower uncertainty. Subscription beats transaction beats advertising on stickiness. Check whether expense growth tracks customer growth.
- **Quote:** "Value of user-based company = Value of existing users + Value added by new users - Value drag from corporate expenses" (S037)
- **Sources (5):** S032, S035, S036, S037, S038.

## 12. Cash burn: benign or malignant
- **What:** cash burn is negative free cash flow to the firm, not negative earnings. Benign: margins rise and reinvestment falls as the company scales. Malignant: costs spiral, prices fall, no light at the end.
- **Parts:** five-question checklist: why it burns, whether the operating business has pricing power and scale economies, how management sees the burn, the growth versus reinvestment trade-off, and capital market access. Run the bad case.
- **Quote:** "It is cash flow, not earnings, that is at the heart of a cash burn problem." (S043)
- **Sources (3):** S030, S036, S043.

## 13. Reverse-engineering a price and bounding the market
- **What:** take the price, assume margin and cost of capital, solve for the revenue needed in a future year. Bound a total addressable market by the cost line it replaces (all employee compensation, say) and by the revenues of the largest firm in the sector.
- **Scoring guidance:** sum the stories across companies; the implied collective market share must not exceed 100 percent.
- **Quote:** "it does provide a framework for reasonableness and a constraint on story telling." (S005)
- **Sources (6):** S002, S005, S024, S030, S058, S065.

## 14. The IT proposition (intangibles and key people)
- **What:** if it does not affect cash flow or risk, it cannot affect value. Brands, management and connections show up in margins, growth, reinvestment or the discount rate. Toggle the input on and off to value one. A key person's value is value with them minus value without.
- **Scoring guidance:** key people matter more at small, young, micro-driven firms with transitory moats; less at large, mature firms. A brand claim with margins below the industry average is not a brand.
- **Quote:** "for anything, tangible or not, has to show up in either the expected cash flows or in the risk (and the resulting discount rate)" (S014)
- **Sources (2):** S013, S014.

## 15. Scale versus profit: eight combinations and the Field of Dreams company
- **What:** scale depends on market size, growth, industry structure, capital intensity, customer inertia; profit depends on unit economics, economies of scale and edges. The combinations: Lightning in a Bottle, Field of Dreams, Field of Nightmares, Niche Star, Big and Broken, Small winner, Small loser, Cut your losses.
- **Scoring guidance:** scale-first works for those disrupting a huge, atrophied incumbent with real unit economics; it is a cookbook error elsewhere.
- **Quote:** "scaling up is not a mantra that automatically translates in profitability" (S004)
- **Sources (5):** S004, S018, S034, S035, S058.

## 16. Story break, story change, story shift
- **What:** a break kills a key component and makes the valuation moot; a change expands or shrinks the business; a shift keeps the model but moves growth, profit or risk. Test news against the narrative: no change to the story, no change to value.
- **Quote:** "In a story break, you learn something about a company that renders your story moot and makes your valuation irrelevant (perhaps making it zero)." (S038)
- **Sources (4):** S018, S038, S039, S044.

## 17. Sugar daddy syndrome
- **What:** entities with assured funding (corporate venture arms, sovereign funds, green funds) underperform from assured funding, mixed mission, stakeholder distractions and no accountability. Winners have independence, transparency, separation of motives and accountability.
- **Quote:** "being dependent on an entity to meet your financial needs will impede your capacity to be self sufficient and will undercut accountability." (S010)
- **Sources (2):** S010, S007.

## 18. Flexibility and risk capital
- **What:** flexibility is the speed and cost of responding to change, in investment, operating costs, financing and cash return. Firms high on each did better in the 2020 crisis. The equity risk premium reads the state of risk capital.
- **Quote:** "the flexibility of an organization measures the speed and cost with which it responds to changed circumstances" (S028)
- **Sources (3):** S025, S027, S028.

## 19. Pre-money, post-money and protection as a put option
- **What:** post-money = capital divided by ownership share; pre-money = post-money minus the capital. Anti-dilution protection is a put option, so the headline price overstates value (a 1 billion headline was worth 748.84 million in his example).
- **Quote:** "At a fair price, the protection delivers peace of mind but no additional value." (S053)
- **Sources (3):** S042, S053, S055.

## 20. Terminal value with negative growth
- **What:** terminal value = after-tax operating income divided by (cost of capital minus growth). Where new investment earns less than the cost of capital, shrinking and liquidating assets is the value-maximizing choice. Never set reinvestment to zero in stable growth.
- **Quote:** "negative growth is neither uncommon nor unnatural" (S040)
- **Sources (4):** S040, S041, S048, S054.

## Books

### Books: Aswath Damodaran

Built from 65 of his blog posts (2011 to 2026). This corpus contains no book text. Only the books and papers he names on the blog are listed, in my own words.

#### Narrative and Numbers: The Value of Stories in Business (Columbia University Press, 2017)
- **Thesis:** valuation is a bridge between stories and numbers; a number cruncher learns to tell stories and a story teller learns to count. Cases: Uber, Amazon, Alibaba, Ferrari, Vale, Yahoo. He calls it his tenth book (S039).
- **Frameworks:** five steps from story to value (story, reality check, valuation, feedback loop); story change, shift and break (S038, S039).
- **Taught in:** S018, S022, S039, S057, S059.

#### The Corporate Life Cycle (Penguin Random House, 2024)
- **Thesis:** businesses age; stage drives corporate finance, valuation and investing. Tech firms age in dog years; the right CEO and investing tool differ by stage (S011).
- **Frameworks:** six stages, length-height-slope, accept-deny-desperation, CEO fit by stage.
- **Taught in:** S011, S020, S007, S013.

#### His valuation work generally
- He refers to his valuation book and its story-to-numbers process (S022) and to a class on iTunes U (S059), but the posts do not summarize those texts. No other book content is in the corpus.

#### Books he cites by others
- The Intelligent Investor (Graham), Security Analysis (Graham) and Margin of Safety (Klarman): he credits Graham with the margin of safety but says Graham's techniques are "akin to using a hammer to do surgery." for tech (S048). Klarman's book is respected but he thinks the practice has drifted (S046).
- The Founder's Dilemma (Wasserman): to make a business bigger, a founder has to step down or compromise on control (S004, S020).
- Michael Mauboussin's work on moats and excess returns (S007, S011).
