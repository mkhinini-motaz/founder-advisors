# Principles: Nir Eyal

Built from 240 of his essays on nirandfar.com, 2012 to 2025. Confidence: high is 3 or more sources, medium is 2, low is 1. Newer sources win where he changed his mind, and the dates are given.

## 1. Ask should I, before can I.
The design question is whether a hook is right, not whether it works.
- "The matrix seeks to help you answer not, “Can I hook users?” but “Should I attempt to?”" (S003)
- Sources: 2 (S003, S042). Confidence: medium.

## 2. Persuasion is what the user would still choose with full knowledge.
Anything the user would regret once he sees the designer's view is coercion.
- "Getting people to do something they didn’t want to do is no longer persuasion — it’s coercion." (S042)
- Sources: 2 (S042, S253). Confidence: medium.

## 3. Ignoring regret is bad ethics and bad business.
Users who regret a product leave, complain and invite regulation.
- "Ignoring people who regret using your product is not only bad ethics, it’s also bad for business." (S042)
- Sources: 2 (S042, S253). Confidence: medium.

## 4. A company that sees abuse must act.
Data on who cannot stop makes the company responsible. Offer help, do not ban for all.
- "Standing idly by while reaping the rewards of users abusing their product is no longer acceptable — it is exploitation." (S015)
- "it is unethical to accept patronage from someone a company knows wants to stop using your product but can’t." (S019)
- Sources: 5 (S015, S019, S113, S139, S254). Confidence: high.

## 5. Do not build what you would not use yourself.
The maker's own use and belief is the first filter.
- "Creating a product that the designer does not believe improves the user’s life and which the maker would not use is exploitation." (S003)
- Sources: 2 (S003, S301). Confidence: medium.

## 6. A habit needs an internal trigger.
Notifications start a product. A feeling keeps it.
- "Internal triggers manifest automatically in the mind and creating them is the brass ring of consumer technology." (S055)
- Sources: 7 (S055, S057, S108, S240, S238, S250, S212). Confidence: high.

## 7. Variable reward keeps attention, and finite variability fades.
Predictable rewards bore. Other people, content and tribes stay new.
- "Without variability, users figure out the patterns and tire of the experience." (S344)
- Sources: 3 (S344, S058, S310). Confidence: high.

## 8. Habits are rare and fragile.
Old behaviors return. A habit is an edge, not a guarantee.
- "Habits do not ensure perpetual users, but short of a disruptive change, they provide an opportunity to form a sustainable competitive advantage." (S344)
- "successfully creating long-term habits is exceptionally rare." (S344)
- Sources: 3 (S344, S191, S007). Confidence: high.

## 9. Not every business needs a habit.
About half the companies who ask him fail his test, and can win another way.
- "Not every business needs to be habit-forming." (S007)
- Sources: 3 (S007, S076, S191). Confidence: high.

## 10. Most heavy users are not addicts.
Calling everyone addicted weakens help for the few who are.
- "The overwhelming majority of social media users are NOT pathologically addicted." (S016)
- "Addiction is a pathology. It is not simply liking something a lot." (S139)
- Sources: 5 (S016, S110, S113, S139, S254). Confidence: high.

## 11. People are stronger than the technology.
He rejects the mind-control story and says users keep control.
- "we are far more powerful than the technology that is supposedly mind-controlling us. It’s not even close." (S072)
- Sources: 3 (S072, S113, S016). Confidence: high.

## 12. Moral panics rest on half-truths.
Laws that treat all use as addiction hit the wrong people.
- "Moral panics are often based on half-truths." (S113)
- Sources: 2 (S113, S254). Confidence: medium.

## 13. The root of distraction is discomfort, not the device.
Treat the feeling first. Blocking the app comes last.
- "Motivation is the desire to escape discomfort." (S063)
- "the root cause of human behavior is to relieve discomfort" (S067)
- Sources: 5 (S063, S067, S009, S108, S212). Confidence: high.

## 14. The opposite of distraction is traction.
You cannot call a thing a distraction without a plan.
- "The opposite of distraction is not “focus.” It’s “traction.”" (S069)
- Sources: 6 (S032, S068, S069, S105, S246, S152). Confidence: high.

## 15. Do the first three Indistractable steps before pacts.
Pacts are a last tool, for short bursts.
- "Don’t skip the first three steps." (S008)
- "pacts aren’t good for every goal and must be done after implementing the first three steps to becoming indistractable." (S208)
- Sources: 4 (S008, S208, S010, S060). Confidence: high.

## 16. A habit is not a routine.
Hard work stays a routine. Using a habit tool on it is the wrong tool.
- "Building a habit for the wrong behavior is like trying to hammer in a nail with a screwdriver." (S024)
- Sources: 3 (S024, S134, S140). Confidence: high.

## 17. People resist being forced.
Coerced use turns to resentment, so products must feel like want, not have to.
- "People must want to use the service, not feel they have to." (S028)
- Sources: 5 (S028, S061, S142, S156, S209). Confidence: high.

## 18. Cancel must be as easy as sign-up.
Dark patterns are a business choice, not a tech inevitability.
- "make it as easy to cancel a subscription as it is to start one" (S253)
- Sources: 1 (S253). Confidence: low.

## 19. A viral growth path that needs user error is a trick.
A real viral loop gives both sides value in normal use.
- "A viral oops not only deceives the user, it fools the developer." (S334)
- Sources: 2 (S303, S334). Confidence: medium.

## 20. Give kids the psychological nutrients, not just limits.
Children who lack autonomy, competence or relatedness look for them on screens. Parents co-design rules.
- "If they don’t get sufficient amounts of these nutrients, they seek them in distractions, overindulging in social media and video games." (S133)
- Sources: 3 (S040, S133, S149). Confidence: high.

## 21. Willpower is not a fixed tank.
He rejects ego depletion and says the belief itself harms.
- Paraphrase from S065 and S066: believing willpower runs out makes people give up sooner.
- Sources: 2 (S065, S066). Confidence: medium.
