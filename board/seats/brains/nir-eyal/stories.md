# Stories: Nir Eyal

Built from 240 of his essays, 2012 to 2025. Each story is retold in short form from the essay named. Guest stories belong to their authors.

## 1. The baby and the dog (S344)
A baby meets a dog. First fear, then delight, then boredom once the dog is predictable. Attention moves to dump trucks. Use: finite variability fades.

## 2. Zynga's "-Ville" games (S344)
Players left when they saw the same frames again. The point is made by quoting Tadhg Kelly, so the observation is Kelly's; Eyal uses it for the fade of finite rewards.

## 3. Heckling Steve Jobs (S344)
Early iPhone users cursed AT&T and heckled Jobs, yet stayed. Facebook users revolted at interface changes and did not leave. Use: habit buys tolerance.

## 4. The product managers who let players play on (S019)
Game product managers told him they let players keep playing after those players asked to be cut off. A tiny share of players, about 0.15 percent, gave 50 percent of revenue. Use: the Q-tip effect, where a perceived solution becomes the problem.

## 5. Infinite scroll as a slot machine (S058)
He compares infinite scroll to a loose slot machine: it gives users fast access to variable rewards. Use: how a technique can raise use without raising value.

## 6. The roach motel (S253)
People check in but never check out: a subscription that is easy to start and hard to cancel, as with a newspaper. His fix: make cancel as easy as sign-up.

## 7. The Kardashian game and viral oops (S303, S334)
The Kardashian game spreads in part by a sneaky tactic that exploits user error and can post messages to players' Twitter accounts (S303). MessageMe pre-selected his whole contact list, so two taps invited hundreds (S334). Use: a viral oops makes users blame themselves, not the app.

## 8. The founder who cannot put the phone down (S240)
He wrote the book on habit-forming tech and admits he cannot put his own technology down; he checks email on waking. Use: the maker is not immune.

## 9. Two companies hooked on products used rarely (S007)
Products used seldom still built habits by building them around content and community. Use: the toothbrush test has a way around.

## 10. About half fail the test (S191)
Companies ask him to make a product habit-forming. About half fail his test. He tells them so.

## 11. The Habit Testing cohort (S054)
A young company finds the few users who stick, writes down what they did, and pushes others down that path. The 5 percent line sets the bar.

## 12. Writing is a routine (S134)
He wrote two bestselling books by timeboxing a writing routine, not by building a habit. Use: the habit versus routine split.

## 13. The burned $100 bill and the $10,000 pledge (S208, S212)
He once pledged to exercise daily or burn a $100 bill (S208), and pledged $10,000 to an accountability partner if he missed his book draft deadline (S212). Use: pacts come after the first three steps.

## 14. The summer schedule for kids (S133, S156)
He advises parents to meet a child's needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness rather than ban devices. Use: meet the need before capping the screen.
