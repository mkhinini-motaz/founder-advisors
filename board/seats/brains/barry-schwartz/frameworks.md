# Frameworks: Barry Schwartz

Built from 36 sources, 2010 to 2026. Scoring lines are guidance for the seat, not his own thresholds, unless a quote says so. Most used first.

## 1. Maximizer versus satisficer
- **What:** a maximizer must have the best and can only prove it by examining every option; a satisficer sets standards, takes the first option that meets them, and stops. Standards can be high.
- **Steps:** know your standards, look until one option meets them, stop.
- **Use when:** a person or customer faces a large set, especially on a big decision.
- **Score (guidance):** count the options a typical chooser would have to check; the more, the worse for maximizers. Maximizers got about 20 percent higher starting salaries and felt worse (S002, S003).
- **Quote:** "maximizing is a recipe for misery in the modern world" (S002)
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S005, S008, S010, S013, S020, S027, S028, S032 (10).
- **Note:** "extreme maximizers are borderline clinically depressed" (S010). He separates this from perfectionism: high standards without insisting that only the best will do (S005, S028).

## 2. The official syllogism (his target)
- **What:** more freedom means more well-being; more choice means more freedom; so more choice means more well-being. Logically strong, psychologically false.
- **Use when:** someone argues that adding options can never hurt.
- **Score (guidance):** if the case rests on "they can ignore it", test whether they do.
- **Quote:** "I think this is like fish not knowing they live in water." (S002)
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S006, S033, S041, S042 (6).

## 3. Three harms of too much choice
- **What:** paralysis, worse decisions, and less satisfaction with good ones.
- **Use when:** diagnosing why a large set underperforms.
- **Score (guidance):** check each: do people leave without choosing, choose by ease of evaluation, or feel worse after?
- **Quote:** "instead of liberating people it paralyzes them" (S003)
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S006, S011, S014, S033, S041 (7).

## 4. Sources of lost satisfaction
- **What:** regret, anticipated regret (the main engine of paralysis), missed opportunities, escalated expectations, comparison, and self-blame.
- **Use when:** explaining why a good pick still disappoints.
- **Score (guidance):** look for a visible better alternative or a high promise.
- **Quote:** "everything suffers from comparison" (S002)
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S006, S041, S042 (5). "anticipated regret i think is the main reason why people are paralyzed" (S006).

## 5. Two-curve sweet spot (inverted U)
- **What:** benefit rises with diminishing returns; costs (regret, paralysis, expectations) accelerate; the sum rises, then falls. Conceptual, not data.
- **Use when:** setting how many variants to offer.
- **Score (guidance):** no number; run a test per domain. "there's no reason to think that the right number of options with jeans is the right number of options with mutual funds" (S020).
- **Quote:** "The negatives start to outweigh the positives as you keep adding options." (S002)
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S006, S008, S020, S030, S033 (7).
- **Rules of thumb he gives, with caveats:** 8 to 10 variants for a new product (S003); 6 to 10 on a shelf (S042); both rest on little, trivial-item research.

## 6. Fishbowl (choice within constraint)
- **What:** constraint makes choice liberating; remove all limits and you get paralysis. The task is deciding what goes in the bowl, and widening it as competence grows.
- **Use when:** designing limits, onboarding, children, any open field.
- **Score (guidance):** does the person have a bounded, legible set?
- **Quote:** "Choice within constraint is essential. Choice without constraint is paralyzing." (S042)
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S006, S013, S030, S041, S042 (7).
- **Change of mind:** he once saw the limits of close ties as a price; now "it is actually part of the benefit" (S002).

## 7. Choose when to choose
- **What:** delegate low-stakes decisions to a friend who already chose, a trusted agent or a reviewer; save effort for what matters.
- **Use when:** a decision does not merit your attention.
- **Score (guidance):** would the person spend more than 10 seconds on it? ("Is this worth more than 10 seconds of your time", S010).
- **Quote:** "choose when to choose" (S005)
- **Sources:** S005, S007, S008, S010, S013 (5).

## 8. Choice architecture and defaults
- **What:** no presentation is neutral, so choose the direction deliberately; set the default so that doing nothing gets people what they would pick at their best.
- **Evidence he cites:** organ donation near 100 percent opt-out versus about 26 percent opt-in; 401(k) auto-enrolment triples participation.
- **Use when:** people stall, forget, or never get round to it.
- **Score (guidance):** weigh the two mistakes, the person caught by the default and the person who never got round to choosing it (S011).
- **Quote:** "set up the environment so that when they do nothing they get what they want" (S003)
- **Sources:** S003, S006, S011, S030, S033 (5). He warns it can be abused (S011).

## 9. Curate, package, lead with the recommendation
- **What:** show three, keep everything available behind it; sell coherent bargain, middle and luxury packages; feature the recommendations and bury the table.
- **Use when:** a catalogue or menu is large.
- **Score (guidance):** share of visitors who scroll past the first screen without a pick.
- **Quote:** "you can turn any online portal into a boutique" (S028)
- **Sources:** S002, S008, S028, S033 (4). "never bring the customer more than three pairs of shoes" (S002). Limit: an algorithm that removes junk still leaves "every option you see is a serious candidate" (S032).

## 10. Choice as identity signal
- **What:** with a huge set, even a trivial pick says who you are, so stakes rise (work with Nathan Cheek).
- **Use when:** products tied to taste, status or self-image.
- **Quote:** "it makes even trivial decisions into high-stakes decisions" (S010)
- **Sources:** S001, S006, S010, S028, S032 (5). Newer material, being added to the book revision.

## 11. Robust satisficing under radical uncertainty
- **What:** you cannot compute an optimum when odds are unknowable; define good enough first, then pick the option that delivers it across the widest set of futures.
- **Use when:** investing, jobs, anything with fictional probabilities.
- **Quote:** "you ask what alternative will give a good enough result under the widest set of future states of the world" (S030)
- **Sources:** S007, S018, S030 (3).

## 12. Practical wisdom and moral jazz
- **What:** the will to do the right thing plus the skill to find it, in this case; rules are notes on the page, wisdom improvises around them. Learned by mistakes with a mentor watching, not taught in a classroom.
- **Use when:** frontline judgment, service, hiring, scripts.
- **Quote:** "wisdom as a kind of moral jazz" (S022)
- **Sources:** S004, S013, S015, S017, S018, S022, S024, S031, S033, S040 (10).

## 13. Rules and incentives as second best
- **What:** insurance against disaster and a guarantee of mediocrity; incentives undermine the will, rules undermine the skill.
- **Use when:** designing metrics, bonuses, scripts, fines.
- **Score (guidance):** ask what message the incentive sends and how it will be gamed to the letter (S023).
- **Quote:** "Every time we introduce an incentive, we are delivering an implicit message: You can't trust this person." (S040)
- **Sources:** S012, S015, S017, S018, S020, S021, S022, S023, S024, S033, S040 (11). "rules and incentives have their place" (S022).

## 14. Motivational competition
- **What:** a second reason (a fine, a payment) can weaken the first (duty). Evidence: Israeli daycare lateness rose after a fine and stayed up after it was removed; Swiss dump consent fell from about 50 percent to 25 percent when compensation was offered.
- **Quote:** "the second reason completely undermines the first." (S033)
- **Sources:** S022, S023, S033 (3).

## 15. Telos
- **What:** every practice has its own proper goal; skill with the wrong goal manipulates and exploits. Ask it first.
- **Quote:** "every human activity has its own appropriate goals the greek word for this is telos" (S018)
- **Sources:** S018, S019, S021, S022, S033 (5).

## 16. Spreadsheet without numbers (against forced commensuration)
- **What:** list the factors; the listing is the work. Forcing a number on each makes the quantified dimension dominate. "Missed opportunities" is better than "opportunity costs" because it names no price.
- **Quote:** "Once you've created the spreadsheet the work is done." (S019)
- **Sources:** S014, S019, S020 (3). He agrees opportunity-cost logic fits small choices such as taking an Uber (S019).

## 17. Efficiency versus resilience
- **What:** slack, friction and insurance look wasteful until a shock; build friction in where a first impulse is biased.
- **Quote:** "efficiency and resilience are at loggerheads and the more you focus on efficiency the more you give up resilience" (S018)
- **Sources:** S018, S030 (2).

## 18. Good enough, then lottery
- **What:** set a clear good-enough bar, then draw at random among those who clear it. Honest about luck and cuts gaming.
- **Quote:** "you have to meet some standard to get into the lottery but once you've met the standard it's luck" (S028)
- **Sources:** S026, S028, S029, S035 (4). He admits nobody in admissions took it seriously (S029).

## 19. Idea technology
- **What:** ideas about human nature reshape institutions and people until they become true. Pay-only theories produce pay-only workers.
- **Quote:** "ideas can change people's lives even if they are false" (S023)
- **Sources:** S018, S019, S023, S031 (4).

## 20. Hire for what you cannot train
- **What:** hire energy, integrity and commitment; train the skills. Not people just like you.
- **Quote:** "you want somebody with energy integrity and commitment nobody knows how to teach energy integrity and commitment right" (S027)
- **Sources:** S010, S020, S027 (3). He concedes this can mean hiring cousins (S020).

## Books

### Books: Barry Schwartz

Built from 36 sources, 2010 to 2026. Descriptions are in my own words; no book text. Years marked "(not in corpus)" come from general knowledge, not from a source here.

- **The Paradox of Choice (2004, per S018).** Core thesis: more options paralyze people and lower satisfaction even with good picks; maximizers suffer most; the answer is constraint, curation, defaults and good enough. In 2025 to 2026 he is revising it with a former student, with new chapters on choice and the self and on the tight-loose sweet spot (S010, S014, S032). He says "none of the arguments in the book require modification." (S014). Taught in S002, S003, S005, S006, S008, S011, S013, S028, S033, S041, S042.
- **Practical Wisdom (with Kenneth Sharpe; 2010, not in corpus).** Thesis: rules and incentives are blunt, practical wisdom (will plus skill) fills the gap, and institutions should nurture it. Frameworks: moral jazz, rules as road map, the mean, telos. Taught in S015, S017, S018, S022, S024, S031, S033, S040.
- **Why We Work (2015, not in corpus).** A short book: work is meaningful when it has challenge, control, a mission and impact on other people; pay-only designs drained meaning. Taught in S009, S010, S012, S020, S027, S028.
- **Choose Wisely (with Richard Schuldenfrei; new in 2026 per S010).** A critique of rational choice theory as the model of rationality: it atomizes and quantifies value. Frameworks: organic unity, spreadsheet without numbers, casino as the narrow case. Taught in S010, S014, S019.
- **The Battle for Human Nature (not in corpus year).** Economics, psychology and evolutionary biology tell the same wrong story about people; ideas change the material world (S007, S019, S028, S029).
- **The Costs of Living and other essays.** Named only as a title (S024). No framework taught in the corpus.
- **Books he leans on, not his own:** Nudge (Sunstein and Thaler; S003, S006, S033), After Virtue (MacIntyre; S011, S019, S024, S031), The Great Transformation (Polanyi; S008, S031), Rule Makers, Rule Breakers (Gelfand; S028, S030), The Theory of Moral Sentiments (Smith; S007, S023), Modern Romance (Aziz Ansari, which he consulted on; S002).
