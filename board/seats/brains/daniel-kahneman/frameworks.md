# Frameworks: Daniel Kahneman

Built from 27 ok sources (quick depth). Most-used first. Scoring lines are guidance inferred from the sources, not his thresholds.

## 1. Delay intuition (structured judgment)
Break the problem into dimensions, judge each independently from facts, form no overall impression until all are in, then use the gut last. Origin: the Israeli army interview he built at 21 or 22, six traits plus a close-your-eyes score. Use for hiring, vendor choice, deal review. Score: did each dimension get its own rating before anyone spoke of the whole? Quote: "Break up the problem, evaluate the aspects independently, and then, and only then, use your intuition." (S020) Sources: S002, S015, S017, S019, S020, S029, S033, S035 (8).

## 2. Inside view and outside view (planning fallacy)
The inside view works from your own plan; the outside view treats the project as one of a class and uses how the class fared. Steps: write individual estimates alone, name several reference classes, ask how many finished and how long they took, place yourself in that spread, and ask for a reason you differ. Score: if the class data says most fail or run over, the plan needs a reason to be different, or it stops. Quote: "The inside view is looking at your problem and trying to estimate what will happen in your problem. The outside view involves making that an instance of something else—of a class." (S004) Sources: S004, S012, S015, S020, S027 (5).

## 3. System 1 and System 2
Two fictional characters. System 1 is automatic and associative and holds all skills; System 2 is slow, effortful, and mostly endorses System 1 without checking. He says they are not real agents. Use to ask: is this a first answer I should check? Quote: "Those are fictitious characters. They don’t exist." (S018) Sources: S001, S002, S005, S018, S020, S022, S026, S032 (8).

## 4. When expert intuition can be trusted (with Gary Klein)
Judge the environment, not the person or the confidence: a regular world, lots of practice, rapid clear feedback. Chess and firefighting pass; stock picking and long-range political forecasting fail. Score: no regularity or no feedback means treat the expertise claim as unproven. Quote: "don't look inside, don't look at the person, look at the environment." (S005) Sources: S005, S015, S018, S022, S035, S039 (6).

## 5. Noise and bias
Bias is the average error; noise is the scatter of errors on the same case. They are independent, and noise is invisible because nobody compares notes. Kinds: occasional, level, pattern. Remedy: noise audit, then decision hygiene. Quote: "noise and bias are independent sources of error, so that reducing either of them improves overall accuracy." (S015) Sources: S015, S020, S026, S033, S035, S039 (6).

## 6. Noise audit
Give the same realistic cases to many professionals and measure the spread. In an insurer, executives expected about 10 percent between underwriters; the answer was 50. Quote: "our advice is to measure the problem. Do a noise audit." (S033) Sources: S015, S026, S033, S035 (4).

## 7. Decision hygiene
Design the process before the decision: independent assessments before discussion, dimensions judged separately, intuition delayed. It applies to one-off decisions too. Quote: "If decision hygiene procedures work in repeated cases, there is every reason to believe that they will apply as well to unique or singular cases."  Sources: S033, S035, S039 (3).

## 8. What you see is all there is (WYSIATI)
The mind builds the best story from the evidence at hand and ignores what is missing; confidence comes from coherence, not from quality of evidence. Quote: "What you see is all there is." (S005) Sources: S005, S012, S018, S026 (4).

## 9. Attribute substitution
Asked a hard question, System 1 answers an easier related one (similarity for probability, dates for happiness) and the answer feels fine. Quote: "Respondents who substitute one attribute for another are not confused about the question that they are trying to answer – they simply fail to notice that they are answering a different one." (S001) Sources: S001, S004, S005, S018, S037 (5).

## 10. The 12-question decision checklist (HBR, co-written)
Credit: this is the article by Kahneman, Lovallo and Sibony, not Kahneman alone. Questions for the decision maker (self-interest, affect, groupthink), challenge questions for recommenders (analogies, alternatives, anchoring, halo, sunk cost), and evaluation questions (optimistic base case, worst case, over-caution). Includes the premortem and reanchoring. Quote: "Partial adherence may be a recipe for total failure." (S012) Sources: S012 (1).

## 11. Premortem (from Gary Klein, in S012)
Quote: "imagine the worst has already happened, and make up a story about how it happened." (S012) Sources: S012 (1).

## 12. Prospect theory: reference point, loss aversion, framing
Value sits in gains and losses from where you are now, not in total wealth. Losses weigh about twice gains. Equivalent descriptions change choices. His own limit: not for ruin-sized losses. Quote: "steeper for losses than for gains by a factor of about 2–2.5" (S001) Sources: S001, S002, S004, S017, S027 (5).

## 13. Peak-end rule and the two selves
Memory of an episode follows its peak and its ending and ignores duration; the remembering self makes the decisions, the experiencing self lives them. He later softened the claim that only experience matters. Quote: "The point is that the experiencing self doesn't make decisions. All the decisions are made by the remembering self." (S017) Sources: S001, S002, S015, S017, S022, S026, S032 (7).

## 14. Formulas over feelings
In low-predictability work a simple formula beats clinical judgment, and overriding it usually makes things worse. Quote: "when people override formulas, by and large, they do worse than if they hadn’t." (S015) Sources: S002, S015, S018, S039 (4).

## 15. Pooling independent judgments (and the inner crowd)
Averaging removes noise only if errors are independent; a shared mistake gets worse and more confident. Asking yourself twice, apart in time, also helps. Quote: "The wisdom of the crowds will work, and pooling opinions will work when errors are independent" (S015) Sources: S015, S019, S035 (3).

## 16. Adversarial collaboration
Opponents run joint research in good faith and publish the disagreement. He admits he rarely changed his mind under such pressure. Quote: "Adversarial collaboration involves a good-faith effort to conduct debates by carrying out joint research" (S002) Sources: S002 (1).

## Books

### Books: Daniel Kahneman

Built from 27 ok sources (quick depth). Own words only; no book text.

- **Attention and Effort (1973).** Attention as a limited resource; he calls it his most significant independent contribution. Taught in S002, S028.
- **Choices, Values and Frames (with Tversky, 2000)** and **Heuristics and Biases (Gilovich, Griffin, Kahneman, 2002).** Research collections on loss aversion and judgment heuristics. Cited in S001.
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011).** The two systems, WYSIATI, substitution, planning fallacy, the two selves, prospect theory for a general reader. It gives no advice (S020). He later said some results in it, notably the priming chapter, are unreliable: "There were whole sets of results that I published in Thinking, Fast and Slow that I wish I hadn’t published because they’re not reliable." (S015) He holds that the two-systems idea itself rests on experience, not on those studies (S015). Also S017, S018, S019, S022, S027.
- **Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment (2021, with Sibony and Sunstein).** Noise as an error as large as bias; level, pattern and occasional noise; noise audits; decision hygiene; delay intuition; the inner crowd. Practical advice, unlike the earlier book (S020). Taught in S015, S019, S020, S026, S033, S035, S039.
- **Essay (HBR 2011) Before You Make That Big Decision with Lovallo and Sibony.** An article, not a book; the 12-question checklist. Credit the three authors (S012).
- **Nobel lecture Maps of Bounded Rationality (2002).** The mature statement of accessibility and attribute substitution (S001).
