# Stories: Richard Thaler

Built from 32 sources, 2011 to 2026. The stories he reuses, with the lesson and source ids.

1. **Snow shovel survey (1985, with Kahneman).** A hardware store raises shovels from $15 to $20 the morning after a blizzard; 82 percent called it unfair. Lesson: demand alone does not make a rise fair; the firm needs goodwill. Business students call it fair because they learned it in micro. (S001, S024, S026)
2. **Uber surge on 9/11 and in a snowstorm.** A surge to thousands of dollars in a crisis would have ended the company; a 10x surge "Uber was making a little bit of money and pissing off millions of people and that was dumb" (S026). Uber settled with the New York Attorney General over a snowstorm surge. Lesson: cap surge in emergencies unilaterally. (S001, S024, S026)
3. **Home Depot and Walmart after hurricanes.** Trucks of plywood sold at cost or below, water given away, to build long relationships with rebuilders. Lesson: the long game beats short-run gouging. (S024, S039)
4. **Cashew bowl.** He hid the bowl before a dinner for econ grad students; everyone thanked him; theory says fewer choices cannot help. Lesson: people want self-control and will pay a small cost to get it. (S001, S002, S009, S018, S022, S024, S026, S032)
5. **Professor Rosett's wine.** Bought at about $5, worth $100; he would not sell at that price and would not buy another at above $30, but would drink it. Lesson: endowment effect. (S002, S009, S024)
6. **Mugs at Cornell.** 44 students, about 11 trades expected, 1 to 4 seen; owners demanded about twice what buyers would pay. Lesson: real goods do not trade as tokens do. (S009, S010, S024, S026, S038)
7. **Basketball tickets in Buffalo.** A friend would have driven in the snow if they had paid for the tickets. Lesson: sunk cost. (S009, S010, S023, S024)
8. **GM 2.9 percent financing.** It sold cars like crazy and was worse than the rebate; no one at GM had the job of finding out why a promotion worked. Lesson: firms do not study their own promotions. (S022)
9. **Daughter and the Mets tickets.** He offered $1,000 for tickets; she said she would not spend it all on tickets; for the concert he sent the tickets. Lesson: give the thing, not the cash. (S026, S040)
10. **Nobel call at 4 a.m.** Asked what he would do with the money, he said he would spend it as irrationally as possible, and later wished for a Nobel credit card. Lesson: money labels are real. (S008, S019, S031, S034, S040)
11. **The Times trial subscription.** A one-pound trial whose cancellation needed a call to London in London business hours. Lesson: sludge; and he accepts a little blame for the one-click join that later jumps in price. (S002, S011, S012)
12. **Save More Tomorrow's first company.** Saving rates almost quadrupled; the first adopter was a small Chicago firm with an adviser willing to try it. Lesson: it took years to find one company to try it. (S009, S015, S023, S024)
13. **Swedish premium pension.** 450 funds, the biggest ad campaign in Swedish history, two thirds picked their own, then almost no one; the default added leverage and few left. Lesson: inertia is forever; the default needs a careful designer. (S009, S024, S035)
14. **NFL draft.** The first pick is overpaid; the earlier pick beats the next about 52 to 53 percent of the time; teams will not trade down because they cannot short the first pick. Lesson: overconfidence prices a coin flip like a sure thing. (S004, S010, S022, S026, S040)
15. **CUBA closed-end fund.** Jumped from a 15 percent discount to a 70 percent premium on a Cuba announcement while owning nothing there. Lesson: prices are sometimes visibly wrong. (S001, S003, S008, S013, S039)
16. **Amos Tversky at dinner.** Told an economist that everyone he knew was an idiot but the agents in his models were geniuses. Lesson: the essence of behavioral economics. (S004, S020, S026)
17. **Atlantic Richfield engineers.** Winning bids had less oil than predicted; they wrote a paper. Lesson: the winner is cursed. (S008, S018, S026, S040)
18. **Schiphol urinal fly.** Spillage down 80 percent. Lesson: a cheap, salient target beats a lecture. (S015, S035)
19. **Healthcare.gov metal tiers.** A bottom category named "catastrophic". Lesson: a scary name drives people away from a plan many should choose. (S019, S038)
20. **Amazon as the cheapest place.** "buyer beware"; it runs experiments constantly. Lesson: the biggest economics department in the world sits inside a firm. (S020)
