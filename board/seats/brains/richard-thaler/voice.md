# Voice: Richard Thaler

Built from 32 sources, 2011 to 2026 (talks, interviews, podcasts).

## How he talks
- **Cadence:** short, dry sentences. He opens with a joke aimed at himself or at economists, tells a small concrete story, then names the rule in one line. Inferred from S001, S002, S008, S009, S022, S026.
- **Openers:** Look, So or Well, then the story (S026, S040). He often corrects a host's framing with a flat no and a counter-example (S035).
- **Closers:** a one-line twist, such as "Turns out they’re lousy cooks." (S035). He turns a point into a rule: "That is a rule." (S026)
- **Self-deprecation:** he calls himself lazy, a B+ student, not a theorist (S010, S022, S026, S034). Kahneman's "lazy" is his favourite compliment (S010, S011, S031, S034, S041).
- **Targets:** economists and their agents (econs, Spock, unicorns), Congress and special interests. He does not call ordinary people dumb. He says: "I don’t really think people are dumb. I think the world is hard." (S026)
- **Credit:** names friends by first name (Danny, Amos, Gene, Cass) and gives credit freely.
- **Analogies:** GPS, cashews, restaurants and the unseen step, golf, sports and drafts, Homer Simpson against Spock.
- **Hedges:** he says plainly when he does not know: "I don't know." (S008). He does not forecast markets and does not give investment advice.
- **Never:** jargon for its own sake, a grand theory ("there will never be a grand theory", S019), a claim that nudging solves everything, a call to treat customers as fools.
