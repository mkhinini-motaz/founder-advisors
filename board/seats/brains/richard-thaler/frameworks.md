# Frameworks: Richard Thaler

Built from 32 sources (talks, lectures, interviews and podcasts, 2011 to 2026) and his 2018 Nobel lecture article. Most-used first. "Scoring" lines are guidance built from his own thresholds, not his words.

## 1. Mental accounting
- **What:** people keep notional accounts (food, rent, gas, retirement, windfall, gambling) and money is not interchangeable across them. Theory says money has no labels. He calls it his favourite subject and says "I think everything is mental accounting" (S005).
- **Parts:** the label the money carries; where it sits (house, retirement, cash); the reference point for gains and losses; opening and closing accounts; out-of-pocket versus opportunity cost.
- **When to use:** pricing, bundles, gift cards, fees, refunds, what a customer will spend a windfall on, which budget a purchase draws from.
- **Scoring:** ask which account this purchase hits. A price that draws from an account with room (a gas budget that overflowed, a gift budget) passes easily; one that draws from an account the customer feels is already spent gets resisted. Rise in house value: spend about zero; stock cheque: a lot; lottery win: about half (S040).
- **Quote:** "where the money sits has a big effect on how much of it you spend." (S040)
- **Sources (12):** S005, S008, S010, S012, S018, S019, S024, S026, S031, S034, S039, S040.

## 2. Fairness norms and the snow shovel test
- **What:** people judge a price change against a reference price. A rise with no cost increase, taken because demand jumped, is called unfair, even by people who would never refuse the sale. He and Kahneman ran telephone surveys rating actions from completely fair to totally unfair (S001, S024).
- **Parts:** the reference price; whether the firm's cost changed; whether the customer is in need (emergency); surcharge versus discount framing; whether the firm needs long-run goodwill.
- **When to use:** surge pricing, delivery fees, emergency or weather demand, any price rise, fee design.
- **Scoring:** a change that rewards the firm only because the customer is stuck scores as unfair. Charging a surcharge reads worse than a discount of the same size (S024). Guidance: if more than a rough majority of a survey would call it unfair, expect lost goodwill that outweighs the extra money.
- **Quote:** "People hate that. They hate it." (S001)
- **Sources (6):** S001, S024, S026, S039, S013, S032.

## 3. Transaction utility
- **What:** the pleasure or pain of paying more or less than the expected price, separate from what the good is worth (S024). A deal feels good because of the gap to the reference price.
- **Parts:** acquisition utility (value of the good against price) plus transaction utility (price against reference).
- **When to use:** promotions, financing offers, rebates, "was/now" prices.
- **Scoring:** a small saving against a high reference can feel bigger than a large saving against a low one. His GM story: a financing cut felt huge, though the rebate was the better deal (S022, paraphrase).
- **Quote:** "I use the term transaction utility to capture the pleasure or pain that a consumer receives from paying more or less than the expected price for some good." (S024)
- **Sources (2):** S024, S022.

## 4. Supposedly irrelevant factors (SIFs)
- **What:** things theory says have exactly zero effect and that matter: sunk cost, framing, defaults, mental accounting, the scale of an exam.
- **When to use:** whenever someone says that should not matter. It is a prompt to test whether it does.
- **Scoring:** list the SIFs in the decision; for each, ask which way it pushes customers and whether you can test it.
- **Quote:** "These are things that economists are sure don't matter at all, but they matter." (S003)
- **Sources (8):** S003, S004, S006, S009, S013, S031, S034, S005.

## 5. Endowment effect and loss aversion
- **What:** owners value a thing above what they would pay for it. Mugs: owners demand about twice what non-owners will pay, so trades are a third to a fifth of the predicted count. It hurts more to give up than to acquire.
- **When to use:** free trials, loyalty points already earned, price increases (a rise is a loss), subscriptions, anything a customer "has".
- **Scoring:** treat anything the customer already holds (a price, a free delivery, a perk) as an endowment. Removing it costs about twice what adding it gains.
- **Quote:** "the people who have a mug demand twice as much to give it up than the ones who don’t have a mug are willing to pay to get it." (S026)
- **Sources (7):** S001, S009, S010, S020, S024, S026, S038.

## 6. Sunk cost
- **What:** theory says a sunk cost matters precisely zero; people let it matter (the paid-for ticket, the dessert).
- **Rule he gives students:** ignore sunk costs, but assume everyone else does not.
- **When to use:** customer behaviour (prepaid credits, minimum orders), your own decisions to keep funding something.
- **Quote:** "So, my rule to my students is ignore sunk costs, but assume everyone else doesn’t." (S035)
- **Sources (8):** S001, S008, S009, S010, S018, S019, S024, S035.

## 7. Nudge, choice architecture and the GPS test
- **What:** choice architecture is the environment in which people choose; there is no neutral design. A nudge points people toward what they themselves want and can be avoided almost costlessly, ideally with one click. GPS is the ideal: you pick the destination.
- **Parts:** default; make it easy; salience; reminders; social norms; transparency (a nudge that depends on the person not understanding it fails the test).
- **When to use:** sign-up flows, checkout, menus, fee display, defaults for tips or add-ons.
- **Scoring:** can the customer undo it in one click? Would they object if they understood it? Two yeses pass.
- **Quote:** "Our ideal. Our ideal of nudging is GPS." (S011)
- **Sources (15):** S002, S005, S007, S009, S011, S012, S015, S018, S020, S023, S024, S026, S032, S035, S041.

## 8. Sludge
- **What:** friction that works for the seller: easy to join, hard to leave; forms that only the poor must fill in; hidden buttons. Most is incompetence, some is intentional.
- **Test:** cancelling should be as easy as joining.
- **When to use:** audit every flow for a step that exists only to make the customer give up (refund claims, cancellations, coupon redemption).
- **Scoring:** count steps to leave against steps to join. Guidance: any gap is sludge to remove or justify.
- **Quote:** "it should be as easy to cancel as it is to join" (S002)
- **Sources (9):** S002, S007, S011, S012, S019, S020, S024, S032, S035.

## 9. Default and Save More Tomorrow
- **What:** automatic enrollment (doing nothing joins), plus automatic escalation tied to future raises, plus a good default fund. Enrollment goes to about 90 percent. Saving went from 3.5 to 13.6 percent over four raises (S006, S024).
- **Parts:** future self-control, loss aversion (the increase comes from a raise), inertia.
- **Conditions:** it works where the choice is easy and the person has no strong preference; it fails where an ad pushes against it, where the setting asks people to build their own choice, or where the default is set too low.
- **Scoring:** set the default at the level most people would pick if they thought it through, not at the cheapest level for you. A default that is too low becomes the rate for everyone who accepts it.
- **Quote:** "Maybe the most powerful nudge we have in our arsenal is simply to change the default." (S009)
- **Sources (12):** S001, S002, S003, S006, S007, S009, S013, S014, S015, S023, S024, S035.

## 10. Planner and Doer (self-control)
- **What:** with Hersh Shefrin: a farsighted Planner and a myopic Doer. The Planner uses rules (commitment) and guilt. The cashew bowl: removing a choice made everyone happier.
- **When to use:** products that help customers hold to their own budgets or limits; commitment devices; temptation bundling.
- **Scoring:** offer a commitment product only if the customer sees it as helping them.
- **Quote:** "how is it that having a choice removed can make us better off?" (S009)
- **Sources (7):** S009, S015, S022, S023, S024, S026, S032.

## 11. Econs versus Humans, and two theories for two tasks
- **What:** textbook agents optimise, are selfish and have perfect self-control; humans differ on all three. Keep the rational model as a normative benchmark; build a descriptive model of what people do.
- **Quote:** "Economists are using one theory for two different tasks." (S003)
- **Sources (12):** S001, S003, S004, S006, S013, S018, S021, S022, S024, S026, S041, S019.

## 12. Winner's curse
- **What:** in a common-value auction the winning bid is the most optimistic estimate, so the winner overpays. More bidders means bid lower.
- **When to use:** bidding wars, acquisitions, competing for a shop, a lease or a hire.
- **Scoring:** if you win easily, suspect you overpaid. Ask for exclusivity or walk away.
- **Quote:** "the more bidders there are, the less you should bid." (S026)
- **Sources (7):** S008, S018, S020, S021, S026, S039, S040.

## 13. Ultimatum game (fairness against money-only agents)
- **What:** a proposer splits $100 and the responder can reject, leaving both with nothing. Offers under about 20 percent are usually rejected; the profit-maximising offer is about 40; most offer half.
- **When to use:** partner terms, commission splits, field-staff pay, anything a counterparty can refuse out of principle.
- **Scoring:** a split that leaves the other side under about a fifth risks a refusal that costs you both.
- **Quote:** "real people find low offers insulting" (S018)
- **Sources (4):** S018, S020, S024, S040.

## 14. Conditional cooperation and costly punishment
- **What:** people cooperate if others do and punish free riders even at a cost to themselves; fairness norms are enforced this way.
- **Quote:** "they are conditional cooperators." (S032)
- **Sources (2):** S032, S010.

## 15. Better than the next guy (the draft pick test)
- **What:** measure how often the earlier pick beats the next one (53 percent in the NFL). When forecasting is near a coin flip, do not pay a premium to pick first; trade down for several lower picks.
- **When to use:** hiring, shop selection, any "pick the best" decision where your forecast is weak.
- **Quote:** "the better than the next guy statistic" (S039)
- **Sources (6):** S004, S010, S022, S026, S039, S040.

## 16. Systematic error, and predict the mistakes
- **What:** errors are predictable and directional, not noise. A mistake is useful only if you can say its direction. Fuller and Thaler builds each strategy on one bias.
- **Quote:** "We're trying to predict the mistakes." (S040)
- **Sources (7):** S002, S008, S009, S020, S022, S023, S040.

## 17. Big peanuts and the stakes argument
- **What:** stakes that look small against a big potential prize are treated as peanuts. Raising stakes does not make people rational; higher stakes mean fewer chances to learn.
- **Quote:** "there's no evidence to support the claim that people improve as the stakes go up. None." (S013)
- **Sources (6):** S003, S004, S006, S010, S013, S018.

## 18. Prices first, then nudge
- **What:** where there is an externality, fix the price (carbon tax) or use a mandate; nudges help after.
- **Quote:** "You’ve got to get the prices right. And then nudging can help, and every 1 percent helps." (S032)
- **Sources (5):** S007, S011, S032, S035, S038.

## Books

### Books: Richard Thaler

Built from 32 sources, 2011 to 2026. Summaries are in my words and are not book text.

#### Misbehaving: The Making of Behavioral Economics (2015)
- **Thesis:** a memoir of how economics was pulled back toward real human behaviour; textbook "econs" misbehave by human standards, he misbehaved by studying humans. Economics was behavioural until about 1940. (S001, S005, S022, S023)
- **Key ideas:** supposedly irrelevant factors, mental accounting, endowment effect, the NFL draft test of Becker's conjecture, the fight with Chicago.
- **Taught in:** S001, S005, S012, S022, S034.

#### Nudge (2008, with Cass Sunstein) and Nudge: The Final Edition (2021)
- **Thesis:** choice architecture is unavoidable, so design it to help people reach their own goals while preserving choice (libertarian paternalism). The final edition adds sludge, smart disclosure and a rewritten climate chapter, and fixes the organ-donation chapter (S007, S032).
- **Key ideas:** default, make it easy, GPS test, sludge, prices first.
- **Taught in:** S002, S007, S009, S011, S015, S018, S020, S024, S032, S035.

#### The Winner's Curse (1992) and Behavioral Economics: Anomalies Then and Now (2025, with Alex Imas)
- **Thesis:** each chapter is an anomaly from his Journal of Economic Perspectives column; the new edition checks whether it replicates and adds what it means for economists and for humans. He says none needed to be disowned (S008, S011, S018, S021, S039).
- **Taught in:** S008, S011, S018, S019, S020, S021, S026, S039, S040.

#### Quasi-Rational Economics (1991)
- Named once; its introduction mentions the winner's curse (S004). No more is recorded.
