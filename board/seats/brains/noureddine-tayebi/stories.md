# Stories: Noureddine Tayebi

Built from 18 sources (quick depth, thin). Lessons are the notes' reading; quotes are verbatim.

1. **Weighing cash.** A friend who owned a food-distribution business counted cash by weighing it. Lesson: whoever taps cash flows has a big opportunity. (S019)
2. **Food delivery before and during Covid.** Launched in Algeria in November 2019 as a rough product, then in many cities at once in March 2020. Users trusted them, drivers were idle, and merchants who had questioned the roughly 20 percent commission came to them. He admits luck helped. "so we took the risk to actually launch uh food delivery in multiple G I mean multiple cities simultaneously by March 2020 and that was a lifesaver" (S019). Also S020, S021.
3. **From Intel to founder.** Intel Labs ran projects like internal startups, which taught him idea, pitch, funding and sales, but "being an entrepreneur is totally different than being a great engineer" (S046). He left a comfortable job a month before his third child was born. Also S040, S019.
4. **Mentoring then building.** He first mentored and wrote small cheques for regional teams, then decided he had to build it himself. Lesson: learn by doing. (S019, S020, S034, S048)
5. **The 300 investors.** Pitched many investors; talked to more than 200 venture firms over about four months to a term sheet. "on avait discuté facilement avec plus de 200 firmes de capital risque à travers le monde" (S037). Also S016 (300), S017.
6. **Waiting for Y Combinator.** He accepted the programme the day before it began, and rounds were "a lot easier" afterward. (S019)
7. **The Palo Alto school programme.** A pupil spends six months with a local successful person and presents at the city museum. Lesson: a zero-dollar way to build confidence and ambition. (S046, S034, S043)
8. **Stanford as proof.** Alumni founded about 40,000 companies and 6 million jobs; mutual help compounds, and Dean Terman funded alumni companies (HP). Lesson: Silicon Valley is values and help, not only money. (S034, S046, S048)
9. **The driver who saved for a wedding.** A senior executive drove for Yassir after work to save for her daughter's wedding. Lesson: sharing-economy income is small but matters, and trusting a stranger via a phone builds trust in society. (S038, S034)
10. **The false Careem message.** A former employee kept access to the notification system and sent users a false message that Careem had bought Yassir. Lesson: close access when people leave. (S037) He also called the buyout rumour false in 2020 (S048).
11. **The helper who asked nothing.** An early helper in the US said: "pourquoi tu m'aides sincèrement parce que j'ai vu du potentiel en toi" (S034). Lesson: mutual help and continuing to trust.
12. **The first US pitch.** An investor wanted the path to the US, not the Intel years, and decided that evening seeing ambition. (S034)
13. **Parents' perseverance.** His mother, told a girl like her could not be a doctor, persevered; his father slept in a hammam after being barred from a dorm. Lesson: values are absorbed in childhood. (S046)
14. **Baghdad.** The round city was left open to all, so people felt they belonged. Lesson: securing something can mean opening it. (S034)
15. **Reid Hoffman's cliff.** An entrepreneur jumps off a cliff and builds the plane on the way down. "يقول لك واحد راح يرمي روحه من لافاليز" (S040). Also S034, S048.

## Added from S049 to S053 (topup, batch 05)

16. **Dean Terman and the first Stanford founders.** The dean got the university to fund student startups so graduates stayed (Hewlett, Packard, Varian). Lesson: the Valley is a pay-it-forward loop. Figures: about 40,000 companies, 3 trillion dollars and 6 million jobs (S049; S052 says 2.7 trillion in 2015). Also S034, S046, S048.
17. **The stranger who introduced him to 20 VC firms.** He met a man at an event who called at 11 pm to check progress and asked nothing back, because someone had once done that for him. "il m'avait présenté à plus de euh 20 firme de capital risque" (S049). Lesson: help without a counterpart.
18. **Why he left Intel (two versions).** S049: big-company inertia and incremental innovation, shareholders who want quick profit. S052: a senior VP answered an Amazon comparison with that's Amazon, they have Jeff Bezos, which showed a lack of ambition. Lesson: leave where ambition is capped.
19. **The cash in bags.** A friend's visitor ran a food-distribution firm with almost no schooling; daily cash deals worth millions of euros were done with cash weighed on a scale. "il mettait le cash dans des sachets, il pesaient les sachets pour faire leur transaction" (S052). Same story as S019 (item 1), now with the setting: the office of a juice-factory friend.
20. **No starting idea, only a mission.** After the sale of his first company he decided to build something in the region and had no idea what; the cash anecdote supplied it. "il y avait pas d'idée en fait" (S052). Lesson: mission first, idea second.
21. **First company: indoor navigation.** Hardware and software; US, China and Singapore; Stanford invested; sold six years later in about 2016 to 2017 for strategic synergy, not as the aim (S052). S049 calls the company "insens" in the caption, the same firm.
22. **PSG.** After the 150 million round the club and others approached Yassir; chosen for the worldwide brand and proximity to the markets; a three-year renewable deal (S052, 2025-06-02). Lesson: raising capital draws partners; keep a network.
23. **Applying to Stanford.** He applied to about 20 US universities, got about 12 acceptances, most with scholarship; learned English alone in evening classes. Lesson: do not default to the usual route (France) (S052).
24. **The scholarship offer.** He still sponsors about five students a year to apply to US universities for more than twenty years, and pays when they cannot; he tells them to repay nothing (S052, S050). Lesson: pay it forward.
25. **A Yassir leaving party.** Team members who exchange emails at 2 am: he says he sold a dream and everyone starts to believe it (S052, paraphrase). Lesson: the mission is the retention tool.
26. **First launch of Yassir in Algeria.** Few people understood an app, and administrative barriers got in the way; it began with friends (S050, paraphrase).
