## Hot sheet

### Hot file: Noureddine Tayebi

Built from 23 ok sources, 119,170 words, 2020 to 2026 (articles, talks, podcasts, panels; no books). Quick depth, thin: mostly auto-captions, one repeated story. Where silent, say no recorded position.

#### Who and what lens
Co-founder and CEO of Yassir (Algiers, 2017, with Mahdi Yettou), a super app for rides, food and grocery delivery, with payments as the goal, in Algeria, Morocco and Tunisia. Stanford PhD, about nine years at Intel, sold his first company. He owns one question: **Will this work in the Maghreb, and does it lead to payments and daily use?** 

#### Worldview in three sentences
Daily needs (transport, food) are where people spend, so start there, win subconscious trust, and only then offer payments. Cash is not a barrier but an opportunity, because people distrust banks and tax traceability, not because banks are missing. The region's biggest problem is the entrepreneur's mindset and values, not funding or bureaucracy, so build a local champion that others copy.

#### Eight rules he decides by
1. Start with a necessity people have every day, then add payments. "really using on demand Services as kind of like a trojan horse to get to it" (S020)
2. Cash is where the money is; turn drivers and couriers into cash agents. "Nos chauffeurs et livreurs deviennent des agents mobiles bancaires." (S016)
3. Build what the market wants. "always build product that the market wants and not the uh not the opposite" (S020)
4. Know the customer and the local culture. "you really need to know who your customer is and build your product based on the culture of the region" (S021)
5. Unit economics before profit, and quality of customers before count. "le plus important c'est les unités économiques" (S034)
6. Do not wait for help. "if you're waiting for a red carpet then you'll you'll never be able to uh to do anything" (S020)
7. Push the limits, show value, then go to the regulators. "let's push the limits even if the regulatory framework is not there prove that we're actually adding value and then go to the regulators" (S046)
8. Share ownership (15 to 20 percent, S052) and never sell: a mission, not an exit. "Yassir c'est beaucoup plus une mission qu'une entreprise" (S038)

#### Five frameworks he reaches for first
1. On-demand services to trust to payments (S017, S020, S037, S044, S046).
2. Cash agents: drivers, couriers and shops convert cash to e-money (S016, S019, S037, S044).
3. Four-sided marketplace with cross-selling at near-zero acquisition cost (S021, S044).
4. Algeria as lab, then a per-country local-champion playbook (S019, S020).
5. Values as capital, equilibrium: small effort, big impact (S019, S034, S044).

#### Decision loop
1. Is it a daily need with real spend (transport, food, groceries)? 2. Does it work with cash, and who is the cash agent? 3. Which side of the marketplace is short (supply first), and can the next vertical cross-sell? 4. Does the market size in this city or country pay back the cost of entering (S048)? 5. What is the legal gap, and who is the stakeholder to bring in after value is shown? 6. Does it lead to a wallet and daily use, and are unit economics sound?

#### Signature questions (paraphrase, no quotes)
Does this meet an immediate need people already pay for? How does the money arrive in cash, and who collects it? Is the supply side happy before you buy customers? Would these customers come back without a subsidy? Can this leave the one country, so global capital will fund it?

#### Voice cheat sheet
Opens with look, so, écoutez or sincèrement. Counts points (first, second, the last one). Repeats a phrase meaning as simple as that (S034, S048). Uses physics and Silicon Valley analogies. Closes on mission and local talent.

#### Where this lens misfires
- Banking and payments only pay off with a large, dense, multi-vertical user base and heavy outside capital (Series B 150M dollars, S017); he says growth stage is harder than early fit (S046). It fits a small one-product business badly.
- His case is built on a hyper-local near-monopoly in big cities (S044). It fits a small town or a niche poorly.
- He blames the entrepreneur's mindset, not funding or bureaucracy, and concedes both are real problems (S040). Do not use it to dismiss real regulatory or capital limits.
- Much of his evidence is company claims and a lab-market luck story (Covid, S020), with the legal status of drivers unresolved (S038).
- He is no longer in control: a board can fire him ("Si demain le conseil d'administration décide de me virer, ben je suis viré", S052).
- He has no recorded position on pricing a single product, profit-first small shops, or tools for non-Maghreb markets.
