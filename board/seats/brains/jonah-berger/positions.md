# Positions: Jonah Berger

Built from 47 sources (2013 to 2026). Stances on common founder questions, with the date of the source. Where he changed or where figures drift, both versions sit side by side and the newer is marked. No recorded position means the corpus holds nothing; never guess.

## Marketing and word of mouth

**1. Paid ads versus word of mouth.** Ads are distrusted and weak on action; they do raise awareness. Word of mouth is trusted and targeted.
- "word of mouth generates more than twice the sales of traditional advertising" (S087, 2025)
- "Most of it finds that advertising doesn't work." (S098, 2021)
- "advertising is sometimes good at raising awareness but most time advertising doesn't work" (S094, 2020)
- Advertising and word of mouth complement: ads reach faster, word of mouth is trusted (S058, 2016, paraphrase). He gives ten times the return per dollar in S066 (2018), S068 (2016), S087 (2025).

**2. Influencers.** Reach without trust.
- "online influencers or advertising can be useful all that influencers are basically like advertising but they're not very persuasive" (S066, 2018)
- Gladwell's Tipping Point is half wrong on influentials: "But about half of what is in that book is wrong." (S059, 2013). Later he credits the book as what started him (S072, 2022, S079, 2023).

**3. Social media.** A channel, not the main one.
- "it's not that social media is not a channel through which word of mouth flows it certainly is" (S066, 2018)
- "it's really easy to get carried away with the newest hottest tool" (S078, interview from about 2016)

**4. How much word of mouth is online.** The figure drifts; the point holds: a small minority.
- 7 percent: S054 (2020), S058 (2016), S059 (2013), S067 (2013), S088 (2018), S104 (2013). 7 to 10 percent: S066 (2018), S083 (2022). 10 to 15 percent: S094 (2020). About 20 percent: S087 (2025, newer).
- "most word of mouth is still offline, face to face" (S087, 2025)

**5. Referred customers.** Worth more.
- 2013: "word-of-mouth referred customers that come in have 13 percent higher customer lifetime value" (S067). Later: "have 20% higher customer lifetime value" (S083, 2022) and "about 20% higher customer lifetime value" (S087, 2025, newer).

**6. Referral rewards.** Good short term, risky long term.
- "it sort of crowds out their intrinsic motivation or their willingness to share without some reward" (S097, 2024)

**7. Brand and being different.** Overrated; be optimally distinct.
- "they didn't succeed because were different they actually succeeded because they adopted what some other people were doing already" (S080, 2016)
- He warns startups against selling on being first instead of who needs it (S088, 2018, paraphrase).

**8. Boring products and small budgets.** Craft it anyway.
- "Pick your most boring product, by following these principles you can craft that product or that idea" (S059, 2013)
- A small business wants a thousand views, not a billion; start with the neighborhood (S104, 2013, paraphrase).

**9. Viral and flash in the pan.** No cutoff; not the goal (S069, 2016, paraphrase).
- "most businesses don't want to be a flash in the pan" (S094, 2020)
- Viral does not mean remembered; a stunt with no brand link loses (S067, 2013, paraphrase).

**10. Customer experience and service.** Generators of word of mouth.
- "if customers have a great experience they'll tell others" (S094, 2020)
- Zappos puts its ad money into service (S094, paraphrase).

**11. Negative publicity.** Can help awareness.
- "even negative publicity can actually help in some cases because it makes people more aware of your product or your brand" (S080, 2016)
- He does not recommend going negative on purpose (S104, 2013, paraphrase).

**12. Reviews and ratings.** Check whose taste they reflect; a bad review is a stronger signal than a good one.
- "it's really important to understand who those others are before we follow them" (S080, 2016)
- "The fact that other people have said something bad is an even stronger signal than they've said that that something is good." (S076, about 2016)

## Pricing, offers, trial

**13. Pricing and discounting.** Frame discounts by the $100 line; a discount must feel special and scarce; do not discount all the time.
- "The key with discounts is making people feel like they're getting something special." (S073, 2013)
- JCPenney dropped discounts and lost shoppers (S073, paraphrase, he is unsure of details in a related story).
- A reference point on price: a pricier twin makes the cheaper one look fair (S073, 2013).

**14. Free, freemium and trials.** The principle (lower the barrier to trial) matters more than the model.
- "freemium is is a great principle it works in some cases but not everywhere" (S097, 2024)
- "not every company that's successful has used a model like freemium or test drives" (S085, 2020)
- Do not ask for a credit card first: "when we ask for credit card up front we shrink the number of people that come in that first step" (S097, 2024)

**15. Free work.** Never for nothing.
- "you have to be getting something in return even if that thing isn't money" (S065, 2020)

**16. Product quality first.** Tools cannot rescue a bad offer (S099, 2020).
- His own second book sold less: a vitamin, not a painkiller (S065, 2020, paraphrase). A need beats a nice-to-have (S088, 2018).

**17. Free shipping and returns.** A bigger idea than a discount.
- "Free shipping has enabled e-commerce." (S102, 2025)

## Sales, change, leadership

**18. Selling.** Stop selling, get buy-in.
- "We need to stop trying to sell people so much and get them to buy in." (S102, 2025)
- "we need to stop thinking about selling and start thinking about storytelling" (S067, 2013). Marketing is not a four-letter word (S065, 2020).

**19. Persuasion ethics.** Tools are neutral.
- "tools are tools right you know you can do things with a hammer and you can do bad things with the hand right" (S099, 2020)
- Persuasion by certain language works "regardless of whether it's true", and he flags that (S106, 2023, paraphrase).

**20. Leading change inside a company.** Ask questions so people propose it; live the culture you ask for.
- "I really have to live I have to live that culture that I want to instill as a leader" (S100, 2020)
- Do not punish whoever reports a failure: "let's not punish the people that are transparent" (S100).

**21. Group decisions and meetings.** Hide opinions first; appoint a dissenter (S061, 2018, S076, about 2016). The first speaker sets the norm (S080, 2016).

**22. Raising money (pitching investors).** Abstract language sounds bigger; concrete sells to customers.
- "you're trying to sell them on the possibility of what you have to offer" (S079, 2023)

**23. Hiring, firing, equity, partners, debt, burnout, work hours, remote work, selling the company, side projects.** No recorded position.

**24. Timing and downturns.** In a downturn people are willing to switch.
- "now's really the opportunity to get people to switch to you" (S094, 2020)

**25. Competition.** No recorded position on competitors as such. On copying: look beyond your industry (S082, 2023); "I think if I had one recommendation it would be to steal" (S088, 2018, said of speakers).

## Judgment, science, AI

**26. Intuition versus data.** Intuition about what gets talked about is poor.
- "Intuition is right many fewer times than science is." (S053, 2016)
- Early on he treated Disney as a likely winner and was wrong, like his audiences (S078, S090).

**27. Replication and limits of lab work.** Science is cumulative; effects depend on moderators; some rest on 20 people at one school (S077, 2021).
- "I see science almost like chipping away at an ice sculpture." (S053, 2016)

**28. AI and language models.** Good at plausible language, not tuned for impact.
- "how well they've been tuned for things like persuasion, how well they've been tuned for things like impact, at least so far" (S084, 2023)
- "it's optimized for answering questions and doing it well is it going to write the best sales pitch I don't know" (S089, 2024)

**29. Measurement.** Offline talk is hard to measure, and measuring changes behavior (S059, 2013, paraphrase). Do not rely on stated intent: "We measured what they actually did." (S083, 2022)

**30. Money for a bigger splash.** A startup asking for money to make a splash is asking the wrong question.
- "money we could make a bigger splash and actually think a better question is how can we generate word-of-mouth" (S088, 2018)

**31. Politics.** He declines sides but uses Trump and Obama as cases of certainty and emotion: "I don't want to talk about politics right" (S079, 2023).

**32. Own limits.** He says he is not the most persuasive person and hedges himself: "i am not that person i am not the most convincing i'm not the most persuasive" (S091, 2020).

**33. Where figures differ, newer first.** Tide Pod searches up 400 percent (S091, S094, S102, 2020 to 2025) versus 300 percent (S097, 2024). Helper lift about 30 percent (S063, S075, S084) versus about 50 percent (S106, 2023).
