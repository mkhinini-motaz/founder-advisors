# Stories: Jonah Berger

Built from 47 sources (2013 to 2026). Stories he reuses, with the lesson. Only his own tellings count; where the details drift between talks it is said.

1. **Please Don't Tell.** A speakeasy entered through a phone booth in a New York hot dog shop (Crif Dogs), no sign, no ads, always full. Secrecy is social currency: the teller looks in the know. Lesson: access makes people talk. (S049, S059, S066, S078, S087, S098, S104)
2. **Cheerios, Disney, Scrubbing Bubbles.** Audiences guess Disney; Cheerios gets more word of mouth because people meet its cue every morning. He calls the quiz "a cheap parlor trick" (S066). Lesson: frequent cue beats exciting. (S059, S066, S078, S083, S087, S090)
3. **Geico hump day and Rebecca Black's "Friday".** Shares and searches spike every seven days; the content is no better on that day. Lesson: the day is the trigger. (S059, S067, S069, S073, S078, S083, S090, S104)
4. **Blendtec "Will it blend?"** A marketing hire films the CEO blending things; a $50 budget; hundreds of millions of views; sales up several hundred percent (he says 700 percent in S066 and S087, 71 percent in S090; take the figure as drifting). Lesson: show the product's power, the brand rides inside. (S066, S069, S087, S090, S104)
5. **Kit Kat and coffee; Michelob weekends; Budweiser "Whassup".** A brand borrows a frequent cue (Kit Kat sales had fallen about 30 percent). Lesson: pick the cue that fires often. (S059, S066, S078, S087, S104)
6. **Panda cheese, the bar with three types of ice, Maui Jim's dog.** Stories that cannot be told without the brand or the proof. Lesson: Trojan horse. (S059, S066, S067, S073, S090)
7. **The publisher's second copy.** A book arrived twice with a note to pass one on; he gave it to the person he judged would like it most. Lesson: customers target better than you can. (S059, S062, S066, S083, S087)
8. **LinkedIn "top 5 percent" email.** People told friends they were in the top percent, and LinkedIn rode along. Lesson: status that brings the brand along. (S066, S067, S073, S087, S098, S104)
9. **Nipsey Hussle's $100 signed mixtape.** A thousand copies sold fast; he sold being first and belonging. Lesson: scarcity as membership. (S065, S098)
10. **Mountain Dew trucker.** A doctor stepped him from three liters to two, one, zero; over 30 pounds lost. Lesson: ask for less, then more. (S054, S072, S085, S091, S099)
11. **The cousin's email signature.** Five to ten seconds, 300 to 500 emails a week; asked the right questions, the cousin did the sum and automated it that minute. Lesson: the cost of inaction, found by the person himself. (S072, S077, S085)
12. **Tide Pods.** A notice not to eat them, with celebrities, raised searches by 300 to 400 percent and poison-control visits. Lesson: a warning becomes a recommendation. (S091, S094, S097, S102)
13. **The founder and the weekend work.** The boss asked what kind of company they wanted to be, then what a great one does; the team proposed the weekends. Lesson: questions give ownership. (S054, S065, S096, S102)
14. **Dropbox and Zappos.** Free 2 GB; free shipping and returns. Lesson: lower the barrier to trial. (S054, S072, S097, S099, S102)
15. **Stanford tray slogan.** A slogan tied to the tray cue led to 25 percent more fruit and vegetables even though students rated the clever slogan higher. Lesson: a cue at the moment beats a liked message. (S083, S090)
16. **Opower and the neighbor appeal.** Door-to-door energy study: money, environment and good-citizen appeals changed nothing; telling people what neighbors do worked. Lesson: norms beat stated reasons. (S053, S061, S076, S080, S103)
17. **His father and the blue BMW.** All the DC lawyers drive grey BMWs, and his is blue. Lesson: we see influence in others, never ourselves. (S053, S076, S080, S103; S061 tells it of a colleague)
18. **Zoe the dog.** A two-week rescue trial let him say yes. Lesson: reversibility cuts uncertainty. (S062, S065)
19. **His second book.** Invisible Influence sold less than Contagious: a vitamin, not a painkiller. A rare candid miss. (S065)
20. **Thai anti-smoking ad.** Children ask smokers for a light and ask why they smoke; calls to a quit line rose about 40 percent (S102). Lesson: highlight the gap. (S085, S091, S102)
