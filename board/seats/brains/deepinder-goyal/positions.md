# Positions: Deepinder Goyal

Built from 37 public sources, 2015 to 2026. Dates are source dates. Where no source speaks, the entry says so. Newer views are marked.

## Pricing and fees
- Keep customer delivery charges low to drive growth: "Keeping delivery charges low has been part of the strategy to drive growth." (S032, 2023)
- Add a small-order charge on supplier orders even if some buyers leave: "we introduced a delivery charge for orders below a certain minimum value, which led to some churn in restaurants ordering from Hyperpure." (S032, 2023)

## Discounting
- 2022: "we just discount for new customers to try out the platform for the first three orders or something" (S089, 2022).
- No view on coupons beyond this: no recorded position.

## Raising money and debt
- Equity over debt: "So I would rather lower the price for Equity and get equity than take day." (S072, 2026)
- Debt at weakness: "If you are taking a dept at a point of weakness Ho than that's even worse I think." (S072, 2026)
- Debt can suit other businesses: "in some businesses it does Genuinely Makes Sense to Raise Debt We are not in those businesses yet." (S072, 2026)
- 2015: "funding if used in the right way can really accelerate your" (S069, 2015). Newer view (cash conservation, S028, 2022) is more cautious.

- 2015 on hot money: "money only solves one problem and that's money and sometimes money makes you do stupid things" (S090, 2015). 2024: "not because we wanted to but because we had to kind of a thing" (S088); only two of about 14 rounds started with a deck (S100, 2024). On intros: "if you have the right thing to say anybody will listen to you don't need intros" (S090, 2015).
- Trust over price: he refused a lower round offer to hide a cut from the team (S090, 2015); "never had to sell our soul to overcome the like troubles that we were facing" (S088, 2024).

## Break-even and profit
- 2023: burn came from the market and competition, "I don't like to burn cash" (S102, 2023); early years were profitable, with a profit in 2018 (S102). Guidance is a worst case: "whenever we give any timelines it's sort of always the worst case scenario for us" (S102).
- 2015: "Break Even is almost a state of mind right so it's a growth versus Break Even kind of a thing" (S069, 2015).
- 2022: "For now, we are being aggressive about conserving cash." (S028, 2022). Newer.
- "If growth happens, profitability should happen too." (S018, 2025)

## Courier supply and churn
- Short-term shortage: "We think this is short-term in nature" (S028, 2022) and "That was a temporary phase which lasted for 45 odd days." (S031, 2022).
- Churn: "So there is a lot of transient Population. Most of it is transient." (S072, 2026)
- 2022: "the average time span um that a delivery partner stays with us is 2 months it's a very like in between job for people" (S089), with 7 percent weekly churn (S089).
- Flexible supply: "perpetual accessibility - 24x7, 365 days a year" (S032, 2023)

## Rider pay and pressure
- Pay: "we pay well, we pay way more" (S072, 2026) and a rider on six days of 8 to 10 hours makes about 25,000 a month (S072).
- Pressure: "So there is no human pressure being Created to Make This Work." (S072, 2026)
- Time pressure: "our partners have zero uh zero times flashing that this is a time at which they're asked to do it at their own pace" (S089, 2022); no pay cut for lateness (S089). Operator's account.
- Handouts: "I have a problem with Distributing Money for Free ok because it doesn't last us" (S072, 2026).

## Fraud

- Early: a salesman bribe led to ethics classes (S072).

## Gig regulation

- Role of government: "it's nobody should do anything because companies should" (S071, 2018).

## Focus versus diversification
- "I have to pick my battles, I have to prioritise really, really well rather than saying, “I will get into everything”." (S016, 2018)
- Grocery in 2020: "it to get enough work for our riders getting into grocery is not part of our long-term strategy" (S077, 2020). Reversed: Blinkit acquired 2022; "Blinkit will be larger than Zomato in one-tenth the time." (2024). Newer view: .

## Expansion and new markets
- 2015: "we thought that taking the same business to more geographies would be easier than learning new businesses in the same g geography" (S090, 2015). He bought local leaders ("buying a monopoly in a local market", S090) and found "the one sizefits-all doesn't actually work" (S090). Half of ten launches in six months failed; later he said the overseas rush was too fast (S036). Context: S090 speaks as a listings company in 23 countries.

## Super app

- Exclusivity: "So do one thing, stay on both. This Is the best thing for you." (S072, 2026)

## Mentorship

He wants to keep his stake at 5 percent and says sellers sell for "factors at their own end" (S102). Shareholders want near-term returns; he thinks of the longer term (S102).
- 2015: "Zumato is very highly undervalued right now" (S090, 2015). 2022: "valuation is everybody else's mood and what they feel about us" (S089). 2024: "when I meet Founders they think of ipu as a scary thing and I just like fail to understand that" (S100).

## Added from S112 to S115 (dates unsure; S115 is 2024 by title, S112 names September 2023 as the present)

### Competition and Swiggy
- Swiggy has long been a real problem; he does not use it for real (never, zero), is friendly with its founder, talks only of the future. "Swiggy has forever been a problem for us." (S112, S115). He will not call ONDC competition yet: "too early to say is also a risky thing to say because then you can be lazy about it and stop watching it" (S112).

### Delivery, forced into
- "So it was almost a forced hand for us to do this." (S112): Swiggy's customer love pushed Zomato into delivery; he never thought about delivery partners before. In 2005 his first serious startup was a food ordering app (S112).

### Focus and international expansion
- 2013 to 2015 mistake: should have stayed in India; shut international at 93 percent India revenue. "my advice is to stay in India, chill" (S112). Strengthens the existing India-small-market note (S069, S071), whose view he later reversed.

### Unit economics and discounting
- Fixed costs spread with scale; honest flat discounts. "discounting should be honest, it should be 80 rupees off, should not be 50% off up to 80" (S112); "if I look at cost per order, when my orders increase, cost per order will go down" (S112).

### Hiring and promotion
- "it's better to grow from within" (S112); senior hiring "it's like quoting dating like for six months 12 months and then we are able to get people" (S112); fresh graduates can solve as well as veterans (mindset, S112).

### Culture
- "I think only culture." (S112) as the scale factor; his culture book is "about 80% of the core principles" (S112), an onboarding handbook, "no secret sauce". Same views in S115.

### Gen Z and riders
- Gen Z smarter but "less patience than what we used to have at that age" (S112). Riders: "don't break your backs, it's a hard job" (S112); about 20,000 to 30,000 join a month; flagged riders are about 98 percent right, "2% we are also at fault by putting people in a box where we don't need to be" (S115).

### Marketing and brand
- "people say all marketing is good marketing I don't believe that" (S112); "we have to make somebody smile when we have a marketing message" (S112); he writes some notifications himself.

### Stammer and leadership
- No town hall in seven or eight years; the company runs on Slack threads (S113). "even if i stammer people will now listen to me right so that i've earned a little bit of that" (S113). Not anxiety, he says; he avoids stages and fell sick before conferences. Confirms S014, S072, S079.

### Luck and trust
- "you can't dissociate luck from anybody's success" (S113); "Zomato is a system built on trust right so the people trust me i trust them" (S113); father sent two lakh rupees when he quit (S113).

### Identity and motive
- "I think I'm a child-like, curious person who just wants to do things." (S113); not for fame or money. Reads random books on science: "so the more information you download into your brain you are able to use it sometimes" (S112).

### Side projects (Temple, a personal health device)
- A founder with a reputation can still go public with a hypothesis; he published it so outside researchers would test it: "i wanted to spark a new branch of science" (S114). Over a hundred researchers wrote in. Calibration was done for one body site and "we just cannot do it again" (S114 Hindi and English). Decision lens only; no endorsement of the claim, which he calls a hypothesis.
