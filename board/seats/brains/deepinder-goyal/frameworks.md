# Frameworks: Deepinder Goyal

Built from 37 public sources (letters, interviews, podcasts, 2015 to 2026). Source counts list the sources in the notes that use the framework. Scoring lines are guidance, not his words.

## 1. AAAQ: assortment, accessibility, affordability, quality
- What: four markers of service that drive customer acquisition efficiency, repeat and frequency. Accessibility is delivery time; affordability is the cost of restaurant food; quality rises when more good restaurants open in a neighbourhood.
- Use when: judging any change to the food product, a new city, or a pricing move.
- Score (guidance): does the change lift at least one marker without hurting another? Two or more lifted is a go; none is a no.
- Quote: "These underlying markers can be summed up as AAAQ (Assortment, Accessibility, Affordability, and Quality)." (S028)
- Sources: S028, S091 (2). S091 (2019) calls it Triple A Q and says at 10 out of 10 on all four there is no reason to cook at home.

## 2. Our moat is physical
- What: the defence is the coordination of preparation, pickup and delivery in the real world, built for India's informal labour, approximate addresses and cash chains. Software layers and chat agents do not move food.
- Use when: someone says AI, an agent or an open network will displace the platform.
- Score (guidance): can the thing be done without riders, kitchens or stock on shelves? If yes, the moat is weak there.
- Sources: S039 (1).

## 3. Karma scores and three strikes (fraud and abuse)
- What: when a customer and a rider disagree, the system weighs each one's record and believes the better record. Riders get forgiveness twice (and the customer is refunded), and a third incident ends the relationship. When unsure, the platform usually eats the refund.
- Use when: designing refund, dispute or deactivation rules.
- Score (guidance): is there a reputation score on both sides, a coach-first step and a fixed count before termination?
- Quote: "So then the third time we terminate them." (S072)
- Sources: S072 (1).

## 5. Super brands, not a super app
- What: one app per need, so each brand owns a trigger in the customer's mind; merge only the fleet back-end and tech.
- Use when: adding a category or thinking of one app for everything.
- Score (guidance): does the new need have its own trigger and frequency? Then a new brand; otherwise a tab.
- Sources: S018, S031, S039, S088 (4). S088 (2024): customers tie a brand to a use case, and the tech stack could merge in a week.

## 6. Hyperlocal density
- What: store and city openings follow population density and driving distance; speed shrinks as density rises. A city without density gets shut.
- Use when: deciding where to open or whether to keep a city.
- Score (guidance): density first; if it is not there after a fair window, close.
- Sources: S018, S028, S032 (3).

## 7. Rotational Leadership
- What: each business's CEO role is time-bound, typically two years, to keep urgency and make teams run on systems. Fits when culture values systems, product-market fit is reached, the market is volatile. Not for early-stage or survival mode.
- Use when: succession, complacency after a win.
- Score (guidance): all fit conditions true, and not in a crisis.
- Sources: S072 (1).

## 8. The five ifs (agentic commerce)
- What: five scenarios of AI-agent adoption, each ending in an advantage for the platform. Platforms that intermediate pre-existing supply are exposed; platforms that run fulfilment are not.
- Use when: a threat from a new interface or aggregator.
- Score (guidance): does the threat own fulfilment and the outcome of a failed order?
- Sources: S039 (1).

## 9. Multiplier risks and the negative list
- What: take risks where long-term value could be a multiple, explain them to shareholders, and keep a negative list (no owned brands competing with restaurants) longer than the to-do list.
- Use when: choosing what not to build.
- Score (guidance): does it fit one of the declared buckets and avoid competing with partners?
- Quote: "our negative list is always longer than our to-do list." (S031)
- Sources: S028, S031, S077, S102 (4). S102 (2023): keep new bets to a small team and a couple of locations so cost stays low and the lesson is cheap.

## 10. Sum-of-parts break-even and growth versus profit
- What: each business in each country breaks even on its own clock; a new line gets one to two years. Break-even was almost a state of mind while capital was easy; later (2022 to 2026) the mindset turned to cash conservation and a 5 to 6 percent margin guardrail.
- Use when: setting a break-even window for a new city or line.
- Score (guidance): clock stated, and contribution rupees per order positive by the end.
- Quote: "each of these new businesses are expected to break even within like one year to two years depending on which business it is" (S069)
- Sources: S069, S028, S102 (3). Newer view: . S102 (2023): he does not treat the business as one funnel, so Zomato and Blinkit each run on their own path to profit.

## 11. Growth and margin compound
- What: usefulness drives frequency, frequency drives density, density drives efficiency. Not a trade-off; spend margin only if it is needed to grow.
- Use when: someone says to pick growth or profit.
- Score (guidance): is growth coming from the product, not discounts?
- Sources: S018 (1).

## 12. No goals, best effort (management by trust)
- What: no quarterly goals, bonuses, hierarchy or appraisals; people are judged on response to crises, customers and risk. Pay is generous and the same inside the company.
- Use when: org design for a small senior team. Not for hourly shifts.
- Score (guidance): is the team self-selected and senior? If not, do not copy.
- Quote: "We don't have hierarchies. We don't have reporting managers. We don't have goals. We don't have appraisals which are given to you like absolutely nothing." (S073)
- Sources: S072, S073, S079 (3).

## 13. Effort versus impact chart
- What: list what could solve a problem, chart effort against impact, ship the lowest effort and highest impact first. Test with throwaway tech.
- Use when: choosing among fixes, including AI.
- Score (guidance): top-left of the chart first.
- Quote: "The option with the lowest effort and highest impact goes up, out first." (S016)
- Sources: S016 (1).

## 14. Left brain, right brain role fit
- What: ordering is operations, maths and execution (left brain); do not move a leader into a business that suits another temperament just because the leader is smart.
- Use when: placing a manager.
- Score (guidance): match the person's wiring to the business.
- Quote: "Food ordering is very, very left brain" (S016)
- Sources: S016, S072, S079 (3, inferred from S016, S072, S079 on fitting roles to people).

## 16. Close the loop, then beat the phone
- What: users already search for delivery; ordering inside the app completes the loop and must be faster than a phone call. The early largest competitor was the phone.
- Use when: a market where ordering is mostly by phone, like a new town.
- Score (guidance): is the app order faster than a call?
- Quote: "our online ordering experience has to be faster than people like picking up the phone" (S069)
- Sources: S037, S069, S090 (3). S090 (2015) calls it going full stack on food tech: listing, ordering, booking, payments, point of sale.

## 17. Buy the local monopoly *(unnamed)*
- What: where a strong local player holds the network effect, buy it cheaply, switch its traffic to Zomato and keep the local founders. Targets were not VC-backed, so prices were USD 1 to 2 million. First deal: New Zealand, a two-minute call.
- Use when: entering a market where one local leader already has the supply and the habit.
- Score (guidance): is there one local monopoly, cheap, with a founder who wants out? Then buy; if it is a network leader in its home market, do not attack it (his Urbanspoon lesson, S090).
- Quote: "it was essentially buying a monopoly in a local market and we're just completely switching over all traffic to Zamato" (S090)
- Sources: S090 (1), 2015.

## 18. Localise per city, not one product for all *(unnamed)*
- What: after ten country launches in six months failed in half, he customised per market instead of copying the first one.
- Use when: copying a working product to a new town or country.
- Score (guidance): has the product been checked against local habits, not only local demand? If not, expect the half that fails.
- Quote: "we noticed that the one sizefits-all doesn't actually work when you when you're launching multiple markets" (S090)
- Sources: S090 (1), 2015.

## 19. Hand over ownership, do not micromanage *(unnamed)*
- What: for a market you cannot see, give a local lead the whole business and accept losing about a quarter of the markets for a success rate near three quarters. A weekly update call turns it into a reporting relationship.
- Use when: running a second zone or city from afar.
- Score (guidance): is there one named owner per place, with no standing report call? Inferred from S090 and S088 (hire people who do not need managing).
- Quote: "If you get on a weekly update call with someone it becomes a reporting relationship and people don't take ownership anymore" (S090)
- Sources: S090, S088 (2).

## 20. Market size is restaurants times currency strength *(unnamed)*
- What: size a market by the number of restaurants times the strength of the currency, not by population. Beirut has half Delhi's restaurants and about four times the rupee, so about twice the market.
- Use when: ranking cities or countries by prize.
- Score (guidance): restaurants times spend per meal in the local currency; population alone is a no.
- Quote: "For us the number of restaurants into the currency strength is actually the market size" (S090)
- Sources: S090 (1), 2015.

## 21. Quality and consistency of service is the brand and the moat
- What: a name is not a brand; the brand comes after years of consistent service, and marketing without it does not work. The marketing team is seven to eight people with one mandate, to make the audience smile.
- Use when: someone proposes to buy a brand with spend or to rename.
- Score (guidance): is service consistent today? If not, fix it before any campaign.
- Quote: "a brand is all about consistency and quality of service at the end of the day" (S100)
- Sources: S100, S099, S088 (3). S099: support calls per order fell from about 4 percent to a quarter point.

## 22. Two-year survival cycle *(unnamed)*
- What: the company thinks it has cracked it, falls, and rebuilds as a new organisation about every two to three years; it has nearly run out of money about six times. When survival is at stake, only 12-month thinking fits.
- Use when: a founder believes a win is permanent, or is in a crisis and asked for a vision.
- Score (guidance): are you planning for the next fall while it is going well? Inferred from S100, S085, S088.
- Quote: "we think we have cracked it okay and as soon as we think we have cracked it goes down" (S100)
- Sources: S100, S085, S088 (3).

## 23. Mutual moats between businesses
- What: food delivery, Blinkit and Hyperpure each build moats for the others; Eternal is a cultural HQ so the acquired team is not a stepchild. Hyperpure is the supply-chain play, partnering with farmers and restaurants for everything between farm and fork.
- Use when: judging whether a second line helps the first or only splits attention.
- Score (guidance): does line B lower cost or raise quality for line A, and the reverse? Only then add it.
- Quote: "a will like business a will help us be build modes for business B and business B will help us build modes for business a" (S089)
- Sources: S089, S091 (2).

## 24. The founder is the operating system
- What: the founder is the primary product; growth is a change in the founder's personality and outlook, and the business is an outcome. As he worked on himself his to-do list shrank from six to one.
- Use when: a founder blames the team or the market for a stall.
- Score (guidance): has the founder's own habits changed since the last stall? Not a measurable rule.
- Quote: "the operating system is you as the founder" (S088)
- Sources: S088 (1).

## 25. Right risk
- What: take a risk only if you know its downside and can manage it if it happens; a risk whose downside you cannot handle is one not to take. He says risk-aversion inside the company is its own failure, and keeps a poster on the wall for it.
- Use when: sizing a bet such as a new city, a new line or a pilot.
- Score (guidance): can the worst case be named and survived? Yes is a go; no is a no, however big the upside.
- Quote: "right risk is something that you know the downside of and you know that if this downside happens there's a chance to it" (S102)
- Sources: S102 (1). Fits rule 1 (survive) and framework 9; inferred.

## 26. Customer frequency ladder *(unnamed)*
- What: customers enter on rare occasions (birthday cake, a sick family member, the maid absent), then match night and movie night; orders per year climb from about four to 10, 20, 40, 80. Many customers are still in year one, so the base grows with time on platform. Market size is frequency times penetration: about 3.5 orders a month against seven or eight times that in the US and China.
- Use when: forecasting demand in a young market, such as a new town.
- Score (guidance): count cohort age and orders per user, not only user count. Figures are his 2023 numbers.
- Quote: "the first year customers order four times a year the next year they order 10 the next year they order 20" (S102)
- Sources: S102 (1), 2023.

## 27. Performance, possibility, paranoia
- What: three words on a poster in the office: do the work, aim at a very large outcome (internally about 100 billion dollars by 2030), and stay afraid of failing customers, partners, growth and money.
- Use when: checking whether a plan has all three.
- Score (guidance): a plan with a goal but no named fear is incomplete; inferred.
- Quote: "I am paranoid about um failing right about our customers not liking us about us not making money" (S102)
- Sources: S102, S099, S029 (3); S102 and S099 are the same year's interviews (2023).

## 28. Gun to your head *(unnamed)*
- What: when a problem turns real (a rival with a billion dollars, cash running short), set a deadline of about a week, set an ambitious goal, treat it as life or death and solve it; do not spend the time blaming missing money or resources. He ties it to a UAE sale of 170 million dollars (S115) made when Swiggy out-raised Zomato.
- Use when: a competitor or a cash problem has stopped being theoretical.
- Score (guidance): has the problem a date and a goal a stranger could check? Inferred.
- Quote: "take an ambitious goal and take a gun to your head and solve it" (S112)
- Sources: S112, S115 (same interview); fits framework 25 and rule 1.

## 29. Four months in the bank *(unnamed)*
- What: he kept about four months of cash at the current burn, and cut burn each month so the runway stayed at four months. Paraphrase of a garbled passage with the host's hypothetical in it.
- Use when: sizing cash for a young launch.
- Score (guidance): months of cash at current burn; below four, cut before raising. Inferred, one source.
- Quote: "we used to keep 4 months in the bank" (S112)
- Sources: S112, S115 (same interview); date unsure.

## 30. Fixed cost spread *(unnamed)*
- What: engineering, product and ops teams cost the same at one-third scale or ten times; cost per order falls as orders rise, so a few rupees of profit per order at scale pays for them. Per-order arithmetic he gives: average order value 300 to 400 rupees, blended commission about 20 percent (near 80 rupees), customer delivery fee 20 to 25, delivery cost 50 to 70, plus support, payment fees, refunds and discounts (paraphrase, figures his, date unsure).
- Use when: judging whether a thin per-order margin is a business or a trap.
- Score (guidance): is the fixed team sized for the scale you will reach, not today's?
- Quote: "if I look at cost per order, when my orders increase, cost per order will go down" (S112)
- Sources: S112, S115 (same interview); fits framework on contribution per order (rule 2).

## 31. Disprove it first, contra teams *(unnamed)*
- What: before backing a surprising idea he tries to disprove it, with teams assigned to argue against; he counts how many convert and walks away if none does. His health-device hypothesis survived four contra teams (S114); he also asks whether the answer is obvious and in plain sight, since a leverage point missed for centuries must be so.
- Use when: a founder is in love with a contrarian thesis.
- Score (guidance): count of independent attempts that failed to disprove it; one is not enough. Inferred.
- Quote: "we find another contra team they became pro we found another contra team happened four times man" (S114)
- Sources: S114 (1). Fits his "Let's test it out" habit (framework on cheap tests).

## 32. Next-day focus and avoid the trigger *(unnamed)*
- What: he does not plan long term; he lists the problems of the day or week and solves them. In the same spirit he does not fight the stammer: he avoids stages and runs the company by written messages with threaded replies.
- Use when: deciding what to stop doing because it is not your channel.
- Quote: "i have always been focused on the next day or the next week which also comes down to building companies" (S113)
- Sources: S113 (1); echoes S072 "I don't think that I have ever thought about the long term".

## 33. Third-party validation before market *(unnamed)*
- What: benchmark a new product against the accepted standard (MRI, TCD for his device) and go to market only with outside validation; test the objection with senior outsiders a month before release (an 80-page document run past eminent researchers).
- Use when: a founder wants to launch a health, safety or money claim.
- Quote: "if and when we are going to market it we'll have third-party validations" (S114)
- Sources: S114 (1). Personal device project; kept only where it shows how he decides.

## 34. Build for yourself first *(unnamed)*
- What: he put together a team for his own use, built a device, wore it, found it useful, then decided it deserved to be a product; a founder-as-first-user loop. The math check beside it: six hours a day of a healthy routine is a quarter of a life, so look for the small lever.
- Quote: "I put together a team just for myself and that was my personal fitness team okay here I want to measure my brain flow and that's what we started building" (S114)
- Sources: S114 (1). Also "the math doesn't add up" (S114).

## Books

### Books: Deepinder Goyal

Built from 30 public sources, 2015 to 2026. Goyal has written no book. He calls most business books useless: "most of these business books are useless" (S016).

His major written work is the Eternal and Zomato shareholder letters (a Q&A format since May 2022, inspired by a Buffett and Munger book, S028) and the founder letters: "Letter from Deepi" (2021, S012), the Eternal letter (Feb 2025, S010) and the Q4FY26 letter from the founder (April 2026).

Books he names (S102 names none):
- Leading (Alex Ferguson): the last book that inspired him (S037).
- Zero to One (Peter Thiel) and The Hard Thing About Hard Things (Ben Horowitz): each struck a chord at one point of his journey (S037).
- All I want to know is where I'm going to die so I'll never go there (Buffett and Munger): one of his favourites; it inspired the shareholder Q&A format (S028).
- Man's Search for Meaning (Frankl): he never finished it, because he has no destination in mind (S079).

Added from S112 to S115:
- The Art of Thinking Clearly (Rolf Dobelli, author's name from general knowledge, not the transcripts): his most gifted book outside his own; it lists human biases and heuristics, and knowing them yields better decisions. "if you can internalize them, you take better calls, decisions" (S112, S115).
- His own Zomato culture handbook: about 80 percent of the core principles after a decade; version 2 will add the rest (S112, S115).
- Sitting Kills: cited as a clue in his gravity research, not as proof (S114, paraphrase).
- He reads random science books (chemistry, physics, biotech) and cannot name one most impactful book (S112).
