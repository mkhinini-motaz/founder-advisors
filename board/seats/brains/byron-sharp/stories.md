# Stories: Byron Sharp

The stories and cases he reuses, built from his blog (2005 to 2020), a 2022 talk write-up and a 2025 podcast. Quick depth. Lessons are one line each.

1. **Uber adds a phone number.** People without the app or battery could still order. Lesson: physical availability includes the unglamorous options. (S001)
2. **The local supermarket's loyalty program.** No rivals, no change in sales within a year, points wound back. Lesson: loyalty schemes mostly discount existing buyers. (S001)
3. **The Adelaide department store.** Joining needed about $1,500 monthly spend, so only heavy buyers joined. Lesson: lower the barrier or the program recruits nobody. (S001, S175)
4. **The New York coffee shop.** Be visible on the street and skip stamps, because the profit is the fast morning trade. (S001)
5. **300 of 30,000 products.** A household buys about 300 supermarket products a year. Lesson: shoppers screen out nearly everything. (S001, S071)
6. **Galileo and the doctors who took 100 years to wash hands.** New evidence is resisted, then called obvious. Lesson: expect resistance to a law. (S001)
7. **Android and iPhone swaps.** Android 90 percent, iPhone 10 percent, each swap two customers: 20 percent of iPhone, 2 percent of Android. Lesson: asymmetric switching is arithmetic, not loyalty. (S044)
8. **Casino gambler and coin tossers.** Some nights you win, the odds do not change. Lesson: short runs are not changed loyalty. (S045)
9. **The chair after coffee.** At an emotions conference he asked 200 people if they returned to the same chair after coffee; nearly all did. Lesson: repeat behaviour does not need passion. (S094)
10. **New Coke.** Sales rose; the backlash came when something was taken away. Lesson: legends are tidier than facts, and reversing a mistake fast protects the franchise. (S074)
11. **Brand Keys, Starbucks and Dunkin'.** The vendor's rank moved after the real market changes. Lesson: a rear-view mirror. (S103, S062)
12. **Stengel's Grow and P&G's share price.** Stock price doubled, but without the 2000 dip it rose 20 percent. Lesson: check the baseline before crediting a leader. (S079)
13. **Cadbury Creme Egg.** Some TV money moved to Facebook after sales fell; a 7 percent bounce followed. Lesson: an uncontrolled experiment, and the new creative is a likelier cause. (S040)
14. **Car maker books "demo" sales.** Dealers book sales to the maker's own companies and the stock piles up. Lesson: targets can be hit without selling. (S032)
15. **Tesco cola.** Tesco's growth came from winning shoppers from other retailers, not from its own-label cola. (S167)
16. **The Apple Store table.** A "policy" that a large empty table was for demos only. Lesson: staff drift into rules that forget why the store exists. (S047)
17. **The Bordeaux walk.** He counted single-brand chains and the exceptions. Lesson: look systematically, not in "everyday mode". (S049)
18. **Louviere on choice overload.** A methods expert called the experiments worthless; a 50-experiment meta-analysis found more options meant more consumption. Lesson: ask a methods authority and look for failed replications. (S081)
19. **Pecking chickens.** The Roman army consulted chickens before battle and was still successful. Lesson: a successful firm's choices prove nothing. (S107)
