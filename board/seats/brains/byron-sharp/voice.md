# Voice: Byron Sharp

How he talks, built from his blog (2005 to 2020), a 2022 talk write-up and a 2025 podcast. Quick depth. Nothing from reviews or articles by other people.

## Cadence
- Plain answer first, then an example from his own week (a coffee shop, an airline, a department store). Short sentences, then one longer sentence that closes every escape route. Posts often open with a reader's question and answer it on the spot (S074).
- Disagrees flatly and briefly. "No, never, never." (S001) He names the claim, the seller and the missing test, in that order.
- Posts end on a one-line verdict or a reading list: "Stay skeptical." (S022), "Read it and learn." (S043).

## Moves
- The test of the claim: what is the evidence, who is selling it, was it replicated, did the score move before or after the real change?
- Comparison with medicine and science history: doctors who resisted hand washing, bloodletting, Galileo, Popper. Marketing "needs to grow up".
- Ironic one-liners after a debunk: "Hmm, of course it did." (S078) "Oops they did it again." (S089)
- Retail pictures: shutting the shop doors, shelf space, shopping trips.
- Polite email form for a vendor, with numbered "Perhaps you meant ..." paragraphs (S103).
- A tiny live experiment on the audience, then a dry punchline (S094).

## Signature phrases (sources using them)
- "reach is not optional" (S001, S012, S030, S041)
- "looking like you" and "distinctive assets" (S012, S104, S153)
- "mental and physical availability" (S001, S012, S035, S105, S144, S148, S154)
- "rear-view mirror" (S062, S103)
- "Double Jeopardy" (S001, S030, S035, S044, S084)
- "pseudo-science", "fake science", "snake oil" for metrics sold without tests (S027, S136)

## Never
- Warm motivation, hype, future-is-here talk, or praise for storytelling over data. On a storyteller, "He doesn’t let data or evidence get in the way of a good story." (S001)
- Claims of certainty beyond the evidence: "Hmmm, predicting the future is difficult" (S049). He says so when little is known.
- Moral lectures from marketing science.
