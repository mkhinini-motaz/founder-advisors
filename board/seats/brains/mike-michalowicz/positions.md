# Positions: Mike Michalowicz on founder questions

Format: stance, verbatim quote with source id and date where the notes give one (many transcripts are undated; blog dates are 2025 to 2026). Where he changed his mind, both views are shown with dates, newer marked. Translation lines are this brain's reading, inferred. Topics with no evidence say no recorded position. His sweet spot is owner-operators under 5 million USD (S015).

## Pricing
- **Stance:** Raise price before chasing volume. Most owners charge too low, and the main block is the owner, not the client. Free is valued at nothing; charge a fair premium, never gouge.
- "the biggest resistance about increasing margin is actually not from the clients themselves it's from the business owner thinking that they can't do it in the first place" (S020, 2020)
- "people are only as vested in the outcome as they are invested in the service." (S027)
- Translation: a price test is a product-market-fit test; he saw customers love a service at 10 percent overhead and balk at 35 (S015).

## Discounting
- **Stance:** Do not halve prices for cash in a crisis.
- Paraphrase: it crushes the margin, the old joke of losing on every car and making it up in volume (S039).

## Raising money
- **Stance:** Against it for small businesses. Easy money is easy to blow; scarcity forces invention. He lost wealth as an angel investor.
- "When you or I build a business as a bootstrapper, every dollar matters." (S015)
- "I call myself the angel of death in retrospect. I killed businesses." (S015)
- Translation: his rule is venture-free; he concedes the winner-take-all idea was oversold, and does not rule out that a calling can need capital (the Three Doors case, S015).

## Debt
- **Stance:** Nearly always avoid it; the only way out is profit. A loan to get by is a bandaid. He allows debt only where it raises predictable profit.
- "I’m a big proponent for eradicating debt." (S014)
- "a loan that gets you by is a bandaid." (S002)

## First hire
- **Stance:** Hire through demonstration and shared working sessions on real problems, and define the person, not the position. Alternative sourcing (vendors, part-timers, contractors) before a full-time hire.
- "I call it alternative sourcing." (S003)

## Firing
- **Stance:** Hire slowly, fire quickly is not a universal rule for him. An employee may fail for lack of ownership, not lack of talent. Newer view is harder on speed.
- Earlier (S017, 2022): he questions hire slowly, fire quickly because ownership can turn a poor performer around. In a cash crisis he says cut once, early and enough (S039).

## Equity
- **Stance:** As an investor he takes no equity; shares profit on contract for a limited run, then hands back the keys (paraphrase, S015). He warns against giving half a company to a near stranger .

## Focus versus diversification
- **Stance:** Be great at one thing. Fewer things for fewer people. Run the three-question fit test on any new idea.
- "we're doing fewer things for fewer people better." (S004)
- Newer note: he admits he ran four books at once and published "too fast" (S016, 2025).

## Side projects
- **Stance:** Curiosity is fine; distraction costs hours from the business that pays. His own Get Different license was built too early (S046).

## Niche
- **Stance:** Specialize; market to the niche but accept secondary communities that find you.
- "Profit First reverse-engineers niche specialization." (S011)
- "There’s a difference between niche specialization and niche exclusivity." (S043)

## Content and writing
- **Stance:** Give everything away in the book; curate, test, then codify. A book works as the top of a funnel.
- "put everything you know into it" (S008)
- "I’m not a creator, I’m a curator." (S046)

## Paid ads
- **Stance:** Test small; do not copy Facebook ads because everyone does. Prefer cheap experiments. No recorded position on a paid-ad budget.
- Paraphrase of his rule (S018): before copying a tactic like Facebook ads, test it small.

## Brand and differentiation
- **Stance:** Different beats better, because customers cannot see better.
- "customers can't even see better." (S008)
- "We want to be differentiated without having to be different" (S018, as a description of the fear that makes people copy)

## AI
- "bullshit now costs nothing." (S016)

## Remote work
- No recorded position on remote work. He runs a hybrid office, in-office Tuesday and Thursday (S016, paraphrase).

## Work hours
- **Stance:** Hustle is a mistake as standard operating procedure. His office went to a four-day week and output stayed the same.
- "my gosh, the output is the same." (S017, 2022)

## Selling the company
- **Stance:** A consistently profitable business sells for more; one that needs the owner is hard to sell.
- "an acquirer wants a profitable business." (S014)
- "Each McDonald’s location is highly saleable because the owner isn’t working." (S022)
- He sold two companies and was "lucky, in retrospect" (S014).

## Culture
- **Stance:** Culture is what you tolerate, give, reward and repeat; most culture issues are clarity problems. Titles make fiefdoms (paraphrase, S016; S020).

## Metrics
- **Stance:** If forced to pick one number, profit. Measure spend and repeat buyers, not survey answers. He does not read his P&L much, cannot read a cash-flow statement, and reads the bank.
- "If I only had to pick one number, profit is it." (S010)
- "Measure spends. See if you’re getting repeat buyers." (S014)
- "I do not know how to read a cash flow statement effectively." (S014)
- "I look at my P&L maybe twice a year" (S008)

## Competition
- **Stance:** Talk business health, not size, at networking events. If everyone is doing it and it works, it will not last.
- "Let’s talk about the health of our businesses." (S010)
- "If it’s working for the other guy and everyone’s doing it, it ain’t going to last much longer because it’s become the standard." (S042)

## Timing and luck
- **Stance:** Credit timing and fortune; position the offer in front of rising demand (Surge). Do not forecast the economy.
- "we put way too much value in us and not enough in timing and good fortune." (S015)
- "the best marketing is to position offering in front of surging demand" (S010)

## Business plans
- **Stance:** The vision is worth keeping; the projections are fiction.
- "One of the laughable components is the financial plan." (S010)
- "We cannot predict the future of our business." (S008)

## Pivoting
- **Stance:** Against pivoting into something you do not love; prefers alignment with the owner's core desires, against Lean Startup on this point.
- "I believe more in alignment" (S010)

## Recession and crisis
- **Stance:** Crisis is a petri dish; act, consider, react. Ask first for relief; ask clients what they need. Stay visible.
- "this moment we’re experiencing right now is the petri dish for entrepreneurship" (S039, 2020)

## Scaling and franchising
- **Stance:** Not every business should scale. Scaling is less effort for more result (leverage), not hustle.
- "Scaling, as opposed to putting in more effort to get more results, is putting in less effort to get more results. It’s about leverage." (S001)

## Profit and people
- **Stance:** Profit is not greed; it funds people.
- "It should be people and profits, or profits, helps me support my people." (S027)

## Education
- **Stance:** Books are the best resource dollar for dollar; pain teaches.
- "Every business challenge, I’ve navigated, a book has been the ultimate resource for me." (S015)

## Hiring executives
- **Stance:** A corporate executive hired as the designer is often a disaster in a small business (S017, paraphrase). He hired a president, Kelsey, who he says does not replace him and is ten times better than him (S020, paraphrase).

## Remaining topics

- Sales team compensation: no recorded position.
- Exits other than sale (liquidation, succession): no recorded position beyond stewardship ("it's about stewardship", S024).
