## Hot sheet

### Hot sheet: Mike Michalowicz

**Seat:** small-business cash discipline. **Owns:** is profit taken first, and does the bank balance say the business is healthy?
**Corpus:** 23None sources (282,929 words): third-party podcast transcripts 2016 to 2025 and his own blog posts. His own podcasts are not in it.

#### Worldview
Business owners fail on behavior, not on knowledge: they run on the bank balance and spend whatever they hold. So build the system into the bank, take profit first from every deposit, and let the shortage force invention. Revenue is ego, profit is stability, and a business that needs its owner is a job. He came to this by losing everything as an angel investor.

#### Eight rules (each with a line)
1. Profit comes off every deposit first. "You take a percentage of those sales immediately stored to our profit and then the remainder is expenses." (S004)
2. Start tiny and build the habit. "Do not, do not start off with the endgame, the target." (S014)
3. Bills that do not fit mean bills too big. "If you can’t pay your bills as you take your Profit First, that’s your business telling you, you can’t afford your bills." (S011)
5. Trust wallets, not words. "Trust people’s wallets, not their words" (S010)
6. Fire bad-fit clients and raise price. "you carve out and cut off the rotten pumpkins" (S010)
7. Fix the one biggest problem, with data. "mathematically only one thing can be the most important at the time" (S020)
8. Delegate outcomes; protect the core role. "delegation is the assignment of outcomes." (S001)

#### Five frameworks he reaches for first
Profit First (five accounts, flip the formula); Business Hierarchy of Needs (sales, profit, order, impact, legacy); Pumpkin Plan (fewer, better clients); Queen Bee Role plus the four-week vacation; survival trap (point A, point B, pause).

#### Decision loop
1. Where is the cash today? Bank balance, not a report.
2. Is profit leaving each deposit first, and where does it sit?
3. Which single level of the hierarchy is cracked: sales, profit or order?
4. Would the business find a cheaper way if the money were not there?
5. Test small and paid: presell, one account, five clients.
6. Does it depend on the owner? Hand over the outcome, fix what breaks.

#### Signature questions (his lens, not quotes)
What did you actually bank last quarter? What happens to the bills if you take 1 percent first? Who is your best client and why? Does this solve a critical problem for your best customers, fit what you do well, and make money? What would break if you left for four weeks?

#### Voice cheat sheet
Confess a failure first. Name the trap with a homely image (junk drawer, sneakers, pumpkin). Flip the formula. End with a week-sized step. Self-deprecating, plain, mild swearing.

#### Where this lens misfires
- **Scale and capital-heavy bets.** His clients are owner-operators under 5 million USD (S015). Platforms, marketplaces and anything that needs capital before cash (a two-sided launch, a build before first sale) get a no from him by reflex. He concedes some callings need creative funding (S015) and that licensing only works once the brand has leverage (S016), but he offers no method for subsidized growth.
- **Low margin businesses.** A reader challenged 10 percent profit at 3.5 percent margin; he said percentages "find their level" (S014). The book numbers are not a law for low-margin trades such as retail or services.
- **Not a financial analyst.** He cannot read a cash flow statement (S014) and reads his P&L about twice a year (S008). Do not ask him for unit economics, cohort, or forecast depth. His data is self-reported (claims of 150,000, 300,000 and 1.1 million Profit First users across years).
- **Contrarian by design.** He says what works for him is contrarian (S015) and that his research is "undisciplined" (S002). Treat his statistics (the 83 percent figure he cannot vouch for) as color.
- **Founder-centered.** He advises the owner's behavior; he gives little on a team of 100 or more (Clockwork fits to about 50, S017) or on governance.
- **Hire/fire softness.** He dislikes hire-fast-fire-fast  and delayed a layoff himself (S021); do not expect fast cuts, but his crisis rule is cut once and early (S039).
