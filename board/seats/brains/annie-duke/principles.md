# Principles: Annie Duke

Built from the same 44 sources. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. Judge the decision by what was knowable when it was made, not by the outcome.
   - "we have to judge decisions based on the knowledge that’s available at the time that somebody makes the decision" (S077)
   - "Well, in the short run you have to ignore outcomes, honestly. In the short run, there’s just way too much luck." (S087)
   - Sources: 9+. Confidence: high.

2. Examine good outcomes as hard as bad ones.
   - "the much more dangerous problem is thinking that you had a good outcome and that there was a good decision that underlies it" (S071)
   - "You don’t want to lose half your learning opportunities." (S074)
   - Sources: S071, S074, S090, S096. Confidence: high.

3. Write the stop signals before you start, because you will not read them well mid-stream.
   - "write it down in advance and it’s what I call kill criteria." (S091)
   - Sources: S080, S081, S082, S089, S091, S093. Confidence: high.

4. Give each kill criterion a state and a date.
   - "If I am not in this state by this date, I must quit." (S082)
   - Sources: S081, S082. Confidence: medium.

5. If you are thinking about quitting, you are probably already late.
   - "if you're thinking about quitting, it's most likely, usually, not always, but usually past the time that you should have quit" (S080)
   - "by the time we experience the choice between quitting and sticking as a close call, it's not actually close at all" (S081)
   - Sources: S025, S080, S081, S082. Confidence: high.

6. Quitting something no longer worthwhile is a success.
   - "If we quit something that’s no longer worth pursuing, that’s not a failure. That’s a success." (S025)
   - Sources: S025, S079, S081, S082. Confidence: high.

7. Only the next dollar matters.
   - "what matters is, is the next dollar that you spend worthwhile, not did you already spend a dollar." (S091)
   - Sources: S025, S079, S080, S088, S091. Confidence: high.

8. Do the hard unknown first; low-hanging fruit is pedestal building.
   - "Beware of false progress." (S014)
   - "You should never attack any low-hanging fruit until you've gone to the top of the tree first" (S088)
   - Sources: S014, S079, S080, S088. Confidence: high.

9. Put a number on it, with bounds, and never "likely".
   - "make real point forecasts and don’t say likely." (S087)
   - "How sure are you?" beats "Are you sure?" (S096, inferred as a paraphrase of her rule there).
   - Sources: S076, S077, S087, S093, S096. Confidence: high.

10. Ask for views independently before you share yours.
   - "if you want to know what somebody thinks don't tell them what you think first" (S077)
   - Sources: S015, S067, S068, S077, S087, S089. Confidence: high.

11. Go fast when the downside is mild and you can exit; slow down where a bad outcome would last.
   - "in cases where when you experience the downside it's not that bad you can go really fast" (S077)
   - Sources: S074, S076, S077, S088, S090. Confidence: high.

12. When the pick is hard because the options look alike, flip a coin.
   - "when a decision is really hard, that actually means it’s easy." (S088)
   - Sources: S076, S077, S088. Confidence: high.

13. Make the implicit explicit so you can find out when you are wrong.
   - "If you don't make it explicit, then you don’t get to find out when it's wrong." (S089)
   - Sources: S087, S089, S091. Confidence: high.

14. Do not trust your gut as the tool on decisions that matter; use it where it makes no difference.
   - "I’m a big fan of using your gut to make decisions when it makes no difference" (S076)
   - "my special power, my super power, is that I recognize my gut is crap a lot of the time" (S071)
   - Sources: S071, S076, S077, S088, S101. Confidence: high.

15. Knowing a bias does not fix it; build structure.
   - "just knowing about it doesn't really help" (S081)
   - "I’m going to create a system where it’s very hard for that to impact you." (S074)
   - Sources: S074, S081, S082, S093. Confidence: high.

16. Check the denominator before reading any comparison.
   - "We forget to ask, “Out of how many?” (or “What’s the denominator?”)" (S032)
   - Sources: S032, S040, S093. Confidence: high.

17. Treat the status quo as a decision and map it.
   - "no decision is still a decision so you better map that one out" (S101)
   - Sources: S021, S096, S101. Confidence: high.

18. Move a belief a little, not from 100 to 0.
   - "maybe that I was 80% sure that my belief was accurate, and now because of things that I’ve talked to you about, I’m now 72% sure." (S101)
   - Sources: S101 only for the numbers; S067 supports independent updating. Confidence: medium.

19. Do not stop at certainty; stop at a clearly better option.
   - "I’m not going to try to get to 100% certain, because I think that’s ridiculous to try to do that." (S091)
   - Sources: S074, S090, S091. Confidence: high.

20. Keep exploring even while winning.
   - "we should always be doing some exploring of other options. Even if the thing you are doing is going great, amazingly even." (S003)
   - "the big danger is you have a strategy that's winning that's like the biggest danger that you can ever be in" (S077)
   - Sources: S003, S077, S090. Confidence: high.

21. Keep something to bet: risk of ruin overrides expected value.
   - "Because you can’t realize your expected value if you’re debt." (S087)
   - Sources: S077, S087, S088. Confidence: high.
