# Strategy, money and the founder's discipline

Notes on strategy and money for a small, self-funded company, built only from free public material: talks, free blog posts, a free official page and a public-domain text. Rewritten 21 Nov 2026. Each point cites the free page that states it, opened that day. Lines marked "Application" are our own reasoning, not the author's. Greene moved to `people-and-power.md`.

## Thiel on competition and the first market (Stanford CS183 lecture notes, 2012)

Source: Blake Masters' notes of Peter Thiel's course, posted free by Masters, who says the ideas are Thiel's and the errors his. Opened as a copy under a CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 label (the original Tumblr page refused the fetch): https://gist.github.com/harperreed/3201887

- **Competition erases profit.** The notes say "In perfect competition, no firms in an industry make economic profit." They add that the valuable businesses are monopoly businesses (Class 3). Application: do not fight a funded incumbent head-on on its home ground; win where it is absent, weak or disliked.
- **Start small and dominate.** The recipe is to find a small target market, become the best in the world at serving it, then take adjacent markets (Class 4). Application: for a local marketplace, density in one zone before a second zone.
- **Ways to own a market:** brand, scale cost advantages, network effects and proprietary technology (Class 4). Application: a local marketplace's moat is density and trust more than its software.
- **Last mover.** "More important than being the first mover is being the last mover" (Class 3). Much of a fast-growing company's value sits 10 to 15 years out.
- **Power law.** The notes say startup outcomes and VC returns follow a power law (Class 9). Application: a few clients, channels and decisions give most of the result, so concentrate on them.
- **Distribution.** The notes name poor distribution, not the product, as the number one cause of failure (Class 9). Application: a plan with no channel, no channel cost and no reason it reaches this audience is not a plan.
- **Caveat (ours).** The lectures speak to venture-backed technology firms. "Monopoly" means owning a niche by being better, never exclusivity clauses or collusion. A self-funded company makes the power-law bet in small, survivable pieces.

## Acquisition.com: the scaling roadmap (Hormozi)

Source: the company's free roadmap pages, https://www.acquisition.com/training/scalingstart and https://www.acquisition.com/training/improvise

- **Ten stages:** Improvise, Monetize, Advertise, Stabilize, Prioritize, Productize, Optimize, Categorize, Specialize, Capitalize. The pages list the names with team size or revenue bands and no stage questions.
- Application: name the stage before taking advice. Advice meant for a later stage (dashboards, org charts, a head of marketing, a second product) is a distraction now. In the first stages the founder sells and delivers by hand; automate only what already works by hand.
- For offers, leads and money models, read `persuasion-and-offers.md`.

## Housel on money and behaviour (Collaborative Fund blog)

- **Survive first.** "Getting money is one thing. Keeping it is another." Few gains are worth wiping yourself out over, and staying in the game lets compounding work (Getting Wealthy vs. Staying Wealthy, 28 Nov 2022). Application: never take a risk that can end the company, however good the expected value.
- **Room for error.** Housel advises "plan on the plan not going according to plan" (same post). He quotes Ben Graham: "The purpose of the margin of safety is to render the forecast unnecessary" (The Psychology of Money, 1 Jun 2018). Application: budget for a worse month than the forecast. The reserve is the plan.
- **Reasonable beats rational.** "Rational is hard. Being reasonable is often the most practical goal" (Rational vs. Reasonable, 11 Dec 2018). Application: a plan the founder keeps through a bad month beats an optimal plan dropped in week three.
- **Tails drive everything.** In his data, 65% of 21,000 venture financings (2004 to 2014) lost money, and a few outliers drove the returns (Tails, You Win, 26 Jul 2022). Application: keep each try small enough to lose, and keep trying.
- **Wealth is what you do not see.** "Wealth, in fact, is what you don't see" (The Psychology of Money, 1 Jun 2018). Application: money spent to look successful (an office, a car, a launch party) is not growth.
- **Volatility is the price.** Returns cost "volatility and uncertainty", and "Occasional upheaval is usually part of a standard plan" (same post). Application: slow weeks are the price of the model; do not redesign on one bad week.
- **Caveat (ours).** Personal-finance essays. For a company they become cash-reserve and risk rules, not investment advice.

Sources: https://collabfund.com/blog/getting-wealthy-vs-staying-wealthy/ · https://collabfund.com/blog/the-psychology-of-money/ · https://collabfund.com/blog/rational-vs-reasonable/ · https://collabfund.com/blog/tails-you-win/ (all opened 21 Nov 2026).

## Clason on cash discipline (The Richest Man in Babylon, 1926, public domain)

Free full text on Wikisource. The page says the work is in the public domain in the United States because it was published in 1926, before 1 Jan 1931: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Richest_Man_In_Babylon (opened 21 Nov 2026). Chapter references below are to its chapter pages.

- **Pay yourself first.** Seven cures for a lean purse (ch. 3): out of every ten coins earned, spend nine and keep one. Application: the reserve and the tax collected go into a separate account first; the business runs on the rest.
- **Control expenditures** (ch. 3). Budget the necessities so savings stay untouched.
- **Make the money work** (ch. 3, 5). Savings should be put to productive use. Application: only in what already earns, such as a proven channel or a client who already buys. Experiments are never paid from the safety money.
- **Guard against loss.** The Five Laws of Gold (ch. 5) say to invest where the principal is safe, on the advice of people wise in handling money, and not in ventures you do not know. They warn against impossible returns and the advice of tricksters. Application: decline the high-return offer from a persuasive friend or stranger.
- **Debt** (Dabasir, ch. 9). He listed each creditor and the sum owed, then split his earnings: one tenth saved, seven tenths for living, two tenths shared among creditors. He told each creditor the plan.
- **Increase the ability to earn** (ch. 3): skills, like money, grow with use.
- **Caveat (ours).** Parables. The tenth is an illustration; pick the share from the numbers.

## Lavingia on building less (his free skills, based on The Minimalist Entrepreneur)

Source: the free Claude Code skills Sahil Lavingia published himself. The repository README says they are based on his book, but everything below is from the free files: https://github.com/slavingia/skills, with raw files `skills/processize/SKILL.md`, `skills/validate-idea/SKILL.md`, `skills/pricing/SKILL.md` and `skills/mvp/SKILL.md` on raw.githubusercontent.com/slavingia/skills/main (all opened 21 Nov 2026).

- **Processize before you productize.** Deliver the value by hand and write one page: trigger, numbered steps, tools, time per customer, handoff. Automate only when four things hold: 10 or more paying customers served by hand, predictable requests, a page unchanged for weeks, and delivery taking more of your time than finding customers. Then automate one step at a time, starting with the step that costs the most time per customer. Application: where the platform already exists, name the customers a new feature or order type was done for by hand before building it.
- **Do not build if** nobody uses a workaround today, people must be taught they have the problem, you cannot name 10 specific people who have it, the only validation is friends finding it interesting, or you build for a community you do not belong to. People already paying for a worse solution is a green flag.
- **Scope test:** "Can I ship it in a weekend?" If not, reduce scope until you can.
- **Where the skills do not fit a two-sided local marketplace (ours).**
  - The pricing skill says "Charge something. Always." A marketplace can make one side free on purpose and charge the other; free sign-ups still say nothing about who will pay (`persuasion-and-offers.md`).
  - It says "Start low, raise over time." Raising a partner's agreed commission is a broken promise in a small town: start at the target rate, and put any launch rate in writing with its end date.
  - The mvp skill lists "Don't build a mobile app when a website works." Field staff and shop workers work on phones.

## Fried and Hansson on saying no (REWORK podcast, 37signals)

- **Say no by default.** In episode "Say No by Default" (16 Oct 2024) Jason Fried says "yes is very cheap now, but very expensive later". David Heinemeier Hansson adds that you do not get a great product by saying yes to everyone. For requests he suggests putting them in a pile and reading through it now and then. https://37signals.com/podcast/say-no-by-default/
- **Build half a product, not a half-assed product.** The episode (22 Feb 2022) is about cutting things before launch and starting at the epicenter. https://37signals.com/podcast/build-half-a-product-not-a-half-assed-project/
- Application: for a marketplace, say no to a feature until a named customer has asked for it twice; keep the cut list in writing.
- **Caveat (ours).** A software firm has near-zero cost per extra user. A two-sided marketplace needs density, field staff and cash handling that starting small does not cover.

## Keller on the one priority (The Tim Ferriss Show #401, 18 Dec 2019)

Source: Keller's own words in the free transcript, https://tim.blog/2019/12/18/gary-keller-transcript/ (opened 21 Nov 2026; first 100,000 characters read).

- **The focusing question:** "what's the one thing I can do such that by doing it everything is either easier or unnecessary?"
- **Chain it down.** Keller chained the question: first what single change would help the company grow, then who the one hire would be. Application: ask it for the year, the month, the week and today.
- **Time blocking.** "Time blocking is just this idea of you pre-planning your time." He suggests protecting two or three hours a day for core work, and says morning is the best time for it.
