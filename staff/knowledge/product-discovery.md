# Product discovery

How to decide what to build and test before building it, for a founder who is the product owner and has few users yet. Built only from Teresa Torres's free Product Talk articles, all opened 21 Nov 2026. Lines marked "Application" are our own reasoning, not hers. Rewritten 21 Nov 2026.

## Torres on continuous discovery (Product Talk articles)

- **Discovery is weekly and small.** The product trio (a product manager, a designer and an engineer) leads discovery together and interviews customers together. Torres argues for ongoing discovery over project-based interviews, and says good teams engage with customers at least weekly. https://www.producttalk.org/continuous-discovery/ Application: the founder holds all three roles and says so; the advisors stand in for the missing views.
- **One outcome per team.** "As a general rule, set one unique outcome for each team." A rich picture of the opportunities cannot be built for several outcomes at once. https://producttalk.org/2023/12/opportunity-solution-trees/
- **Outcome, opportunity, solution, test.** An opportunity solution tree has a desired outcome at the root, then opportunities, solutions and assumption tests (same page and https://www.producttalk.org/glossary-discovery-opportunity-solution-tree/).
- **An opportunity is a need, not a fix.** It is "an unmet customer need, pain point, or desire", drawn from customer stories in interviews and not from memory (OST page).
- **Compare and contrast.** Torres recommends considering more than one solution for the target opportunity, and notes that time can prevent it (OST page).
- **One small opportunity at a time.** "I encourage product teams to work on one small opportunity at a time" (OST page).
- **Interview every week, with stories.** "I recommend you interview at least one customer every week", and the habit matters more than the number of people. Ask for a story about a specific past event: "Tell me about the last time you ..." Asking what people typically do invites overestimation. Ask for a small amount of time, such as 20 minutes. https://www.producttalk.org/customer-interviews/
- **Write one snapshot per interview.** A snapshot is a one-page overview with a quote, quick facts, an experience map of the story and the opportunities heard. https://www.producttalk.org/2021/05/interview-snapshot/
- **Automate recruiting.** The aim is an interview on the calendar without weekly manual effort. Some teams set up triggers with customer-facing colleagues, who send interested customers to a scheduling link. Another page suggests inviting people at natural moments, such as after a transaction or a support contact. https://www.producttalk.org/glossary-discovery-automate-your-recruiting/ · https://www.producttalk.org/customer-interviews/ Application: whoever signs or onboards suppliers can recruit and book; the product person then runs the interview, apart from any pitch, and asks only about past events.
- **Few or no customers.** Torres says that without customers yet, you talk to prospects who match the ideal customer. He also says "You don't need access to customers" to start mapping hidden assumptions, which takes about 20 to 30 minutes. Interviews: customer-interviews page; assumptions: https://www.producttalk.org/getting-started-with-discovery/ Application: before launch, interview people who did the job your product does in the last week, and the suppliers on the other side.
- **Test assumptions, not ideas.** Assumptions fall into five categories: desirability, feasibility, usability, viability and ethical. Story mapping and a pre-mortem help find them. Map them by importance against evidence, test the riskiest, and "define success upfront, before you run your assumption test." https://www.producttalk.org/2021/08/assumption-testing/

## Dropped from this file

Further reading, paid and not used here: *Continuous Discovery Habits* (2021).

## Our caveat

Product Talk writes mostly for software teams with live users. For a two-sided marketplace with cash payment and low-trust sellers, treat its tests as signals. The data-analyst's sample-size rules do not apply to interview counts.
