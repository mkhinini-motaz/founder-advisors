# Tunisia: tax, social charges and reporting

General information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Check the official text and a licensed professional before relying on it.

**As of 30 Sept 2026.** Covers the Finance Laws for 2025 (LF 2025) and 2026 (LF 2026). The next Finance Law is voted in December and applies from 1 January: re-verify this whole file every January.

Items marked **(verify)** come from general practice or a single secondary source. Confirm them against the official text before relying on them. Everything else cites a source listed at the end.

## Corporate income tax (IS)

- **Standard rate 20%** on profits earned from 1 January 2024 (LF 2025 art. 37). [1]
- 10% for listed activities (agriculture, regional development, anti-pollution, crafts and others under the Code's art. 11 conditions). 40% for banks, financial institutions and insurers. 15% for payment institutions. [1] Some sectors pay 35%, such as telecoms, oil, car dealers and large retail. **(verify)** [2]
- **Pitfall:** the LF 2025 *draft* proposed turnover bands of 15% below 5M TND, 20% from 5M to 20M and 25% above. The adopted art. 37 sets a flat 20%. Many websites still quote the draft. [1][3]
- A minimum tax applies even when there is a loss or an exemption. Art. 37 sets it at 25% of profit before deductions for exempt entities, reduced to 10% for those at 20%. [1] There is also a turnover-based minimum. **(verify rate and floor)**
- **Social solidarity contribution (CSS)** on top of IS. Per Deloitte, LF 2026 sets 3% for companies taxed at 20% (minimum 400 TND) and 3% for those at 10% (minimum 200 TND). [4] Finco says CSS is 3–4% for 2025–2026, up from 1%. [5] **(verify)**
- Tax is prepaid in three instalments (acomptes provisionnels), each 30% of the prior year's tax, and the annual return is due around 25 March. **(verify dates)**
- A company with the **Startup Act label** is exempt from IS while it holds the label. See `tunisia-business-law.md`.

## Personal income tax (IRPP)

Scale in force since 1 January 2025 (LF 2025), on annual net taxable income: [3][6]

| Income band (TND) | Rate |
|---|---|
| 0 – 5,000 | 0% |
| 5,000 – 10,000 | 15% |
| 10,000 – 20,000 | 25% |
| 20,000 – 30,000 | 30% |
| 30,000 – 40,000 | 33% |
| 40,000 – 50,000 | 36% |
| 50,000 – 70,000 | 38% |
| above 70,000 | 40% |

Individuals also pay a CSS of 0.5% (LF 2026). [5]

## VAT (TVA)

- Rates: **19% standard, 13%, 7%.** [7] LF 2026 cut VAT on hybrid and electric vehicles to 7%. [7]
- Businesses under the real regime are VAT-registered. Individuals under the flat-rate regime (forfait) generally are not. [7]
- **Quote commissions with their VAT status.** An intermediary charges VAT on its commission: a 10% commission before VAT is 11.9% with VAT at 19%. A merchant told "10%, nothing else" who then sees 11.9% on the statement has been misled. Quote TTC, or say HT out loud.
- **Delivery fee:** if the platform sets the delivery fee and pays the rider independently of it, it is probably principal for delivery. The fee is then its revenue and bears VAT, while an auto-entrepreneur rider's fee carries no deductible VAT (about 0.48 TND of VAT on a 3 TND VAT-inclusive fee). This is a question for the expert-comptable.
- **Marketplace question:** a platform acting as an *intermediary* charges VAT on its commission and fees only. A platform acting as a *reseller* charges VAT on the whole sale and records the whole sale as revenue. This one choice drives VAT, revenue, audit thresholds and the withholding position. The expert-comptable decides it from the contracts and the money flows, not from a label.

## Withholding at source (retenue à la source)

The payer withholds, declares and pays the tax monthly, and gives the payee a certificate. Certificates go through the Ministry of Finance's TEJ platform. [8] Common rates for 2026: [8]

| Payment | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Amounts, VAT included, paid by delivery service providers to people who sell goods online, collected from the customers, when the seller shows no tax ID card** | **3%** (LF 2025 art. 68, Code IRPP/IS art. 52-I h; from 1 Jan 2025; no 1,000 TND threshold per note commune 6/2025) [15] |
| Fees (honoraires) and general non-commercial activities | 10% (3% for some cases) |
| Rents on buildings | 10% (hotels 5%) |
| Purchases of 1,000 TND or more, VAT included | 1–1.5% (0.5% if the supplier is taxed at 10%) |
| General commissions | 10% |
| Payments to non-residents for services or royalties | 15% (25% for privileged tax jurisdictions) |
| Salaries | IRPP scale, spread over the pay periods |

Whether the 3% base is the gross value of the goods or the payout net of commission is not settled by the text. Ask the expert-comptable.

The monthly return is filed by the **15th** for individuals, the **20th** for companies filing online, and the 28th for companies filing on paper, of the following month. [16]

**Certificates:** TEJ is the only channel from 1 Jan 2026. A certificate is due by the end of the month after the payment. A late or missing certificate costs 30% of the tax withheld, minimum **50 TND per certificate**. **(verify article)** [17]

**Penalties** (Code des droits et procédures fiscaux): late payment 0.5% per month or part month when paid spontaneously (art. 81); failing to withhold costs a fine equal to the tax not withheld, doubled on a repeat within 2 years (art. 83). [18]

## Electronic invoicing

**Status unsettled as of 30 Sept 2026. Plan for it, but do not tell anyone the fines apply today.**

- **The text:** LF 2026 art. 53 says that from **1 January 2026**, every VAT-registered person must issue invoices for **services** electronically, whatever the amount, the size of the business or the kind of service (LF 2026 art. 53). Invoices go through **TTN's El Fatoora platform** in TEIF XML format. [9][10]
- A paper invoice issued instead costs **100–500 TND per invoice, capped at 50,000 TND a year**. [9]
- Businesses exempt from VAT are outside the obligation. [9]
- **What happened since:** a Ministry of Finance note commune of 23 Jan 2026 limited the obligation in practice to service providers already on the TTN network. [19] On 2 Apr 2026 Parliament's finance commission voted to defer art. 53's application to a date set by a later law. Business News reads this as the obligation being deferred; Le Temps reads it as only the sanctions being deferred. [20] On 30 Sept 2026 a deputy said the file was still under discussion. [21] No published law (JORT) settling it was found. The 2027 Finance Law, voted in December 2026, is the likely place it gets settled.
- Setup means a TTN El Fatoora subscription and an electronic signature certificate. **(verify provider and cost)** A platform's commission invoice to its merchants is a service invoice.

## Cash payments

- **LF 2026 art. 54 repealed LF 2019 art. 45** from 1 January 2026. The 5,000 TND cash ceiling is gone, and so is the rule that made cash payments above it non-deductible. Cash is deductible when the operation is real and documented. [11][12]
- The administration says the general framework for rationalising cash still stands. Keep a receipt for every cash movement. [12]

## Social charges (CNSS)

- Private non-agricultural employees: **employer 16.57%, employee 9.18%**, plus 0.5% each for the job-loss insurance fund since 2025, giving 17.07% and 9.68%. [13]
- Employers also pay the vocational training tax (TFP, 1–2%) and FOPROLOS (1%). **(verify)**
- A majority gérant or a working associate without a salary falls under the non-salaried scheme, with a contribution set by income bracket. **(verify with the expert-comptable)**
- Auto-entrepreneurs pay their own flat contribution. See `tunisia-business-law.md`.
- Under the Startup Act the State pays the social contributions for a set period. See `tunisia-business-law.md`.

## Other levies

- Local tax on establishments (TCL) of about 0.2% of local turnover. **(verify)**
- Stamp duty per invoice. **(verify amount)**
- LF 2026: **2 TND per day per rented vehicle** for car rental companies, and a stamp duty of 1.5–2 TND per transaction for large retailers. [5]
- LF 2026: residents may open foreign-currency accounts without prior central bank approval. [4][5]

## Audit, books and filing

- **Statutory auditor (commissaire aux comptes).** A SARL, SUARL or other non-share company must appoint one once it exceeds **two of three limits**: total assets **100,000 TND**, revenue excluding tax **300,000 TND**, **10 employees** on average (loi 2005-96). [14] For a marketplace, whether revenue means commissions only or the whole sale matters here (see VAT above).
- The accounting standard is the Système comptable des entreprises (loi 96-112) and its NCT standards. Keep a general journal, a general ledger and an inventory book. Annual financial statements go to the national business register (RNE). **(verify deadline)**
- Keep records for 10 years. **(verify)**

## Calendar (verify each year)

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Monthly, by the 15th (individuals), 20th (companies online) or 28th (paper) | Withholding, VAT and TCL return and payment |
| Monthly, end of the following month | Withholding certificates on TEJ |
| Quarterly, within 15 days | CNSS returns; auto-entrepreneur single contribution |
| 28 June, 28 Sept, 28 Dec | IS instalments |
| 25 March | Annual IS return |
| Every invoice | El Fatoora, if VAT-registered and once the obligation is settled (see above) |
