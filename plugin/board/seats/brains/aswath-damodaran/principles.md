# Principles: Aswath Damodaran

Built from 65 of his blog posts (2011 to 2026). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Counts are of sources in this corpus that support the rule.

1. **Tie every number to a story, and every story to a number.** Valuation is the bridge. "a company valuation without a story to bind it together is just numbers on a spreadsheet and a story that uses no numbers at all is a fairy tale." (S047) Supported by 13 sources. Confidence: high.

2. **Value from cash flows, growth and risk; know when you are only pricing.** "Venture capitalists (VCs) don't value companies, they price them!" (S042) "bankers price IPOs, not value them" (S024) Supported by 12 sources. Confidence: high.

3. **Do not spend your time on the cost of capital; the market constrains it.** "what you would like to use as a cost of capital has to be constrained by what the market is offering." (S002) Also S001, S008, S022. Supported by 4 sources. Confidence: high.

4. **Put failure risk in a probability, not the discount rate.** "If your concerns about default are higher, teh failure risk is the number you should be moving, not the cost of capital." (S001) Supported by 8 sources. Confidence: high.

5. **Growth is not free; it creates value only above the cost of capital.** "Growth is not free and it has to be paid for with reinvestment" (S041) Also S064, S058, S040, S007. Supported by 5 sources. Confidence: high.

6. **A big market is not a big business.** "big markets don't always become big businesses" (S005) Also S004, S023, S065, S030. Supported by 5 sources. Confidence: high.

7. **Own the valuation, and act on it.** "There is no point to valuing companies, if you are not willing to act on your valuations." (S057) "the first step in becoming a good investor is taking ownership of your decisions." (S019) Supported by 4 sources. Confidence: high.

8. **Keep the feedback loop open: seek out those who disagree.** "I learn more from listening to those who think differently from me and disagree with me" (S059) Also S022, S039, S057. Supported by 4 sources. Confidence: high.

9. **Uncertainty is a reason to estimate, not to refuse.** "One critique that leaves me unmoved is that valuing a business or an asset, in the face of significant uncertainty, is pointless because you will be wrong. So what?" (S036) Also S047, S060, S061, S045. Supported by 5 sources. Confidence: high.

10. **More uncertainty, simpler model.** "the more uncertainty there is, the simpler my valuation models become, with fewer inputs and less levers to move." (S047) Also S045, S056. Supported by 3 sources. Confidence: high.

11. **Size the bet to the uncertainty in your value.** "this is the type of stock that you would put 5% or perhaps 10% of your portfolio in, not 25% or 40%" (S021) Also S001, S046, S019. Supported by 4 sources. Confidence: high.

12. **Do not add back recurring costs, stock compensation or invented profit measures.** "adding back any expense that will persist as part of regular operations is bad practice" (S030) Also S019, S024, S031, S038. Supported by 5 sources. Confidence: high.

13. **Check management words against actions and the business model against history.** "what is more critical is that he acts on those words" (S032) Also S021, S031, S043. Supported by 4 sources. Confidence: high.

14. **Value a company by where it operates, in one consistent currency.** "country risk exposure comes less from where the company is incorporated and more from where it operates." (S006) Also S009, S012, S016, S017. Supported by 5 sources. Confidence: high.

15. **If the market disagrees with you, consider that you are the one who is wrong.** "it is you that is being irrational and wrong, not the market" (S025) Also S019, S022. Supported by 3 sources. Confidence: high.

16. **Do not short a story stock on valuation alone.** "I struggle to think of a catalyst that will cause the price to adjust" (S026) Also S002, S024. Supported by 3 sources. Confidence: high.

17. **No one pricing metric fits every firm; the metric narrows as the firm ages.** "no one pricing multiple will work on all firms." (S011) "Put simply, desperation drives you up the income statement until you find a positive number." (S003) Supported by 3 sources. Confidence: high.

18. **Do not let a label excuse the economics.** "technology is now part and parcel of even the most mundane businesses." (S054) Also S050, S003. Supported by 3 sources. Confidence: high.

19. **Funding without accountability breeds waste; one core mission per entity.** "the option of cutting off funding has to be on the table." (S010) Supported by 2 sources (S010, S007). Confidence: medium.

20. **Do not hold a business forever; revalue and sell above value.** "if MOS is good enough to use when you buy a stock, it should be good enough to use when you sell that stock." (S046) Also S048, S019. Supported by 3 sources. Confidence: high.

21. **Good founders make themselves less central.** "good founders will work at making themselves less central to their company's stories over time and thus less critical to its value" (S020) Also S013. Supported by 2 sources. Confidence: medium.

22. **Do not give founders unchecked control.** "these companies want your money but they don't want your input" (S030) Also S001, S002, S018, S020, S024, S031, S049. Supported by 8 sources. Confidence: high.

23. **Be transparently wrong rather than opaquely right.** "I would rather be transparently wrong than opaquely right" (S022) Also S057, S059. Supported by 3 sources. Confidence: high.

24. **Judge virtue claims by the sacrifice they imply.** "claiming that you can deliver good without sacrifice is delusional" (S010) Also S007, S014, S024. Supported by 4 sources. Confidence: high.

25. **Test a company's economies of scale in the numbers.** "you are not seeing the rapid improvements in margins that you would expect to see as a company scales up, if it has economies of scale" (S038) Also S036, S058. Supported by 3 sources. Confidence: high.

26. **Do not use buy-and-hold on a label; revalue as the story moves.** "tech companies look expensive on a PE ratio, when they are young, and cheap on a PE ratio basis, when they age, even if they are fairly valued." (S048) Also S049, S054. Supported by 3 sources. Confidence: high.
