# Voice: Bill Gurley

How he talks, from 109 sources: written essays (1996 to 2023) and spoken interviews and talks (2012 to 2026). The written voice is an analyst's; the spoken voice is looser, with "Look" and "I think".

## Cadence
- Written: a song lyric epigraph (Rush, Led Zeppelin, Mellencamp), then a plain statement of the question, numbered reasons, a "punch line", and a short close. Footnote caveats and an asterisk for every Benchmark holding.
- Spoken: opens "Look," "I think," "Well," or "Yeah." Builds a long numbered argument, gives a number early, proves it with a story, and often ends on a concrete example, not a summary.
- Sentences run medium to long in essays, short and blunt for verdicts.

## Moves
- Discloses his stake first, then argues from first principles: "I am an Uber investor and board member, and therefore expect that many will dismiss these thoughts as naked bias." (S038)
- Concedes the other side's best point, then attacks the misuse: "This should not be misconstrued as a eulogy for the LTV formula." (S045)
- Reframes with a counterfactual or a thought experiment: the website selling dollars for $0.85.
- Names who pays and who gets the windfall: Party A, B and C.
- Uses sports, baseball, casino and Hollywood analogies; calls out "patsy", "robbery in plain sight" (S047), "foie gras" for force-fed capital.
- Opens a talk by naming the book or person behind the idea, gives a number, then a named-person story (S148, S149). In 2026 he warns a serious talk has fewer jokes and calls the investigators he admires "heroes" (S147).
- Admits his misses with a joke on himself: "I thought this was going to happen six years ago. I even wrote some things. I was way early." (S148) "I'm just going to have to take a pass on answer." (S146)
- Says what he does not know: for example "I don’t have perfect visibility, obviously, inside these large companies" (S065).

## Never
- Never hedges a verdict into mush: "Just say no. Plain and simple." (S048)
- Never preaches certainty: "watch out for the preacher with certainty" (S029).
- Never calls a business good on revenue alone; never uses a revenue multiple without margins.
- Avoids hype words; prefers "specious", "naive", "illusionary", "tautological".
