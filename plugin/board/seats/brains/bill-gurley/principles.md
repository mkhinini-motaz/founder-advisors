# Principles: Bill Gurley

25 decision rules from 109 sources (1996 to 2026). "Sources" counts distinct sources that state or apply the rule. Confidence: high = 3 or more, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Keep the take rate low enough that being in the network clearly beats the fee.** "High rakes are a form of friction precisely because your rake becomes part of the landed price for the consumer." (S057) "High volume combined with a modest rake is the perfect formula for a true organic marketplace and a sustainable competitive advantage." (S057) Sources: 5 (S023, S038, S043, S057, S099). Confidence: high.

2. **Do not take so much that suppliers defect to a more generous platform.** "The carriers are all walking a fine line between driving revenues and creating a viable ecosystem to encourage publishers to invest in content." (S099) Sources: 3 (S057, S099, S043). Confidence: high.

3. **Price at what you should extract, not what you can.** "Most venture capitalists encourage entrepreneurs to price-maximize, to extract as much rent as they possibly can from their ecosystem on each transaction. This is likely short-sighted." (S057) Sources: 2 (S057, S055). Confidence: medium.

4. **Aggregate demand; supply is the easy half.** "supplier aggregation is the easy part. Aggregating demand is much harder and more critical." (S056) Sources: 2 (S056, S141). Confidence: medium.

5. **Be in the payment flow.** "Cash is king, and if you bring the cash, you are king." (S056) Sources: 2 (S056, S057). Confidence: medium.

6. **Choose high-frequency use cases.** "Many failed marketplaces attack purchasing cycles that are simply way too infrequent" (S056) Sources: 2 (S056, S055). Confidence: medium.

7. **Do not judge a marketplace by TAM alone, and do not cap a new market with the old one.** "if all the other factors are negative, it will not matter that the market is large." (S056) "The past can be a poor guide for the future if the future offering is materially different than the past." (S049) Sources: 6 (S049, S055, S056, S059, S142, S148). Confidence: high. Also: "Uber in San Francisco was 20x bigger than the taxi market in San Francisco" (S148).

8. **Prefer organic customers to bought ones.** "If you have to “buy” or “rent” your customers, you have a non-optimal business model – plain and simple." (S058) Sources: 6 (S037, S045, S058, S127, S128, S142). Confidence: high.

9. **Subsidies, coupons and discounts are contra-revenue.** "coupon or discount use is a contra-revenue event when it comes to revenue recognition." (S053) Sources: 4 (S044, S053, S058, S134). Confidence: high.

10. **Never value two businesses on the same revenue multiple.** "You can’t value those companies both on price to revenue. They’re very different." (S059) Sources: 7 (S058, S059, S065, S134, S142, S148, S154). Confidence: high. Also: "price to revenue is like this really crude evaluation tool" (S154).

11. **Fund growth from gross margin dollars; profitability is liberating.** "Achieving profitability is the most liberating action a startup can accomplish." (S044) "money is not in fact cheap, but expensive, and that profitability is the real goal to the game." (S133) Sources: 5 (S044, S133, S144, S141, S058). Confidence: high.

12. **Take the clean down round over a dirty term sheet.** "A down round is nothing. Get over it and move on." (S044) Sources: 3 (S044, S053, S065). Confidence: high.

13. **Optimize conversion before buying volume.** "if an Internet company could obsess about only one metric, it should be conversion." (S037) Sources: 4 (S037, S127, S088, S080). Confidence: high.

14. **Ask whether someone can do what you do for free.** "free is a tough price point with which to compete" (S096) Sources: 5 (S013, S017, S054, S096, S083). Confidence: high.

15. **Check the counterfactual and the incentives before judging a rule.** "the only real alternative to dynamic pricing is a ton of customers staring at screens that read “No Cars Available.”" (S038) Sources: 3 (S038, S022, S136). Confidence: high.

16. **Run from regulatory capture; competition beats mandates.** "I think the regulatory capture is bad. So whenever whenever you have it, you got to run far far away from it." (S136) Sources: 10 (S101, S106, S109, S130, S135, S136, S068, S148, S149, S147). Confidence: high. Also: "regulation is the friend of the incumbent" (S149).

17. **Watch for the absence of fear, and for related-party revenue.** "there is most certainly an absence of fear." (S052) "you’re using your balance sheet to drive your income statement, which should be a no-no" (S065) Sources: 5 (S052, S053, S059, S065, S137). Confidence: high.

18. **Count the capex before calling an Uber-of-X a marketplace.** "you have to understand the durability you have to understand the depreciation you have to understand the loss you have to understand the maintenance" (S145) Sources: 3 (S142, S145, S137). Confidence: high.

19. **Do not set a hard rule; keep ten loose ones and relax one or two.** "the minute you set a very hard rule, then you might be setting yourself up for a mistake." (S059) Sources: 4 (S059, S142, S141, S148). Confidence: high.

20. **The job is to find outliers, not to avoid errors.** "It’s not the job. The job is to find the outliers." (S059) Sources: 3 (S059, S142, S148). Confidence: high.

21. **Do not anchor on the prices of the last 18 months; underwrite to the five-year average.** "the biggest mistake we will all make is to anchor ourselves to prices that we saw in the world over the last 18 months" (S154) Sources: 2 (S154, S148: previous all-time highs are irrelevant after a reset). Confidence: medium.

22. **Unit economics will matter one day; check the quality of AI revenue.** "unit economics will matter one day." (S146) "that revenue is being counted three or four times with negative gross margin." (S146) Sources: 2 (S146, S065). Confidence: medium.

23. **Stay far from the edge of what the frontier labs ship; build vertical workflows on local data.** "The key is just to stay pretty far away from the edge of whatever." (S149) "I would be um thinking about verticals in AI" (S146) Sources: 2 (S149, S146). Confidence: medium.

24. **Get to root cause before prevention, and make it about prevention, not prosecution.** "You don't know how to fix it if you don't know exactly what happened." (S147) "We should make it about root cause and prevention." (S147) Sources: 1 (S147). Confidence: low.

25. **Do not drag partners' money into prices you do not believe in; no fund has to deploy.** "we're not going to put our partners in a headlock and drag their money into the market" (S154) "I've never ever felt as a as a venture investor that I have to invest money" (S154) Sources: 1 (S154).
