# Stories: Bill Gurley

20 stories he reuses, built from 109 sources (1996 to 2026). Each carries the lesson and the source ids.

1. **Missing Google.** At about 25 employees, search looked unattractive and the price was high. He calls it the biggest mistake of his career, and it produced "What could go right?": a miss costs 10,000 times your money, a loss one times. (S059, S142, S148). In 2002 Larry asked 120 pre and the deal went at 80; "We should have laid chase, but we didn't." (S148)
2. **How to Miss by a Mile (Uber's market size, 2014).** A professor valued Uber using the taxi market as the ceiling. San Francisco Uber was already about 20x the taxi market. Forecasts built on the old market miss new ones (McKinsey's 900,000 cell subscribers against 109 million). (S049, S059, S142, S148)
3. **Booking.com vs Expedia.** A 10% agency model with better supplier cash flow beat 30%-plus merchant models and signed nearly every small European hotel. Low rake, supplier cash flow. (S057)
4. **oDesk cut its commission from 30% to 10%.** Current revenue fell to a third, and it became the leader by 2009. Cut the rake when it buys the lead. (S057)
5. **Taobao beat eBay in China** by launching free to use. Low or zero rake as an entry weapon. (S055, S057)
6. **Apple's 30% pushed Amazon and Facebook toward Android.** Demanding to be paid on both sides was overconfidence, though it worked a long time. (S057)
7. **Fab.com.** Four months after a $150 million raise, the CEO said they had spent $200 million without proving the model. Big rounds push proof into the future. (S053)
8. **Skype vs Vonage.** Skype got a user for $0.001 against Vonage's $400. Viral, organic growth beats LTV-justified spending. (S058, S045)
9. **OpenTable at the 2000 crash.** $100K a month revenue against $1.1 million a month burn and sales teams in 20 cities. A cut and refocus to cash-flow positive saved it; the TAM looked too small for years. (S141, S059, S056)
10. **The New Orleans nurse.** A single-mother nurse drives Fridays and Saturdays for supplemental income that shift work cannot give her. Flexible supply is the product. (S043, S138)
11. **Boston surge test.** Higher pay at 1am on weekends raised supply 70 to 80% and cut unfulfilled requests by two-thirds in two weeks. Test elasticity before arguing. (S038)
12. **Tropos and the municipal Wi-Fi fight.** Verizon and Comcast killed Philadelphia's plan, and AT&T helped outlaw municipal broadband in over 22 states. Incumbents write the laws. (S136, S106, S109, S135)
13. **Epic and the HITECH Act.** $44,000 per doctor to buy software, $17,000 more for meaningful use. His "10" in the Olympic regulatory-capture competition. (S136)
14. **The healthcare messaging startup.** It charged $50 a message to tell hospitals when the rehab reimbursement limit ran out, so it helped the hospital charge more. Follow the money flows. (S135)
15. **nCino's IPO.** A 195% day-one pop; the bank's own email showed the targets. Underpricing is by design. (S046, S047)
16. **Uber, Masa and the profit competed away.** He invested expecting winner-take-all, then SoftBank and Saudi money subsidized rivals for seven years; he sat at the board table deciding whether to run negative gross margin two more years while the other company raised another billion. Capital competes away profit. (S154, S146)
17. **Howard Marks and the sawtooth.** Marks questioned him for 15 minutes about venture and called it "a really shitty industry" (S154) because the structure builds in collapse. That produced the sawtooth idea; 2001 was abrupt, 2009 shorter. (S154, S148)
18. **Feynman, Challenger, the 737 Max and Surfside.** Searchers find root cause and blockers obstruct; Feynman dipped O-ring material in ice water on live television; the Surfside investigations changed condominium rules. His 2026 talk, a search for what caused COVID. (S147)
19. **Buffett and the zero-rate DCF.** He told Buffett at a 20-person fundraiser that a DCF fails when entry rates are zero; Buffett said "you betcha". Zero rates postponed the correction. (S146)
20. **OpenTable's CFO and the 17 percent cap.** The CFO froze penetration at 17 percent; Gurley said the network would reach 99 and the CFO quit. A single salesperson in San Francisco at 90 percent penetration closed 35 restaurants in a month against a plan of four. Build liquidity city by city. (S148)
