# Frameworks: Katy Milkman

Built from 30 sources (2021 to 2026), standard depth. Most-used first. Scoring lines are guidance for the council, not her thresholds, unless a number is quoted. Study sizes differ between tellings; where they do, both are recorded with ids.

## 1. The barrier map (diagnose, then match the tool)
Seven internal barriers: getting started, impulsivity (present bias), procrastination, forgetting, laziness (path of least resistance), lack of confidence, conformity. She says they come from the field (about 180 scientists in the Behavior Change for Good network, S001, S032), not from her alone. Method is medical: name the barrier in this context, then pick the tool. Matches: starting, fresh start; impulsivity, fun route and bundling; procrastination, commitment device and bite-size goals; forgetting, planning prompts and reminders; laziness, defaults and habits; confidence, advice giving and growth mindset; conformity, peers and copy and paste. A person can have several barriers, and one person has different barriers per goal. She says a premortem or a few direct questions is enough to find it, no large survey (S010, S023).
- Score (guidance): can you name the barrier in one sentence in the user's words, and does the chosen tool address that barrier and no other? A tool pulled from a bag of tricks scores zero (S023).
- Quote: "we need to understand it to treat it properly." (S001)
- Used in more than 20 sources, among them S001, S002, S004, S006, S007, S008, S010, S011, S013, S016, S017, S018, S023, S024, S027, S028, S029, S030, S032, S034, S035, S036, S044.

## 2. Fresh start effect (temporal landmarks)
People treat life as chapters. A new week, month, birthday, new job or move makes them step back, think big picture, and feel like a new self, so past failures belong to the old one. It raises goal-setting, gym visits and diet searches. It fixes only the getting-started barrier. Born from a Google HR leader's question about the best time to nudge change (S001, S006, S007, S008, S013, S016, S018, S023, S024, S030, S035, S036). Labelling a date works: invitations to save "after your birthday" or on the "first day of spring" raised saving about 20 to 30 percent over nine months (S008, S016, S020, S026), about 30 percent (S007, S032); S007 puts it at retirement saving. Use for one-and-done acts (401k, colonoscopy booking). She also says a Monday works as a cheap one (S010).
- Limits: it cannot be invented ("we don't see much evidence that you can just invent them.", S020; a "hundredth day of the year" fell flat, S001, S036). It can hurt people on a roll: baseball players traded across leagues after a good season got worse, weak ones got better (S002, S008, S011, S013, S017). It fades, often by mid January (S010, S028).
- Score (guidance): is the date meaningful to this person, is the target a one-off or a long effort, is he currently failing or winning? Failing plus one-off: strong. Winning: protect the routine instead.
- Quote: "all a fresh start is, is motivation to begin." (S001)
- Used in more than 20 sources (S001, S002, S006, S007, S008, S010, S011, S013, S016, S017, S018, S020, S023, S024, S026, S028, S029, S030, S032, S035, S036, S046).

## 3. Fun route over efficient route (the Mary Poppins effect)
Most people choose the most efficient path (the StairMaster) and quit; a minority choose the enjoyable one (Zumba with a friend) and persist (Fishbach and Woolley). Make the good thing rewarding now, because people overweight the instant. Levers differ by person: social, bundling, games, music. She calls it her "greatest hit" (S027).
- Score (guidance): would the person do it a second time, unprompted? If the honest answer is no, redesign before adding any other tool.
- Quote: "If it is painful to pursue your goal, you will quit." (S036)
- Used in S001, S002, S004, S006, S007, S008, S011, S017, S018, S020, S023, S024, S026, S027, S028, S029, S030, S035, S036.

## 4. Temptation bundling
Allow a craved pleasure only while doing the chore (audiobooks only at the gym; a coffee only on the way to the library). It works through complementarity: two things better together, and a bad pairing fails (S020). Original gym study: the bundled group went about 50 percent more at first (S032, S034), 56 percent more in her other telling (S001, S036); the effect vanished when the gym closed for Thanksgiving (S032). It also raised math persistence in children with snacks and music (S002, S008, S046). She keeps her own bundle "forever" and says to keep cheap crutches (S027). It does nothing when the barrier is forgetting or confidence (S008, S028).
- Score (guidance): is there a pleasure the person already craves, only available during the chore, and complementary to it?
- Quote: "I only let myself enjoy indulgent entertainment while I was exercising at the gym" (S007)
- Used in S001, S002, S004, S007, S008, S010, S011, S016, S017, S020, S023, S024, S026, S027, S028, S029, S030, S032, S034, S035, S036, S046.

## 5. Commitment devices (hard and soft)
A tool that restricts your future self. Ladder: a hard lock (a savings account you cannot touch), a cash forfeit with a referee (stickK, Beeminder; money goes to a cause you dislike), a soft pledge (public, tied to identity, tracked). Evidence: Philippines locked savings, about 30 percent took it and savers saved about 80 percent more over the year (S002, S006, S007, S013, S016; she was not part of it, S013); smokers with an optional deposit quit about 30 percent more (S001, S013, S016, S024, S027, S036, S044, S046), which she puts at roughly 10 versus 13 percent in absolute terms (S028); a doctors' antibiotic pledge posted in waiting rooms (S002, S026). Hard beats soft; keep it bite-size and near-term, with a hatch for a real disaster (S044). Use it when the goal is important enough (S028). Poor fit when the person lives paycheck to paycheck or the goal is financial (S046). Underused because it sounds odd (S028).
- Score (guidance): does the penalty hurt, is a referee named, is the cycle daily or weekly, and can the person bear losing it?
- Quote: "so you're basically increasing the price of your vice" (S016)
- Used in S001, S002, S004, S006, S007, S013, S016, S023, S024, S026, S028, S030, S034, S036, S044, S046.

## 6. Planning prompts and implementation intentions
Say when, where and how, tied to a cue (a time or an event). It works because memory stores through cues, it turns a vague wish into a commitment, and it shows obstacles. Flu-shot mailer asking people to pick a date and time: about 4 percentage points more vaccination at near zero cost (S032, S035). Better for starting a behaviour than for stopping one (S006). Too many plans overwhelm (S007).
- Score (guidance): are day, time, place and route written down, and is the cue something that will actually happen?
- Quote: "when will you do it where will you do it how will you get there" (S023)
- Used in S001, S002, S006, S007, S010, S016, S023, S030, S032, S034, S035, S044.

## 7. Defaults, friction and laziness as a feature
Laziness is the path of least resistance and a feature: make the good thing the default and put distance in front of the bad one (auto-deduct to savings, home page, pantry, workout gear at home). 401k default enrolment: about 40 percent more employees saving (S023, S034), about 30 points (S030). Set it up once in a motivated moment (S018). Defaults work for money, not food ("there is no autopilot that works the same way for eating", S018), and social defaults beat personal ones (S044). Think of subtracting as well as adding (S030).
- Score (guidance): what happens if the person does nothing at all? If the answer is the goal, the design is done.
- Quote: "the best algorithms are lazy they hunt for efficient Solutions and the human operating system is the same" (S030)
- Used in S001, S004, S008, S016, S017, S018, S023, S029, S030, S034, S035, S036, S044, S046.

## 8. Elastic habits (flexible, with a fallback)
Rewarding a fixed time of day built brittle habits. At Google, a group coached to go at the same time (85 percent at the planned time) did worse after the rewards stopped than a group that went flexibly (about half at the planned time). Study size differs: about 200 Google employees in S024, about 2,500 in S027 and S016. Plan a first-best time and a backup. She says she would have got this wrong five years earlier (S016).
- Score (guidance): does the routine have a second and third window, and has it been practised "under duress"?
- Quote: "we made them rigid not routine but rigid" (S027)
- Used in S007, S016, S024, S027.

## 9. Emergency reserves (mulligans)
Keep the ambitious goal and add a small number of free passes (Marissa Sharif). Seven days with two reserves beat both a plain seven-day goal and an easy five-day goal, about twice as successful (S001, S007, S011, S020, S035, S036). It counters the what-the-hell effect. She notes it might invite misbehaviour in some settings (S020).
- Score (guidance): does the goal tolerate a slip, and is the number of passes limited and visible?
- Quote: "they achieve far more than if you give them the goal of doing it just five days a week" (S011)
- Used in S001, S007, S011, S020, S035, S036.

## 10. Advice giving (ask them to advise someone else)
Do not give advice to a struggling person; ask for theirs, or have them coach someone further behind (Lauren Eskreis-Winkler). Three reasons: it signals respect, brings insight, and creates a pull to follow your own advice. High-school study: about 2,000 students, 10 minutes of advice, about one point on a 50 to 100 grade scale (S007, S032, S035); other tellings say about 1,000 (S016) or eight minutes (S001, S046). The effect is small ("we weren't turning C students into validictorians.", S024) and helps confidence problems, not knowledge gaps (S016). Her own "advice club" with peers is a version of it.
- Score (guidance): is the barrier confidence, and is there someone the person can advise?
- Quote: "when we invite someone to advise others it actually improves their own motivation and performance" (S016)
- Used in S001, S002, S006, S007, S011, S016, S018, S023, S024, S028, S029, S032, S034, S035, S036, S046.

## 11. Peers within reach, and copy and paste
Peers move behaviour through norms and information (roommate studies). The gap must be small: the Air Force Academy re-pairing of weak and strong cadets backfired (S002, S026, S028, S032). Choose peers a little ahead; deliberately copy a successful person's tactics, which beat absorbing them by osmosis (S001, S016, S017, S026, S027, S036). A social norm must be reachable (S013), and a norm message can backfire: her own 401k "everyone is saving" test lowered enrolment, cause unproven (S013).
- Score (guidance): can the person see himself reaching this peer's level within weeks, not years?
- Quote: "except they have to be within reach" (S028)
- Used in S001, S002, S007, S013, S016, S017, S026, S028, S029, S032.

## 12. Megastudy
One very large field experiment with many sub-experiments by different scientists, run at once on one outcome, with a placebo and a best-practice arm. Gains: apples to apples, shared fixed cost, no file drawer, faster discovery (S014, S018, S026, S032, S040, S045). 24 Hour Fitness: about 63,000 members, 30 scientists, 53 treatments (S014, S040, S045; S018 says 54 programs), 28 days; about 45 percent beat placebo, only 9 percent beat the best-practice arm. Best arm: a 9-cent bonus for returning after one missed visit, plus 27 percent (S014, S040, S045). Rising majority norm: about 24 percent more (S014, S045). Experts could not predict results; the winner is overestimated (winner's curse). Cost about one million dollars (S045). Needs big samples; does not replace regular research (S014).
- Score (guidance): do you have enough users per arm to detect an effect, and a benchmark arm built from known best practice?
- Quote: "a mega study is a very large field experiment with many smaller sub-experiments embedded in it synchronously run with the same dependent variable." (S045)
- Used in S014, S018, S026, S032, S040, S045.

## 13. Test, do not predict
Run an A/B test even on a proven idea, because the diagnosis can be wrong; she says leaders under-test. With a team of five it will not help (S023). Public-health professors, practitioners and online workers had no predictive power (S014, S040, S045), and her own favourite joke text flopped (S014, S040, S045).
- Score (guidance): is the sample big enough, is the outcome objective, is there a control?
- Quote: "predictions were awful" (S040)
- Used in S013, S014, S023, S040, S045.

## 14. Ownership wording and repeated reminders
Flu and COVID texts saying the shot is "reserved for you" or "waiting for you" beat every other wording in health-system and pharmacy tests (about 10 percent lift; S014, S017, S032, S035, S040, S045). Likely ingredients: implied endorsement, less hassle, endowment, the reminder itself (S017). Several messages beat one, and formal wording that matches what the sender normally sounds like beats jokes (S014, S035, S040, S045). Sample sizes: about 50,000 at Penn Medicine and Geisinger and about 700,000 at Walmart (S014, S045), 734,000 in S040.
- Score (guidance): does the message sound like the sender, name something held for the person, and arrive more than once?
- Quote: "reminders that used ownership language, like the waiting for you, reserve for you, claim your vaccine, they were better than others." (S014)
- Used in S014, S017, S032, S035, S040, S045.

## 15. Bite-size goals (temporal reframing)
Turn a yearly goal into a weekly one. Crisis Text Line volunteers told four hours a week instead of 200 hours a year volunteered about 8 percent more (S004, S010, S030, S032). Stretch a little, not a lot (S001, S016).
- Score (guidance): does the person see progress inside a week?
- Quote: "reframing those goals led to about an 8% increase in volunteering over and above what they were achieving normally." (S004)
- Used in S001, S004, S010, S016, S024, S030, S032.

## 16. Process nudges, transparency and choice
Process nudges (planning prompts) help whatever the goal; outcome nudges (social norms) need agreement on direction; boosts teach a tool. Be clear that you are guiding, and keep the choice open (S032). She sees system change and individual nudges as complements (S014, S020).
- Score (guidance): can the user say no easily, and would the nudge be defended in public?
- Quote: "I think process nudges are the ones we should be advancing the most." (S032)
- Used in S014, S020, S032.

## 17. Structure over awareness (gateways and pathways)
Replace gut calls on casual interactions with a rule. A gateway is a formal decision (hire, promotion); a pathway is casual (who gets a reply). Hire in sets, not one at a time: people choose more diverse hires when choosing five at once (S004, S010). Her 3,000-person test of diversity training moved attitudes, not behaviour (S020); she calls online diversity training weak (S004, S007).
- Score (guidance): is the decision now made by a procedure, and who audits it?
- Quote: "I come down from the work I've done pretty, pretty strongly that I think structural change is more impactful than individual change." (S020)
- Used in S004, S007, S010, S020.

## 18. Growth mindset and belief
A setback is feedback, not a verdict. Belief changes results: hotel housekeepers told their work was exercise lost weight (S001, S026, S036). Mindset is quick to shift but behaviour is slower (S011). Effects are small and she says so.
- Score (guidance): after a miss, does the person read it as information?
- Quote: "you need that placebo effect just to believe something different is going to be the outcome." (S001)
- Used in S001, S007, S008, S011, S026, S030, S036.

## Books

### Books: Katy Milkman

Built from 30 sources (2021 to 2026). Book text is not reproduced; theses are in my words.

#### How to Change: The Science of Getting from Where You Are to Where You Want to Be (2021)
- Launched 4 May 2021 (S040). She calls it "my life's work" (S029, 2021).
- Thesis: people stall on predictable internal barriers, and science has a tested tool for each, so you diagnose the barrier and match the tool instead of using one trick. Each chapter takes one barrier: getting started (fresh starts), impulsivity (fun route, temptation bundling, commitment devices), procrastination, forgetting (planning prompts), laziness (defaults, habits), confidence (advice giving, mindset), conformity (peers, copy and paste), then change that lasts (S008, S013, S017, S023, S024, S035, S044).
- Opens with Agassi and Brad Gilbert (S008, S013, S018, S024). The barrier structure arrived late in the writing (S027). She says it is by a scientist and not only about habits (S024), and that it will not turn a person "into a wunderkind" (S026). Bullet takeaways were added on a colleague's advice (S027).
- Taught in S001, S002, S004, S006 to S008, S010, S011, S013, S016 to S018, S020, S023, S024, S026 to S030, S032, S034 to S036, S044, S046.
