# Stories: Tony Xu

Built from 45 public sources, 2016 to 2026.

1. Palo Alto Delivery. A static page of eight PDF menus, a Google Voice number ringing the four founders, Find My Friends to track drivers. Ten orders a day, a high of 21; the first order came within about 45 minutes. Lesson: prove demand by hand before building. (S006, S053, S066, S080, S085)
2. The macaroon shop. A one-person Palo Alto baker showed a binder of refused delivery orders (told in the 2015-16 talk as a bakery manager's clipboard: "I didn't we didn't open this store 20 years ago because we love delivery" (S085)). Lesson: the unmet need is visible in what merchants turn away, and no merchant starts a business for delivery. (S002, S006, S040, S066, S085)
3. The Stanford football night, September 2013. About 100 orders, all late. The refund cost over 40% of the bank account and the founders decided in seconds, then baked cookies and delivered them at 5 a.m. Lesson: customer obsession; trust is lost on one order. (S002, S006, S040, S052, S055, S066, S074, S090)
4. Founders delivered every order for the first six months to a year and a half, and learned a delivery has about 20 steps. He drove for Domino's two weeks and saw that a single store always has too many or too few drivers. Lesson: expertise comes from doing. (S001, S036, S042, S052, S066, S078, S083, S085)
5. Series D, March 2018. Both the Sequoia and SoftBank checks, $535 million total. After it, about a dozen cities a month became 3,000 cities. Lesson: take the money when you hold the best product. (S002, S038)
6. Over a thousand days without a raise, 2016 to 2018. A quarter of staff left (20 to 25%, about 80% stayed); he showed the falling cash balance every week; order growth without marketing kept the rest. Lesson: retention data keeps you honest. (S002, S038, S040, S066, S074, S079)
7. The driver test, Palo Alto 2013. With $25 an hour offered, 1 of 40 drivers switched between UberX and DoorDash. Lesson: the two groups were different people. (S006, S053, S066, S079)
8. The first COO. Christopher Payne drove deliveries with his son and wrote a 2,000 word email about the algorithm (one source says 30,000 words). A CFO candidate brought a 10 MB financial model to a coffee chat. Lesson: judge by action unprompted. (S006, S038, S066, S079)
9. Pandemic response. Commissions cut in half (over $100 million) against his management team, plus ads naming competitors, and the IPO plan thrown out. Lesson: pay for the right side of history. (S002, S011, S040, S075, S084, S090)
10. DoorDash Drive. Eighteen months of zeros, and the team was promoted for milestones. Restaurants and Walmart grocery later ran on it. Lesson: protect new projects from the core scoreboard. (S038, S076)
11. The YC application forecast hundreds of millions of revenue (a source says $100 million) from counting Palo Altos, and was wrong by orders of magnitude. Lesson: early size estimates are too small. (S071)
12. Alcohol delivery, tested for years first, then rolled out with ID checks at the door. Lesson: earn regulated categories. (S046, S075)
13. His mother ran small restaurants; he washed dishes from age nine and mowed lawns. Lesson: the mission is local owners, for whom the business "is life". (S001, S036, S042, S043, S066, S071, S072, S077, S080, S083, S084)
14. Second market chosen as East San Jose because its household income matched the national average. Lesson: test where the economics are normal. (S040, S080)
15. The toner raids, summer 2013. He ran off 110,000 flyers on campus printers at night because a print shop would have cost $33,000 against $18,000 in the bank. Lesson: be resourceful and a learn-it-all. (S072)
16. The Cheesecake Factory at Macy's Union Square. Dedicated parking, a staff elevator, three or four stations to collate. Lesson: one order, one restaurant, hundreds of details. (S077, S080)
17. Taco Bell, July 2015. He doubted anyone would pay a delivery premium, and was wrong. Lesson: he admits his guesses on demand failed. (S071)
18. The In-N-Out test. DoorDash listed and delivered In-N-Out as a test, told them, they declined, and DoorDash stopped. Lesson: test with a merchant in the open and stop when asked. (S083)
19. The lawyer who ran customer service, then HR, then business development. Lesson: a beginner's eye plus first principles. (S072)
20. Domino's, 2013. Drivers checked in and out on a paper clipboard; mobile gave a "why now". Lesson: find what changed. (S079)
