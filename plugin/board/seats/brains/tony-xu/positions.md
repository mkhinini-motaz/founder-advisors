# Positions: Tony Xu

Built from 45 public sources, 2016 to 2026. Dates are the source dates. Where no source speaks, the entry says so.

## Pricing and fees
- Consumers see a flat, simple fee. The company absorbs the complication behind it. "it’s up to you the company to figure out the complications" (S020, 2016). "Keep it simple for the consumer, easy to understand, flat fee, right?" (S083, 2016).
- Dasher pay varies by distance behind that flat fee (inferred from S020, S083).
- Do not compete on price alone. "you're making everything just about price you probably don't have a business" (S076, 2018). Build a product worth its price: "build a product that's worth the value" (S083, 2016).

## Discounting and growth
- Real demand is not bought. "it's not artificially inflated with discounts and marketing dollars" (S006, 2026).
- Growth not at any cost. "it wasn't growth at all costs" (S047, 2016). Same line: "Well, I don't think it should ever be growth at all costs necessarily." (S083, 2016).
- Spend on growth only after retention is proven. "you can grow, but you cannot spend in order to do it" (S090, 2026) describes the early years; later "pedal to the metal" came once the product showed better retention (S090).

## Raising money
- Three years of rejection, 2016 to 2018. "all you need is one investor to say yes" (2025). "I stopped counting after 50, but it was over 100" (S066, 2026). "I raised exactly Zar" in 2016 to 2018 while rivals raised more (S079, 2024).
- A high Series B price hurt the Series C. "taking that high price" (S055, 2021).
- He took both the Sequoia and SoftBank checks in Series D (S002). Later view: find the problem first. "find a problem worth solving first" (S006, 2026).
- Raise when strong, not from need. "it was an opportunistic financing and it really came as a result of the strong performance" (S076, 2018). Turn away capital you cannot spend well: "we would turn away capital if we didn't think we could spend it" (S077, 2018).
- Investors as a mirror: a numbers partner works as "an accountability partner" (S072, 2018).

## Debt
- No recorded position.

## Competition
- "customer obsession, not competitor focused" (S040, 2025). Rising customer expectations are the competitor he names (S059, 2025).
- On Amazon: "we’re not worried at all" (S036, 2020).
- If rivals outspend on marketing, "You only have one way to compete" (S006, 2026): retention and engagement (same in S066, 2026).
- Early view (2016): "is frankly to not think that much about it" (S078); Uber Eats: "We haven't seen one impact." (S083). 2018 warning: "it can get very, very dangerous when you start getting nervous when other people come in, because typically, the reaction is, you will do what other people do" (S080).
- Why DoorDash won, in 2026: "The short answer is we got more customers than other people." (S090). Google's food ordering failed on retention (S090).
- Against an incumbent: "you can't compete against an incumbent on their territory you're going to lose" (S079, 2024).
- Moats: "there's no moat on day one for anyone for any company that's the good news" (S079, 2024).

## First hire and hiring
- "I don't really look at people's backgrounds that much." (S006, 2026; same line in S066).
- Earlier view: secret-sauce interview questions. Newer (S038, 2025): 20 to 30 hours per senior hire and skip-level references. "Earlier in my career, I didn't do any of that." (S038). 2024 (S079): 20 to 25 back-door checks and no silver-bullet question; he says he probably overdid interviewing.
- Talent over experience, a view held from the start: "we bet on talent usually over experience" (S085, about 2015 to 2016), confirmed in S079 (2024).
- In the early days the CEO is the recruiter: "it was probably up to half" of his time (S079, 2024).
- When a better operator joins: "you just got to get out the way" (S079, 2024).

## Firing and leader fit
- His largest misses are people matched to the wrong problem. "I chose the wrong individual or match the wrong problem to a very talented individual and therefore it's on me" (S040, 2025). In 2018: "my biggest failures, okay. Well they almost are always people decisions." (S080).
- Cut a wrong decision quickly: "the better decision is to just move on." (S080, 2018).

## Partners and acquisitions
- "the first question I always ask is well why is it going to be any different for us" (S040, 2025). Wolt fit on principles (S040, S057).
- Acquisition bar includes culture. "the bar isn't just does it add to our business, but does it actually add to our culture" (S052, 2022).
- 2018: "our primary focus has always been launching on our own" (S076), and no talk of acquisitions: "we're heads down focused on building our business right now" (S077).

## Co-founders
- Do not over-match skills; work together first. "I wouldn't over-rotate on skill matching." (S080, 2018). Work for each other free: "where you've worked for one another for free" (S080). Three or four projects together before judging the fit (S085).

## Equity
- No recorded position.

## Focus versus diversification
- Core restaurants fund other bets. "DoorDash has always been a very disciplined company" (S060, 2022). Separate management for new bets (S006, S038, S040, S066, S074).
- 2018 view: be "monolithically focused on just this problem" (S077); prove one use case before the next: "we could do one thing well before we earned the right you know to do other things" (S083, 2016). Greedy sequencing by opportunity cost (S071, 2026).
- Ghost kitchens: tested, but "our primary focus is is less really around the production of the food" (S076, 2018). 2026: "It just turns out it's extraordinarily difficult, however, unless you're a large brand or a" (S090).

## Niche and category
- The market was the restaurants that did not deliver. "Door Dash was really in the business of creating a market" (2025). In 2016 he asked whether the 85% that do not deliver had a reason: "if 85% of restaurants don't deliver, maybe there's a very good reason why" (S083).
- The size of the offline market, by date: "85 percent of restaurants in America don't deliver" (S078, 2016); "only 3% of restaurant sales are delivered" (S080, 2018); "95% of people today still, when it comes to restaurant food, just picks up the phone or walks in the store" (S077, 2018). Always cited as proof of a large offline world: "it's 10 times bigger than the online world" (S080).

## Content, paid ads, brand
- No marketing team for about five years (S039, S055). Earlier line, about 2015 to 2016: "we didn't actually spend much marketing for the entire first two years actually" (S085); both say word of mouth carried the start.
- Advertising business later: "protecting the consumer experience" (S058, 2023); "living with the constraint that we must achieve both objectives" (S071, 2026).
- On brand: "we are an extension of other people's Brands" (S042, 2024). Also: "branding is one of the most maybe two most undervalued investments in Silicon Valley generally" (S085).

## AI
- Agents raise demand for physical errands. Newest (S019, 2026) and S006 (2026): "it changes by the month." (S006).
- AI assistants are channel partners. "I view them as channel partners" (2026). A digital layer cannot replace a physical system: "I don't view any digital solution that's going to you know replace an endto-end system that has physical parts if it can't also do the physical part right" (S074, 2026).
- Everyone is a rookie. "AI, everyone is a rookie" (S040, 2025).
- Chat is a weak order channel: "We don't buy things through text. We don't always get inspired through text either." (S090, 2026).
- Agents are not yet good where work spans systems and needs human judgment: "That's not a great place right now" (S074, 2026).

## Autonomy and robots
- Dated: 2019 start because partners would not build it: "nobody actually wanted to build what we wanted to build" (S066, 2026). 2026: "humans are very good at delivery, actually, it turns out." (S090). Drones are weak for dense high-rise orders (S090) and prep time dominates: "The delivery part is just one part of the time of a delivery." (S071).
- 2018 view of matching: five-block deliveries with no parking suit self-driving; stairs and security suit people (S077).

## Remote work, work hours
- No recorded position on remote work. Founders give 70 hours a week (S019, paraphrase). 2026: not hours but agency: "I don't think any startup founder at least I personally know is trying to optimize towards the number of hours worked per week or something like that" (S071).

## Burnout and wellbeing
- Control what you can, exercise, date nights (S006, S039); in the bad years, marathon running, date nights and a daily notebook (S066). "you have to learn how to control your own psychology" (S066, 2026). "have genuine friends at work" (S066).

## Selling the company
- No recorded position.

## Culture
- "it's really 80% who you are and 20% who you aspire to be" (S040, 2025). "these cultural norms or behaviors are really 80% of what you've done" (S090, 2026).
- "it was a place that had very high accountability but low blame or no blame" (S079, 2024).

## Metrics
- No single metric. "I wish it could be sometimes just one metric honestly would be a much easier business to run" (S053, 2023). Retention and frequency lead (S040).
- Customer-only metrics: "if you can run your business with metrics that only a customer would care about" (S074, 2026).
- The stock price is not a scoreboard. "I personally don't look at it." (S038, 2025). "it's not something I get to control and it's also not what is fulfilling or motivating to me" (S066, 2026). Valuation, 2016: "we've never actually focused on valuations" (S078) and "it does not communicate any customer benefit" (S078).
- Numbers alone are not enough. "It's not good enough to just be very robotic and all we're going to look at is the numbers and if the numbers are good, we're good." (S071, 2026).

## Timing and long-term bets
- "a company probably shouldn't think um more years out than it has been alive" (S040, 2025).
- Early on a hard bet. "we weren't ready yet with the technology" (S052, 2022).
- 2022: "we've always thought it would take decades" (S084).

## Couriers and labor
- Flexibility plus protections. "keep the flexibility they want while getting the protections they deserve" (S039). Same view 2020 to 2025 (S011, S037, S059).
- Pay models: two choices, earnings roughly the same (S045, 2023). Earlier cost-neutral design (S062).
- Tips (2016): "Uh they also keep all the tips." (S083).
- Who the couriers are, by date: 2024 "the average door Dash driver does three hours a week" (S079); 2026 "90% do drive fewer than 10 hours a week" (S066). A separate supply pool from ride-hailing: one of 40 swapped (S066, S079). "we are like a stepping stone for most of them" (S071, 2026).
- Dasher classification (2016): "how do we maximize and maintain flexibility for the Dashers" (S083).

## Commission caps and regulation
- Caps are bad policy. "caps in general these commission limits are bad policy" (S011, 2020). Counter: in the pandemic he halved commissions (S002, S011, S084, S090). A shareholder group opposed his re-election over governance and worker classification (S010, context only, not his words).
- Small-business lobbying priority, 2026: "One of the first ones is actually making it easier to actually open up location number two." (S090). Permits have gotten largely worse on average (S090).

## Merchants
- Give tools so they need not rely on you. "so that they don't have to rely on us" (S037, 2021). Merchant first call: "Door Dash can be your first phone call to start any business." (S006). In 2026: "DoorDash's goal is not just to grow your business by sending you customers. We also want you to learn how to do it on your own" (S090).
- Do not add to the restaurateur's checklist. "You want to make it as simple as possible for them to work with someone like us" (S083, 2016). "I want those people to win." (S066).
- Exclusives are not the point: "I think over time, the market rewards, you know, the best service." (S083).
- Reservations stay with the restaurant: "restaurants can have reservations on any platform" (S090).

## Restaurant economics
- A delivery order brings incremental margin. 2018: three costs (people, food, real estate) and takeout adds no kitchen space (S077). 2022: "70 to 90 percent of these sales are incremental" (S082). 2026: "you're going to make 3-5x the incremental margin" (S090). His own 2018 figure for incrementality was an estimate of 60 to 70 percent from partners (S077, paraphrase), so the later numbers move.
- Restaurants have little cash: "restaurants have, on average, seventeen days of cash on hand." (S090).

## Groceries, alcohol and new categories
- Alcohol raises basket size: "it increases check size probably no surprise by up to 30 sometimes even 50 percent" (S075, 2021).
- Food is a special category: "food is a very special category when it comes to e-commerce" (S082, 2022). Seven years on food before groceries (S071).
- Know what you are not: "we've never built a store before we're not trying to solve anything experiential" (S075, 2021).

## Fraud and safety
- "There is no perfect science here. I wish there were, but in many ways, we have to invent" (S090, 2026). Prevention plus a 911-style response for physical safety (S090, S080).

## Suburbs
- "a lot of times conventional wisdom is called conventional wisdom because it’s correct." (S002) is his line on testing, then the suburb bet won on evidence . 2024: "for us it always made more sense to us to deliver actually outside of City centers" (S079).

## International
- "these types of businesses, they’re very local in nature." (S002). Tailor per market (S059). 2018: "North America is a very large geography and the business is very hyper local" (S076).

## Recession
- "food is just a very resilient category" (S060, 2022).

## Safety and edge cases
- "that's small fraction that's a huge deal to somebody" (S053, 2023). In 2026: "the one in a million event happens a lot and the one in a thousand event happens way more than that" (S066).

## Top-up (batch 07: S091 2024-03-27, S099 S-1 letter 2020, S100 January 2025)

## Equity (fills the earlier gap)
- Use equity as pay for owners. "we believe in using equity as an important part of our employee compensation" (S099, 2020). No recorded position on founders' splits or option pools beyond this.

## Raising money (added)
- 2016 to 2018 told again: "I felt like I was wandering in the desert asking for money from investors" (S091, 2024). With no marketing spend, the product needed higher retention and order frequency before an investor said yes. (S091)

## Competition (added)
- "Be customer-obsessed, not competitor-focused" (S099, 2020). No consumer remembers last year's speed: "no consumer is going to remember the fact that you know we've delivered faster today than a year ago they're just going to expect us to deliver faster" (S091, 2024).

## Pricing and fees (added)
- DashPass was a flat monthly fee ($9.99 in the S-1) for unlimited deliveries from eligible merchants. Paraphrase, S099 (2020).
- Merchant commissions, pandemic: halved for restaurants with five or fewer locations, about 180,000 restaurants in the US, Canada and Australia. Paraphrase, S099 (2020).

## Culture (added)
- A team that plays for one another carries a company through losses. "it's really the team that dictates your day-to-day satisfaction" (S091, 2024). A quarter of staff left when the cash fell; the remainder formed a bond hard to break. Paraphrase, S091.
- High ownership and high accountability, because delivery is a utility, not entertainment. "it's more of a utility service versus an entertainment system" (S091, 2024). "our operators are relentlessly focused on execution and hold themselves to the highest accountability levels while carrying no blame" (S099, 2020).

## Hiring (added)
- Listen for ownership. A senior executive who opens with five reasons not to hire him signals accountability; no magic question. "I don't think there are any magical interview questions" (S091, 2024). Also count followership: how many people follow a candidate when they change jobs. Paraphrase, S091.
- Everyone has a superpower; the leader's job is to bring it out. "everyone has a superpower and it's my job as the leader to get it out of them" (S091, 2024).

## Metrics (added)
- Distribution, not average. "Averages in our industry are meaningless, it’s the distribution that matters." (S099, 2020).
- Four inputs for the product: selection, affordability, speed and accuracy, customer service. Quote: "a consistent focus on improving the inputs to our product" (S100, 2025).

## AI (added)
- 2024: very early. "we're very small percentage of the progress bar" (S091). Use: cataloguing the physical world, support, fraud. No silver bullet; "enough shots on goal". Paraphrase, S091. 2025: AI used for safety, in SafeChat+ (S100). Matches the 2026 view that a digital layer needs the physical system.
- Offline data is a prize: "there's still so much information that is Offline that still needs to be organized and assembled" (S091, 2024).

## International (added)
- Partnered with Wolt (Helsinki); its founder runs international. Over 30 countries (S091, 2024; S100, 2025). #1 or #2 in 20 of 25 countries with data, and positive international unit economics two years running; non-restaurant products grow faster abroad than in the US. Paraphrase, S100. "While it hasn’t been easy and we’ve certainly made mistakes, we’ve grown our confidence in building a global business." (S100, 2025)

## Merchants (added)
- Small merchants are the mission: the Palo Alto baker who turned away delivery orders. "we strive to become a merchant’s first call when they want to grow their business" (S099, 2020). Local commerce "democratized into the hands of all merchants, and not just a few" (S100, 2025).

## Focus and diversification (added)
- 2025 letter: five areas (US Restaurants, International Marketplace, US New Verticals, Commerce Platform, Ads). "we also won’t pursue 1,000 things at once or begin a project without enough resources" (S099, 2020). Each new line starts small and earns capital. The two management systems idea (S091).

## Timing and luck (added)
- Skill and luck: "there's always elements of skill and elements of luck and you need both as the entrepreneur" (S091, 2024). On his own sizing: "there was a lot of luck around how large these categories could be" (S091).

## Work and burnout (added)
- Exercise every day, a routine adjusted as life changed (single then married with two kids). Paraphrase, S091 (2024).
- Advice to the young: be proud of the person in the mirror; build life strategy on the things that do not change. Quote: "you kind of have to bet on the things that don't change" (S091).

## Growth and profit (added)
- "solving for growth “or” profitability can be a false dichotomy" (S100, 2025). Profit rose in 2024 through unit economics and disciplined headcount, not from cutting investment. Paraphrase, S100.
