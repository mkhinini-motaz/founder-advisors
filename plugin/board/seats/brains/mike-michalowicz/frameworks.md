# Frameworks: Mike Michalowicz

Built from 32 sources (podcast transcripts 2016 to 2025 and his own blog posts, to late 2026). Each entry: what it is, parts, when to use it, how to score a decision, one verbatim quote and the sources that carry it. "Score" lines are guidance from his own thresholds, labeled as such. Most-used first. Dates come from the notes; many transcripts are undated.

## 1. Profit First (flip the formula)
- **What:** Sales minus profit equals expenses. On every deposit, move a set percentage to a profit account first, then run the business on what is left. He calls it a behavioral system, not an accounting one.
- **Parts:** five accounts (Income, Profit, Owner's Comp, Tax, Operating Expenses); allocate by percentage twice a month (the 10th and 25th) or weekly; profit held at a second bank; profit paid out quarterly, about half to the owner and half kept (S011).
- **When:** any owner who reads the bank balance instead of statements, which is nearly all of them.
- **Score (guidance):** Is profit leaving every deposit before bills are paid? If bills do not fit afterward, the business is telling you the bills are too big. Cash in the account, not accounting profit.
- **Quote:** "In the Profit First system, what we do is we flip the formula. It’s sales minus profit equals expenses." (S011)
- **Sources:** 10 (S002, S004, S010, S011, S014, S016, S021, S027, S042, S048).

## 2. Start slow: 1 percent first
- **What:** A business that never paid itself profit starts at 1 percent, then 2, then 3. The habit comes before the amount. Target percentages can take 18 months to two years.
- **When:** a first Profit First setup, or a business with thin margin.
- **Score (guidance):** Is the skim small enough not to cause a crisis, and does it happen on every deposit?
- **Quote:** "Do not, do not start off with the endgame, the target." (S014)
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S014, S021, S042).

## 3. Parkinson's Law and constraint breeds innovation
- **What:** A resource you hold gets consumed. Take profit first and the shortage forces invention. Examples: toothpaste tube, Apollo 13 box of parts, the Savannah Bananas dropping the scoreboard.
- **When:** judging a raise, a big budget, or a need-more-money-first argument.
- **Score (guidance):** Would the business find a cheaper way if the money were not there?
- **Sources:** 8 (S002, S003, S008, S010, S011, S014, S021, S042).

## 4. Business Hierarchy of Needs (Fix This Next)
- **What:** Maslow turned into business: sales (oxygen), profit (stability), order (efficiency, owner not required), impact, legacy. A hierarchy, not a ladder: each level need only be adequate to carry the next, and you cycle back down.
- **Parts:** ask at each level whether you have any, and whether you have enough; fix the lowest cracked level; use data, not gut.
- **When:** the owner has many problems and cannot say which is biggest.
- **Score (guidance):** Name one most important problem. Anything you cannot link to it waits.
- **Quote:** "this is not a ladder it’s not about climbing to the top and waving to your friends from the top" (S020)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S010, S020, S024, S039).

## 5. Survival trap: point A, point B, pregnant pause
- **What:** In a crisis every move is an arrow away from point A and brings relief, but few arrows point at B, the vital need. Insert a pause: act, consider, react.
- **When:** loans, price cuts, layoffs, panic buying.
- **Score (guidance):** Does this move point at B, or only away from A?
- **Quote:** "We are looking at very short-term relief in trade for long-term agony." (S039)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S020, S024, S039).

## 6. Pumpkin Plan (fewer, better clients)
- **What:** Grow the strong, cut the weak, as a regular discipline. Big pumpkin client is the overlap of clients who pay most and clients you like working with. Fire bad-fit clients, raise price as the gentle way, uber-cater to the best, clone them.
- **Parts:** sort clients by revenue and by liking; ask the best client what you are doing right; advertise where they gather; keep a niche share, not niche exclusivity.
- **When:** revenue ceiling that will not lift, drained team, price pressure.
- **Score (guidance):** Which 20 percent of customers produce most profit, and what are the energy drains?
- **Quote:** "It is the intersection of clients who pay you the most and who you like working with them." (S043)
- **Sources:** 5 (S010, S014, S018, S042, S043).

## 7. Queen Bee Role (QBR)
- **What:** The single activity the brand promise hinges on. Name the promise, list what delivers it, pick the one most important. Protect it, give it redundancy, let secondary work slip. The role is the egg production, not the owner.
- **When:** deciding what to delegate, what to cut, what the people at the core should never do.
- **Score (guidance):** Is anyone at the core role doing anything else?
- **Quote:** "If we compromise the core competency, we are done." (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S005, S010, S022, S048).

## 8. Four-week vacation test
- **What:** If the owner can be fully disconnected for four consecutive weeks (one full monthly cycle), the business can run without him. Reaching it can take a year or two, in steps ("a throttle").
- **Score (guidance):** What broke while you were gone? That list is the next to-do.
- **Quote:** "if you can disconnect yourself from four weeks, you have proven that you can remove yourself from your business permanently." (S048)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S008, S010, S017, S020, S022, S048).

## 9. Doing, Deciding, Delegating, Designing (plus Downtime)
- **What:** Stages of the owner. Deciding for everyone is a trap ("salt": a little helps). Delegate outcomes, not tasks. Designing (vision, people, tools, systems) is the owner's real work, an hour a day of thinking time.
- **When:** owner is the bottleneck, employees keep asking "what should I do".
- **Score (guidance):** How many hours a week is the owner in design versus decisions others could make? Optimized business spends 80 to 90 percent of its time in Doing (S017).
- **Quote:** "delegation is the assignment of outcomes." (S001)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S005, S017, S022, S048).

## 10. Four T's (Trash, Transfer, Trim, Treasure)
- **What:** Track time for two weeks once a year, sort each activity. Abandon what nobody misses, hand the rest to whoever enjoys it, make the necessary cheaper, keep the joy work (aim near 80 percent).
- **When:** the owner is overloaded and "more productive" is the proposed fix.
- **Quote:** "I’m consistently confounded by how much I misperceive my effort." (S022)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S022).

## 11. ACDC: Attract, Convert, Deliver, Collect
- **What:** Every business runs these four stages. Find where throughput stalls and open that first. Strengthening the weakest link lifts the chain; a "slow delivery" may be a conversion problem upstream.
- **Quote:** "if you strengthen just the weakest link, the entire chain gets stronger" (S048)
- **Sources:** 2 (S003, S048).

## 12. Behavioral intercept and bank balance accounting
- **What:** Do not change the person; find a path they already walk and put the system on it. Owners walk to the bank login, so pre-allocate there. Gym shoes on the toilet seat is the image.
- **When:** any plan that depends on willpower.
- **Score (guidance):** Does the system sit on the screen the owner already watches?
- **Quote:** "Instead, observe how you behave and then channel to an outcome you want." (S014)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S010, S014, S021, S027, S042, S048).

## 13. Sell it to tell, presell, trust wallets
- **What:** Announce an idea in a few bullets and ask for a deposit before building. A paid engagement or preorder is the test; compliments are not.
- **Quote:** "I call it sell it to tell with small businesses." (S015)
- **Sources:** 4 (S010, S011, S015, S042).

## 14. Get Different (DAD)
- **What:** Different, Attract, Direct. The mind ignores nearly everything except threat, opportunity and the unexpected, so copying competitors makes you invisible. Ask what no one does. Direct needs a Goldilocks next step. Run cheap marketing experiments, not plans. By 2023 he calls the book "a dud" for licensing purposes (S046).
- **Quote:** "If everyone's front door is black, paint yours red" (S008)
- **Sources:** 4 (S008, S015, S018, S046).

## 15. Tacking, not a business plan
- **What:** A vision as destination, move in about three-month increments, pause each quarter to reassess. Three sheets: vision, metrics, immutable laws.
- **Quote:** "set a vision and then zigzag your way there" (S008)
- **Sources:** 2 (S008, S010).

## 17. Profit by job, and scaling Profit First (Vault, Drip)
- **What:** Give each active job its own accounts so profit per job is a number in a bank account. Vault holds three to six months of cash; Drip releases seasonal cash month by month. When it stops working, change the setup, not the rule.
- **Sources:** 1 (S014).

## 18. Licensee model and the 15 percent override
- **What:** A book becomes a funnel: an operator licensee buys the rights to teach it, pays an upfront fee and shares revenue. 15 percent was the number between choking the licensee (50) and mutual indifference (1 to 2). Sign about a year before publication; build a pilot first. Licensing works only once the brand has leverage.
- **Quote:** "I found the magic number to be 15%." (S016)
- **Sources:** 3 (S016, S046, S022).

## 19. OMEN goals
- **What:** Objective, Measurements (two or three), Evaluation frequency, Nurture (adjust with outside eyes).
- **Quote:** "Nurturing is allowing ourselves an objective view of our progress and then give yourselves a flexibility to make adjustments." (S024)
- **Sources:** 2 (S024, S039).

## 20. FASO (All In)
- **What:** Fit, Ability (potential judged by curiosity, desire, thirst), Safety (small exchanged risks), Ownership (psychological ownership: personalize, intimate knowledge, control). Hire by demonstration, not interview.
- **Quote:** "Demonstrative skills and assessments are way underutilized." (S015)
- **Sources:** 2 (S015, S017).

## Books

### Books: Mike Michalowicz

Summaries are in this brain's words; no book text is reproduced. He says the title carries the core concept ("if you read the title, you’ve actually consumed the books core concept." S048). He describes himself as a curator, not a creator, and each book has a licensee. About eleven books by 2023 (S046).

#### The Toilet Paper Entrepreneur (first book, self-published)
- **Core thesis:** Start with no money and no experience; lack of resources breeds invention. Sell before you build; tack toward a vision instead of writing a business plan.
- **Key frameworks:** Tacking, three sheets of paper (vision, metrics, immutable laws), sell it before you do it.
- **Sources:** S008, S010, S021, S048. He calls it an "angry" freshman book (S048) and says mnemonic titles like this are gone (S003).

#### The Pumpkin Plan
- **Core thesis:** Grow the best clients and products like giant pumpkins and cut the weak ones, as a regular discipline.
- **Key frameworks:** seed clients, good/bad/no client, big pumpkin as the overlap of top-paying and liked, niche specialization not exclusivity, cull and uber-cater.
- **Sources:** S010, S014, S018, S042, S043, S048 (his favorite).

#### Profit First (2014; second edition with Penguin, 2017)
- **Core thesis:** Flip the formula so profit comes first; set up accounts at the bank and run the business on the rest. A behavioral system, not an accounting one. He claims over 1 million businesses and 1.2 million copies (self-reported; counts shift from 150,000 in S011 to 300,000 in S014 to 1.1 million in S027).
- **Key frameworks:** five accounts, bank balance accounting, start at 1 percent, second-bank profit hold, debt payoff from profit, quarterly distribution, profit by job.
- **Sources:** S002, S004, S010, S011, S014, S016, S021, S027, S042, S048. He says the ideas are "not original" (S048): envelope system, pay yourself first.

#### Surge
- **Core thesis:** Put yourself in front of rising demand; look for customers building their own fix.
- **Sources:** S010, S042.

#### Get Different
- **Core thesis:** Marketing that wins is different, attracts, and directs. The brain ignores the expected; break the pattern.
- **Key frameworks:** DAD, rip off and duplicate from other industries, marketing experiments.
- **Sources:** S008, S015, S018, S046. He calls it a "dud" in licensing terms (S046).

#### Clockwork (2018; revised and expanded 2023)
- **Core thesis:** Design the business to run itself, as a team empowerment first and time freedom as the result. Revised partly because readers read the Queen Bee Role as "the owner".
- **Key frameworks:** four-week vacation, Queen Bee Role, Doing/Deciding/Delegating/Designing (and Downtime), Four T's, captures instead of SOPs, ACDC.
- **Sources:** S001, S003, S005, S017, S022, S048.
- **Limits:** for a handful of employees up to about 50; past 50, something like EOS fits better (S017).

#### Fix This Next
- **Core thesis:** Owners do not know their biggest challenge. Use the Business Hierarchy of Needs to find the one next problem. He calls it his pinnacle work, greater than Profit First (S014).
- **Key frameworks:** hierarchy of needs, survival trap, OMEN, free 25-question test.
- **Sources:** S002, S010, S020, S024, S039.

#### All In (January 2024)
- **Core thesis:** Great teams come from psychological ownership.
- **Key frameworks:** FASO, hire by demonstration, no counter offers, outcomes not steps.
- **Sources:** S015, S017, S046.

#### My Money Bunnies
- A children's book that teaches a Profit First-like system. (S008)

#### Books he teaches from (not his)
- How to Stop Worrying and Start Living (Dale Carnegie), The Goal (Goldratt), Total Money Makeover (Ramsey), The Lean Startup (Ries; likes it except pivoting), Influence (Cialdini), Quit (Annie Duke), Write a Must Read (AJ Harper). (S008, S011, S014, S015, S016, S042)
