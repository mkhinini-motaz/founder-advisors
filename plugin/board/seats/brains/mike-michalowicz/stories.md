# Stories: Mike Michalowicz

The stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson and source ids. Paraphrased, no quotation marks except where stated. His own accounts differ in detail across years; flagged where noted.

1. **Two exits, then the angel-investor wipeout.** Sold two companies (one to private equity, one to a Fortune 500) by about his early 30s, then started or backed ten companies at once and lost everything in about two years; the house went about 30 days after the accountant's call. Lesson: arrogance and status chasing destroy money; he calls himself "the angel of death". (S004, S010, S015, S039, S042)
2. **The piggy bank.** His nine-year-old daughter fetched her piggy bank to help the family. Seed of Profit First. Versions differ: the 2017 interview ties it to the bankruptcy advice (S004, S011); his 2025 blog puts the Valentine's Day cash call as Day 1 (flagged in the notes). Lesson: the turning point was behavior, not information.
3. **The accountant's call and the five accounts.** The accountant said profitable on paper but no cash; he opened five accounts and began a 1 percent transfer. A banker asked why five; he said he could not be trusted with one. (S027)
4. **The journal and the sneakers.** Two years of depression, then a 30-day journal challenging business sayings. Left sneakers on the toilet seat to run, which became behavioral intercept. (S011, S014)
5. **Savannah Bananas.** Profit First showed they could not afford the scoreboard or ticket machine; they cut it, taught players to dance, printed banana tickets; sold out for years. Lesson: lack of money forces innovation. (S014, S021)
6. **Geek Squad beat his better firm.** Uniforms and a Beetle won over technical quality. Lesson: customers notice different, not better. (S008, S018)
7. **The hedge-fund client and response time.** The best client named response time; he got to half an hour and became known for it. Lesson: ask your best customer what you do right. (S018, S043)
8. **A friend's $250 million company that collapsed.** One bad decision, no profit runway. Lesson: size is not health. (S002, S010, S020)
9. **Bragging about seven million with lawn furniture in the house.** Now proud of about 12 mostly part-time employees and comfortable margins. Lesson: revenue is an ego number. (S020)
10. **The beach phone call.** On a family trip his wife took the phone and told him to walk; he saw rich owners typing on their decks. Origin of Clockwork. (S048)
11. **McDonald's and the owner who is never there.** Fifty cashiers, no owner; the owner collects money and thinks. Lesson: saleable businesses do not depend on the owner. (S001, S022)
12. **The newsletter nobody missed.** Eight hours a month; he forgot to send it once and "no one said boo". Lesson: trash tasks. (S001, S022)
13. **The beehive and the Queen Bee Role.** A bee sting while frustrated that interviewed firms shared no definition of efficiency. Lesson: find the one function the whole colony serves. (S003, S005, S022)
14. **Stephen King's barbecue house.** After COVID a bad new waiter got ownership of small things, then bigger, and became the best employee. Lesson: ownership beats hunting for A players. (S017)
15. **The 2000s layoffs.** Thirty employees, cash stopped because he chased sales; laid off 12 or 13, then cut the rest 10 percent, a mistake. Later cut 15 of 30. Lesson: cut once and early. (S021, S039)
16. **Barnes and Noble.** Friends slipped copies of his self-published book into stores; the buyer phoned, ordered thousands. Lesson: do not accept no. (S003, S021)
17. **Penguin refused Profit First as "an accounting book".** He self-published; Penguin asked to republish. He later left a publisher over the numbers. (S004, S016)
22. **Get Different, a dud.** A licensee relationship ended after about a year; he took the license back. Lesson: do not license too early. (S046)
23. **Cutting 10 percent and 3 a.m. interviews.** Admitted mistakes in crisis management. (S039)
