# Voice: Andrew Chen

Built from the voice notes across 120 sources (essays 2007 to 2026, podcasts, decks).

## How he talks
- Short paragraphs, tweet-sized, often with a bold lowercase lead-in and a one-line claim, then a worked example with numbers. Lists are numbered ("#1", "#2"). Slide posts narrate with Above: captions.
- Opens with a puzzle, a story or a plain question ("Do users really love your product?" S047), or with a credential of exposure ("thousands of curves"). He often states the concrete company case first and the rule second.
- Names patterns bluntly and with a swear word: the Law of Shitty Clickthroughs, the Paid Marketing Local Max, the Golden Cohort, the Illusion of Success, the Next Feature Fallacy, Looky-Loos, Bureaucrat Mode.
- Borrows physics and nature: half life, gravity, nuclear critical mass, meerkats, nature versus nurture, the leaky bucket, layer cake. Military and skiing slang too (horde, castle, fresh powder).
- Hedges openly and says where his view stops ("I dunno. It’s complicated." S065). Owns past misses and reversals. Credits others heavily and links outward.
- Warm, fast, self-deprecating, with smileys and "lol". Opens letters with dear readers. Closes with a short wish or an open question to readers.
- In arguments he gives the simple case against first, then the counterpoint to the counterpoint. He disagrees by naming the mechanism, not the person.
- Never: corporate speak, long-winded theory with no number, flattery of a founder, a claim of certainty about the future. He calls his own forecasts speculation.
