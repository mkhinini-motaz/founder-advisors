# Principles: Andrew Chen

Built from the notes on 120 sources. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. Start with one small network that works, then build network by network. No big bang.
   - "You can’t do the top-down – I call it a big bang launch." (S058); "there’s a reason why the best network effects-driven products have started college-by-college, or team-by-team, rather than a big launch" (S127)
   - Sources: S058, S060, S115, S127. Confidence: high.

2. Density beats raw user count. Start with a hyper-connected niche.
   - "you focus on getting 1,000 users who are densely connected already." (S115)
   - Sources: S041, S115, S110. Confidence: high.

3. Evaluate a marketplace from the supply side.
   - "Because all these marketplaces tend towards supply constrained, you should evaluate each opportunity/company from the POV of the supply side." (S006)
   - Sources: S006, S007, S056, S059, S106. Confidence: high.

4. You cannot fix bad retention with notifications, features or A/B tests. Pivot big and early or fix first-week activation.
   - "adding more notifications will not fix your retention curve. You can’t A/B test your way to good retention" (S068)
   - Sources: S008, S010, S068, S109, S119, S125. Confidence: high. Newer nuance (S109): better first-week activation and network effects can move it.

5. Never trust viral growth with bad retention.
   - "Highly viral products with shitty retention do not last because it's so hard to fix retention." (S068)
   - Sources: S012, S068, S127, S044. Confidence: high.

6. Spikes are dangerous because they look like working.
   - "In fact, these spikes are dangerous." (S127); "I’m anti-spike! I’ll say it." (S080)
   - Sources: S080, S109, S119, S127. Confidence: high.

7. Fix the product before the growth work.
   - "If your product isn’t working, you won’t solve that by doing growth activities." (S053)
   - Sources: S019, S053, S057, S107. Confidence: high.

8. Compute CAC per channel, not blended, and keep paid a minority of acquisition.
   - "be super disciplined about not spending ahead of where you want it to be and not to do it on some, you know, blended number that doesn’t make any sense." (S054)
   - Sources: S011, S054, S002 (intro only), S122. Confidence: high.

9. Build the loop into the core product, not into the marketing.
   - "Design viral into your product, not into your marketing strategy" (S038); "the best implementations are strongly tied to the core product" (S102)
   - Sources: S038, S042, S102, S012. Confidence: high.

10. Aim for a viral factor above 0.5; treat virality as an amplifier.
    - "You really need high viral factors, like >0.5 to feel the difference." (S066)
    - Sources: S012, S066, S080. Confidence: high.

11. Find the next channel before the current one dies.
    - "we have to continually find the fresh powder, because inevitably whatever worked in the past will no longer work." (S056)
    - Sources: S033, S056, S059, S135. Confidence: high.

12. Early on, do things that do not scale; choose little channels.
    - "just trade off scalability." (S057)
    - Sources: S057, S061, S060. Confidence: high.

13. Compare metrics only inside your own category and pick metrics that fit your strategy.
    - "You just want to make sure that the metric reflects whatever strategy that you’re putting in place." (S054)
    - Sources: S014, S026, S054, S132. Confidence: high.

14. Count active users and cohorts, not signups, pageviews or registered totals.
    - "the aggregate numbers hide the problem." (S044)
    - Sources: S044, S047, S051, S061, S119. Confidence: high.

15. Prefer a known category with a 20% twist to a brand-new category.
    - "you’re probably talking more about a 20% remix rather than an 80%" (S068)
    - Sources: S020, S022, S053, S068, S074, S085. Confidence: high.

16. Do not compete on features; rework the position.
    - "Don’t compete on features. If your core concept isn’t working, rework the description of the product rather than adding new stuff." (S028)
    - Sources: S028, S082, S112. Confidence: high.

17. Be skeptical of products that need a large critical mass to start.
    - "For products that require a large critical mass to get started, I’m already skeptical." (S115)
    - Sources: S041, S115, S058. Confidence: high.

18. Make the founder own growth early and speak directly to users.
    - "the people heading the growth efforts here should actually be one of the founders." (S110); "People want to hear from the founder/CEO." (S071)
    - Sources: S071, S083, S110. Confidence: high.

19. Brand marketing is a lagging indicator; put it after fit.
    - "But great brand is the lagging indicator of success." (S103)
    - Sources: S103, S052, S061, S122. Confidence: high. Later view (S083, S071, 2024-25): constant founder voice, which is not paid brand.

20. Measure incentives by incrementality, not redemptions.
    - "measure “Cost Per Incremental Trip” by A/B testing different types of discounts" (S133)
    - Sources: S133, S054. Confidence: medium.

21. Test any network-effect claim: does the product get better when other people use the same one?
    - "does your agent get better when other people are all using the SAME agent?" (S064)
    - Sources: S064, S058, S079. Confidence: high.

22. Network effects are weak as a moat alone; every rival claims them too.
    - "Well, it turns out they have network effects too" (S058)
    - Sources: S058, S079, S060. Confidence: high.

23. Fix signup, invite and payment, the unsexy parts.
    - "This is why when I assess product UX, I tend to focus on the less sexy stuff: Signup flows, invitations/referrals, and payment." (S109)
    - Sources: S008, S109, S119. Confidence: high.

24. Judge the company in front of you, not its category.
    - "Remember that you’re helping/investing/working for the company right in front you, not a mutual fund of all companies with that characteristic!" (S034)
    - Sources: S016, S030, S034. Confidence: high.

25. More users means lower intent; filter spikes with a waitlist.
    - "getting more users generally leads to lower intent (and targeting) of that audience" (S080)
    - Sources: S068, S080, S127. Confidence: high.

26. Model inputs you control, not an assumed growth rate.
    - "Focus on the inputs because that’s what you can actually control." (S017)
    - Sources: S017, S119. Confidence: medium.

27. At zero-to-one, trust qualitative judgment; optimise on data later.
    - "only optimize based on data later on, when it’s more appropriate" (S131)
    - Sources: S074, S131. Confidence: medium.

28. Do not go free to fix indifference; more products should charge.
    - "Funny enough, I've never seen this work." (S085)
    - Sources: S085. Confidence: low.

29. Never use retention hooks without giving the user value.
    - "you should never use these techniques without contributing value to the user, lest you get uninstalled and reported to Symantec" (S051)
    - Sources: S051, S066, S012. Confidence: high.

30. Judge growth by what survives, so expect the trough and do not restart.
    - "Resist the urge to start over." (S020)
    - Sources: S020, S085 (restart test). Confidence: medium.
