# Stories: Andrew Chen

Built from the notes on 120 sources. Stories he reuses, with the lesson. Facts are his, as told in the sources.

1. **Tinder's USC party.** The first version had swiping and photos, but friends churned with only tens of users. The team threw a party with bouncers and made guests install the app; about 500 people entered at once. Lesson: find where hundreds of your users already gather and seed one atomic network. (S058, S060, S061)
2. **Uber as hundreds of hyperlocal marketplaces.** The job was to get more drivers; the driver view multiplies understanding. Driver retention was about 40% after a year; Uber paid drivers $30 an hour, recruited on Craigslist. Lesson: supply first, per city. (S007, S060)
3. **"Uber for X" failures.** Valet, car wash, massage: demand infrequent and spiky, supply idle. Lesson: test whether work fills a worker's week and when subsidies can end. (S006)
4. **Dropbox.** Paid search lost money; growth came from folder sharing and the give/get space program; retention was the secret weapon; usage showed documents retained paying users (high value active versus low value active). Lesson: intrinsic virality needs retention. (S011, S012, S056, S059, S061)
5. **Facebook, twice.** He doubted it could be a billion-dollar business and was off by 200-300x on pageviews; it started in one college and rolled up many small spaces. Lesson: stay open to weird young companies and judge the one in front of you. (S032, S034, S041)
6. **Instacart, toothpaste and the coupon.** Claude Hopkins told grocers an ad was coming so they stocked the product; Candler's coupons moved fountains and consumers together; Instacart built an app and shoppers before approaching grocers. Lesson: chicken-and-egg is solved by stimulating both sides together. (S061, S104, S133)
7. **Google+ and the firehose.** Linked from Google's properties, it claimed 300 million actives, but the users were not connected to each other, so the network collapsed when the firehose stopped. Lesson: engagement, not headline user count. (S060)
8. **Early Twitter's empty feed.** It dropped you on a blank feed; later it asked you to follow accounts. Lesson: a strong opinion on first use beats open exploration. (S008)
9. **DoorDash v1.** Eight PDF menus on a static page and a Google Voice number, built in about 45 minutes, heard from Tony Xu. Lesson: a simple v1 can work, but the trajectory took years. (S074)
10. **Airbnb versus Wimdu.** Network quality beat funding and headcount. Airbnb's Craigslist integration was built by an engineer. Lesson: niche atomic networks beat a copy. (S018, S110)
11. **The Trough of Sorrow.** The emotional side is most of his advising time ("Probably 80%, actually."). Lesson: expect it, do not restart. (S020)
12. **Dribbble and designing the community.** Posting is limited by nomination. Lesson: half the work is designing the right users; curate early. (S025)
13. **Branchout.** Grew to 14 million DAU on the Facebook platform and shrank to a tenth in 4 months. Lesson: do not invest at the peak of a platform-dependent loop. (S119)
14. **Uber's 1.7-star rider app.** Only unhappy riders rated it; asking every rider after every trip took it above 4.7. Lesson: broad sampling is a cheap lever. (S119)
15. **Marc Andreessen's cold email.** He started his blog at 25; Andreessen found it in 2007. He gives ideas away on purpose to meet people. (S056, S059, S061)
16. **Meerkats.** Borrowed from ecology: a group needs a minimum size and overcrowds past a maximum. (S061)
17. **Uber's mix.** When he was there, about 50% of first trips came from paid, 10 to 20% from referrals. Fewer than 20% of people shared contacts with the app. (S012, S066)
18. **Clubhouse by country.** He asked the CEO for retention curves for Sweden, Nigeria and the US before the Series B. Lesson: read the network market by market. (S060)
