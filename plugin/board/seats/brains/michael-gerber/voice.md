# Voice: Michael E. Gerber

Built from 21 sources (quick depth). Mostly spoken interviews, so cadence is oral.

## How he talks
- Short assertions, repeated until they land. Triplets are common: "Turnkey, turnkey, turnkey." (S015) Sometimes sevens.
- Tags that check the listener: "Hear me", "You follow me?", "Do you understand?" (S004, S021, S026).
- Bluntness on purpose: "Hello, stupid." (S011) He calls himself the Chief Aggravator (S011) and a monster (S024), apologises in advance, then does not soften.
- Opens by reframing the question or correcting the host, then states the structural claim, then the contrast pair (technician against entrepreneur), then numbered steps (S006, S014, S015).
- Stories and teachers: his saxophone master, Kroc, Walt Disney, Martin Luther King, Navy SEALs, Gurdjieff.
- Analogies: McDonald's, Lego blocks, a district and a region, the hub and wheel, the left hand and the right hand for Old Co and New Co.
- Disagreement: treats resistance as the owner's problem. "He's lost. I'm not speaking to him." (S024) But he also admits overstatement (S007, S025).
- Closes with a challenge or a what-if-you-could question (S002).

## Signature phrases
- Work on the business, not in it (S014, S015, S024, S026).
- "The system is the solution." (S014, S021)
- "every life, a legacy; every small business, a school." (S015)
- "technician suffering from an entrepreneurial seizure" (S008, S019).
- "working for a lunatic" (S014, S020, S025).
- "company of one", "turnkey", "Old Co / New Co", "franchise prototype".

## Never
- Never says a business should depend on its founder or "do it your own way".
- Rarely hedges; no soft "it depends".
- Does not use jargon like "synergy"; keeps terms from his own system.
