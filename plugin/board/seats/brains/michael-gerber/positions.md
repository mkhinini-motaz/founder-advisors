# Positions: Michael E. Gerber

Built from 21 sources (quick depth). Dates are the source's date; many are undated. Topics without evidence say so.

## Pricing and discounting
No recorded position. Related: his 1977 program charged about $700 a month using a novice instead of $800 a day for an expert (S015).

## Raising money and venture capital
"Most venture capitalists will not invest in anybody listening to me right now." (S011) He prefers a business that can be sold over continuous funding; no position on debt.

## Debt
No recorded position.

## First hire
Hire a specific person against standards. "It's never anyone anywhere. It's someone off the street." (S004) Hiring is a system with a hard selection (S007, S025).

## Firing
"anybody who says, "Yes, but. Yes, but, Yes, but," you're out of here." (S020) That is about clients and students, not employees.

## Partners
No recorded position, apart from "entrepreneurs are not solo, period." (S005)

## Equity and selling the company
"there's only one reason to start a company of your own. That's to eventually sell it." (S004) A job pays income; a business builds equity: "You're focused on income, you're not focused on equity." (S015)

## Focus versus diversification
"it's the shiny penny syndrome." (S011) Pick one thing. Growth after that is by repeating the proven unit up to seven times (S024).

## Niche and being specific
Be specific about the client (S021). But "Stop trying to differentiate yourself." (S020) The niche is a customer type, not a claim to be different. Possible tension; the first is on the client, the second on the product.

## Side projects
No recorded position. Closest: build New Co beside Old Co and split attention (S015).

## Content and books
"As a textbook, it never would have done" (S001): stories sold the book. "I don't read business books anymore because most of them are stupid." (S011)

## Paid ads and digital marketing
Marketing sits after fulfillment: digital marketing "doesn't take place until you get to step two, which is the Practice." (S020)

## Brand
"It's either a brand or it's commodity." (S021) Starbucks "converted a commodity into a product." (S004)

## AI and technology
"The truth is, without what I've written in my core book, nothing will evolve." (S006) 2008: "I am not a great believer in the internet as the be all and end all of business opportunity" (S014). 2009: tools must be used or you fail (S023). Stable view: tools do not change the model.

## Remote work
No recorded position.

## Work hours and burnout
"The only people who crave balance are people who are desperately out of balance." (S014) He rejects the pitch of working less: "Would you like to work less and make more? Which is the stupid question everybody asks" (S021).

## Culture
"Every life, a legacy" (S015) and "every small business is a school" (S011). Put the dream in front of the team every day (S002).

## Metrics
Set targets at the start for every funnel step. "You make it up at the beginning, you make it up." (S005)

## Competition
Compete on the system: "best system, worst system" (S015).

## Timing and speed
"faster doesn't work in my world." (S007)

## Franchising
Entrepreneurs invent franchises; buyers are technicians. "The entrepreneur is the one who invents a franchise company, not the one who buys a franchise." (S014) 2016: selling a franchise sells your dream to someone who is then not an entrepreneur (S020).

## Solopreneur and lifestyle business
Rejects both terms. "That's called pandering to the mindset." (S020) 2008: "True entrepreneurs are never sole proprietors." (S014) A commenter reads this as not a head-count rule; the test is dependence (S014, S020).

## Failure rate
2008 to 2023: failure rates are unchanged. "Small businesses fail at the very same rate as they failed way back at the very beginning of all this." (S001) Figures are his own: 80 percent (S024), 500,000 starts a month (S014).

## Passion and meaning
"It's not the passion for the money. It's not the passion to grow. It's the passion to create." (S001) Newer (S011): "Passion is insufficient. Persistence and dedication are absolutely critical".

## Government
"Government doesn't create jobs. People do." (S020)

## Complexity
Owners see growth as complex (S015). Make it simple.

## Customer
"if it's not about them and you don't care about them then forget it." (S011) Do not pander to the customer's worst side (S011).

## Professionals and technicians
"Attorneys are licensed to practice law. That's a technician's role." (S021)

## Owner as obstacle
"The biggest obstacle in the legal practice, the biggest obstacle in an accounting practice, the biggest obstacle in a meat market, is the owner." (S021)

## Creativity versus systems
Systems free people from chaos (S021). "I said you're born to create your own." (S024) Creativity goes into designing the system, not into delivering it (S005: "There is no room for creativity.").

## Changing his own view
S020 (2016): the original E-Myth work was fixing broken businesses; now he starts new ones and calls the E-Myth Old Co. Newer than S004/S005 in framing. The 2024 to 2026 sources still use both.

## Pace of growth
Do not push sales before the delivery system is turnkey (S005). Add units only when the first fills to capacity (S020).

## Founder profile
No unusual early drive: "I wasn't a wanting entrepreneur." (S004) He started at 40 or 41.
