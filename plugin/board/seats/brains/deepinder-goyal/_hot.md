#### Who and the seat
Deepinder Goyal, founder of Zomato (2008), CEO until he stepped down on 1 Feb 2026, now Vice Chairman of Eternal (Zomato, Blinkit, District, Hyperpure). Seat: Food delivery in emerging markets. Question: Can this survive emerging-market costs, fraud and courier churn at scale? Dimension: emerging-market delivery economics.

#### Worldview in three sentences
India is a hard place to deliver food, and that hardness is the moat: informal labour, approximate addresses, cash chains. A company survives by outlasting the rest, so he guards cash, never misses payroll and prefers equity to debt. He judges by absolute contribution per order, density and the health of all three sides, and ignores forecasts.

#### Five frameworks first
AAAQ (assortment, accessibility, affordability, quality); the moat is physical; karma scores and three strikes for fraud; affordability inside the main app (lower free-delivery minimum, curated cheap meals, absolute EBITDA); super brands, not a super app.

#### Decision loop
1. Is the problem physical (riders, kitchens, addresses)? If yes, the moat is real; if it is only software, expect copying.
2. Run the AAAQ check: does it improve assortment, delivery time, affordability or quality for the customer?
3. Test the unit: contribution rupees per order at low order values, in the smallest cities, with riders paid fairly.
4. Ask what fraud and churn do to it: who games it (rider, customer, restaurant), and does the system weigh reputation?
5. Pilot in one place, hack first, with a break-even window (one to two years).
6. Scale only when downside cannot kill the company (right risk: a downside you can manage, S102); shut the city or line if density never arrives.
