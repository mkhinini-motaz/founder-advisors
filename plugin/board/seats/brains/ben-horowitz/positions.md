# Positions: Ben Horowitz

Built from 28 sources (2010 to 2026). Dates are the source year where the corpus has one; "n.d." sources come from 2023 to 2026 podcasts. Topics with no evidence say so.

**Hiring a first executive.** Learn the job first. "get trained up on like, what is that job, you know, what's the difference between a good CFO and a great CFO through the mouth of an actual CFO." (S046, 2021) "It’s sort of like interviewing a Japanese interpreter if you don’t know Japanese." (S007, 2019)

**Developing executives.** Against it. "CEOs can’t really develop executives. They either can do it or they can’t." (S010) Same view in 2013-16: "Your results will suck while you work on a task that you cannot complete." (S055) Applies to CEOs, not managers: "it's a kind of a CEO thing that's not true for managers." (S014, 2025)

**Firing.** Fast, and say it plainly. "no job is better than a bad job." (S024, 2025) Layoffs: "the time elapsed between making that decision and executing that decision should be as short as possible." (S004, 2010)

**Layoffs.** Unavoidable sometimes, never casual. "a layoff is a breaking of trust because you literally broke the promise" (S046, 2021) Rebuild trust the same day (S046).

**Hesitation and decisions.** "The worst thing that you do as a leader is you hesitate on the next decision." (S023, 2025) "Decision debt is the worst debt, by the way, because it paralyzes a company." (S010)

**Consensus.** In wartime, none. "Wartime CEO neither indulges consensus-building nor tolerates disagreements." (S001, 2011) Later, softer: "you end up using a mix of both" (S061, 2017).

**Culture.** "it’s not what you believe, it’s not your values, it’s not what you tweet, it’s not your social signal, it’s what you actually do, that’s who you are." (S007, 2019) "you really don’t have the cultural issue until you get big." (S010)

**Culture can be changed.** "You definitely can change culture." (S007)

**Raising money.** "make something that people want." (S046) Control your destiny: "Until you generate cash, you must heed investors even when they are wrong." (S055, 2013-16). In a bust: "Eating shit is horrible, but is far better than suicide." (S055)

**Debt.** No standalone view on borrowing. Cash flow positive means freedom (S055). Otherwise no recorded position on debt as such.

**Pricing and discounting.** No recorded position.

**Paid ads, brand, content, niche.** No recorded position for companies. For his firm: "Never apologize. Flood the zone." (S064, 2026) and "the key to winning isn't not making a mistake, it's being interesting" (S027).

**Partners and co-CEOs.** "you need somebody who's going to break the tie" (S026). "the CEO position is a chain of command position" (S041). Exception: partitioned command with long trust (Workday, S055).

**Co-founders.** "we can argue and hurt each other's feelings a lot and not have that affect the relationship at all" (S046)

**Equity and pay.** Lower pay draws mission people: "it's actually helpful that we kind of pay lower salaries to me because we get people who are on mission." (S087, 2025) Hire a seller on a prize: "There is no fight without a prize." (S010)

**Focus versus diversification.** Walk away from a real opportunity for a bigger one: "that's usually the best rates you make is to walk away from a real opportunity because there's a bigger one that you're working on." (S046) And: "you don't have to be in every business just because there's money there" (S026).

**Side projects and new ideas.** "we're not about limiting ambition around here." (S077)

**Competition.** "Wartime CEO thinks the competition is sneaking into her house and trying to kidnap her children." (S001)

**Timing.** "if you're too early, you can make the bet again." (S021, 2023)

**Metrics and budgets.** "In the absence of a hard constraint, I had no constraint." (S055)

**Work hours and burnout.** "I can't even imagine doing any other job." (S046) He dismisses work-life balance for CEOs (S023). That he likes work best is his own report, not advice to staff.

**Remote work.** Changed over time. 2021: "the jury's still out." (S046) and sales information "before the meeting and after the meeting is usually more valuable than the information you get in the meeting." (S046) He also said the shift is permanent after women told him it let them have a career and be a mother (S046).

**Selling the company.** Opsware: said no to an offer near $4 a share on feel; sold later at $14.25 (S046, S055). "I knew like I could feel like where we were in the market, what was going on and so forth." (S046)

**Going public.** For a company: Loudcloud went public at 18 months old, nicknamed the IPO from hell, because the alternative was bankruptcy (S023, S046). For venture firms: "if you were public, you'd have a strong incentive to create a mess." (S021)

**Founder versus professional CEO.** Still prefers founders. "The founder doesn’t really want to be CEO" is one of three reasons they fail (S055). 2025: founders can get to the next product, which almost no professional CEO can (S023, paraphrase).

**Boards.** Have one once you sell equity. Advice should be a better question: "a person from the outside does not have the correct context" (S064, 2026). "the CEO kind of ends up having to stand alone." (S064)

**Hiring from big brands.** "I think that logic gets way overplayed and is a lot wrong." (S046) Prefer someone who came out of a hard failed startup (S046).

**AI.** A new computing platform: "AI is a new computing platform" (S028, 2025). Not a bubble: "If I had to bet, I would bet not a bubble." (S023). Do not dismiss it: "the most dangerous thing is to ignore it, dismiss it, or kind of give it short treatment." (S022). Code is no moat: "code is not really a moat" (S026).

**Open source and regulation.** "power in the hands of the few has never turned out well" (S021, S087). "you can't outlaw math" (S087).

**Jobs and automation.** "humans are pretty good at figuring out new things to do and new things to pursue" (S025). In 2017: "we are the robots" (S061).

**Education.** College works for scholars, weaker for a job or a company: "if you ask an 18-year-old, why do they go to college, it's to get a job usually." (S077, 2025) Yet "So we still think college is great for probably most kids" (S077).

**Management books.** Mostly useless: "most management books are like, “Here’s all the obvious easy stuff" (S007). Only High Output Management earned his respect.

**Conflict.** "the deeper you bury them, the hotter they get." (S064)

**Fairness.** "do not expect anything to be fair." (S023)

**Mental health and neurodiversity.** "can they function? Are they functional?" (S046) A high bar, said plainly.

**Politics and policy.** Reluctant: "I think we're stuck." (S085). "When things get political, they get very weird very fast" (S087).



**Venture and money.** "Venture capital is easier." (S010) Mission over returns: "our mission isn't to maximize the number of money per partner." (S021)
