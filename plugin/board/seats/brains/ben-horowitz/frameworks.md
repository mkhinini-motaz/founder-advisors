# Frameworks: Ben Horowitz

Built from 28 sources (2010 to 2026): his a16z essays and podcast transcripts. Most-used first. Scoring lines are guidance built from his own thresholds, not his words.

## 1. Peacetime CEO / Wartime CEO
What it is: two operating modes. Peacetime is a large advantage in a growing core market: expand the market, reinforce strengths, delegate. Wartime is fending off an imminent existential threat: one mission, no consensus, precision, urgency, inspection. Parts: paired behaviours (protocol vs breaking it, big picture vs the speck of dust, consensus vs none, culture defined vs defined by the war). When to use: any time the environment turns and the old plan may be wrong. Scoring (guidance): if a lost deal, a missed quarter or a market shift can end the company in under a year, treat it as war. He later said the post "didn't do a good job of explaining" why you are in either mode (S046), and that it is "a mix of both" (S061).
Quote: "In wartime, a company is fending off an imminent existential threat." (S001)
Quote: "you have to recognize that you're at war and then you have to be able to make the tough decisions." (S022)
Used in: S001, S007, S010, S022, S024, S046, S061, S087 (8 sources).

## 2. Culture is actions, not beliefs
What it is: from Bushido. Do not list values. Define the exact daily behaviour, tie it to a visible penalty, and enforce it. Parts: pick the behaviour that proves the value; make it shocking enough to be remembered ($10 a minute for being late to an entrepreneur meeting); sign it before the offer letter; teach it to every new hire. When to use: once the company is big enough that people cannot see the CEO. Scoring (guidance): can a new hire name three things people do and three things they never do? If not, it is a poster.
Quote: "a culture is not a set of beliefs, it's a set of actions." (S007)
Quote: "your culture is probably hypocrisy if if that's how you define it because nobody's doing that" (S014)
Used in: S007, S010, S014, S022, S024, S026, S043, S061, S085 (9 sources).

## 3. Shocking rules and decisions that show priorities
What it is: to change a culture, make one rule people cannot believe, and make decisions that demonstrate priorities. From The Black Jacobins: Toussaint banned officers from cheating on their wives so a word meant something. Second move: bring in leaders from the culture you want.
Quote: "you need shocking rules like if you're going to change somebody's behavior you have to put a roll down that they can't even believe you put that" (S061)
Quote: "let me just make a rule that shows you yesterday has nothing to do with today." (S085)
Used in: S007, S022, S024, S025, S061, S085 (6 sources).

## 4. Hesitation is the worst act (decision debt)
What it is: the CEO's central failure is the gap between seeing what must be done and doing it. Both options hurt, so the leader avoids the subject; the company senses it anyway. Parts: look into the abyss, pick the slightly better path, go. Confidence-competency loop: costly mistake, lost confidence, hesitation, senior people fill the void, politics, the founder cannot run it. Scoring (guidance): count open decisions older than a month; each is debt.
Quote: "Decision debt is the worst debt, by the way, because it paralyzes a company." (S010)
Quote: "The worst thing that you do as a leader is you hesitate on the next decision." (S023)
Quote: "any decision, even the wrong decision, is better than no decision." (S055)
Used in: S010, S023, S024, S055, S085 (5 sources).

## 5. Run at the dark area (the pain in darkness)
What it is: the hard thing you want to avoid is the thing to do first. Avoiding it feeds the fire. Pair with: tell the company the truth, normalise bad news (his staff meeting entry fee was one broken thing).
Quote: "if you run away from that, that's when you really get a huge raging fire, and that will potentially burn down your company." (S007)
Quote: "You have to run at the pain in darkness." (S024)
Used in: S003, S007, S010, S024, S046, S064 (6 sources).

## 6. Constructive confrontation / sharpen the contradictions
What it is: from Andy Grove. Do not smooth over disagreement, because it carries information. Steps: isolate the one behaviour, stop thinking about yourself, say what is true, offer help, state the consequence. No "shit sandwich". Caution: it is also a way to start a revolution (S007).
Quote: "the right answer is usually to sharpen the contradictions." (S007)
Quote: "a lot of leadership is getting all the way to the truth." (S024)
Used in: S001, S007, S010, S024, S046 (5 sources).

## 7. The Struggle (five aids)
What it is: the named state when product, market and team all go wrong. No answer, five aids: do not carry it alone, this is chess so there is always a move, focus on the road not the wall, play long enough and you may get lucky, do not take it personally.
Quote: "There is no answer to The Struggle, but here are some things that helped me:" (S003)
Quote: "There is always a move." (S003)
Used in: S003, S007, S046, S061 (4 sources).

## 8. Product CEO Paradox
What it is: a product founder who stays in every detail bottlenecks the company past about 500 people; one who disengages wrecks the product. Fix: formal involvement. Drive the vision, keep the quality standard, be the integrator, give direction only in writing and in scheduled reviews.
Quote: "The only thing that will wreck a company faster than the product CEO being highly engaged in the product is the product CEO disengaging from the product." (S055)
Quote: "Write it; don’t say it." (S055)
Used in: S055, S010 (founder mode), S023 (3 sources).

## 9. Apply pressure or feel pressure
What it is: as complaints grow, assign every problem to an owner. Four cases: the executive denies it (make it specific); it is cross-functional (one definition, one target, a firm deadline); no logical owner (assign an illogical owner with the incentive); the owner hands it back (empower the team). If you keep applying pressure and get no results, upgrade the position.
Quote: "You either apply pressure or you feel pressure." (S055)
Used in: S055 (1 source).

## 10. A CEO cannot develop executives
What it is: do not coach an executive in a job you cannot do. Evaluate on existing ability. Hire the finished article after learning the job: write criteria, talk to people who do it, try the job yourself. Micromanage new executives for a limited time (task-relevant maturity, from Grove). Scoring (guidance): if you catch yourself asking why you are not doing the obvious thing about your own leader, change is due. He called it a lesson he hates he learned.
Quote: "CEOs can’t really develop executives. They either can do it or they can’t." (S010)
Quote: "Executives are compensated for their existing ability, and therefore should not be evaluated on their potential." (S055)
Used in: S007, S010, S014, S023, S046, S055 (6 sources).

## 11. Managerial leverage
What it is: the CEO cannot make people great; find people who make you great. Leverage is the executive telling you what to do next.
Quote: "What's leverage is if you're telling me what you should do" (S023)
Used in: S023, S028 (2 sources).

## 12. Shared economics, centralized control (no shared command)
What it is: sharing control means you cannot reorganize, because a reorg redistributes power. One decider, no co-CEOs, a tie-breaker. Exception he names: partitioned command with 20 years of trust and a named heir (Workday).
Quote: "if you share control, you can't reorganize." (S043)
Quote: "Command is not actually shared at Workday, command is partitioned." (S055)
Used in: S021, S026, S027, S041, S043, S055, S064, S087 (8 sources).

## 13. Invest in strength, not lack of weakness
What it is: judge a founder or an executive by the magnitude of the strength. You can always rule out a deal on weaknesses. Ask if they are literally the best in the world at a thing.
Quote: "we're investing in strength, not lack of weakness." (S023)
Quote: "it's always a mistake to rule out somebody who's truly world-class on a weakness" (S064)
Used in: S023, S028, S061, S064 (4 sources).

## 14. Little Things
What it is: before acting on a big worry, translate it into small concrete things you can inspect (sales calls, training, the build environment).
Quote: "Focus on the little things and the big things will take care of themselves." (S055)
Used in: S055 (1 source).

## 15. Budget by constraints, not by asking
What it is: set run-rate, loss, headcount growth no more than double in 12 months and function ratios; cut the total 10 to 25%; hold a slush fund for managers who show they earn more. Asking managers what they need turns it into a game of building the biggest organization.
Quote: "In the absence of a hard constraint, I had no constraint." (S055)
Quote: "The enemy of cultural cohesion is super-fast headcount growth." (S055)
Used in: S055 (1 source).

## 16. Control your destiny with cash
What it is: until you generate cash, investors decide when you lay people off. Positive cash flow gives you control. Fine to run negative only if you are Twitter in a friendly market.
Quote: "Until you generate cash, you must heed investors even when they are wrong." (S055)
Used in: S055, S026 (2 sources).

## 17. Layoffs in six steps
What it is: (1) get your head right, (2) do not delay, (3) be clear why, (4) train your managers, (5) address the entire company, (6) be visible. Every manager lays off his own people. Start rebuilding trust that day.
Quote: "the time elapsed between making that decision and executing that decision should be as short as possible." (S004)
Used in: S004, S046 (2 sources).

## 18. Hire sales for courage and qualifying, not enthusiasm
What it is: sellers must be courageous, competitive and hungry. Test: they should qualify you, not gush. Ask who they are bringing and call those people. Do not hire out of monopolies. Engineering hiring methods fail on sales.
Quote: "Who are you bringing with you?" (S010)
Quote: "great sales people must be courageous, competitive and hungry." (S055)
Used in: S010, S046, S055 (3 sources).

## 19. Product first, investors second (a16z's own model)
What it is: build the product the customer (the founder) needs, then the team. Contribute what nobody can do better than you; size the fund to the market; the middle of the venture market is squeezed.
Quote: "we're kind of a product first and then a team of investors second, whereas every other firm I think is the opposite" (S021)
Used in: S021, S041, S043, S085, S087 (5 sources).

## 20. Regulate the application, not the model
What it is: AI is math. Law should hit what is done with it. Banning open source is the unsafe path; power in few hands has not ended well.
Quote: "So you can regulate the model or you can regulate the applications on the model." (S014)
Used in: S014, S021, S025, S087 (4 sources).

## Books

### Books: Ben Horowitz

Built from 28 sources (2010 to 2026). Thesis in my own words; no book text reproduced.

**The Hard Thing About Hard Things (2014).** The sequel in spirit to High Output Management. Its thesis: there is no formula for the hardest CEO moments (layoffs, a failing executive, the cash running out), only the struggle and the conversation. Frameworks: Peacetime/Wartime CEO, The Struggle, layoffs in six steps, do not develop executives, Product CEO Paradox, apply pressure or feel pressure, shared command. He wrote it after cold sweats as a CEO when no business book helped, he named it himself, and he gives the earnings to women's rights work. Taught in: S001, S003, S004, S007, S022, S023, S024, S046, S055, S061, S064, S077, S085. The essays behind it are in S055. His own limit: it does not teach you to be a CEO any more than a book teaches football (S077).

**What You Do Is Who You Are (2019).** Culture as behaviours, drawn from Bushido, Toussaint Louverture, Genghis Khan and Shaka Senghor's prison gang. The more advanced book, meant for companies with scale (S022, S023). Taught in: S007, S010, S014, S022, S024, S043, S085.

**Essays and series, no further books.** Good Product Manager / Bad Product Manager (S002), The Struggle (S003), The Right Way to Lay People Off (S004), Why Startups Should Train Their People (S005), Peacetime CEO / Wartime CEO (S001). In 2026 he said he would write another only for something he understands that others do not (S064).

**Books he leans on.** High Output Management (Andy Grove), the only management book he liked (S005, S007); The Black Jacobins (C.L.R. James) for culture change (S061, S085); Make something people want as a funding rule (S046).
