# Stories: Chris Voss

Built from 24 interviews and talks, 2016 to 2025. Paraphrased, no quotes. Each has the lesson he draws.

1. **The Philippines $10 million demand (S010, S011, S019).** Voss coached a negotiator to summarize a terrorist's long list of grievances. The terrorist said "that's right", the ransom demand went to zero in minutes, and he later called to say the negotiator should be promoted. Lesson: tactical empathy works on a sociopath and needs no agreement.

2. **Chase Manhattan bank robbery, New York (S011, S018, S021, S031, S032, S050, S062).** A robber who blamed absent other guys was mirrored (you have more than one van) and blurted out a getaway driver. A second robber surrendered after gentle confrontation and the truth that he would go to jail. Lesson: mirrors expose hidden facts; plural pronouns hide an influencer; tell the truth even when it costs.

3. **The Burnham and Sobero case, Philippines (S007, S018, S023, S033, S050).** Hostages died, one killed on a national holiday the American side did not know about and one in a botched rescue. He calls it the hardest professional moment of his life. Lesson: never assume you will be told what you need, and do we quit or do we get better. It also led him to rebuild proof of life as a how question.

4. **Pittsburgh drug dealer and the how do I know she's all right? question (S007, S013, S050).** A kidnapper lost control when asked how he could know the victim was alive. Lesson: a how question shifts power without a demand.

5. **The Washington Redskins ticket letter (S002).** A student's blunt demand for unpaid season tickets became "we wouldn't be where we are without you, how can we fix this?" and nearly all accounts settled. Lesson: say what the other side thinks before you ask for money.

6. **The suicide hotline (S012, S013, S018, S032, S058).** Rejected by a negotiation team lead, he volunteered for months on a hotline, was later put in line for training, and learned labels there. Lesson: do what the right person says, and learn the skill where stakes are low and the cases are real.

7. **Dwight Watson's tractor siege, Washington, 2003 (S023, S033).** He saw hints of the man's faith, passed on a tomorrow is the dawn of the third day line through a colleague, and the man came out. Lesson: listen for identity and face-saving, and watch who delivers the line.

8. **Insurance claim audit (S037).** A fifteen-item accusation audit lifted an offer from $10,000 to $25,000. Lesson: the audit works in ordinary money disputes.

9. **Cutting contractors' pay by 75 percent (S021).** He opened with the harsh precursor I've got a lousy proposition and nearly all took the deal within a day. Lesson: warning before a loss makes the number land.

10. **The Harvard Law course and hard bargaining (S002, S019, S062).** He used hostage tactics at Harvard, they worked until word got around that nobody would deal with him again. Lesson: wins by force burn the relationship pool.

11. **Haiti kidnapping of a 12-year-old (S048).** He told the father the kidnappers' habits and timeline instead of reciting a resume, and the boy was out on Saturday morning. Lesson: show you know what is happening and let them correct you.

12. **The wedding in Ireland (S031).** He told the bride what her day had cost her; she later remembered only how good it felt. Lesson: people remember how you made them feel.

13. **Jack Welch's book signing (S007, S036).** Voss asked whether it was a ridiculous idea for Welch to speak at his course, Welch scowled, then gave his assistant's contact. Lesson: the no-oriented ask.

14. **The Pittsburgh negotiator and her jealous boss (S007, S013).** A negotiator asked a jealous boss "Do you want the FBI to be embarrassed?" and the no protected her. Lesson: a no-question puts the other side in control and calms him.

15. **Iger, Jobs and the whiteboard (S013).** Steve Jobs listed every reason against selling Pixar while Iger stayed silent; then Jobs said the positives could outweigh the negatives. Lesson: let the barriers out first.

16. **The film financier's chateau (S013, S036).** A casual mention of a chateau opened a multi-film deal. Lesson: black swans come out of talk, not interrogation.

17. **Lost luggage with a magic wand (S023).** A joke about a "magic wand" made the airline clerk walk him to the bag. Lesson: playfulness gets people to leave the script.

18. **The Tom or Bob impasse (S024).** A deliberately wrong label (seems like Tom and Bob are against the deal) drew the correction no, it's Bob. Lesson: be wrong on purpose.

19. **Voss and his wife (S019).** Early in his marriage he said "you sound angry" and she blew up, because the label blamed her. Lesson: a label of anger that holds the person responsible backfires, and empathy is hardest with those closest to you.
