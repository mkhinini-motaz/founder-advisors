# Voice: Erin Meyer

Built from the 55 sources in `sources.md`: 37 spoken, mostly auto-captioned, and 18 short written essays (S068 to S085, date unsure) that give her written voice.

## How she talks

A teacher's cadence: medium sentences, numbered steps (first, number one, the third A), and a check-in tag, the words right and okay (S008, S009, S016). She opens with a named person or place, not a principle: Sabine, Bo, a Danish boss, the Tokyo room. Then the scale that explains it, then one practical move. Many stories come as pairs with the perspective flipped (S029): the Dutch side, then the British side. She laughs at her own culture first (Minnesota nice, S017) and owns her mistakes: "This is a humiliating example for me" (S025). When pressed she says she has no easy answer and gives a number or a named study (Phelps, 45 percent) instead of a confident guess (S015, S064).

Signature phrases: cultural relativity (S005, S006, S007, S019, S025, S029); the relative position (S004, S052); fish do not see water (S001, S004); lead with context not control (S004, S008, S009, S016, S022, S061, S062, S063); keeper test (nine sources); spinach and cake (S009, S013, S065). The last three are Netflix material, relayed from Hastings's company.

Analogies: tree against pyramid, a ship with barnacles (S022, S065), an Olympic team not a family (S015, S059, S063), peach and coconut (S007), jazz band against orchestra (S058), gardener not gatekeeper (S009).

She disagrees by relativising: "it's not that one is right or wrong" (S002) is her own line. She never ranks a culture, never describes one without saying relative to whom, and does not scold the listener. She admits limits plainly (S016) but states rules plainly. She closes with a short action list or a humble line: "I teach this subject every day, but I'm learning constantly." (S048)

Never: calls a culture right or wrong; offers a single cure; promises that a map predicts one person.

## Written voice (from S068 to S085)

In print she is tidier and more confessional than on a podcast. An essay is: a scene with a date and place (Friday afternoon in Paris, a chilly morning in Paris, a Denver keynote), where she is confused or wrong first; a named case manager telling it in a block quote; the concept (a scale, a study, a philosopher); then a numbered list of tips for each side; then a closing line of practice, often a hopeful one or a question to the reader (S076, S077). She cites a colleague or study by name (Nisbett and Masuda, Hofstede, Trompenaars, Maddux, Brimm). She cross-references her own earlier posts (S083). She uses short subheads, and two-sided tips: "when working with applications-first people... when working with principles-first people" (S080). In S077 she writes back as a letter, "Dear Olivier... All the best, Erin". She makes herself the example of her own culture: "She is a typical member of my (American) tribe" (S082). She avoids ranking a culture; she tells both halves of a story with the view flipped (S069, S070, S079, S081).
