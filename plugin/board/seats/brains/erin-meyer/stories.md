# Stories: Erin Meyer

Built from the 55 sources in `sources.md`. Her stories are mostly told as she heard them; the lesson is one line.

1. **Tokyo and the bright eyes.** No hands after her 20-minute talk; her Japanese colleague called on four people by their bright eyes, each with a good question. She tried it next day; at INSEAD she had missed bright eyes in her own classes for years. Lesson: the signal to speak differs, her own assumption hid input. Told in at least 14 sources (S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S009, S019, S020, S025, S028, S049, S066).
2. **Sabine and John.** French woman in Chicago, American boss opens with praise; she hears the best review ever and stops listening. Lesson: positive anchoring buries the message for a direct-feedback culture. S001, S002, S003, S006, S025, S027, S029, S048.
3. **British and Dutch swap reports.** The Dutchman lists the flaws, the Briton hears arrogance; the Briton praises and offers small suggestions, the Dutchman learns later the report was disliked and calls him a hypocrite. Lesson: each side acted respectfully by its own norm. S002, S020, S025, S027.
4. **French team, British and Indians.** Britons call the French disorganised, Indians call them rigid, because France sits between them on time. Lesson: there is never one truth. S002, S005, S006, S007, S019, S025, S029, S049, S052.
5. **Bo in her Paris training.** A Chinese manager silent for hours; asked, he had pages of typed examples. Lesson: he waited for the leader to call on him; her bias was the problem. S005, S006, S025.
6. **Written recap as distrust.** Indian, Saudi, Indonesian participants read a written recap after a call as "I don't trust you". Lesson: a habit that clarifies in one culture insults in another. S002, S005, S006, S019, S049, S052.
7. **Brazil and New York, the long lunch.** Americans work through lunch; Brazilians take two hours because the lunch is the work of trust. Lesson: trust is built differently. S002, S005.
8. **Heineken in Monterrey.** Mexican managers cannot cope with Dutch staff contradicting them. Lesson: lead the way the population needs. S005, S006, S019, S049.
9. **Malaysian firm wins the China bid.** Spent time with the Chinese outside work; the Americans had the better presentation. Lesson: affective trust beats the pitch. S007.
10. **Russian Igor and the American on the plane.** The American shares everything, Igor thinks they are friends; the American says "have a great trip". Lesson: peach and coconut friendliness. S007.
11. **Dutch students Wim and Peter.** Peter lists Wim's flaws in front of the group; that evening they drink beer together, Wim calls it a gift. Lesson: directness can build closeness in Dutch culture. S007.
12. **Netflix keynote in Cuba.** A woman told her mid-session that asking for volunteers drew only Americans; she changed the method in minutes. Lesson: candor in the moment can save the work. S008, S009, S061, S065.
13. **Netflix, Japan and the Dutch office.** Spontaneous feedback hurt relationships in Tokyo while structured 360 dinners worked; the Dutch laughed at Americans who think they are candid. Lesson: same principle, different behaviour. S058, S063, S064, S048.
14. **Pure Software (Hastings's story, Meyer relaying).** No rules, then rules after abuses, innovators left, company sold. Lesson: freedom breeds innovation, process kills flexibility. S008, S009, S010, S011, S016, S017, S061, S062, S063.
15. **Phelps study with the actor Nick.** Teams with a bored or rude actor performed 45 percent worse. Lesson: an individual performance problem is a systemic one. S008, S009, S013, S016, S017, S021, S061, S063.
16. **Her sons in a French school.** Red marks and "Applique!"; her son says he had the fifth best paper. Lesson: a thick or thin skin starts in childhood. S025, S027, S062.
17. **Botswana and Minnesota.** She taught 90 children in Botswana at 22 and the techniques fell flat; raised in Minnesota nice, she calls herself the outsider who was wrong first. S003, S004, S017, S028.

Added from her written essays (S068 to S085). These are her case stories, told as she tells them; the managers are named in her essays.
