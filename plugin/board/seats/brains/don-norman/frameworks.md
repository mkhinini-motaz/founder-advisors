# Frameworks: Don Norman

Built from the 91 sources in `sources.md`. Each entry has a verbatim quote with its id. Scoring lines are guidance for using the framework, not his own numbers.

## 1. Blame and train versus redesign the system
- **What it is:** when most accidents are called human error, the fault is in the design. Do not retrain or add rules; redesign so errors are detected, corrected and tolerated.
- **Parts:** the verdict ("operator error"), the situation that produced it, the redesign that makes the error harmless.
- **When to use:** any incident, support complaint or misuse pattern.
- **Scoring guidance:** list the errors seen; for each ask whether the system could have caught it. Count those that it could.
- **Quote:** "Whenever I see such a high percentage of accidents blamed on individuals, I get very suspicious." (S002)
- **Sources (5):** S001, S002, S065, S017, S090.

## 2. Conceptual model, system image and the designer-user triad
- **What it is:** the designer holds a model, the user forms another, and the only link is the system image. A conceptual model is a story that makes sense of the situation.
- **Parts:** designer's model, system image, user's model.
- **When to use:** a feature people misuse the same way again and again.
- **Scoring guidance:** ask a new user to explain how it works; compare with the designer's version.
- **Quote:** "the entire communication must take place through the “system image”" (S028)
- **Sources (4):** S028, S035, S012, S001.

## 3. Signifiers over affordances
- **What it is:** an affordance is a possible action; a signifier is the perceivable cue that tells people about it. Designers should supply signifiers.
- **Parts:** affordance, perceived affordance, signifier, convention.
- **When to use:** a control, button, gesture or door that people try wrongly.
- **Scoring guidance:** show it cold to five people and note which action each tries first.
- **Quote:** "Designers of the world: Forget affordances provide signifiers." (S081)
- **Sources (5):** S081, S080, S047, S048, S066.

## 4. Four principles of human-centered design
- **What it is:** solve the core problem, be people-centered, use an activity-centered systems approach, iterate with rapid prototypes and tests.
- **Parts:** root cause, people, activity and system, iteration.
- **When to use:** starting or reviewing any project.
- **Scoring guidance:** check each of the four is visible in the plan; a missing one is the first risk.
- **Quote:** "Continually evaluate and modify. Iterate on solutions. Measure results." (S072)
- **Sources (5):** S049, S022, S072, S062, S011.

## 5. Four principles of understandable interaction
- **What it is:** a clear conceptual model, clear signifiers, discoverability, and feedback on what just happened. They come from psychology, so they hold on any platform.
- **Parts:** model, signifiers, discoverability, feedback, plus undo and standards.
- **When to use:** reviewing a screen, flow or new input method.
- **Scoring guidance:** pass or fail on each; a hidden action fails discoverability.
- **Quote:** "these fundamental psychological principles still apply." (S047)
- **Sources (5):** S047, S025, S027, S018, S066.

## 6. Slips versus mistakes
- **What it is:** a slip is doing something other than intended; a mistake is a wrong intention. They need different fixes.
- **Parts:** slip (execution), mistake (plan or understanding).
- **When to use:** sorting the errors found in observation.
- **Scoring guidance:** slips call for forcing functions, undo and layout; mistakes call for a better model and feedback.
- **Quote:** "there are two different classes of errors, major classes. One of them is a slip" (S045)
- **Sources (2):** S045, S048.

## 7. Collaboration, not supervision (levels of automation)
- **What it is:** a machine and a person should work as a team that communicates. Intermediate automation, where the person is the backup, is the dangerous middle.
- **Parts:** full automation, full human control, partial automation (the trap), continuous feedback.
- **When to use:** any alerting, auto-assignment or auto-decision feature.
- **Scoring guidance:** ask who notices when it fails and how quickly; if the answer is a bored human, redesign.
- **Quote:** "Aim for collaboration, not supervision." (S019)
- **Sources (5):** S019, S012, S002, S089, S036.

## 8. Knowledge in the head versus in the world
- **What it is:** working memory is small, so put reminders and cues in the world instead of demanding recall.
- **Parts:** memory task, perception task, external aid.
- **When to use:** a step users forget, a code they must remember.
- **Scoring guidance:** count the facts a user must hold in mind; move each possible one onto the screen.
- **Quote:** "Good design practice, therefore, will provide external aids to memory." (S002)
- **Sources (1):** S002.

## 9. Three levels: visceral, behavioral, reflective
- **What it is:** a product is judged at first sight, in use, and in the story people tell about it.
- **Parts:** visceral (appearance), behavioral (use), reflective (meaning, brand, memory).
- **When to use:** brand, look and delight questions.
- **Scoring guidance:** rate each level; a strong brand does not rescue an obvious defect in use.
- **Quote:** "Visceral design refers primarily to that initial impact, to its appearance." (S056)
- **Sources (3):** S056, S048, S013.

## 10. Compliance and tolerance
- **What it is:** ask engineers for systems that accept how people naturally act, instead of demanding exact input.
- **Parts:** accept all forms, normalise afterwards, say what was understood and ask.
- **When to use:** forms, phone numbers, addresses, search, error messages.
- **Scoring guidance:** list the formats people type; count how many the system rejects.
- **Quote:** "A compliant, human-tolerant machine should be just as flexible as a person: all it has to do is accept the digits and ignore the non-digits." (S016)
- **Sources (2):** S016, S051.

## 11. Activity-centered design
- **What it is:** design around the activity and its tools, when the activity is well understood and a strong designer holds one clear model.
- **Parts:** activity, tasks, actions, operations; a cohesive design model.
- **When to use:** a mature domain where the work is known.
- **Scoring guidance:** can you describe the activity as a sequence, not one screen at a time?
- **Quote:** "To the Human-Centered Design community, the tool should be invisible, it should not get in the way. With Activity-Centered Design, the tool is the way." (S015)
- **Sources (3):** S015, S053, S049.

## 12. Hill-climbing and the local maximum
- **What it is:** iterative testing improves the hill you stand on and never finds a higher one.
- **Parts:** incremental innovation, radical innovation, different starting points.
- **When to use:** deciding whether to test more or rethink.
- **Scoring guidance:** if gains from tests are shrinking, suspect a local maximum.
- **Quote:** "A simple solution is to use different starting points" (S086)
- **Sources (4):** S055, S086, S011, S033.

## 13. Technology first, invention second, needs last
- **What it is:** breakthroughs come when technology makes them possible; the need shows up over decades. Customer research suits improvement, not invention.
- **Parts:** technology, invention, need; two hurdles, inside and outside the company.
- **When to use:** judging a radical idea or a research claim.
- **Scoring guidance:** use customer research after a direction exists; before it, back the technologists.
- **Quote:** "Technology first, invention second, needs last." (S033)
- **Sources (3):** S033, S079, S074.

## 14. Systems thinking: the whole-experience test
- **What it is:** a product is a service. Think through every stage from first intention to help, repair and reflection.
- **Parts:** discovery, purchase, use, help, service, maintenance, reflection.
- **When to use:** reviewing an end-to-end flow such as order, fulfilment and complaint.
- **Scoring guidance:** list each stage; mark the ones nobody owns.
- **Quote:** "This is what it means to be a system: to think of everything." (S078)
- **Sources (3):** S078, S039, S061.

## 15. Understandable beats simple
- **What it is:** people dislike confusion, not complexity. Make complexity understandable. Buyers still pick features, so make it look powerful and be easy.
- **Parts:** real complexity, apparent complexity, understanding.
- **When to use:** feature versus simplicity arguments.
- **Scoring guidance:** test whether users can explain the product, not whether it has few buttons.
- **Quote:** "Things that are understandable are judged to be simple." (S067)
- **Sources (3):** S067, S052, S088.

## 16. Community-based, humanity-centered design
- **What it is:** design with people, not for them, with experts as facilitators and communities as owners, moving in small opportunistic steps.
- **Parts:** community experts, domain experts, incrementalism, ownership.
- **When to use:** a social or local problem outside the team's own life.
- **Scoring guidance:** who decides the method? If the experts do, expect rejection.
- **Quote:** "It is time to develop with people, not for people." (S009)
- **Sources (6):** S009, S039, S045, S049, S062, S072.

## 17. Trust but verify (AI)
- **What it is:** treat AI output as a claim needing evidence; keep the person in charge and send doubtful cases to a human.
- **Parts:** check references, conservative errors, triage, person in charge.
- **When to use:** any AI-written answer, support reply or automated decision.
- **Scoring guidance:** for each output ask how a wrong one would be caught.
- **Quote:** "that's what we need in AI, is trust but verify." (S091)
- **Sources (2):** S091, S021.

## 18. Security gets in the way
- **What it is:** when security is a nuisance, people build workarounds that defeat it. Make it easy for the right person and hard for others, and explain why.
- **Parts:** identification versus authorization, reasonable effort, understood necessity.
- **When to use:** sign-in, passwords, approvals.
- **Scoring guidance:** look for workarounds in the field: written passwords, shared logins.
- **Quote:** "The more secure you make something, the less secure it becomes." (S014)
- **Sources (2):** S014, S047.

## Books

### Books: Don Norman

Built from the 91 sources in `sources.md`. Summaries are in my own words. The corpus covers his essays and chapters, so some books are known from his mentions of them.

- **User Centered System Design (1986, with Stephen Draper).** An edited collection that helped found the field of human-computer interaction. Mentioned as part of his history; no chapter text in the corpus.
- **The Psychology of Everyday Things, later The Design of Everyday Things (1988; revised and expanded 2013).** His best-known book. It shows how ordinary objects confuse people and what design can do. The revised edition (S048) replaces affordances with signifiers and adds emotion and business. Chief idea: design must fit how people think.
- **Things That Make Us Smart (1993).** On how people and artifacts together extend thinking. Quoted by a host in S091.
- **Turn Signals Are the Facial Expressions of Automobiles (1992).** Collected essays. The corpus holds chapters S002, S003, S004, S005, S008, S017. Themes: human error, automation, team communication.
- **The Invisible Computer (1998).** Argues that computers should become invisible information appliances. The corpus holds chapters S001 and S087 (Being Analog).
- **Emotional Design (2004).** Why attractive things work better; the three levels. Related essays S013 and S056.
- **The Design of Future Things (2007).** On smart machines and how people and machines should talk. Related essays S051, S089.
- **Living with Complexity (2010).** Complexity is not the enemy; confusion is. Related essays S052, S067.
- **Design for a Better World (2023).** Meaningful, sustainable, humanity-centered design. Chapters S021, S029, S034 are in the corpus.
- **Human Information Processing (with Peter Lindsay).** An early psychology text. No text in the corpus.
