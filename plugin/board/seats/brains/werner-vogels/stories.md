# Stories: Werner Vogels

14 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Source ids in parentheses. Guest tales (Warfield, Olson, Matsakis, Bowes) are left out.

1. **The worst day, 12 December 2004.** The customer database, a scaled-up Oracle cluster, hit a bug in shared-disk locking and Amazon was down for 12 hours just before the free-shipping cutoff. He says "it was somewhat ourselves to blame" (S099). Lesson: a black box scaled up is a single unit of failure with no control; this led to cells, purpose-built databases and Aurora (S099).
2. **The shopping cart that must keep taking items.** Customers should be able to add items "even if disks are failing, network routes are flapping, or data centers are being destroyed by tornados" (S070, the paper). Lesson: pick the failure you refuse to show the customer, then push complexity elsewhere (S063, S070).
3. **Retail decomposition by data was wrong.** The first split grouped all customer-data code in one service, so login (hit on every page) and the address book (only at checkout) scaled together. Lesson: split by unique scaling and security needs (S098).
4. **Guessing S3's size.** The team planned for six months, added two orders of magnitude for fun, and blew through it in the first month (S097); in 2017 he tells it as three months (S098). Lesson: you cannot predict scale, so build to evolve. S3 grew from eight services to over 255 (S106).
5. **Multics, UNIX and panic.** Half the code was error recovery; Dennis Ritchie replaced it with one function that rebooted. Lesson: a simple, fast recovery path beats trees of handling for failures you have never seen (S098).
6. **A plane refuelled in flight.** S3 went from a Cessna to a fleet of A380s with customers changing planes unnoticed (S052, S104). Lesson: evolvable architecture means no maintenance outages.
7. **Amsterdam houses and the meter.** In the 1970s oil crisis, houses with the meter in the hallway used about a third less energy. Lesson: what you can see changes behaviour; define your meter (S104).
8. **The unlimited plan that killed a company.** A MiFi startup sold unlimited data; viewing Netflix outran revenue. Lesson: the revenue dimension must match the cost dimension (S104).
9. **Pre-cloud hardware bought for 15 percent over peak.** A post about 1,000 Wiis on sale sent traffic exploding at 11:00 and the team improvised. Lesson: constraints forced creativity; removing them made people forget cost, though "The constraints from the past, I don't want to go back to them" (S104).
10. **PBS re-architected, not lifted.** PBS cut streaming cost about 80 percent only after redesigning for the cloud, and later halved compute while doubling traffic (S104, S015). Lesson: lift and shift keeps inefficiency.
11. **Lambda's deliberate debt.** Lambda began on T2 instances for isolation, took on technical and economic debt on purpose, and repaid it with Firecracker; customers noticed only that it got faster (S104, S016). Lesson: take debt knowingly, with a date to pay it.
12. **Reviews move up a tier.** At Amazon, reviews went from tier two to tier one once teams saw customers do not buy when reviews are offline (S104). Lesson: tier lists change with observation.
13. **The Friday ML demo.** After a neurologist said he would rather be woken at 3 AM for a false positive, he built a stroke-detection demo over five Fridays and said it was "in no way a production system" (S104). Lesson: the alert path matters more than the dashboard.
14. **WeTransfer's idle fleet and iRobot.** WeTransfer kept dozens of instances waiting for uploads, which fed the idea of Lambda; iRobot went serverless because the app is free to buyers and "they never have to pay for idle" (S098); the idle fleet is in S094. Lesson: for a cost-bound product, do not pay for idle.
15. **The disks that fail together.** Studies showed 6 to 10 percent yearly disk failure, so a 100,000-disk site replaces thousands a year; deploy batches carefully (S074). Lesson: correlated failure is real.
