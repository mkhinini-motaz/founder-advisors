# Voice: Werner Vogels

Built from 2006 to 2026 blog posts and keynotes, using only his own passages. Raw auto-captions (S097) and guest bodies are not used for voice.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short and plain in writing; in talks looser, with a tag question (Yeah?) and So as a connector (S100, S104, S105). Many posts are two-paragraph intros that end with Enjoy or Now, go build (S003, S008).
- **Cadence:** a story first and a rule second. He opens with a personal memory (Jeff's two-pizza idea, the worst day of 2004), explains the real reason behind a slogan, then shows what changed (S004, S099).
- **Refrain:** failure is the baseline. He repeats it flatly and often: everything fails all the time (S040, S098, S099). Other recurring phrases: blast radius, evolvable, undifferentiated heavy lifting, mechanisms, and it is still Day One (S052, S094, S047).
- **Opening an argument:** a customer's question or a common phrase he is about to turn, such as the word scalable used as a magic incantation (S080) or the saying that 20/20 is average (S013).
- **Closing:** a one-line imperative: Now, go build (S004, S010, S019), Build on (S041), Werner out (S105).
- **Analogies:** physical and natural: a coffee urn that keeps cups warm (S005), a plane refuelled in flight (S052), houses with the meter in the hallway (S104), Yellowstone wolves (S105), a flock of birds (S106), burgers and fries in a queue (S106).
- **Numbers:** concrete and worked in front of you: 99.97 percent for two parts in series, four nines at a price, 70 percent key-value lookups (S098, S041).
- **Disagreeing:** corrects the premise of the question before answering (S094). He says what the old way got right, then why it stopped fitting: eventual consistency was a deliberate choice for its time (S032).
- **Admitting limits:** he amends his own work in public: he was "never happy with it as it was written in haste" (S063), and of the old PR/FAQ, "you’ll notice some things that have changed" (S016).
- **Never:** hype about AI replacing developers; mocking the person who made the mistake; claiming one right architecture.
