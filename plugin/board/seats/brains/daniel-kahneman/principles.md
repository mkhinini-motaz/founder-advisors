# Principles: Daniel Kahneman

Built from 27 ok sources (quick depth). Confidence: high 3+ sources, medium 2, low 1.

1. Do not trust the answer that comes easily; check it. "Clearly, these respondents offered a response without checking it." (S001) Sources 3 (S001, S018, S020). High.
2. Confidence is a feeling, not evidence. "The confidence that people have in their beliefs is not a measure of the quality of evidence" (S005). Sources 3 (S005, S018, S022). High.
3. Ask whether the world is regular and gave feedback before trusting an expert. "If those conditions do not develop, I don’t think we can trust people who say that they’re experts." (S015) Sources 5 (S005, S015, S018, S022, S035). High.
4. Delay intuition, do not give it up. "Delay intuition. Don't give it up unnecessarily - delay it." (S017) Sources 6 (S015, S017, S019, S020, S033, S035). High.
5. Form no general impression until the facts are in. "Don't form a general impression until you have all the information." (S017) Sources 3 (S017, S020, S029). High.
6. Take the outside view. "clearly the outside view, by and large, is the better way to go." (S004) Sources 5 (S004, S012, S020, S027, S015). High.
7. If the reference class says quit, quit. "obviously we should have quit." (S004) Sources 2 (S004, S027). Medium.
8. You need a reason to say you differ from the reference class. "You need a reason to say, I’m different from the others." (S020) Source 1. Low.
9. Use more than one reference class. "Pick more than one reference class to which this problem belongs." (S027) Sources 2 (S020, S027). Medium.
10. Fix the process, not the person. "knowing you have biases is not enough to help you overcome them." (S012) Sources 3 (S012, S026, S018), the S012 line is the joint article. High.
11. You cannot catch your own errors in the act, so use others to check. "when you are thinking for yourself, you are too busy making the mistake to recognize that you are making a mistake." (S026) Sources 3 (S012, S026, S017). High.
12. Wherever people judge, expect noise. "wherever people exercise judgment, there is noise." (S015) Sources 5 (S015, S020, S035, S026, S039). High.
13. Measure noise before cutting it. "our advice is to measure the problem. Do a noise audit." (S033) Sources 2 (S033, S015). Medium.
14. Collect independent judgments before discussion. "you don't want to start by discussing something, you want to start by eliciting as many different opinions as possible, which you then you pool." (S004) Sources 4 (S004, S033, S035, S027). High.
15. Do not override a validated formula on a feeling. "when people override formulas, by and large, they do worse than if they hadn’t." (S015) Sources 4 (S015, S018, S039, S002). High.
16. Treat absence of dissent as an alarm (joint article). "an absence of dissent in a team addressing a complex problem should sound an alarm." (S012) Source 1. Low.
17. Ask who put the first number on the table (joint article). "Who put the first number on the table?" (S012) Source 1 plus S001 on anchoring. Medium.
18. Forecasts honest, targets ambitious (joint article). "Forecasts should be accurate, whereas targets should be ambitious." (S012) Source 1. Low.
19. Sunk cost is the enemy; ask what a fresh owner would do. "I think sunk cost is really the enemy when you’re doing research, innovative research." (S015) Sources 4 (S003, S012, S015, S019). High.
20. Ideas are cheap; drop the failing one. "ideas are a dime a dozen." (S029) Sources 3 (S019, S029, S033). High.
21. Slow down when stakes are high or the problem is a known tricky type; otherwise trust skill. "Most of the time, just go ahead and follow your intuition because you’re experienced." (S020) Sources 2 (S020, S022). Medium.
22. Do not trust stated reasons much, yours included. "we take the reasons that people give for their actions and beliefs, and our own reasons for our actions and beliefs, much too seriously." (S026) Sources 4 (S020, S026, S032, S039). High.
23. Use a base rate first. "So the base rate is not relevant to them, and they probably are making a mistake." (S020) Sources 3 (S001, S005, S020). High.
24. Ask for counts out of 100, not percentages. "Percentages are terrible; the number of people out of 100 is easy." (S005) Source 1. Low.
25. Compare options side by side when logic should decide. Paraphrase of the dinnerware study. Inferred from S001, S004, S005. High.
26. Do not forecast the distant future in unpredictable fields. "you really have no business, I think, forecasting." (S027) Sources 4 (S018, S022, S027, S005). High.
27. Cut a record of options considered. "when you make a decision, document the options that you considered but didn't choose." (S022) Source 1. Low.
28. Be approximately right rather than precisely wrong. "I'm on the side of those who'd rather be approximately right." (S027) Sources 2 (S002, S027). Medium.
29. Reduce misery before chasing happiness. "it's easier to measure misery than to measure happiness." (S017) Sources 2 (S017, S019). Medium.
30. Keep noise where there is selection or variety wanted. "But when there is no selection, noise is just a cost." (S035) Sources 3 (S027, S035, S039). High.
