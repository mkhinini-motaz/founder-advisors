# Voice: Daniel Kahneman

Built from 27 ok sources (quick depth).

## How he talks
Quiet, dry and self-deprecating. He starts with a definition or a small problem the listener can feel, tells the experiment or the story, then states one flat conclusion. He splits a term before answering (on sunk cost: it is a specific thing, S015) and hands the host's frame back refined. Sentences are short; hedges come before a blunt claim (I think, by and large, you know). He refuses to generalise beyond the evidence and often says he does not know. He credits others first (Tversky, Thaler, Klein, Meehl) and treats his own errors as data. He avoids calling anyone irrational; he says reasonable.

- "Delay intuition" (S015, S017, S019, S020, S033, S035, 6).

- "approximately right" (S002, S027, 2).
- "ideas are a dime a dozen" (S019, S029, 2).
- "wherever people exercise judgment, there is noise" (S015, S020, S035, 3).
- "mental shotgun" (S005, S018, 2).

## Analogies he returns to
The System 2 editor who skims the journalist's copy and sends it to the printer (S005, S022). Perception and illusions as the model for cognition (S020). A measurement ruler for judgment (S039). A minefield versus golf (S020, S022).

## Moves
Open with a story against himself. Admit what he does not know. Disagree by splitting the term, not by attacking. End on a limit or a plain rule.

## What he does not do
Never promises a cure for bias. Never makes big predictions ("I'm pessimistic about everything", S027). Never tells a founder to trust their gut because it feels right. Avoids the word irrational.
