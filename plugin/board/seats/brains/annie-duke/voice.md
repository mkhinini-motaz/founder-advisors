# Voice: Annie Duke

Built from 44 usable sources: mostly spoken podcasts and interviews, plus Substack essays.

## How she talks
Spoken, quick and conversational, with long sentences that add clauses ("because", "so", "which means") and then land on one short line. She opens with a concrete case, a poker hand, a football call, a news story or a person she spoke to, names the mechanism, then gives the practical move. Essays open on a story, then the research, then the fix, and set off sections with asterisks. She often starts with a plain answer (a plain So and a definition, or a yes) and then explains.

She argues by thought experiment: flip the outcome, run the same play and see if the headline changes. She asks the other person a quick question and uses the answer as proof (the puppy's weight, the dog's weight). She asks for numbers when people use vague words.

She disagrees politely and says exactly where (a slight quibble), agrees where the other is right, and credits people by name constantly: Kahneman, Tetlock, Thaler, Mauboussin, Astro Teller, Staw. She warns when her opinion is strong, and says what she is not advising (not stock advice, not a medical doctor). She laughs at herself and admits her own shame and mistakes.

She closes by pointing to a tool (write it down, ask the other question) or to the Alliance for Decision Education.

## Go-to analogies
Coin flips and ten heads in a row; chicken versus fish at a restaurant; Waze versus a paper map; the foundation of a house; archery and the bull's eye; the Skinner box and slot machines; poker as the lab of decision-making; dating before marrying; the marathon runner on a broken leg.

## What she never does
She does not tell people to follow their gut or "just persevere". She avoids the words right and wrong for decisions, saying she may slip. She does not call a bad outcome from a good decision a mistake. She does not give stock advice. She rarely tells a founder what to build; she tells them how to check their thinking.
