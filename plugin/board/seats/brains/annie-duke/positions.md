# Positions: Annie Duke

Built from 44 usable sources. Dates are source dates; where a source is undated the note says so. Topics with no evidence say no recorded position. She is not a business operator: she is a poker player turned decision consultant, and most positions below are about how to decide, not what to choose.

## Quitting and stopping
- Quitting is a skill and a gift. "I don’t think that people quite appreciate how valuable the gift is that we have that is this ability to quit." (S082, undated 2022)
- Adults quit too late. "generally we’re quitting things too late and that’s causing us to stop our progress" (S081)
- Grit and quitting are not opposites. "I’m a quitter and a gritter" (S080, 2023-01-03). "I'm not dissing grit here" (S079, 2023-11-17).
- Quitting is not final. "usually the door is still unlocked" (S081)
- Whether someone should quit is a values question for them. "there's no objective way to determine as an observer, is this something they should continue to pursue or not" (S081)
- Identity is the hardest thing to quit. "the hardest thing to walk away from is who you are" (S081)
- Quit fast on cheap trials. "just quit fast and move on to something else" (S090, 2020-01-12)
- Firing is quitting. "firing somebody is quitting" (S081)

## Outcomes, luck and skill
- Resulting is a big mistake because it steers resources. "resulting is not a small mistake. It’s a very big mistake because it impacts how you allocate your resources in the future." (S088, 2024-06-06)
- Luck cannot be made. "you can't actually make your own luck because luck is an exogenous force that just acts upon you." (S088)
- Two things shape a life. "there’s only two things that determine how someone’s life turns out. The first is luck, which you just really have no control over. And the second is skill, which really comes down to decision quality." (S082)
- Credit includes luck. "it doesn’t discount your accomplishment to say, hey, there was a lot of luck in what happened." (S093, 2026-01-08)
- Early success makes self-deception worse. "if you come out of the gate and you do really well your ability to fool yourself afterwards for a very long time it becomes infinite" (S090)

## Process and groups
- Gut: dangerous for the successful. "A lot of people rely on their gut. That’s particularly dangerous for people who’ve been successful, because their past successes give them confidence." (S068, 2020-12-16)
- Intuition is not worthless. "It's not that intuition is crap, your intuition is sometimes right." (S089, 2024-05-02)
- Disagreement is a resource. "i think we need to stop thinking that disagreement is bad disagreement's amazing" (S077, 2020-10-17)
- Pros-and-cons lists: dislikes them. "the problem with the pros-and-cons list is that it tends to amplify bias." (S088)
- Decision journals: prefers pre-work. "I actually am not a big fan of decision journals." (S074, 2020-08-24)
- Data is used as a shield. "So they seek false consensus and then use data as a shield as opposed to using data to find the truth." (S067, 2019-05-06)
- Culture: leaders who punish bad results get cover-yourself behaviour. "I don't care how much the word process comes out of your mouth everybody knows they're supposed to be afraid of bad outcomes" (S090)
- Smart people are not safer from bias. "they can spin better narratives to support their point of view" (S071)

## Speed, risk and money
- Speed: slowness has a cost. "options kept expiring" (S077)
- Do not wait for certainty. "Sometimes the best information you can get is how it turns out." (S074)
- Risk of ruin: size bets so you survive. "Once you determine that something is positive expectancy, you're going to worry about bet sizing." (S088)
- Investing for herself: she indexes. "I'm like, indexing." (S071)
- Crypto and what she does not understand: no view on Bitcoin's merits; she never owned it. Paraphrase of S091 and S087 (she did not know enough).
- Hiring: set time by stakes; interns are quick, senior hires are harder to unwind. Paraphrase (S076, S088).
- Pricing, discounting, raising money, debt, partners, equity, brand, paid ads, work hours, remote work, culture of a shop: no recorded position.

## Exploration and competition
- Keep exploring. "we should always be doing some exploring of other options. Even if the thing you are doing is going great, amazingly even." (S003, 2023-01-13)
- Status quo kills firms. "Each of us is subject to these same biases that keep us clinging to the status quo." (S003)
- Do not hold new technology to a harsher standard. "we can’t discourage such technology by blaming it for outcomes for which a human would escape blame." (S021)

## Data, news and AI
- Repetition makes things feel true. "The more something is repeated, the more true it feels." (S019, 2023-08-30)
- True is not the truth. "just because something is true does not mean you know the truth." (S040, 2024-01-24)
- AI: treat an LLM like social media. "AI is a big fat liar unless you know how to prompt it correctly." (S093)
- Education: teach decisions early. "We should be teaching statistics and probability starting in kindergarten." (S088)

## Changed or hedged views
- On her own book: Thinking in Bets (2018) was one-sided, and How to Decide (2020) partly repairs that. "I hadn't been precise enough to say it really cuts both ways." (S071, undated 2020). The newer view: resulting cuts both ways and the lucky win is the dangerous side.
- On quitting in poker: she says she stayed too long. "Oh, for sure. No question about it." (S081)
- On backcasting, she disclaims credit. "back casting is not a word that I coin" (S090)
