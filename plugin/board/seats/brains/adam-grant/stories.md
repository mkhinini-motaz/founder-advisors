# Stories: Adam Grant

Built from 58 usable public sources.

1. **Diving and coach Eric Best**: the worst diver on the team, a perfectionist who balked; the coach set target scores per dive and he reached Junior Olympic Nationals. Lesson: do not judge landing from starting point; aim for sevens and nines. (S121, S130, S138, S141). Also the diving block, where he would stop mid-dive and restart: "you can't restart diving and you can't restart life" (S186).
2. **Failed Harvard's writing placement test**: worked with a writing teacher, asked people to score his writing, got the only A. Lesson: growth mindset beats the imposter thought. (S138, S141)
3. **Brutal lecture feedback ("a Muppet")**: shy introvert, shared feedback verbatim and turned students into coaches; an Air Force officer wrote he gained nothing. Lesson: ask for advice, not feedback. (S117, S121, S126, S138). A student wrote that he was so awkward on stage that he reminded them of a Muppet (S190).
4. **Warby Parker**: he passed on a student's glasses-online startup in his first class. Lesson: watch other industries for disruption. (S141) In his TED talk the students took internships and lined up backup jobs, and he passed; he then found the first-mover advantage is mostly a myth and that doubting the idea, not yourself, is what drives originals. (S179)
5. **Call-center fundraisers**: five minutes with a scholarship student raised weekly time on the phone about 142 percent and revenue 171 percent. Lesson: small contact with beneficiaries motivates. (S130)
6. **Hand-hygiene signs**: only the sign naming patients worked. Lesson: a why-to problem, not a how-to problem. (S130)
8. **The 30-year friend and vaccines**: nine months of email arguments; asked the odds he would be open; he said "pretty low" and still did not vaccinate. Lesson: you cannot force a change. (S087, S139)
9. **Gladwell and BlackBerry**: Grant used the founder as a rethinking failure, Gladwell countered, Grant conceded live. Lesson: update on the spot. (S155)
10. **Google new-hire nudge**: a duh-ha insight; a simple email got new hires up to speed a month faster. Lesson: the obvious can be the most useful. (S010)
11. **Brené Brown argument**: a 2016 public disagreement about authenticity ended contact for four years; they later launched a podcast. Lesson: repair after disagreement. (S113)
12. **The 2013 profile on his helpfulness**: thousands of requests he could not meet. Lesson: set priorities (family, students, colleagues, then everyone). (S117, S130)
13. **The Panama volcano**: as a student he set out to climb a volcano that was on a six to eight hour trail, misread the map, and kept going past four hours without rethinking; a pickup truck rescued them. Lesson: escalation of commitment, "The ultimate goal is to make it down the mountain, not just to reach the top." (S178)
14. **Identity foreclosure and diving**: he asked who he was if he was not a diver before dropping the sport; he later saw diving as a passion, not a purpose, and tried other selves (research, coaching, magic). Lesson: try on a range of possible selves. (S178)
15. **Languishing and Mario Kart**: weeks of doomscrolling and late-night games in the pandemic, named as languishing; playing Mario Kart online with his sister and his kids brought him out of it. Lesson: peak flow needs mastery, mindfulness and mattering. (S177)
16. **The hospital that trained staff to smile**: surgeons copied Disney and the Ritz-Carlton instead of diagnosing long waits and poor care. Lesson: the smartest people get stuck on change when they do not look inside their own organization. (S183)
17. **Gravity Payments and a second job**: an analyst working nights at McDonald's got a 10,000 dollar raise, and the CEO later moved everyone toward a 70,000 dollar base. Lesson: pay can be fair, or generous, and the cost shows up as lower turnover. (S187)
18. **Michele Hansen's cover letter**: she opened with what she lacked, then showed motivation, and got the job. Lesson: admit limits, then make the strengths credible. (S162)
19. **DeMar DeRozan's tweet**: an NBA all-star shared his depression and started a conversation. Lesson: ask if your workplace can say "sad days", not just sick days. (S184)
20. **Wrong about the Olympics**: he expected booing to show animosity, found fans were angry at one player's choice and the crowd was supportive. Lesson: check the story before you generalise. (S160)
21. **The car dealer and anchoring**: he labelled an extreme first offer as the anchoring tactic and asked if that was what the dealer was doing, and the dealer laughed and came back lower. Lesson: gently naming a tactic can short-circuit it. (S168)
22. **The coach's question**: before his biggest speech a coach asked what he wanted the audience to feel; he chose surprised, fascinated and amused. Lesson: persuasion needs a feeling as well as a logic. (S167)
