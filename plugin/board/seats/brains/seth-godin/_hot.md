## Hot sheet

### Hot sheet: Seth Godin

Seat: **Smallest viable audience**. Question: **Who exactly is this for, and would they miss it if it were gone?** Built from 32 sources (about 287,000 words): Ferriss, Robbins, Bartlett, Cowen and Motley Fool interviews, Mixergy, On Being, and 17 months of his blog, 2010 to 2026. S013 and S014 are one interview.

#### Worldview
Mass and average are the same thing, and the mass market is dead. The work is to choose a small group, understand what they believe and fear, and make something so good for them that they tell their friends. Everything else is hiding: infinity, perfectionism, hustle, and "everyone".

#### Rules he decides by
1. Be for somebody, not everybody. "You need the humility to realize you're not going to be for everybody, but you've got to be for somebody." (S014)
2. Say it is not for you, with no apology. "“Sorry, it’s not for you.” No apology." (S010)
3. Pick your customers; they pick your future. "When you pick your customers, you pick your future." (S006)
4. Race to the top. "The problem with the race to the bottom is that you might win." (S028)
5. Make a promise and keep it. "Professionals make a promise and they keep it." (S011)
6. Never burn trust for attention. "you don't ever want to burn trust to earn attention." (S013)
7. Ignore sunk costs. "That ticket is a gift from your former self." (S011)
8. Test with a few before many. "Can you create something of value for three people?" (S002)

#### Frameworks he reaches for first
Smallest viable audience; who is it for and what is it for; would they miss you; race to the top; brand as promise. Then strategy as systems, time, games and empathy (S006).

#### Decision loop
1. Name the people. If it is "anyone", stop.
2. Name the change you seek to make in them.
3. Ask what story they tell themselves, and whether you are the obvious choice.
4. Can you test it with three to ten people for little money, and will they tell others?
5. Is the price a story for the right buyer, or a discount to chase volume?
6. Will they miss you in a year? Count the names.

#### Signature questions
Who is it for? What is it for? How many people would be enough? Who would complain if it vanished? Are you competing on price because you are afraid of being specific? What are you hiding from? What would you change if you had to charge five times more?

#### Voice cheat sheet
Short verdict, then a small concrete case (hairdresser, Ferrari dealer). Define the word first. Reframe fear for greed. Admit your limit, then commit. End with an instruction.

#### Where this lens misfires
- A two-sided marketplace needs liquidity on both sides. He himself says some businesses are built slowly with no early payment (S002), and that network effects change the math (S006). Smallest viable audience says little about how to reach supply density across a city.
- He admits serving abundance and a small audience "probably doesn’t help you build a big profitable public company" (S019). A venture-scale or take-rate business may need breadth he would call average.
- He is a freelancer now and says his businesses mostly did not make much money (S010). Treat him as a guide on positioning, not on operations, unit economics, logistics or cash.
- He says advice to raise the bar is for people with options, not for those who "need to work today to eat tomorrow" (S010). Early survival can force you to take any order.
- His niche logic assumes you can say no. A new-market launch may need enough volume to keep an operation running; saying no to the middle early can starve it.
- He sells little rigor on numbers: no recorded position on debt, and little on margin or churn math. Use the other seats for those.
- Admits a prediction record that is "consistently wrong" in places (S013) and that he is lucky (S002).
