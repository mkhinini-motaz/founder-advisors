# Stories: Seth Godin

The stories and cases he reuses, from 32 sources. Each is a lesson he draws, with source ids. No quotes here.

1. The curly-hair hairdresser: cuts only curly hair, ten heads a day, sends everyone else down the street. Specificity as a business. (S010)
2. The Ferrari dealer who sends the six-kid family to the Volvo dealer: generosity of being specific; proof you mean your niche is that you refer out. (S006, S010)
3. Yahoo versus Google: Yahoo's home page had 183 links and a stay-here strategy; Google had two links and a come-and-go one. Marketing problem, not a technology one. (S006, S012, S013, S014)
4. Purple Cow: kicked out of publishing, he self-published 10,000 copies in a milk carton, sold at $5; millions followed because people put it on their desk. Attention is earned by spread. (S013, S014)
5. The book packager: 120 books in ten years, and his Stanford-MBA brand got zero sales until he changed it to what the editors needed. Change the story the buyers reject. (S002, S005, S015)
6. Over 100 businesses before one worked, three weeks from bankruptcy six years in a row, 800 to 900 rejection letters. His public record hides private failure. (S004, S015, S019)
7. The altMBA: built in about two weeks after three days in the desert, with a paper prototype and play testing, about seven constraints. Constraints are the point. (S001, S003)
8. VisionSpring in an Indian village: two thirds left without buying glasses; asking people to keep what they could now see or give it back doubled sales. Change the choice, not the product. (S016)
9. Ron Johnson at J.C. Penney: removed the sales and coupons his shoppers loved. No practical empathy. (S016)
10. The Grateful Dead: one Top 40 record, hundreds of millions in tickets, fans who came 42 times. A small devoted audience beats a shallow big one. (S016)
11. Danny Meyer's Shake Shack switched to fresh-cut fries to please critics, then went back; profits rose. Listen to your customers, not to non-customers. (S011)
12. SriPraPhai, the Thai restaurant: served the owner's family food to the edge customer without apology while others dumbed it down for the center. (S012)
13. Squidoo: eight employees, the 40th biggest US website; Google shut it in a day. He has never run a layoff and keeps small promises. (S004, S005)
14. His wife's bakery, By the Way: she asked who needs what she can make, and chose the people left out of the dinner table. She does not bake, which shows the owner's job. (S010, S016)
15. Yoyodyne and email: 82% open and 33% response, then the race to the bottom ruined it. He calls himself naive about it. (S013, S014)
16. The Carbon Almanac: 1,900 volunteers in 90 countries, a book in 150 days, nobody paid, no meetings. Meaning was the pay. (S004, S011, S012)
17. His first internet company had almost no voluntary turnover; he later called that a mistake that cost him a fortune. (S017, S010)
