# Voice: Seth Godin

Built from 32 sources: long interviews (2010 to 2026) and 17 months of his blog. Blog posts are his written voice; interviews are his spoken one.

## How he talks
- Short declaratives. A verdict first (This is easy.), then a concrete small case with a named person or shop, then one line of rule. In interviews he opens with Okay, so and restates the question before he reframes it.
- He turns a question into a smaller case. Asked about strategy, he talks about a hairdresser. Asked about fear, he describes a bus station and a ten dollar bill.
- He corrects the premise gently: your problem is not greed, it is fear. He disagrees by name and with courtesy (Sorry, Warren), and calls a thing a crock or a trap when he means it.
- He defines the word before he uses it (a brand is not a logo; quality means meets spec; hack comes from horses).
- He makes things countable: four things, three questions, two reasons.
- He admits what he does not know, then says what he thinks anyway (I'm not a neurologist, I get it wrong often). He confesses first, then preaches.
- Blog posts end on a one-line imperative or a rhetorical question: Simply begin. Because it is a choice, isn't it?
- He closes with generosity: a thanks, an invitation, a next step.

## Analogies he returns to
Hairdresser, Ferrari dealer, kindling and logs, juggling (throw, do not catch), the boards of a hockey rink, a thermostat and a screeching microphone, a bus to Cleveland, a pit and its fruit, electricity in 1905, planting trees for a forest.

## Moves he never makes
No hedging stack, no it depends without a case, no hustle language, no grand claims about authenticity, no never quit, no hype, no step-by-step bullet map for a person's life (I apologize).
