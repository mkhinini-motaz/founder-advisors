# Frameworks: Morgan Housel

Built from 129 ok sources (about 358k words): his collabfund.com essays 2016 to 2025 and long interviews 2023 to 2026; batch 04 (S018 to S097) folded in. Scoring guidance below is the builder's reading, not his words. Lines he borrows from others (Munger, Buffett, Taleb, Kahneman) are theirs and are marked as such.

## 1. Getting rich vs staying rich
- What it is: two separate skills. Getting rich takes risk and confidence; keeping it takes room for error and paranoia. Success often erodes the second.
- Parts: (1) the getting skill, (2) the keeping skill, (3) the loop where success breeds certainty and certainty breeds fragility, (4) humility to the point of paranoia.
- When to use: any plan that depends on a win staying a win; any founder or shop owner on a hot streak.
- Scoring guidance: score high a plan that names what it would do after a bad year; low a plan whose survival needs everything to go right.
- Quote: "There are a million ways to get rich. But there’s only one way to stay rich: Humility, often to the point of paranoia." (S115)
- Sources: 9 (S106, S115, S116, S162, S016, S352, S363, S283, S073).

## 2. Room for error (margin of safety)
- What it is: build slack so a wrong forecast does not end you. Survival comes first; the edge comes second.
- Parts: financial slack, mental slack (survive an imperfect outcome without being forced out or scared out), plan on the plan failing, avoid forced sales, no single acceptable version of the future.
- When to use: any bet with leverage, a fixed cost, or a cash need on a date.
- Scoring guidance: high if the downside is survivable and no outside party can force a sale; low if one bad month triggers a forced move.
- Quote: "Planning is important, but the most important part of every plan is to plan on the plan not going according to plan." (S116)
- Quote: "Bad decisions happen when there’s only one acceptable version of the future." (S052)
- Sources: 13 (S116, S225, S258, S283, S292, S302, S352, S363, S162, S052, S050, S078, S041).

## 3. The three sides of risk
- What it is: risk has odds, average consequences, and tail consequences. The third is what matters and is learned late.
- Parts: odds of being hit, average cost, tail cost, plus the scars that shape later choices.
- When to use: before any risk you cannot undo.
- Scoring guidance: rank a risk by its worst case and whether it is permanent, not by its likelihood.
- Quote: "The first two are easy to grasp. It’s the third that’s hardest to learn, and can often only be learned through experience." (S294)
- Sources: 5 (S294, S162, S258, S271, S052).

## 4. Tails drive everything
- What it is: a small minority of bets produce most of the result, so most of what you try will fail and that is normal.
- Parts: power-law output, comfort with frequent failure, staying in the game long enough to catch the tail.
- When to use: portfolios of experiments, product lines, marketing tests, careers.
- Scoring guidance: favour a strategy that survives many misses; distrust one that needs every try to work.
- Quote: "no matter what you’re doing, you should be comfortable with a lot of stuff not working. It’s normal." (S251)
- Sources: 9 (S251, S271, S284, S304, S168, S196, S013, S093, S064).

## 5. Expectations vs reality (the goalpost)
- What it is: wealth and happiness are what you have against what you expect. If expectations outrun income, you are never satisfied.
- Parts: the two-part equation, the moving goalpost, the peer group that widens, low expectations as a skill, managing expectations as hard as results.
- When to use: pay, lifestyle, growth targets, team morale.
- Scoring guidance: high if the plan holds an explicit gap between ambition and spending.
- Quote: "If expectations grow faster than income you’ll never be happy with your money." (S117)
- Quote: "All wealth, all success, all accomplishment, is a two-part equation: A result relative to expectations." (S050)
- Sources: 14 (S117, S131, S168, S163, S160, S162, S016, S001, S315, S093, S050, S075, S028, S091).

## 6. Different games
- What it is: people with other time horizons and goals play another game; their advice does not transfer.
- Parts: name your game and horizon, spot whose game a source is playing, ignore cues from the wrong one.
- When to use: when copying a competitor, an influencer or a famous founder.
- Scoring guidance: high if the plan states its own horizon; low if it imitates someone with different constraints.
- Quote: "If you start taking cues from people playing a different game than you are, you are bound to be fooled and eventually become lost" (S311)
- Sources: 9 (S311, S283, S133, S287, S016, S136, S143, S050, S052).

## 7. Reasonable over rational
- What it is: a choice you can stick with beats the optimal one you abandon. Sleeping well is a goal.
- Parts: maximise sleep and pride, accept small itches in small doses, pick the strategy you can endure.
- When to use: when a spreadsheet answer feels unlivable.
- Scoring guidance: high if the owner can hold it through a bad stretch; low if it is optimal only on paper.
- Quote: "Rational is hard. Being reasonable is often the most practical goal." (S210)
- Sources: 5 (S210, S136, S259, S317, S005).

## 8. Expectations, not forecasts
- What it is: expect recessions, crashes and surprises as a class; do not forecast their date.
- Parts: base rates, broad lessons not precise ones, the biggest risk is the one nobody discusses.
- When to use: planning under uncertainty; when someone presents a forecast as a plan.
- Scoring guidance: low for a plan built on a dated prediction; high for one built on base rates.
- Quote: "Try surrounding yourself with more expectations and fewer forecasts." (S238)
- Sources: 11 (S238, S106, S162, S258, S284, S324, S339, S126, S271, S058, S029).

## 9. Calm plants the seeds of crazy (cycles)
- What it is: good times breed complacency and leverage; bad times breed problem-solving. Regular is not predictable. A cycle needs no outside push: getting big is enough to make you smaller.
- Parts: greed and fear cycle, bubble versus cycle, unsustainable things last longer than expected, success plants the seeds of loss, the traits that drive growth also cause collapse.
- When to use: after a streak of good results or a rush of copycats.
- Scoring guidance: flag any plan that extrapolates the last two years in a straight line.
- Quote: "Calm plants the seeds of crazy." (S271)
- Quote: "Calm plants the seeds of crazy, growth plants the seeds of decline, success plants the seeds of loss. Everything is cyclical." (S091)
- Sources: 11 (S271, S115, S197, S260, S302, S214, S106, S091, S073, S076, S033).

## 10. Most convenient size (too much, too soon, too fast)
- What it is: every system has a speed and size at which it works; past it quality falls.
- Parts: natural growth rate, culture does not scale, respect the horizon (about ten years for investing).
- When to use: any plan to open fast, hire fast, add locations or products.
- Scoring guidance: low for growth that compresses the time horizon; high for growth paced by quality.
- Quote: "Too much, too soon, too fast." (S309)
- Sources: 4 (S309, S163, S196, S276).

## 11. Brand as knowing what to expect
- What it is: consumers hate surprises more than they enjoy a shot at perfection. Be good all the time.
- Parts: repetition, consistency across the whole line, familiarity over quality, selling out as the risk of scale.
- When to use: service quality, offers, lead times, marketing promises.
- Scoring guidance: high for tight outcomes; low for great-sometimes, awful-sometimes.
- Quote: "The big insight on brands is that consumers hate surprises more than they enjoy the chance at perfection." (S276)
- Sources: 6 (S276, S162, S214, S303, S049, S083).

## 12. Behaviour beats analytics
- What it is: how people act with money matters more than what they know. Good behaviour with little data beats the reverse.
- Parts: keep the plan simple, communicate the soft side to clients, remove temptation rather than lean on willpower.
- When to use: pricing, incentives, field-staff and shop behaviour, any plan that relies on discipline.
- Scoring guidance: high if the design removes the tempting option; low if it relies on people being rational.
- Quote: "The behavioral side of investing will always be more important than the analytical side" (S271)
- Sources: 9 (S271, S283, S279, S287, S334, S259, S295, S001, S082).

## 13. Incentives bend good people
- What it is: incentives tell convincing stories and pull honest people toward bad conduct.
- Parts: who gets paid for what, the Goodhart and Campbell effects, do not assume a moral boundary.
- When to use: commission, bonus, quota, ranking design.
- Scoring guidance: low for any metric that can be gamed with no cost to the player.
- Quote: "Incentives are expert storytellers, able to tell a convincing narrative in anyone’s head that the actions they’re promoting are actually good." (S334)
- Sources: 7 (S334, S157, S319, S317, S132, S052, S033).

## 14. Innocently out of touch
- What it is: beliefs come from lived experience, so disagreement is usually different histories, not stupidity.
- Parts: ask what the other person experienced, widen your sources, expect your view to be a fraction.
- When to use: when a field worker, shop or customer reacts unlike you expected.
- Scoring guidance: high for a plan tested with people unlike the author.
- Quote: "What have you experienced that I haven’t that makes you believe what you do?" (S196)
- Sources: 7 (S196, S302, S327, S363, S127, S356, S083).

## 15. Tool versus yardstick
- What it is: each dollar is a tool for a better life or a yardstick against strangers. Saving buys independence.
- Parts: the desert island test, social debt, who you want admiration from, the humble bubble.
- When to use: owner pay, branding spend, a status purchase.
- Scoring guidance: high if the spend survives the desert island test.
- Quote: "if nobody could see how I was living, if nobody could see my house, my car, my clothes" (S001)
- Sources: 5 (S001, S002, S016, S208, S255).

## 16. Base rates and levels of confidence
- What it is: begin any forecast with the success rate of everyone who tried it before you, then adjust only for traits that are truly yours. He lists fourteen levels of confidence; base rates are level eight, and the list loops back to level one.
- Parts: base rate, the adjustment for unique traits, the inflated sense of your own uniqueness (level nine), "I don't know" and the average (level ten), preparing to be wrong (level twelve), success breeding overreach.
- When to use: any projection of orders, shops signed, or a new product's chances.
- Scoring guidance: high if the plan quotes the outcome of similar attempts; low if it rests on "we are different". Guidance, not his thresholds.
- Quote: "Base Rates: The success rate of everyone who’s done what you’re about to try." (S018)
- Quote: "Nine times out of ten the only confidence you have is that your ability to predict something will be similar to everyone else’s attempt at predicting something." (S078)
- Sources: 2 (S018, S078).

## 17. Feedback loops (it is usually crazier than you expect)
- What it is: small trends, once they start, feed on themselves, so prices, fame and collapses overshoot. Cumulative advantage and the Matthew effect are the same loop.
- Parts: a trend, a loop where one event fuels the next, attention that sustains itself, momentum a spreadsheet cannot model.
- When to use: a sudden surge in orders, a shop's rating spiral, a viral post, a failing competitor.
- Scoring guidance: low for a plan that assumes a smooth line through a loop. Guidance only.
- Quote: "The reason is usually small trends, once they get going, take on a life of their own, so things are usually crazier than you’d expect." (S071)
- Sources: 3 (S071, S076, S018).

## 18. Bet on what never changes
- What it is: hold the few things that stay constant and adapt what evolves. Change drives competition; constancy drives compounding. Permanent information teaches you what to do with expiring information.
- Parts: list the timeless wants (lower prices, faster solutions, control over time, more choices, comfort, trust), sort information by how long it will matter, check whether an old rule still fits the era.
- When to use: choosing what to build, what to promise customers, which advice to trust.
- Scoring guidance: high for a plan rooted in a lasting customer want; low for one that depends on a fad or an expiring tactic.
- Quote: "Every successful investment is some combination of change that drives competition and things staying the same that drives compounding." (S055)
- Quote: "Permanent information is what teaches you what to do with expiring information." (S081)
- Sources: 5 (S055, S081, S097, S087, S072).

## 19. Dangerous feelings after success
- What it is: three feelings that creep in once you win: the paranoia that made you drops away; you look for others' flaws rather than your own improvements; you feel you have mastered an evolving field.
- Parts: the paranoia loop, an own-improvement yardstick, a field that keeps changing, quitting while ahead.
- When to use: a founder after a good year; any team that has copied its own playbook for a while.
- Scoring guidance: high if someone is assigned to argue the other side; low if the team measures itself by rivals' errors.
- Quote: "Paranoia leads to success because it keeps you on your toes. But paranoia is stressful, so you abandon it quickly once you achieve success." (S073)
- Sources: 4 (S073, S064, S091, S097).

## 20. Common plots of economic history
- What it is: a few plots recur in every story: a good idea taken to the extreme; an invincible advantage squandered; future progress underrated; surprises from power laws; belief in things not yet true that drives growth and decline.
- Parts: the five plots, and for squandered advantage the forces of confidence, growth changing the organisation, one-trick devotion and unmasked luck.
- When to use: reading a boom, a competitor's collapse, or your own success.
- Scoring guidance: flag a plan that treats a good idea as having no upper limit. Guidance only.
- Quote: "Money and brains are attracted to good ideas more than they are capable of knowing the limits of good ideas." (S064)
- Quote: "The only thing harder than gaining a competitive edge is not losing an advantage when you have one." (S064)
- Sources: 3 (S064, S070, S076).

## 21. Counterintuitive advantages
- What it is: a few things that look like weaknesses help: a little debt, a clean start, a low valuation, just enough competition. An advantage can also seed your undoing. He flags this "hurts admitting because I’m so against debt" (S070).
- Parts: scarcity as a teacher, necessity as a spark, a forced restart, rivals that keep you sharp.
- When to use: a site or team that starts with few resources; a founder who wants to avoid every constraint.
- Scoring guidance: high if constraint is manageable and survivable; he warns stress can turn from motivating to debilitating (S062).
- Quote: "Business competitors destroy each other; businesses without competitors destroy themselves." (S070)
- Quote: "The best way to avoid too much stress is to constantly surround yourself with a little of it, keeping yourself just scared enough to fight off the complacency that leads to serious problems." (S062)
- Sources: 3 (S070, S062, S064).

## 22. Different kinds of easy (the delayed cost)
- What it is: things look easy when cost comes after the benefit or is hidden. Six reasons: delay between benefit and cost, others' role, luck, diminished advantage, backstage labour, viewing a task in isolation.
- Parts: price the cost beside the reward; ask who did the unseen work; ask how much was luck.
- When to use: any claim that a model, shop or founder made it look simple.
- Scoring guidance: low for a plan with all reward and no named cost. Guidance only.
- Quote: "Whenever there’s a delay between benefit and cost, the benefits always seem easier than they are." (S080)
- Quote: "It’s a package deal, and you can’t pick and choose the reward while ignoring the cost." (S058)
- Sources: 3 (S080, S058, S028).

## 23. Appealing fictions and active disconfirmation
- What it is: an appealing fiction is something you want to be true, with some data behind it, one layer deep. The cure is trying to prove yourself wrong, as Darwin did.
- Parts: want it true, thin evidence, one layer of depth; dig deeper, borrow from other fields, assume you are still wrong.
- When to use: a business case that confirms what the owner hoped.
- Scoring guidance: high if the plan lists what would show it wrong. Guidance only.
- Quote: "Real open-mindedness is like Darwin: Trying as hard as you can to disconfirm your own ideas, even when you want them to be right." (S049)
- Sources: 3 (S049, S073, S083).

## 24. Cumulative vs cyclical knowledge
- What it is: some fields pass knowledge down across generations; money knowledge cycles because it must be lived to be believed, so the same mistakes come back.
- Parts: no formula, subjective, experienced firsthand; accept volatility and fragility as a result.
- When to use: when someone says this time people will have learned.
- Scoring guidance: low for a plan that needs customers or investors to have learned from the last cycle. Guidance only.
- Quote: "Even when firm financial rules exist, some truths have to be experienced firsthand to be understood." (S072)
- Sources: 3 (S072, S087, S075).

## Books

### Books: Morgan Housel

Built from 129 ok sources (about 358k words). One entry per book. The notes carry no book text, so these are my own summaries of how he points to the books in essays and interviews.

#### The Psychology of Money (2020)
His first book. Short stories on how people behave with money: luck and risk, enough, getting and staying wealthy, room for error, and tails. S283 (2018) is a free essay report on the same ideas, not book text. He waited about 14 years to write a book and is glad he did (S013). Critics say it speaks from a college-educated, US, male position; he agrees (S206, S005).

#### Same As Ever (2023)
Short chapters on things that do not change in human behaviour, and the forecasts that rest on them. Excerpts appear in S358 (Wild Minds) and S330. Core idea: bet on what never changes. (S055, S127)

#### The Art of Spending Money (2025)
Spending as a mirror of what a person values: tool versus yardstick, social debt, utility versus status, contentment over happiness. The 2025 interviews S001, S002 and S003 discuss it. (S001, S002, S003)
