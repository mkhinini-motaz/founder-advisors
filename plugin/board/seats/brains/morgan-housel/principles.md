# Principles: Morgan Housel

Built from 129 ok sources (about 358k words); batch 04 (S018 to S097) folded in. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

## 1. Survive first
Rule: staying in the game matters more than any single return.
- "If I had to summarize money success in a single word it would be “survival.”" (S116)
- "Everyone’s odds of winning are proportionate to their odds of sticking around." (S352)
Supporting sources: 8 (adds S041, S078). Confidence: high.

## 2. Never risk what you need for what you do not
Rule: no upside justifies a risk that could wipe you out.
- "Never risk what you need in pursuit of what you don’t." (S311)
- "No risk that could wipe you out is ever worth taking." (S283)
Supporting sources: 3. Confidence: high.

## 3. Plan for the plan failing
Rule: the more a plan needs specific things to be true, the weaker it is.
- "The more you need specific elements of a plan to be true, the more fragile your financial life becomes." (S116)
Supporting sources: 7 (adds S052, S050). Confidence: high.

## 4. Do not interrupt compounding
Rule: good returns held a long time beat great returns interrupted. Mostly do nothing.
- "Merely good returns sustained uninterrupted for the longest period of time – especially in times of chaos and havoc – will always win." (S116)
- "“Don’t do anything,” are the most powerful words in finance." (S283)
Supporting sources: 6. Confidence: high.

## 5. Keep a gap between ego and income
Rule: expectations must grow slower than income.
- "The only way to build wealth is to have a gap between your ego and your income." (S162)
- "it’s just making sure that there's a gap between your net worth and your expectations" (S016)
Supporting sources: 9 (adds S093, S050, S028). Confidence: high.

## 6. Obsess over permanent damage
Rule: guard against risks that cannot be undone; shrug at temporary harm.
- "You should obsess over risks that do permanent damage and care little about risks that do temporary harm, but the opposite is more common." (S162)
Supporting sources: 3. Confidence: high.

## 7. Do not forecast, expect
Rule: expect crashes and surprises as a class; do not predict the date.
- "Unsustainable things can sustain for a long time." (S106)
- "History is driven by surprising events but forecasting is driven by obvious ones." (S162)
Supporting sources: 11 (adds S058, S029). Confidence: high.

## 8. Read history for behaviour, not detail
Rule: take broad lessons; specific lessons fit the last war.
- "The more specific a lesson of history is, the less relevant it becomes." (S106)
- "Read fewer forecasts and more history. Study more failures and fewer successes." (S162)
Supporting sources: 10 (adds S087, S063, S072, S064). Confidence: high.

## 9. Choose a strategy you can endure
Rule: what you can hold through a bad stretch beats what is optimal on paper.
- "It’s about earning pretty good returns that you can stick with for a long period of time." (S283)
- "Rational is hard. Being reasonable is often the most practical goal." (S210)
Supporting sources: 5. Confidence: high.

## 10. Do not take cues from people in another game
Rule: name your game and horizon first.
- "Few things matter more with money than understanding your own time horizon and not being persuaded by the actions and behaviors of people playing different games." (S283)
Supporting sources: 7 (adds S050, S052). Confidence: high.

## 11. Ask what they lived through
Rule: before judging a view, ask what experience produced it.
- "What have you experienced that I haven’t that makes you believe what you do?" (S196)
Supporting sources: 5. Confidence: high.

## 12. Slow growth builds the durable thing
Rule: pace growth to what the system can absorb.
- "Every business and every industry has a natural growth rate – push beyond it and short-term growth comes at the cost of long-term quality, and eventually survival." (S163)
- "Respect the most convenient state." (S309)
Supporting sources: 4. Confidence: high.

## 13. Consistency is the brand
Rule: be good all the time rather than great some of the time.
- "Maybe we’ll look back and realize that the best way to grow big is to have a powerful brand, and the best way to build a powerful brand is to be good all the time rather than great some of the time." (S276)
Supporting sources: 3. Confidence: high.

## 14. Be comfortable when most things fail
Rule: results come from a few tails; expect many misses.
- "The takeaway from tails is that you should be comfortable when a lot of what you do and see doesn’t work." (S271)
Supporting sources: 5. Confidence: high.

## 15. Success is a hazard
Rule: after a win, guard against certainty, size and last-war thinking.
- "The more successful you are at something, the more convinced you become that you’re doing it right." (S115)
- "Adaptation is key to survival, and willingness to adapt is often the inverse of however successful you were in the past." (S352)
Supporting sources: 8 (adds S073, S091, S064). Confidence: high.

## 16. Hold strategies loosely; strategies expire
Rule: know what is timeless (behaviour) and what is not (tactics), and ask how you will know a strategy has stopped working.
- "How do you know when your strategy has permanently stopped working?" (S183)
- "Most tactical changes are regretted." (S296)
Supporting sources: 5. Confidence: high.

## 17. Ask what bet you are really making
Rule: many bets fail because the hidden bet differed from the stated one.
- "Many bets don’t work not because your bet was wrong, but because you didn’t realize the bet you were making in the first place." (S162)
Supporting sources: 2. Confidence: medium.

## 18. Money buys independence, not happiness
Rule: use money for control over your time.
- "Money’s greatest intrinsic value is its ability to give you control over your time." (S162)
- "all I want out of money is independence." (S001)
Supporting sources: 6. Confidence: high.

## 19. Be careful with incentives
Rule: do not assume people draw a moral line around a reward.
- "Don’t give people incentives and assume they’ll draw a moral boundary around them." (S334)
Supporting sources: 5 (adds S052, S033). Confidence: high.

## 20. Stories beat statistics
Rule: the best-told idea wins, so put the truth in a story.
- "It’s always the case that it’s not the best idea, it’s not the right answer, it’s not the correct answer, it’s the best story." (S013)
Supporting sources: 5. Confidence: high.

## 21. The long run is a string of short runs
Rule: you reach the long term only by surviving each short run, and even a confident bet needs room for error.
- "Short-term thinking can be the only way you’ll survive long enough to experience long-term results." (S041)
- "That gives you confidence that you can survive long enough to bet on the next thing you’re confident about." (S078)
Supporting sources: 3 (S041, S078, S082). Confidence: high.

## 22. Start from the base rate
Rule: begin any forecast with the success rate of everyone who tried it, and assume you are average unless data says otherwise.
- "Base Rates: The success rate of everyone who’s done what you’re about to try." (S018)
- "Nine times out of ten the only confidence you have is that your ability to predict something will be similar to everyone else’s attempt at predicting something." (S078)
Supporting sources: 3 (S018, S078, S052). Confidence: high.

## 23. Price the cost with the reward
Rule: distrust anything that looks easy; every reward has a cost, often delayed or paid in stress.
- "It’s a package deal, and you can’t pick and choose the reward while ignoring the cost." (S058)
- "Everything worthwhile has a cost, so few things worth pursuing are even a shade of easy." (S080)
Supporting sources: 4 (S058, S080, S028, S033). Confidence: high.

## 24. Know what enough is
Rule: be able to turn down an extra dollar, and decide your goal so you can tell when you have met it.
- "So having some ability to deny an extra dollar of work, or a potential opportunity, a bigger house or a nicer car, is essential if you want to use money to make a better life." (S050)
- "Refusing to recognize that you’ve met your goal can be as bad as never meeting the goal to begin with." (S028)
Supporting sources: 2 (S050, S028). Confidence: medium.

## 25. Bet on what does not change
Rule: root a strategy in constant human wants; test a business for what stays the same, not only what changes.
- "You can make big, long-term bets on these things, because there’s no chance people will stop caring about them in the future." (S055)
- "To me, the point of paying attention to history is not the specific details of certain events, which are always random and never repeat; it’s the big-picture behaviors that reoccur in different eras, generations, and societies." (S087)
Supporting sources: 4 (S055, S087, S097, S081). Confidence: high.

## 26. Expertise expires
Rule: the most experienced adapt least, and success lifts confidence faster than ability, so keep updating.
- "The biggest risk to an evolving system is that you become bogged down by experts from a world that no longer exists." (S097)
- "Success has a nasty tendency to increase confidence more than ability." (S073)
Supporting sources: 3 (S097, S073, S083). Confidence: high.

## 27. Look for the loop
Rule: when a price, a fame or a collapse looks absurd, look for a feedback loop; success snowballs apart from skill.
- "The reason is usually small trends, once they get going, take on a life of their own, so things are usually crazier than you’d expect." (S071)
- "Equal talent does not get equal recognition because customers, employers, and fans like associating with known winners." (S076)
Supporting sources: 3 (S071, S076, S018). Confidence: high.

## 28. A little necessity helps
Rule: scarcity and a little stress sharpen you, but stress pushed too far debilitates.
- "Nothing sparks resourcefulness like necessity and scarcity." (S062)
- "Scared and running > fat and happy." (S070)
Supporting sources: 3 (S062, S070, S064). Confidence: high. Limit, in his words: "Stress can quickly go from motivating to debilitating." (S062)

## 29. Try to prove yourself wrong
Rule: open-mindedness is active; seek disconfirming evidence, and argue why you might be wrong, not only why the other side is.
- "And the irony is that you’re more likely to be right if you’re constantly trying to prove yourself wrong." (S049)
- "A dangerous feeling is when your position on a topic centers around criticizing the other side versus evaluating why you’re right – or even better, why you might be wrong." (S073)
Supporting sources: 3 (S049, S073, S078). Confidence: high.

## 30. The luckier you are, the nicer you should be
Rule: do not read all of an outcome as skill; extreme success and failure sit close to luck.
- "My rule of thumb to avoid that form of bullshit is that the luckier you are the nicer you should be." (S058)
- "Knowing you’re part of a group but thinking your talents directly lead to your results is cognitive dissonance on steroids." (S080)
Supporting sources: 2 (S058, S080). Confidence: medium.
