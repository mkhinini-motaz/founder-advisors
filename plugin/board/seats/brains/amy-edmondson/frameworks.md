# Frameworks: Amy Edmondson

Built from 54 sources, 2016 to 2026 (talks, podcasts, webinars). Most-used first. "Scoring" lines are guidance written for the council, not her words.

## 1. Three kinds of failure: basic, complex, intelligent
- What: a sorting step before any judgment. Basic: one cause, usually human error, in known territory. Complex: several factors line up, each harmless alone, in familiar territory. Intelligent: the undesired result of a thoughtful foray into new territory. Type is not size: "basic doesn't mean small" (S074), e.g. a $900 million wire sent by ticking the wrong box.
- When: any time something has gone wrong, before deciding blame or celebration.
- Scoring: ask how much was already known. If a recipe existed, it is basic or complex: prevent and do not celebrate. If nobody could have looked it up, it may be intelligent.
- Quote: "The third kind of failure are intelligent failures, and those are the undesired results of thoughtful forays into new territory." (S049)
- Sources: about 30, including S049, S050, S055, S060, S061, S062, S063, S068, S069, S071, S073, S074, S076, S078, S080, S081, S083, S085, S089, S092, S094, S099, S101, S102, S105.

## 2. Four and a half tests of an intelligent failure
- Parts: (1) genuinely new territory, you could not look up the answer; (2) in pursuit of a credible goal; (3) homework done, a hypothesis stated in advance; (4) no bigger than necessary; (4.5) you learn from it and share it.
- When: before any uncertain move (pilot, new product, hire), not after.
- Scoring: all four must pass; failing one is a lesson, not an intelligent failure. Ray Dalio's 1982 bet met the first three and failed the fourth (S074, S094, S096).
- Quote: "an intelligent failure in your organization the second time around is no longer intelligent" (S074)
- Sources: S050, S055, S060, S061, S063, S068, S074, S083, S089, S092, S094, S099, S101, S102, S105 (15+).

## 3. Set the stage, invite, respond (frame, ask, respond)
- Parts: before, frame the work as uncertain and interdependent so voice is needed; during, ask real questions; after, respond to bad news with appreciation and a look forward.
- When: any leader, at any level, who wants early bad news.
- Scoring: count what the last person who brought you bad news got back. Thanks and "how can I help" is a pass. A bitten head is a fail and it lasts.
- Quote: "setting the stage, inviting input, and then responding to what people say in a thoughtful and interested way." (S059)
- Sources: S048, S055, S056, S059, S064, S066, S070, S071, S079, S084, S085, S087, S090, S092, S093, S094, S097, S101, S104, S105 (20+).

## 4. Safety by standards: four zones
- Parts: low both is apathy; safety without standards is comfort; standards without safety is anxiety; both high is learning, the same place as high performance. Two independent axes, not one dial.
- When: a leader asks will safety lower my bar?
- Scoring: place the team. A high-pressure team that hides errors is in the anxiety zone, "the one that keeps me up at night" (S105).
- Quote: "it's a both/and story not an either-or story" (S092)
- Sources: S048, S058, S061, S067, S081, S084, S087, S092, S093, S102, S105 (11).

## 5. Context by stakes: consistent, variable, novel
- Parts: consistent work (assembly line): follow the recipe. Variable work (operating room, supply chain): vigilance, since complex failures live here. Novel work (lab): experiments. Cross it with stakes: human safety first, then money, then reputation.
- Scoring: high stakes and known: execute flawlessly. High stakes and uncertain: break it into small pieces. Low stakes and uncertain: "go to town". She sees leaders err both ways (S049).
- Quote: "And if the stakes are very high and yet it’s high certainty, please follow the protocols vigilantly carefully." (S049)
- Sources: S049, S050, S055, S060, S062, S074, S080, S082, S089, S092, S094, S099, S102 (13).

## 6. Green and red: listen for what you are not hearing
- What: track the ratio of good news to bad, agreement to dissent. In an uncertain world there is red everywhere, so all green means silence.
- Scoring: if a week of reports is mostly green and the work is uncertain, assume people are holding back and go hunt for red. Routine work is the exception (S058).
- Quote: "if you're only hearing the green and no red, it's probably suboptimal." (S058)
- Sources: S058, S059, S070, S074, S075, S083, S084, S094, S095, S099 (10).

## 7. Reported errors are not real errors
- What: counts of errors are detected, not actual rates. Her first study found better hospital units reported more errors. Read a gap as a possible reporting gap first.
- Scoring: before ranking teams by error count, ask which team feels safe enough to count honestly.
- Quote: "we don't know the actual error rates. What we actually have measured are detected or reported error rates." (S050)
- Sources: S047, S048, S050, S055, S056, S060, S063, S069, S073, S080, S084, S087, S092, S095 (14).

## 8. Choose learning over knowing; stop, challenge, choose
- Parts: self-awareness tagline, plus a three-beat habit (from Larry Wilson): stop the hot reaction, challenge whether it rests on fact, choose a healthier response, at least curiosity.
- When: the moment you hear bad news about your own work or someone disagrees with you.
- Quote: "the single tagline for this topic for me is choose learning over knowing." (S055)
- Sources: S055, S060, S062, S063, S064, S067, S069, S071, S075, S081, S094, S097, S102 (13).

## 9. Three awarenesses: self, situation, system
- Parts: self (blind spots, jumping to conclusions), situation (what is at stake, how much is known), system (who and what else is affected, now and later).
- Scoring: pause on every consequential decision and ask the three. Pair it with the pre-mortem.
- Quote: "choose learning over knowing" (S060)
- Sources: S060, S080, S103 (3, low).

## 10. The good question
- What: a question you do not know the answer to, about something that matters, with room to answer. Broad (what are we missing, who sees it differently) or deep (what leads you to think so, what data).
- Scoring: one of each, daily. Avoid yes or no and leading questions.
- Quote: "a good question is one that a you don't know the answer for" (S077)
- Sources: S064, S065, S077, S078, S084, S085, S088, S091, S092, S097, S105 (11).

## 11. Productive versus interpersonal fear
- What: fear of the virus, a deadline or a competitor is shared, discussable and fine. Fear of colleagues is private and leaves you alone. Fear motivates hiding, and works only on simple, individual, observable work.
- Quote: "I don't think there is ever a useful amount of interpersonal fear." (S082)
- Sources: S070, S082, S087, S088, S092, S093, S095, S097 (8).

## 12. Pilot diagnostics (the telco case)
- Parts: is it tested under typical conditions or rigged to succeed; is the goal to learn or to prove; are pay and ratings independent of the result; were changes made after it.
- Scoring: any wrong answer and the pilot is a demonstration. A good pilot fails small behind closed doors so the launch succeeds.
- Quote: "I'm going to say failed by succeeding it should have succeeded by failing" (S083)
- Sources: S074, S083, S085, S094, S099 (5).

## 13. Rethinking excellence
- Parts: drive basic failures out (checklists, training, error-proofing, the Andon cord), anticipate and mitigate complex failures (amplify weak signals), welcome intelligent failures.
- Quote: "We're not in the perfection business." (S094)
- Sources: S074, S083, S094, S099, S101, S105 (6).

## 14. Teaming (verb, not noun) and execution as learning
- Parts: work happens across shifting groups, so put the lens on the skills (curiosity, inquiry, advocacy with openness), not on fixed structure. Execution is itself a learning process.
- Quote: "it's not learning then execution it's execution with a learning mindset" (S067)
- Sources: S061, S064, S065, S066, S067, S082, S085, S091 (8).

## 15. Quality of conversation (three tests)
- Parts: people contribute or listen hard; a healthy mix of statements and real questions; a sense of getting smarter.
- Scoring: apply to consequential decisions (hire, launch, acquisition), not to the party venue.
- Quote: "there's a sense that we're getting smarter." (S103)
- Sources: S062, S076, S103 (3, low).

## 16. Make it discussible
- What: name the problem, name the bind, name your own part, ask for help. Hiding the stress response is the harm, not having it.
- Quote: "What isn't okay is not to make it discussible." (S103)
- Sources: S075, S076, S094, S103, S104 (5).

## 17. Pixar Brain Trust
- What: peer-based, project not person, comments are suggestions, the owner keeps the decision.
- Quote: "the critique is of the movie not of the person" (S077)
- Sources: S077, S079, S093 (3).

## 18. Discounting the future (the 95 percent rule)
- What: embarrassment now looms large, harm later looks fuzzy, so people speak only when about 95 percent sure of a good reception. The gray zone is where they hold back.
- Quote: "they hold back in the gray zone they hold back when they have a worry but they don't have enough data to support it" (S079)
- Sources: S079, S087 (2, medium).

## 19. Fuel and brakes
- What: safety takes the foot off the brake. Purpose and motivation are the fuel. Safety alone is a recipe for comfort.
- Quote: "psychological safety is like taking your foot off the brakes" (S068)
- Sources: S048, S068, S075, S082 (4).

## Books

### Books: Amy Edmondson

Built from 54 sources. Her own words are quoted only from talks. No book text is reproduced.

#### The Right Kind of Wrong: The Science of Failing Well (2023)
- Thesis: failure is not one thing. Sort it into basic, complex and intelligent; prevent the first two and welcome the third. Won the FT and Schroders business book of the year (S094).
- Frameworks: three failure types; four and a half tests; context by stakes; three awarenesses (self, situation, system); practices of elite failure practitioners; last chapter on thriving as a fallible human being.
- Taught in: S049, S050, S055, S058, S060, S063, S066, S071, S073, S074, S078, S080, S083, S086, S089, S094, S095, S096, S099, S101, S102.
- She says it is a thinking guide, not an instruction manual (S101).

#### The Fearless Organization (2018)
- Thesis: psychological safety, a climate where people can speak up, is the soil for learning, innovation and the prevention of preventable failure. Three leader moves: set the stage, invite, respond.
- Cases: Volkswagen, Wells Fargo, Columbia, Pixar, Anglo American, Google's study.
- She says safety is "the soil but by no means the seed" (S079).
- Taught in: S047, S056, S059, S062, S065, S069, S070, S075, S077, S079, S084, S090, S092, S093, S097, S098, S104, S105.

#### Teaming (2012)
- Thesis: teamwork is a verb; teams form and dissolve around work, so skills (inquiry, advocacy, boundary spanning) matter more than structure. Execution as learning.
- Taught in: S061, S064, S065, S066, S067, S088, S085.

#### Building the Future: Big Teaming for Audacious Innovation (2019)
- Thesis: cross-industry teaming for large projects; adaptable vision, safety, knowledge sharing across boundaries, execution as learning. Taught in S091.

#### A Fuller Explanation (her first book)
- On Buckminster Fuller's geometry, written after she worked as his engineer. Mentioned in S047, S071, S090, S096.

#### Others she names
- Building a Fearless Organization (field book, S085). Teaming to Innovate and Extreme Teaming (S056, S090).
- Cited with approval: Ed Catmull's Creativity, Inc.; Edgar Schein's Humble Inquiry; Chris Argyris on advocacy and inquiry; Sam Culbert on the performance preview; Daniel Coyle's The Culture Code; Carol Dweck on mindset.
