# Positions: Robert Cialdini

Built from 16 sources (quick depth). Most topics are his views on influence; he is a social psychologist, not an operator. No recorded position means the sources hold nothing. Dates come from sources.md; many are undated.

- **Ethics of persuasion.** Point to truths that exist. "it can be completely ethical, because you’re simply pointing to truths that already exist" (S014, 2026).
- **Manipulation.** He calls artificial scarcity by limited production manipulation (S003). Fake same-hour viewings of used cars are "entirely dishonest" (S003).
- **Trickery.** "As soon as they recognize the trickery, they’re gone to you." (S008, 2021).
- **Short-term versus long-term.** "Dishonest or high-pressure tactics work only in the short run, if at all." (S019, 2021 repost of a 2001 piece).
- **Pricing.** Precise numbers earn more assent. "people assume that you have done your homework" (S028). A large anchor first makes your price look small: "I won't be able to charge you a million dollars for this." (S015).
- **Discounting.** No recorded position.
- **Scarcity in marketing.** Real rarity is honest. "They are not pointing to something that is rare or unique and is dwindling." (S003). Limited quantity beats limited time: "limited quantity forces immediate action." (S014).
- **Supply spikes.** "The problem is ramping it up too fast." (S026). Coors and Peloton lost the pull of scarcity (S026).
- **Online reviews.** Five stars looks too good. "it is a sweet spot of between 4.2 and 4.7 stars." (S003). Say "reviewers", not reviews (S026).
- **Bad reviews.** Reply and move attention to the majority (S026).
- **Authority.** Show real expertise first. "What I mean by authority is expertise, knowledge, credentials on a particular topic." (S007).
- **Authority online.** Shifted toward peers. "Yelp, it’s about people like me." (S003).
- **Brand and trust.** Admitting a weakness builds trust so strengths are believed (S012).
- **Content and free samples.** Give your best material free; a sample proves worth (S014)..
- **Hiring and interviews.** Ask why they invited you; they list your strengths. "Why did you invite me here today?" (S028).
- **Management and meetings.** "don’t let anybody out of that meeting until you say, will you be able to properly finish this task by our next meeting?" (S007).
- **Leadership.** "leaders should model the behavior they want to see from others." (S019).
- **Culture.** Dishonesty toward outsiders breeds a cheating culture (S029).
- **Technology.** "what has changed with these new digital platforms and routes to possible influence is the technology, but not the psychology." (S011, AI transcript; same idea in S026 and S029).
- **AI.** No recorded position.
- **Metrics and tests.** "multiple data points tell what a single datapoint can’t say." (S008). Test across settings; the best approach varies (S005).
- **Culture differences.** "the weights assigned to the various rules can differ across cultures." (S018).
- **Politics.** Avoids live campaigns (S025); says liking carried Reagan and Clinton (S025).
- **Focus and writing.** Waited years for an idea big enough for a book (S025, 2016; S029, 2018). Regret: waiting for tenure (S026).
- **Happiness and profit.** "what we really want in our businesses is to be happy, not to be profitable necessarily." (S029).
- **Retirement and work hours.** He retired to write Pre-Suasion and needs unitary focus (S012, 2017).
- **Raising money, debt, partners, equity, firing, selling the company, competition, burnout.** No recorded position.
- **Tipping and gifts.** A small personalized extra raises tips; gifts to physicians and legislators move behavior (S003).
- **Charity requests.** He keeps a filter: "We do this one a year" (S026).
- **Changed view.** On social proof by share: he now advises showing a rising trend, not a bare low share. "I don’t say that anymore because of new research" (S026, undated; newer than the 2001 articles S018 and S019 that warned only against showing bad behavior as common).
