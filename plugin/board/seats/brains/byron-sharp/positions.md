# Positions: Byron Sharp

His stances on founder questions, from blog posts (2005 to 2020), a 2022 talk write-up and a 2025 podcast. Quick depth: where the sources are silent the entry says no recorded position. Cite the newer view when dated sources differ.

## Loyalty programs
- "Well, the research is pretty clear that they don’t do a lot." (S001, 2025)
- "This is so fashionable, and so misguided." (S175, 2005)
- Make entry cheap so the program recruits occasional buyers. Same view across 2005 and 2025.

## Targeting heavy or best customers
- "So targeting heavy category buyers could be described as a “let become a smaller brand” strategy." (S041, 2013)

## Narrow targeting and niche
- "reaching a narrower audience means becoming a smaller brand." (S015, undated)
- On whether the smallest brand is ever the loyalty leader: "No, never, never." (S001, 2025)
- Targeting may be tailored when it adds reach: "So tailor wisely, not too much because you want scale" (S012, 2022). He credits Mark Ritson with changing his view on targeting.

## Pricing and price premiums
- "Anyone can have a price premium, it isn’t necessarily a sign of strength or good strategy." (S038, 2013)
- "a price premium might be nice for profits today but it holds back reach and scale, and that increases the riskiness of future profits." (S038, 2013)
- Apple: "It annoys me how people keep citing Apple as a company that charges price premiums. They don’t." (S083, 2011)

## Discounting and promotions
- "More than half of sales occur on-deal, hardly anyone pays the normal price anymore for this brand." (S063, 2013)
- Cutting advertising and booking discounts as marketing is financial manipulation: "converting brand value into temporary sales or profits really lowers the value of a company" (S032, 2016)
- A deal on one brand steals full-priced sales from your others (S140, 2008).

## Brand versus performance advertising
- "You got to have both." (S001, 2025)
- Digital performance ads: "It’s only going to it’s only catching the people who are like in the market now" (S001, 2025)

## Brand image and differentiation
- "brand image is often neither a key factor in driving growth nor an obstacle to it." (S015)
- "Distinctiveness (branding) is legally defensible, while differentiation is not (other than time limited patent protection)." (S153, 2008)
- "Evaluation is over-rated." (S154, 2008)

## Brand purpose
- "Brands should be good corporate citizens, but the idea of turning them into saints is nuts." (S029, 2017)
- "Wishful thinking… fashion." (S030, 2016)
- Position on the merits is political opinion as much as marketing science (S029).

## Emotion and brand love
- "most lovers still buy other brands, and most of a brand’s buyers don’t love it." (S066, 2012)
- "These strong emotions are thought to underpin loyalty but we’ve known for decades that that isn’t true." (S094, 2011)
- He accepts quick emotional decisions: "consumers are quick emotional decision makers" (S030, 2016).

## Brand metrics, trackers and equity valuations
- "In short they predict nothing. They just tell us things we already knew." (S053, 2013)
- "Spider graphs, perceptual maps – none of them tell us how much any perception is worth." (S027, 2018)
- "marketers are wasting their time/money in tracking attitudes" (S030, 2016)
- What he does endorse: tracking sales and share and splitting share into penetration and loyalty. "This sort of market research data is very valuable." (S117, 2010)

## Net Promoter Score
- "In sum, this is snake oil, fake science." (S136, 2008)

## Mix modelling and big data
- "Astrology, like econometric 4Ps modelling, has many fans, many of whom are very intelligent capable people." (S168, 2005)
- "It’s as if chaos theory taught us nothing at all." (S028, 2017)

## Programmatic and digital advertising
- "So again we have overblown promises based on marketing theory and fashion not facts." (S022, 2019)
- "I’m not a ‘new media’ zealot if that’s what you mean." (S033, 2015)
- "nothing drives social media exposure like paid-for advertising in big media like TV, radio and print." (S040, 2013)
- Social media in 2011: "Use social media as research (into media), but you can’t justify it as part of your advertising budget." (S107). No later recorded view on social beyond S040 and S001.

## Word of mouth and social
- "research shows that advertising is the major stimulus for brand word-of-mouth" (S093, 2011)
- About 0.4 percent of a brand's Facebook fans interact in a typical week (S076, 2012).

## Customer data lists and CRM
- "There’s nothing obviously, that is an asset to have a list of names and email addresses, but it’s not a growth strategy." (S001, 2025)

## Product range and assortment
- "More choices increases sales." (S081, 2011)
- "Add (or don’t remove) any product from the tail so long as it wins some customers." (S071, 2012)

## Retail, distribution and availability
- "Brands largely compete in terms of mental and physical availability." (S154, 2008)
- "getting into as many repertoires as possible would seem to be the number one priority." (S070, 2012, on online stores)
- 2025 example: Uber added a phone number so people without the app could still order (S001).

## Market share and competing with rivals
- "growth should be an outcome of a strategy to grow profits – profits should not be sacrificed for growth" (S073, 2012, a guest essay on his blog).
- "but that sort of thinking has been shown to hurt company performance and increase the chances of going company failure." (S087, 2011, on hurting rivals for margin).

## Growth leadership and organisation
- "company growth will be stymied if the company isn’t playing in the growing categories, in the growing markets/countries, in the growing distribution channels." (S007, 2020)
- Marketing's cultural job is to keep the firm focused on customers: "One of the most important functions of marketing is cultural, to prevent the organisation from slipping into a production orientation, to keep it focussed on the customer and the market." (S047, 2013)

## Evidence, intuition and research
- "It’s quite normal that people use intuition and what feels good, but I that just doesn’t work for marketing" (S001, 2025)
- "I urge you to be proud of descriptive research." (S021, 2020)
- "We have to be humble and realistic about our collective lack of knowledge." (S027, 2018)

## Business books and consultants
- "Each of these books are based on severely flawed research that ‘proves’ just what the author wanted to say in the first place (which is the opposite of a surprising discovery)." (S079, 2011)
- "Now in marketing practice the tinniest whiff of evidence that something might be useful is enough to send lemmings running for the cliff!" (S050, 2013)

## Media agencies and reach
- "media agencies are essentially buyers of media, not planners." (S149, 2008)
- Regional TV: "It’s an astonishingly easy way to enhance the sales effectiveness of the ad spend." (S149)

## Competition and being first
- "Very many brand leaders were not the first into their category" (S134, 2008)

## Economics and trade (off-topic for most decisions)
- "Economic growth is good." (S126, 2009)
- "What the developing world desperately needs is freer trade. Fewer subsidies and tariffs, not more." (S163, 2006). Written outside marketing science.

## Raising money, debt, equity, hiring, firing, partners, burnout, remote work, AI, work hours, culture, selling the company
- No recorded position on any of these.

## Timing and market entry
- "But there are thresholds it has to reach in distribution for synergy effects." (S035, 2014, on new brands). Otherwise no recorded position.

## Advice on a tiny budget
- "When budgets are limited (i.e. always) it’s tempting to think small and go for low reach, but this isn’t a recipe for growth, or even maintenance." (S030, 2016)
- He also allows an exception: with three months of cash left, "you can you can turn it off for a while." (S001, 2025) refers to advertising; physical availability cannot be switched off.
