# Principles: Byron Sharp

Decision rules built from his blog (2005 to 2020), a 2022 talk write-up and a 2025 podcast. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Reach everyone who buys the category, all the time.** "Reach is not optional unless you want to shrink or be small." (S001) "For maintenance or growth you have to reach light buyers." (S041) Sources: 5 (S001, S012, S030, S041, S125). Confidence: high.
2. **A growth plan adds customers; it does not squeeze existing ones.** "You can’t write a growth plan that says we’re going to get more out of our existing customers." (S001) "No brand ever got big by simply trying to get more out of its most loyal customers." (S175) Sources: 5 (S001, S015, S084, S175, S030). Confidence: high.
3. **Make the brand easier to buy, every day.** "You should be thinking every day. How do I make it easier for people to buy me." (S001) Sources: 3 (S001, S087, S084). Confidence: high.
4. **Spread exposures; never burst.** "never bunch exposures" (S012) Sources: 2 (S001, S012). Confidence: medium.
5. **Do not pay for more than fleeting attention.** "Most exposures are fleeting … and that’s okay." (S012) Sources: 2 (S012, S164). Confidence: medium.
6. **Look like yourself, consistently.** "So you have got to look like you." (S012) "Consequently many firms produce campaign after campaign where each looks and feels different – as if each were for a different brand." (S144) Sources: 4 (S012, S144, S153, S140). Confidence: high.
7. **Test any vendor's metric against later results, on public data, with dates.** "I found that the changes in the brand rank in their Customer Loyalty Index occured after real market place changes, not before as they had implied." (S103) Sources: 6 (S061, S062, S103, S117, S136, S053). Confidence: high.
8. **Ask for independent replication.** "I’d love to see an independent replication." (S093) "I think any claims made for these proprietary products should be subject to independent examination." (S117) Sources: 4 (S093, S117, S082, S068). Confidence: high.
9. **A model that fits history is not a model that predicts.** "Models that show wonderful fit to historic data routinely fail in their predictions" (S028) Sources: 3 (S028, S168, S039). Confidence: high.
10. **Trust patterns that repeat across conditions, not one study or one case.** "And a single study shows nothing." (S040) "these sorts of case studies are just stories, told after the event." (S030) Sources: 5 (S030, S040, S039, S068, S081). Confidence: high.
11. **Check whether a scary number is just natural wobble.** "All of this is wrong, because this would still happen even in perfectly stationary conditions where no brands are growing or declining" (S089) Sources: 3 (S089, S127, S045). Confidence: high.
12. **Do not target the loyalty ladder or the loyalty card at your best customers.** "Loyalty ladders distract marketers from the real issue which is how to grow penetration (reach all sorts of category buyers)." (S125) "This is so fashionable, and so misguided." (S175) Sources: 4 (S001, S125, S175, S050). Confidence: high.
13. **Do not chase ROI into a smaller brand.** "However chasing ROI can send you broke, and this is a good example of the dangers of ROI." (S041) Sources: 2 (S041, S098). Confidence: medium.
14. **Do not trust claimed motivations; watch behaviour.** "This ignores the very weak link between claimed motivations and behaviour." (S084) Sources: 3 (S084, S051, S144). Confidence: high.
15. **Price is not strength.** "Anyone can have a price premium, it isn’t necessarily a sign of strength or good strategy." (S038) Sources: 3 (S038, S083, S063). Confidence: high.
16. **Aim at profit; share is an outcome.** "growth should be an outcome of a strategy to grow profits – profits should not be sacrificed for growth" (S073) Sources: 2 (S073, S087). Confidence: medium. Note: S073 is a guest essay on his blog; the cited line is the one credited to him.
17. **Do not cut the range by 90 percent.** "So my point is don’t make the mistake of thinking that a store can do without 90%+ of its range." (S071) Sources: 3 (S071, S081, S167). Confidence: high.
18. **Do not demand deep meaning from names and logos; they identify.** "Brands, and their distinctive assets, identify – that’s it." (S104) Sources: 2 (S104, S090). Confidence: medium.
19. **Hunt for what turns buyers away.** "Smart marketers should always be on the look-out for such features." (S154) Sources: 2 (S154, S070). Confidence: medium.
20. **Treat new media as an experiment, paid from the research budget.** "That’s why I say it should be part of the research budget, not the media budget." (S107) Sources: 3 (S107, S033, S040). Confidence: high. Dated 2011; his later view on digital is only in S001.
21. **Halve digital impressions for viewability and quarter them for fraud before comparing with TV.** "a good guide for digital seems to be to quadruple the cost of each impression" (S033) Sources: 1 (S033, 2015). Confidence: low, and dated.
22. **Do not pick winners by outcome and then claim the traits caused it.** "But as these companies were picked for their financial performance then their stock price performance over the same period shows nothing." (S078) Sources: 3 (S078, S079, S082). Confidence: high.
23. **Look for what contradicts you.** "We should be looking for contradictions and those things that violate what we want to believe." (S079) Sources: 2 (S079, S078). Confidence: medium.
24. **Do not rely on intuition in marketing.** "It’s quite normal that people use intuition and what feels good, but I that just doesn’t work for marketing" (S001) Sources: 2 (S001, S093). Confidence: medium.
25. **Promotions have a portfolio cost.** "When you put one of your brands on special you aren’t only giving away some full priced sales that would have happened anyway, you are also stealing full-priced sales from your other brands." (S140) Sources: 2 (S140, S063). Confidence: medium.
