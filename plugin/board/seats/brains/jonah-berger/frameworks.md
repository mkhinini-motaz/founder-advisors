# Frameworks: Jonah Berger

Built from 47 sources (2013 to 2026): talks, podcasts and interviews. The books themselves were not fetched; book frameworks come from his own descriptions of them. Most-used first. Scoring guidance is labeled as guidance, not his words.

## 1. STEPPS (Contagious)
- **What:** six drivers of why people talk and share: Social currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical value, Stories. Built from thousands of pieces of content, tens of thousands of brands, millions of purchases. Works for B2B, B2C, products, services, ideas, cheap or expensive (S049, S087, S098).
- **Steps:** (1) pick the kernel, the one thing you want people to say; (2) push on as many of the six as you can, hardest ones first; (3) count shares, not followers.
- **Use when:** a launch, a campaign, a name, a menu item, a referral ask.
- **Scoring (guidance):** score each of the six 0 to 2 for the product as it is today. Under 4 of 12 means no reason to talk yet; fix the product or the story before spending on reach.
- **Quote:** "Again and again we saw the same six factors come up." (S049)
- **Also:** "It's not random. It's not luck, and it's not chance. There's a science behind why people talk and why people share." (S087)
- **Sources (17+):** S049, S058, S059, S060, S066, S067, S068, S069, S073, S078, S083, S087, S090, S094, S098, S104. A cobb salad: the six need not all be present (S104).

## 2. Social currency
- **What:** people share what makes them look good, smart, in the know or special. Ask how the sharer looks, not how the message looks. Levers: insider access, scarcity, status, finding the inner remarkability of a product (S066, S067).
- **Use when:** a small shop wants talk; a feature should be a thing people can brag about.
- **Scoring (guidance):** would a customer be proud to repeat this line to a friend, with the brand riding along? Yes, no, or only if the brand falls out.
- **Quote:** "the better something makes us look, the more likely we are to talk about it and share it." (S049)
- **Quote:** "So the best way to get people to talk about us is to make them look good." (S087)
- **Sources (12+):** S049, S059, S060, S066, S067, S068, S069, S073, S078, S083, S087, S098, S104. Caution: do not fake it (S104).

## 3. Triggers (top of mind, tip of tongue)
- **What:** a cue in the environment that reminds people of you. Link the product to something frequent (peanut butter and jelly, Kit Kat and coffee, Michelob and weekends). Four questions: who, when, what is around them then, how to link (S087, S090). Liking is not enough; being thought of comes first.
- **Use when:** a good product is liked but not mentioned; timing of a reminder or push.
- **Scoring (guidance):** how many times a week does the target customer meet the cue, and does it fire before the decision, not after? Daily and before beats weekly and after.
- **Quote:** "Ideas have a habitat, a set of cues or triggers that make people think about them." (S059)
- **Quote:** "You may love something, but if you're not thinking about it, you're never going to talk about it." (S083)
- **Sources (14+):** S059, S066, S067, S069, S073, S078, S083, S087, S090, S104.

## 4. Emotion: arousal, not valence
- **What:** when we care we share, but what counts is activation. Anger, anxiety, awe, excitement, humor raise sharing; sadness and contentment lower it. Studied on thousands of New York Times articles (S104, S060, S061).
- **Use when:** choosing the angle of a message.
- **Scoring (guidance):** does the line wake people up (surprise, awe, worry, laughter) or soothe them? Soothing scores low.
- **Quote:** "it's not about being positive or negative. It's about activation or deactivation." (S076)
- **Sources (8):** S059, S061, S064, S068, S076, S083, S104, S060. He does not recommend going negative (S104).

## 5. Public (built to show, built to grow)
- **What:** people imitate what they can see, so make private behavior visible: Apple's logo flipped to face onlookers, white headphones, branded bags, Movember (S067, S104).
- **Use when:** a product is used in private (a field worker's bag, a package, a receipt).
- **Scoring (guidance):** could a stranger see someone using it? If no, add a visible residue.
- **Quote:** "we can't imitate them if we can't see them." (S059)
- **Sources (5):** S059, S067, S104, S087, S060.

## 6. Practical value and the rule of 100
- **What:** news you can use is shared to help others and to look helpful. Framing: under about 100 say percent off, over 100 say the money amount, because the bigger-looking number wins (S066, S073, S087, S090).
- **Quote:** "Practical value is just news you can use, basically useful information." (S059)
- **Quote:** "it's something I call the rule of a hundred numbers are not just numbers" (S066)
- **Sources (7):** S059, S066, S073, S087, S090, S104, S060.

## 7. Trojan horse story and the kernel
- **What:** a story is a vessel; the brand or benefit must be inside it so it cannot be told without it (Panda cheese, Blendtec, Maui Jim, the bar with three types of ice). First pick the kernel, then the moral, then build the story. Show, do not tell (S060, S066, S087).
- **Use when:** an ad, a founder story, a viral video; test with: can someone retell it without naming us?
- **Scoring (guidance):** retell test. Fails if the brand can be dropped from the retelling.
- **Quote:** "Good stories aren't just stories. They're vessels or carriers of information. They bring brands and ideas along for the ride." (S087)
- **Quote:** "what is that thing or things you want them to think about? That's your kernel." (S087)
- **Sources (13+):** S059, S060, S066, S067, S068, S069, S073, S087, S090, S104.

## 8. Word of mouth beats ads: trust and targeting
- **What:** a friend is trusted and finds the right listener, like a searchlight through the social network. Ads are distrusted because they are self-interested. Turn customers into advocates and let them do the targeting (second copy of the book, S059, S066, S083, S087).
- **Quote:** "Word of mouth is like a searchlight that works through your social network" (S059)
- **Sources (12+):** S058, S059, S066, S067, S068, S069, S083, S087, S090, S098. Figures (twice the sales, ten times the return, 13 or 20 percent more lifetime value) drift between talks; see positions.md.

## 9. REDUCE (The Catalyst)
- **What:** five barriers to change: Reactance, Endowment, Distance, Uncertainty, Corroborating evidence. A catalyst does not add heat; it lowers the energy needed. Find the barrier, remove it. The order is not a sequence, and he admits the acronym stretches (S062, S072).
- **Use when:** any why will they not switch, order, refer or try.
- **Scoring (guidance):** name the one barrier that is biggest; if you cannot, you are still pushing.
- **Quote:** "put them together they spell the word reduce which is exactly what great catalysts do" (S085)
- **Sources (14+):** S054, S060, S062, S065, S072, S077, S085, S091, S094, S096, S097, S099, S100, S102.

## 10. Why hasn't this person changed already? (the parking brake)
- **What:** the diagnostic question. When the car does not move, check the brake before pressing the gas. Pushing is what almost everyone writes down first (98 to 99 percent, by his count). Barriers by stage: not aware, does not see the problem, doubts it works, too expensive, hard to integrate (S077, S085).
- **Quote:** "I love to think about these obstacles as parking brakes." (S054)
- **Quote:** "Why hasn't this person changed already? What's stopping them?" (S102)
- **Sources (12+):** S054, S065, S072, S077, S085, S091, S094, S097, S099, S100, S102. Only one barrier at a time usually; he says there is no time for all five (S097).

## 11. Reactance: menu, ask, highlight a gap
- **What:** people have an anti-persuasion radar and push back when pushed. Three fixes: a menu of two or three real options (never one, never fifty, never a decoy), questions instead of statements, and pointing at the gap between what people say and do (S102).
- **Quote:** "Two is fine, three is fine, not 54, not 79, not 2012 because it's too many because it comes becomes overwhelming." (S102)
- **Quote:** "people like their attitudes and their actions to line up." (S102)
- **Sources (12+):** S054, S062, S065, S077, S085, S091, S096, S099, S102. Including one honest rival option raises trust (S085).

## 12. Endowment and the cost of inaction
- **What:** the old thing feels safe: ease, attachment, risk aversion, the three horsemen of inertia. Make doing nothing visible as a cost: add up small repeated losses (the email signature, minor injuries) (S072, S077, S085, S102).
- **Quote:** "got to highlight the cost of inaction and show them it's not as safe as they might think." (S102)
- **Quote:** "And so ease, attachment, and risk aversion are the three horsemen of inertia." (S102)
- **Sources (7):** S054, S072, S077, S085, S102, S097.

## 13. Distance: ask for less, then more
- **What:** each person has a zone of acceptance; beyond it is a region of rejection where the message is ignored. Break a big ask into stepping stones (Mountain Dew from three liters to zero) (S054, S062, S072).
- **Quote:** "start by asking for less, chunk that change and then ask for more." (S054)
- **Sources (10):** S054, S062, S065, S072, S085, S091, S097, S099, S102.

## 14. Uncertainty: lower the barrier to trial
- **What:** costs are certain and now, benefits uncertain and later. A discount makes you pay before you know; a trial removes the doubt. Freemium, test drives, free shipping and returns, samples, a flight simulator. Free helps only if the product is good (S054, S072, S100, S102).
- **Quote:** "How can I make it easier for people to experience the offering?" (S054)
- **Quote:** "the barrier is not just the cost the barrier is uncertainty" (S072)
- **Sources (11):** S054, S062, S065, S072, S085, S097, S099, S100, S102.

## 15. Corroborating evidence
- **What:** one source is a joke, five close together make you look; a boulder needs more proof than a pebble. Similar and concentrated sources count more (S085, S096, S102).
- **Quote:** "Because if five people say the same thing in short succession, it's a lot harder to believe they're not right." (S102)
- **Sources (7):** S054, S060, S085, S091, S096, S102.

## 16. SPEACC / SPEAK (Magic Words)
- **What:** six kinds of language that change impact: Similarity and difference, Posing questions, Emotion, Agency and identity, Confidence, Concreteness. A toolkit, each tool for a job (S049, S075, S084). He jokes the acronym needs two Cs (S049, S089).
- **Quote:** "subtle shifts in the language we use can have a huge impact" (S063)
- **Sources (14+):** S049, S063, S064, S075, S079, S081, S082, S084, S089, S092, S096, S101, S106.

## 17. Actions into identities
- **What:** ask people to be a helper or a voter, not to help or vote. Reported lifts: about 30 percent for helper, about 15 percent for voter (a 2023 talk says 50 percent for helper, S106; treat the numbers as single studies). It works in reverse for bad acts (cheater). The identity must be desirable and believable (S092).
- **Quote:** "when we want to motivate others, turn actions into identities." (S049)
- **Sources (9):** S049, S063, S075, S079, S082, S084, S089, S092, S101, S106.

## 18. Language of confidence: drop hedges, own the doubt
- **What:** hedges cost persuasion; certainty persuades even when the speaker is no more accurate. But say where the uncertainty is, and use "to me" if you must soften. Certainty is a tool: it persuades but does not hold attention (S064, S084).
- **Quote:** "When we hedge, we undermine our impact." (S084)
- **Quote:** "There are times to be certain and there are times to be uncertain, just like there are times we need a hammer and there are times we need a screwdriver." (S084)
- **Sources (11):** S049, S063, S064, S075, S079, S082, S084, S089, S092, S096, S101.

## 19. Because, can't versus don't, could versus should, you versus I
- **What:** small word swaps. A reason after the word because lifts yes by about 50 percent even if empty; saying I don't keeps a goal; could opens options; you stops the scroll but reads as blame on help pages (S063, S082, S092).
- **Quote:** "just using that word because makes it seem like there is a reason" (S063)
- **Sources (9):** S063, S075, S079, S081, S082, S084, S092, S101.

## 20. What holds attention
- **What:** familiar and concrete words lower reading effort; uncertain, high-arousal emotion (anxiety, hope) keeps people reading; open a curiosity gap and do not give the punchline away (S064, S081).
- **Quote:** "style how we write can compensate for it" (S064)
- **Sources (4):** S049, S064, S081, S088.

## 21. Invisible influence: imitate and differentiate
- **What:** influence is a magnet, it attracts and repels. We see it in others, never in ourselves. Optimal distinctiveness: similar but different; wrap the new thing in a familiar skin (the "Horseless" carriage, the digital video recorder). Mere exposure; mimicry builds trust; being behind a little motivates (proximal peers); neighbor norms beat money, environment and good-citizen appeals (S053, S061, S080, S103).
- **Quote:** "there's one place we consistently never see influence and that is ourselves" (S080)
- **Quote:** "it's not important to be different it's important to be what's called optimally distinct" (S080)
- **Sources (10):** S053, S061, S068, S076, S080, S088, S095, S103.

## 22. Groupthink fixes
- **What:** hide opinions before the meeting (private votes, written first), and appoint a dissenter and tell the room (S061, S076).
- **Quote:** "to tell people, look, this person's job in the meeting is is to disagree with the rest of the group." (S076)
- **Sources (2):** S061, S076. Confidence medium.

## 23. The tastier test
- **What:** ideas, like food, win by fit with how minds work, not by being good for us. Test two true messages for which is tastier, and engineer the true one (NASA, cervical screening) (S083).
- **Quote:** "I'm not asking at the moment which is true. I'm asking which is tastier." (S083)
- **Sources (1 talk):** S083. Confidence low.

## 24. Pratfall and roller-coaster stories
- **What:** once seen as competent, a small shown mistake makes you more likable. A story with ups and downs holds attention; failure before the success is relatable (S081).
- **Quote:** "as long as we're seen as competent already revealing those failures revealing those moments of difficulty can actually make us more interesting" (S081)
- **Sources (1):** S081. Fails if you are not already seen as competent.

## Books

### Books: Jonah Berger

Built from 47 sources (2013 to 2026). Book text was not fetched; summaries are in my own words from his talks and interviews.

#### Contagious: Why Things Catch On (2013)
- **Core thesis:** what gets talked about is not random. Six drivers, STEPPS, explain it, and they can be engineered into products, messages and ideas, including boring ones.
- **Frameworks:** social currency, triggers, emotion, public, practical value, stories. The rule of 100, the kernel, Trojan horse stories (S066, S087).
- **Taught in:** S059 (launch talk, 2013), S066, S067, S069, S073, S078, S083, S087, S090, S104. It sold half a million copies in 35 languages (S060) and changed his life: Google, Apple and Nike called (S077).
- **Limit:** he says it did not cover emotion in every talk, and online data under-count offline talk (S104).

#### Invisible Influence (2016)
- **Core thesis:** others shape what we choose, buy and believe, mostly unseen. Influence attracts and repels; we notice it in others, never in ourselves.
- **Frameworks:** optimal distinctiveness, mere exposure, mimicry, proximal peers, descriptive norms, groupthink fixes.
- **Taught in:** S053, S061, S068, S076, S080, S088, S095, S103. He admits it sold less than Contagious: a vitamin, not a painkiller (S065).

#### The Catalyst: How to Change Anyone's Mind (2020)
- **Core thesis:** change is chemistry, not physics. Do not push; find and remove the barriers. It came out of consulting calls after Contagious (S060, S079).
- **Frameworks:** REDUCE (reactance, endowment, distance, uncertainty, corroborating evidence), the parking brake, the menu, highlight a gap, ask for less then more, lower the barrier to trial.
- **Taught in:** S054, S062, S065, S072, S077, S085, S091, S094, S096, S097, S099, S100, S102.
- **Limit:** not every tactic fits every audience (S062); he says there is no time in a talk for all five barriers (S097).

#### Magic Words: What to Say to Get Your Way (2023)
- **Core thesis:** small word choices change persuasion, attention and connection. A toolkit, each tool for a job. About two-thirds tactical, the back third on what language reveals about people (S084).
- **Frameworks:** SPEACC or SPEAK (similarity and difference, posing questions, emotion, agency and identity, confidence, concreteness); because; can't versus don't; could versus should; you versus I; identity nouns; hedging.
- **Taught in:** S049, S063, S064, S075, S079, S081, S082, S084, S089, S092, S096, S101, S106. Built from about ten years of text analysis (S089).

#### Not books
- A research paper on unused items becoming more special is the basis of the hoarding talk (S070).
- He recommends The Tipping Point, Made to Stick, Diffusion of Innovations, Predictably Irrational (S062, S079). Those are others' books.
