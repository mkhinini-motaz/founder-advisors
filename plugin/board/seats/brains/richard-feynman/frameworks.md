# Frameworks: Richard Feynman

Built from 17 sources (quick depth). Entries are his methods for finding out what is true. Scoring lines are guidance for a founder, not his words. S012 is excluded.

## 1. Guess, compute the consequences, compare with experiment
- What: his one-sentence method. Guess, work out what the guess predicts, check against experiment.
- Steps: make the guess definite; derive a prediction that could fail; run the check; if it disagrees, it is wrong.
- Use when: any claim about what customers, field staff or shops will do.
- Scoring guidance: a plan with no test that could fail scores zero. Rank, beauty and who proposed it add nothing.
- Quote: "if it disagrees with experiment it's wrong and that simple statement is the key to science" (S022)
- Sources: S004, S022, S021 (his on-camera line), S014.

## 2. You must not fool yourself
- What: the first duty is not to deceive yourself; then not deceiving others is easy.
- Steps: list how you could be wrong; list what you wanted the answer to be; give those to the people who judge.
- Use when: reading your own dashboard, pilot or survey.
- Scoring guidance: the more you want the result, the more checking it needs.
- Quote: "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself" (S015)
- Sources: S015, S016, S003, S011.

## 3. Leaning over backwards
- What: report everything that might make your result invalid, every alternative you ruled out, every fact that disagrees with you.
- Use when: any report, pitch or review sent to a partner, a manager or a supplier.
- Scoring guidance: if the report holds only facts pointing one way, it fails.
- Quote: "a kind of leaning over backwards." (S015)
- Sources: S015, S016.

## 4. Cargo Cult Science
- What: work that has every outward form of investigation but lacks the one thing that makes it work.
- Use when: a process looks right (dashboards, meetings, surveys) but nothing lands.
- Scoring guidance: ask what the process would show if you were wrong. If nothing, it is ritual.
- Quote: "They're doing everything right. The form is perfect. It looks exactly the way it looked before. But it doesn't work." (S015)
- Sources: S015.

## 5. Publication must not depend on the answer
- What: decide before the test that you will report either result.
- Use when: choosing which numbers to show a board, a shop or a field worker.
- Scoring guidance: if you would only share the good outcome, the test is rigged.
- Quote: "publication probability depends upon the answer. That should not be done." (S015)
- Sources: S015.

## 6. Test on something the idea was not built from
- What: a theory must make something else come out right, besides the facts that gave you the idea.
- Use when: a pilot in one street is used to justify the whole town.
- Quote: "makes something else come out right, in addition." (S015)
- Sources: S015, S001 (the Moon check), S005.

## 7. Replicate the baseline first
- What: before changing X to Y, repeat the other person's X result in your own setting; then the difference is the thing you changed.
- Use when: copying a competitor's tactic or a number from another city.
- Quote: "it was necessary first to repeat in her laboratory the experiment of the other person" (S015)
- Sources: S015.

## 8. Vague theories explain everything and so tell nothing
- What: a claim that fits any outcome cannot be wrong, so it is worthless. State ahead what would count as failure.
- Use when: brand awareness will help, it is a long game.
- Scoring guidance: no stated failure condition means no score.
- Quote: "by having a vague theory it's possible to get either result" (S022)
- Sources: S022, S004. He allows vagueness at the start of a science, with care (S022).

## 9. Try to prove yourself wrong as quickly as possible
- What: put the experiment where the idea is most likely to fail. Agreement only means not yet wrong.
- Quote: "we're trying to prove ourselves wrong as quickly as possible" (S022)
- Sources: S004, S022, S011.

## 10. Check before you call it wrong
- What: before an experiment kills a guess, check the report, the dirt, and the calculation. Then the guess goes.
- Quote: "the thing has been rubbed back and forth a few times" (S022)
- Sources: S004, S022.

## 11. Replace, do not just object
- What: saying something might be wrong helps little; offer a definite replacement, which dies fast if wrong.
- Quote: "the problem is not what might be wrong but what might be substituted precisely in place of it" (S022)
- Sources: S004, S022.

## 12. A why rests on what you are willing to accept
- What: any explanation needs a base you grant; without one the whys go on forever. Do not fake a familiar picture.
- Quote: "when you explain a why you have to be in some framework that you allow something to be true" (S020)
- Sources: S020, S003.

## 13. Names are not knowledge
- What: knowing the name of a thing in every language tells you nothing about it; look at what it does.
- Quote: "names don't constitute knowledge" (S009)
- Sources: S009 (his own words, from his father's lesson).

## 14. The Challenger method: reality over public relations
- What: compare the managers' failure number with the engineers' number; a near-failure that nobody understands is a warning, not a pass.
- Quote: "For a successful technology, reality must take precedence over public relations, for nature cannot be fooled." (S016)
- Also: "When playing Russian roulette the fact that the first shot got off safely is little comfort for the next." (S016)
- Scoring guidance: an unexplained anomaly that did not matter this time counts against the plan.
- Sources: S016, S021.

## 15. A limit usually binds one design, not the goal
- What: ask whether any law forbids the goal. If none does, redesign.
- Quote: "these limitations that you hear about mean only that you can't go on with the same design" (S005)
- Sources: S005.

## 16. Scaling check
- What: when size changes, different effects dominate; work out what scales as length, area or volume before trusting a plan.
- Quote: "when you go to different scales different things at different proportions become important" (S005)
- Sources: S005.

## 17. Learn from every group, then ask the plain question
- What: sit with each group until you can work on its problem, then ask the naive question out loud.
- Quote: "Why don't you do it that way?" (S007)
- Sources: S007.

## 18. Tell the people doing the work what it is for
- What: rules without reasons fail; give the reasons so people can handle the case the rules missed.
- Quote: "I felt that you couldn't make the plant safe unless you knew how it worked" (S007)
- Sources: S007.

## 19. Probe a system by trying to break in
- What: pick the locks, find the hole in the fence, and tell the owner.
- Quote: "we have important secrets and we need better locks" (S007)
- Sources: S007, S021.

## 20. A cheap demonstration beats a debate
- What: in the hearing he put a piece of the seal in ice water and showed it. Credit only his lines in S021.
- Quote: "I took this stuff that I got out of your seal and I put in ice water and I discovered" (S021)
- Sources: S021, S016.

### Books: Richard Feynman

Built from 17 sources (quick depth). No book is in the corpus. His major pieces are lectures and essays, listed here.

- **The Character of Physical Law (Messenger Lectures, Cornell, 1964).** How a physicist finds laws: guess, compute, compare; keep several formulations; nature is simpler than you think. Taught in S001, S002, S003, S004, S011, S022. He names it himself in S005.
- **The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 1, "Atoms in Motion" (26 September 1961).** Experiment is the test of all knowledge; every law is an approximation. S014.
- **"Cargo Cult Science" (Caltech commencement, 1974).** Scientific integrity as leaning over backwards. S015.
- **Personal Observations on the Reliability of the Shuttle (Rogers Commission, Appendix F, 1986).** Reality over public relations. S016.
- **Lectures: Auckland (1979, S013), "There's Plenty of Room at the Bottom" retelling (S005), computer science (c. 1985, S010), "Los Alamos From Below" (S007), quantum workshop (S024).**
- **Documentaries: Fun to Imagine (BBC Horizon, 1983, S020); The Fantastic Mr. Feynman (BBC, S021); The World from Another Point of View, with Fred Hoyle (S009).** Colleagues' and narrator's lines in S021 and S009 are theirs.
