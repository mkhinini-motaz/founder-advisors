# Stories: David Heinemeier Hansson

The stories he reuses, with the lesson and source ids. Built from 90 sources.

1. **Basecamp's first version.** One person billed 400 hours in 2004 and the product went on to gross hundreds of millions; first ran on a single 249-dollar-a-month server and served about 18 months of customers. Lesson: small team as cause, not limit. (S001, S008)
2. **Why Basecamp exists.** The team kept dropping the ball on client projects over email and FTP, so they built one page per project, simple enough to need no training. Lesson: build for the problem you have. (S001, S003)
3. **Bezos and the secondary sale.** In 2006 Jeff Bezos bought a slice of each founder's shares, no money to the company; a vaccine against taking venture checks. Lesson: security without growth pressure. (S001, S003)
4. **The sales-tax mistake.** Nineteen years without a finance head, then about two years and 5 million dollars in settlements. Lesson: quirkiness can go too far; count the beans earlier. (S001, S008)
5. **HEY and Apple, June 2020.** Approved Friday, live Monday, then the fix blocked unless they used in-app payment at 30%. They refused, went public a week before WWDC and won a truce. Lesson: only a founder can risk a business over a principle. (S001)
6. **Leaving the cloud.** The bill failed his smell test in early 2023; seven apps moved in about six months to Dell servers, no extra hire, spend cut by a half to two thirds. He wishes he had done the math sooner. (S001, S054, S062)
7. **Cutting four products to one.** Around 30 to 40 people, four products stretched thin; Jason argued for hiring, DHH refused to run a large company, and they cut to Basecamp. Backpack and Ta-Da List still have users. Lesson: they went too long first. (S004)
8. **Testing the opposite on managers.** Hired engineering managers, then tried layers again at 80 people; both rejected as "not us". Lesson: retest your convictions. (S001, S006)
9. **System tests deleted.** Rails shipped system tests in 2016 to 2017; after years 37signals deleted 5,000 lines. Lesson: sorta-kinda working is a trap. (S026, S038)
10. **Twitter versus Shopify.** Twitter left Rails and stalled for ten years; Shopify stayed and scaled to about one million dynamic requests a second on Black Friday. (S001)
11. **Campfire before Slack.** Campfire in 2005 was Slack before Slack; Remote came a decade early. Lesson: timing, and do not draw a lasting rule from a failure. (S011)
12. **Backpack, not 50 containers.** Basecamp 2004 launched without file sharing; HEY launched without signatures or vacation responders but with the screener. Lesson: what fits in the backpack is what you leave with. (S007)
13. **Dot-com education.** A VC-funded chat and forum startup (Caput) and an incubator taught him what not to do; the founder personally repaid a loan for 10 to 15 years. A one-man site outlived the hockey-stick spreadsheet by 20 years. (S010)
14. **The COO and the group therapy.** Hired a COO, let them go after a year, and a company meetup turned into hours of group therapy. Lesson: wrong setting for emotions. (S030)
15. **Basecamp 5 and the agents (2026).** Designers vibed many PRs into Basecamp 5 and the architecture was destroyed; humans cleaned it by hand. Lesson: on a large existing code base, someone must protect the architecture. (S002)
16. **Omakub to Omarchy.** Omakub drew a few thousand users and faded; Omarchy, different, took off. Lesson: be different, not a cheaper copy; keep going. (S002)
17. **Support from the founders.** Jason ran customer service for three years with millions in revenue before the first agent came. Lesson: do not hire support early. (S025)
18. **The 37,000 dollar campaign.** An agency wanted 1 million for a 30-second spot; the new marketing head did it for 37,000. (S080)
