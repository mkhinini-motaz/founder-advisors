# Positions: David Heinemeier Hansson

Stances on founder questions, with source id and date (from `sources.md`). Where he changed his mind, both views are shown with dates and the newer is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Money and ownership
**Raising money.** Refuses outside money; wants a business he and his partner own and enjoy, not a unicorn. "the VC unicorn of bust mentality that dominates everything." (S001, 2025-07-12). Earlier: "Not accept any of the strings that would normally go with a venture capital contract." (S003, 2018-06-05).
**Venture odds.** Realism about failure rates. "But let’s just be realistic about the odds." (S005, 2024-10-31). Also "it seems like an utter waste of human potential to have 999 teams chase these impossible things such that we can have one outlier give us the next Facebook." (S011).
**Unprofitable SaaS.** "It's a truism that if you bleed long enough, you will eventually die" (S061). The bubble has popped for losers; the opportunity is in profitable software (S096, 2022-11-09).
**Debt.** No recorded position.
**Equity and partners.** Disagree and commit with a partner; do not co-found with someone who does your exact job. "I’m going to disagree and commit." (S001, 2025-07-12).
**Selling the company.** Do not sell after a hard stretch. "There are endless stories of founders who regret selling their business when all they needed was a six-week break from the startup sprint." (S013). Sale followed by retirement does not work for creative people: "It’s really difficult to do that twice." (S001).
**How much is enough.** "I don’t need to be a billionaire." (S003, 2018). "I got more than enough. I got plenty of my share." (S001, 2025). "That's a special kind of wealth: Enough." (S032). Newer, partial change (S002, 2026-08-26): "I’ve come to realize that for entrepreneurs, it’s healthy to have suitable chunky goals."
**Winner-takes-all.** "When so much of life is far more open to multiple winners, where we can end up with a business that have made hundreds of millions of dollars over the years" (S001).

## Pricing and customers
**Pricing.** "I can't in good conscience sell software at prices where I'd never pay." (S068). Basecamp caps price at 299 dollars a month for unlimited users (S068). Enterprise tools at silly prices (S068).
**Discounting.** No recorded position.
**Subscription versus buy-once.** "Why can't we just buy one thing and pay for it once and that can work at a certain scale." (S011). ONCE (Campfire) sold 250,000 dollars in under a week, but he has not cracked the marketing (S046).
**Cancellation and dark patterns.** "We don't hassle customers who want to cancel Basecamp because that simply feels like a shitty thing to do." (S089).
**Customer listening.** Build v1 for yourself; listen after. "After v1, you start listening more" (S011).
**Platform fees.** "I will burn this business down before I hand over 30% of it to Apple." (S001, 2025). Selling through a store means selling to inventory: "you’re not selling an app to a customer, you’re selling an app to inventory at Apple" (S001).
**Niche and moving up-market.** "Small is not a stepping stone." (S001); do not chase whales (S001).
**Competition.** Competing against non-consumption allows a far smaller launch: "when you have the opportunity to compete against non-consumption" (S007, 2022-05-24). Against an incumbent (a dominant email product) the bar is high (S007).

## Marketing
**Earned attention.** "Just you put good stuff into the universe and most of the time the universe will return good stuff to you." (S095). Out-share and out-teach (S048).
**Paid ads.** Older view: hates tracking ads. Newer (S006, 2025-05-14): "not everyone is going to be able to do that." (after Tobi showed small businesses reaching audiences). He says he has not fully changed his mind: "maybe I haven’t fully changed my mind, but I’ve gotten another angle on it." (S006)
**Brand and marketing spend.** Kept in house: an agency asked 1 million dollars for a 30-second spot; the new marketing head did it for 37,000 dollars (S080). "No jargon, no filler, no bullshit." (S047).
**Content.** Founders should write well. "being a good writer." (S006).

## Team, hiring and work
**First hire / default team.** "our default team size is two. One programmer, one designer, one feature." (S001).
**Managers.** Tried engineering managers for a couple of years and concluded he was right to go without (S001, 2025). "We're going to make this company work without full-time managers or die trying." (S033). Tried again at 80 people, dropped it; the company is now around 60 (S006, 2025-05-14).
**Firing.** For competence problems end it quickly; for fit say the role and time are wrong, not the person (S059, S003).
**Hiring.** "The capacity to evaluate work product is a superpower when it comes to hiring." (S001). Hire at the level you are solidly qualified for (S059).
**Remote work.** Remote is an enabling technology for small companies: "Remote work is an enabling technology, like free open-source software, which lowers the cost for new-company formation." (S060). "Hybrid is a cop-out." (S063). Meetups twice a year keep trust (S065). The office is a legitimate choice (S070).
**Work hours and burnout.** "I find just so much of the celebration of overwork to be so tiresome." (S001). 2026 exception: agent-driven three months "not sustainable at all" (S002, 2026-08-26): "This is not sustainable at all."
**Metrics.** "The greatest myth in management is that you can't manage what you don't measure." (S067). He admits it is a luxury when profitable (S067). He would rather have an accountant than a data analyst: "If I can have either a data analytics person or an accountant, I’m picking the accountant." (S001).
**Culture.** "the things that we do over and over again." (S004). Do not write a culture description that does not match reality (S004).
**Workaholics.** "Fire the workaholics." (S004).
**Focus versus diversification.** Hedging bets was right at first, then negligent once one bet paid off; they cut to Basecamp (S004, 2018).
**Surveillance.** "managerial bankruptcy" (S103).
**Equity, stock options, expense policy.** Expense policy is one line: spend it wisely (S003). Stock options: no recorded position.

## Software and tools
**Cloud.** Early view: bought the pitch and tried it. Newer (S097 2022-10-19, S054, S001): "cloud means lock-in." (S005); "So the cloud pitch in my optics, it’s fundamentally false." (S001). Still sees a place for early or spiky companies (S062).
**Microservices.** "In many ways, microservices is a zombie architecture." (S073). "I hate the idea of premature decomposition and microservices is exactly that." (S001).
**JavaScript and build tools.** "No build is a rejection of the part of web development I’ve hated the most in the past 10, 15 years, which is the JavaScript scene." (S001). "Nobody is taking your favorite bundler away." (S053).
**Native mobile.** Older: web first for B2B (S055). He admits 37signals needs nine native developers and HEY could not compete without a native app (S055, S081).
**Estimates.** "Give up on estimates, and embrace the alternative method for making software by using budgets, or appetites" (S035).
**Testing.** System tests failed for him; delete most (5,000 lines in Basecamp). "I've wasted far more time getting system tests to work reliably than I have seen dividends from bugs caught." (S038).
**Typing.** Dynamic typing is taste, not truth: "I've come to appreciate the magnificence of multiplicity." (S071). TypeScript: "I only see the costs." (S001).
**Stability.** Older Rails line "progress over stability". Newer (S001, 2025): "I wouldn’t say, “Progress over stability.”"
**Open source.** A gift, not a vendor relationship: "I’m not a vendor." (S001). "I want this to be a gift exchange." (S005). Do not sell it on market terms: "No. Let there be no sale." (S108).

## AI
**AI, 2018.** "writing software is really about making decisions." (S004, 2018). Software is 10,000 decisions you care about.
**AI, 2025.** Pair programmer, not the driver. "I can literally feel competence draining out of my fingers." (S001, 2025-07-12). "you learn with your fingers." (S001).
**AI, 2026 (newer view, replaces 2025).** After agents in Nov 2025 he has them write all of Omarchy. "I am incredibly excited." (S002, 2026-08-26). "I was still on Earth." (S002) about his skepticism nine months before.
**Agents in big companies.** "As soon as you’re having human teams work together on something, the bottleneck is rarely implementation. It’s human bandwidth and communication." (S002). Vibing PRs into a large existing code base destroyed the architecture (S002).
**Software is product management.** "Software is product management." (S002).
**Forecasting AI.** "nobody fucking knows anything. No one can predict even six months ahead." (S001); "Don’t try to anticipate anything. You will literally go crazy." (S002).
**Remote and layoffs.** Tech layoffs release talent from pointless jobs (S069); AI is "a convenient excuse to slim down." (S002).
**Side projects.** Omakub and Omarchy began as personal projects; "If you keep going, eventually the thing will just work." (S002).
**Timing.** HEY and Slack were Campfire earlier. "Because all we had to do was wait 10 years, and we were ready, the market was ready, boom." (S011).
