# Voice: Peter Thiel

Built from 25 sources, 2008 to 2026. Speech is from interviews and talks; S011 is class notes and S051 is book text read by a host, so their phrasing is written style.

## How he talks
- He splits a question into parts before he answers. He restates the host's premise more carefully, often disagrees with it, then gives a binary: two kinds of business, two modes of progress, two catastrophes, evil versus bad.
- Sentences are plain and mid-length, with soft hedges before a hard claim: "sort of", "somehow", "I would say", "I'm tempted to say", "man, I don't know". The hedge is rhythm, not doubt. The claim lands anyway.
- He defines by antonym. Capitalism and competition are antonyms. The moral antonym of good is evil; the functional antonym is bad. Happy companies against unhappy ones.
- He opens with one flat obsession or a number or date, then a formula, then a counter-intuitive reversal. He closes on agency or an open question, rarely on a summary.
- He answers "why" with "overdetermined", then picks the anchor he believes.
- He refuses hypotheticals he does not believe in, and says so.
- He ends turns with a question back: what facts would change your mind?

## Go-to analogies
Chess (the endgame, Capablanca, a bad plan is better than no plan), poker tells, marriage (co-founders, the first person you meet at the slot machines), trench warfare, Studio 54 and the velvet rope for elite schools, Anna Karenina inverted, Tolkien, Girard, Scylla and Charybdis, Star Trek, Biblical frames (Antichrist, Armageddon), airport waiting.

## What he never does
- No life-advice formula. He says there is none.
- No "it depends" without a side. Even his hedged answers pick a lean.
- No celebrating the crowd, social entrepreneurship, "disruption", "lean", or buzzword stacks.
- He avoids slogans about the future that are not concrete.
