# Frameworks: Charlie Munger

Built from notes on 19 sources (quick depth). Scoring lines are guidance, not his words.

## 1. Inversion
Solve backward: ask what causes the failure, then avoid it. Steps: state the goal, list what would guarantee the opposite, avoid those. Use on any plan, hire, deal or launch. Score: a plan with no written failure list scores zero. Quote: "Those of you who have mastered algebra know that inversion frequently will solve problems which nothing else will solve." (S004) Also "Invert, always invert." (S070) and "many hard problems are best solved only when they are addressed backward." (S032) Sources: S004, S009, S031, S032, S046, S070 (6).

## 2. The avoid list
Name crooks, crazies, egomaniacs, the resentful and the self-pitying, and standard ways to fail, then stay away. Use when picking partners, hires, businesses. Quote: "Figure out what they are and avoid them like the plague" (S046) Sources: S009, S046, S061 (3).

## 3. Incentive audit
Incentives change cognition as well as behaviour. Parts: who is paid for what, what can be faked, what the advisor gains. Antidotes: pay for outcomes that cannot be gamed, learn the advisor's trade, double-check. Score: the stronger the incentive and the weaker the check, the more you discount the claim. Quote: "Never, ever, think about something else when you should be thinking about the power of incentives." (S031) Sources: S004, S031, S033, S046, S048, S061, S064, S070 (8).

## 4. Standard causes of misjudgment (checklist)
A list of psychological tendencies: reward and punishment, liking, envy, doubt-avoidance, inconsistency-avoidance, reciprocation, social proof, deprival, overoptimism, authority, contrast and others. Use as a checklist before big decisions. Quote: "Tendency is not always destiny, and knowing the tendencies and their antidotes can often help prevent trouble that would otherwise occur." (S031) Sources: S031, S070, S064 (3).

## 5. Lollapalooza
Extreme outcomes come when several tendencies push the same way. Score: three or more aligned forces means expect an extreme result, good or bad. Quote: "really big effects, lollapalooza effects, will often come only from large combinations of factors." (S070) Sources: S031, S070 (2).

## 6. Circle of competence and the too-hard pile
Know where you are competent; drop what is outside. Quote: "the single most important thing if you want to avoid a lot of stupid errors is knowing where you're competent and where you aren't, knowing the edge of your own competency." (S012) And "I put them on what I call my too hard pile and I just leave them there." (S012) Sources: S009, S012, S033, S048, S061, S064 (6).

## 7. Latticework of mental models
Hang experience on the big ideas from many disciplines and use them as a checklist. Quote: "80 or 90 important models will carry about 90% of the freight in making you a worldly-wise person." (S033) Sources: S004, S033, S071 (3).

## 8. Man with a hammer
One tool applied to everything. Antidote: a full kit. Quote: "To a man with only a hammer every problem looks pretty much like a nail." (S031) Sources: S031, S070, S071 (3).

## 9. Disconfirming evidence (the Darwin habit)
Seek the evidence against your favourite idea, and argue the other side better than its supporters. Quote: "I'm not entitled to have an opinion on this subject unless I can state the arguments against my position better than the people do who are supporting it." (S004) Sources: S004, S031, S032, S064, S071 (5).

## 10. Second-order consequences
Ask what happens at the second and third step. Quote: "He didn't try to determine second-order and higher-order effects." (S071) Sources: S046, S071 (2).

## 11. Few big bets (pari-mutuel and the pie counter)
The odds move with the bets, so the winners bet seldom and heavily. Quote: "They bet very seldom." (S033) And "The trick in life is when you get the one, or two or three that is your fair allotment for a lifetime, you've got to do something about it." (S061) Sources: S009, S013, S033, S061, S064 (5).

## 12. Owner versus careerist
Think as an owner, not as a man climbing a ladder. Quote: "none of those hotshot executives thought like an owner they would have seen that it was hopeless" (S046) Sources: S046, S048 (2).

## 13. Whirlpool rule
Stay far from what is big and dangerous. Quote: "my attitude when something is big and dangerous is to stay a long way away from it." (S010) Sources: S004, S010 (2).

## 14. Deprival super-reaction
People react with irrational force to a loss or threatened loss. Use in negotiation, pricing changes and product changes. Quote: "A man ordinarily reacts with irrational intensity to even a small loss, or threatened loss, of property, love, friendship, dominated territory, opportunity, status, or any other valued thing." (S031) Sources: S031, S070 (2).

## 15. Scale: advantages and disadvantages
Scale brings cost, reach and social proof; it also brings bureaucracy and perverse incentives. Quote: "And in a bureaucracy, you think the work is done when it goes out of your in-basket into somebody else's in-basket." (S033) Sources: S033, S048 (2).

## 16. Autopsy of past decisions
Revisit old predictions against outcomes. Quote: "But at Johnson & Johnson, the rules make everybody revisit old acquisitions, comparing predictions with outcomes." (S031) Source: S031 (1).

## Books

### Books: Charlie Munger

Built from notes on 19 sources (quick depth). The corpus holds no Munger-authored book text. His collected talks appear as Poor Charlie's Almanack (edited by Peter Kaufman), and the talks below were its chapters.

- **Poor Charlie's Almanack** (2005 in the printed edition): the talks collected under Franklin's name. Core thesis: build judgment from many disciplines and avoid standard errors. Teaches: the Psychology of Human Misjudgment (S031), Elementary Worldly Wisdom (S033), the 1996 talk (S070), the Harvard School talk (S032), the USC law talk (S004).
- **Books he recommends, not wrote:** Influence by Cialdini (the experimental base for social psychology, S031), The Selfish Gene , Pilgrim's Progress (S004, S031, S064), Poor Richard's Almanack (S031, S070), Robinson Crusoe (S009), Of Human Bondage (S004).
- No other books. Letters, essays and the Almanack's other talks are not in the corpus.
