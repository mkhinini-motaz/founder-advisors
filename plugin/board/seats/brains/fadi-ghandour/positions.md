# Positions: Fadi Ghandour

Built from 44 ok sources, 2008 to 2026. Stances with dates; the newer view is named where he moved. Many captions are auto-generated, so wording keeps caption errors. No party politics is recorded here.

## Capital and raising money
- Raising: early money was far harder than today; family and friends first. Quote: "just like it is today only 100 times more difficult." (S010, 2013)
- Regional investors would not fund an asset-light firm, so he listed on NASDAQ. Quote: "we failed at raising money in the Arab world because we were a non-asset based company" (S045, 2013). Same story: S055 (2024), S059 (2008).
- Venture capital: none would have funded Aramex early, but he would have taken it. Quote: "I would have loved to take a check from NC if you want my opinion" (S055, 2024)
- Patient capital over private equity. Quote: "this is a more patient Capital than private Equity" (S060, 2010)
- Funding rounds distract. Quote: "there's nothing worse for an entrepreneur than to take his mind off of his business and continuously be in in in rounds" (S046, 2011)
- Funding cycle now: bullish, bigger funds. Quote: "I am very very bullish" (S043, 2024)

## Debt
- No assets means no debt. Quote: "we had no debt" (S062, 2016). Also 2009: revenue down 10 percent, net income up 25 percent (S062, S045).
- Bank loans for small firms: governments should guarantee if banks are risk averse (S010, 2013).

## Going public and selling
- NASDAQ in 1997 gave credibility and discipline. Quote: "we wouldn't be here today and we wouldn't be the the company that we are today if we had not gone public on on on the NASDAQ Stock Exchange" (S045, 2013)
- Newer (2026): profitable firms can stay private. Quote: "if you're profitable stay private" (S064, 2026). He is unsure he would list on NASDAQ again.
- Selling shares: held 30 years. Quote: "I never sold the share until after 30 years" (S055, 2024)
- Exits: do not ask early. Quote: "exits will happen after ten years" (S049)

## Hiring and first hires
- Hire inexperienced believers. Quote: "I wanted people who had no experience" (S055, 2024)
- Always interview, expect churn. Quote: "build into your strategy of hiring that you're going to lose a lot of people" (S064, 2026)
- Early Aramex was a bad career bet. Quote: "it was a lousy company to think that you were building your career with" (S062, 2016)

## Firing and layoffs
- Aramex kept all staff in the financial crisis (S049); in a crisis, no panic firing. Quote: "Don't go hire firing the first minute you hear there's a problem." (S064, 2026)

## Partners and alliances
- Partners keep their name. Quote: "you will maintain your name and you're not going to give your packages to your competitor" (S055, 2024)
- Alliance as the route to global. Quote: "we will be able to go Global only when we partner with others that are like us" (S045, 2013)
- Competitor events turn into openings: DHL buying Airborne freed Aramex from alliance limits (S059, 2008; S032, 2010).
- Partner trouble: three of four founding partners left (S062).

## Equity and ownership
- Share ownership with the team is important. Quote: "sharing ownership with your team is important" (S046, 2011). Not a family business: "it's not a family business" (S055).

## Focus, niche and diversification

- Western money follows Arab money. Quote: "western money or any other non-arab money will not come in unless it sees my money come in" (S074, 2015)
- Capital is not the gap. Quote: "we have more money than we need we know what to do with" (S074, 2015)

The Airborne money first closed debts so suppliers saw a company with liquidity: "لحتى المورد يحس انه نحن شركه قادره عندها سيوله" (S089) [so the supplier feels we are a company that has liquidity].
- 2008 to 2009: no debt, and he gained. Quote: "we had no debt and we were able to benefit actually" (S077, 2023). Profits up about 30 percent per S089 ("ارباحنا طلعت" [our profits went up]); see the differences section in stories.

### Going public and selling

- Nasdaq changed how banks, customers and regulators saw the firm. Quote: "كانت اهم لحظه في حياه الشركه غيرتنا كاملا" (S089) [it was the most important moment in the company's life, it changed us completely]. Also "نقطه تحول في تاريخ الشركه كامله" (S088) [a turning point in the history of the whole company].
- Dubai relisting: the home market knows the brand. Quote: "if you're on the Dubai financial market, they'll say, "Okay, this is our company."" (S083, 2025)

### Market entry and borders
- Twenty-two countries and as many regulatory regimes. Quote: "we are 22 countries and it is a different regulatory environment in every single country" (S082, 2018). Also 20 or 21 countries with different laws (S089).
- Jordan, Lebanon and Syria are not markets. Reconfirmed with Turkey and Saudi Arabia as the markets big enough to absorb a startup for years (S072, 2018, paraphrase).
- Walls. Quote: "I haven't seen walls crumbling as fast as the world requires them to do" (S078, 2018)
- Customs problems of 1984 persist (S082, caption garbled; paraphrase). Books are a standing customs problem: "الكتب هي الازمه العربيه اللي تكون في الشحن" (S088) [books are the Arab crisis in shipping].
- Countries he does not enter: the US would be suicidal for a newcomer (S088). Quote: "في دول نحن قادرين وفي دول قررنا عدم الدخول فيها" [there are countries where we can, and countries we decided not to enter]
- Why Dubai: to escape competition (DHL dominated Bahrain), then the Gulf customs union with 5 percent duty at the first port of entry. Quote: "دبي هربا من التنافس هربا من التنافس" (S088) [Dubai, running away from competition, running away from competition]
- Licences: Egypt asked 250,000 dollars (S088, "250 الف دولار" [250 thousand dollars]); DHL pushed for high licence costs so small rivals could not pay (S070, paraphrase). Saudi: 14 years (S088, S060).
- Protectionists: the business community. Quote: "the worst people that are protectionist in the Arab world are who it's not the regulator it's the business community" (S067, 2017)
- Arab-world market size: 400 million people and a 4 trillion dollar economy (S074, paraphrase); 22 countries and about 2.3 trillion dollars (S075, paraphrase); 400 to 500 million (S089). The figures differ by year and source.
