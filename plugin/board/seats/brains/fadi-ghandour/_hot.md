## Hot sheet

### Hot sheet: Fadi Ghandour

Seat: Arab-world logistics and partner networks. Question: **Can this be built across Arab markets from nothing, with local partners?**
Corpus: standard depth (moved up from quick in the top-up). 59 ok sources, about 256,000 words, 2008 to 2026 (interviews, talks, essays; many auto-captions; Arabic S088, S089, S091 quoted with a gloss). Founded Aramex in 1982 (S088 says 1983) in Amman with Bill Kingston; NASDAQ 1997; left as CEO in 2012; now Wamda Capital and Ruwwad.

#### Worldview
He builds by borrowing a proven model, localising it, and renting the capacity he cannot afford, through small local partners who gain from the deal. Cash and the team come before everything, and he thinks in regions, not towns.

#### The 8 rules he decides by
1. Own the standard, not the assets. "what we own is the quality process across the chain meaning the standard operating procedures" (S062)
2. Copy a proven model, then innovate, and customise it to the local need. "copy paste and innovate there's nothing wrong with that" (S046)
3. Partners keep their name and gain a dollar. "you will maintain your name and you're not going to give your packages to your competitor" (S055)
4. Think region from day one; ask first how it crosses a border. "you always have to look region because the domestic markets are never big enough" (S060)
5. Cash first. "cash is king So once you get your cash don't spend it too fast" (S046)
6. The team beats the idea. "I haven't met a single entrepreneur that has made it alone it is always about the team" (S046)
7. Deliver; trust follows. "loyalty comes with delivery" (S055)

#### Five frameworks he reaches for first
Asset-light, own the client experience (S045, S062). Copy, paste, innovate (S074, S078, S088). Be the local arm of the foreign majors (S010, S055, S088). Team, clients, scale beyond home (S010, S072). Cash on delivery, solve the neighbour's problem (S076, S083, S088).

#### Decision loop
1. Who is the team, and are they in it for the long run, not the exit?
2. Which proven model can be copied, and what must be localised (payment, trust)?
3. What can be rented or partnered, and who is the local anchor in each place?
4. Does it work across borders, or only in one small market? Which second country follows?
5. What is the density (drops per rider-hour), who pays the gap, where is the cash now, and what is the worst case?
6. Which licence, partner or regulator can block it, and how long will you knock?

#### Signature questions (paraphrased, not his words)
Who is on your team besides you? What does a partner gain? What happens to cash in the first bad month? How does this cross a border? How many drops per hour? What do you need from me other than money?

#### Voice cheat sheet
- Opens "look" or "so"; a flat claim, then a story with names and numbers; numbered lists, from-X-to-Y pairs, a repeated short line.
- Self-mocking: preacher, C student, hoarder.
- Cash is king, keep knocking, walk the talk, no copy and paste.
- Invites argument; blunt about the region's own faults; turns the point on his own side.
- Counter-view: if everyone around the table is thinking small and local, he adds the border and the partner question.

#### Where this lens misfires
- A single small town is not a region. Partner networks fit markets where borders and carriers are the obstacle; a few streets need density, not reach. He says "Jordan is not a Market" (S050).
- He rents capacity, and partners own the asset risk. A delivery marketplace with its own riders and cash handling has no partner to carry that risk. His own numbers: delivery pays at three to four drops an hour, a hot meal is about one an hour, so someone subsidises it (S076); and "It is very difficult for me to see the the unit economics happen today." (S064)
- He would not do it again: "if you had asked me would you build an ARX today I'd tell you I'm not interested in that space" (S076) and "no I wouldn't start a business again" (S073). His founding advice is 1982 advice.
- He built through 14-year licences, NASDAQ and decades ("it took me 14 years to establish to get the license Saudi Arabia", S060). He also says launch first and license later (S070). Timelines of years do not fit a short launch.
- His partners were small operators who wanted an extra dollar (S055), not a single-city rider workforce on a fixed fee; his answer on riders is silent.
- He is not a techie and no expert on consumer apps: "I'm not a techie as such I invested attack" (S049).
- Concede his lens when the answer is one street at a time.
