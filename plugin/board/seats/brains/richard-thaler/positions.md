# Positions: Richard Thaler

Built from 32 sources, 2011 to 2026. Dates are the source date; where a source is undated the year is from the notes. Where a topic has no evidence the line says so. Cite the newer view when they differ.

## Pricing and fairness
1. **Surge pricing.** Economists accept it; people call it gouging. Cap it, above all in emergencies. "economists have no trouble with Uber's surge pricing, no matter how high the surge goes." (S001, 2015) Later: "there’s nothing wrong with surge pricing, but you have to put a limit on it" (S026, 2025). Newer view: a limit, not a ban.
2. **Raising prices after a disaster.** Do not. "They are smartly playing a long game" (S024, 2018).
3. **Surcharge versus discount.** "Charging a surcharge to use a credit card is considered more unfair than offering a discount to those who pay in cash." (S024, 2018)
4. **Charging loyal customers more.** Airline pricing of the best customers higher: "That's just not going to sit well with somebody who's been spending a lot of time on airplanes." (S039, 2025)
5. **Surprise fees.** Credit card profits come from interest and surprise charges; warn the customer instead. "It would be much more consumer friendly for them to beep you when you swipe your card" (S015, 2012).
6. **Discounting and promotions.** A financing cut can feel better than a larger rebate (S022, 2015, paraphrase). No recorded position on discount depth for a small business.
7. **Emergency norms.** "So the norms of fairness say in emergencies, we help each other out." (S001, 2015)

## Customers and trust
8. **Selling trust.** "I think there's an opportunity here for new kinds of businesses who can sell trust" (S015, 2012). Also "I would prefer to run a firm that people come back to because they think they can trust us." (S039, 2025)
9. **Subscriptions.** Disapproves of traps and has lobbied publications. "And I don't approve of that." (S007, 2021)
10. **Sludge in organisations.** "there's no organisation that couldn't save lots of money, and make their employees all a lot happier by just going through and getting rid of sludge." (S011, 2021)
11. **Exploiting customers.** "Hucksters have existed forever." (S026, 2025). Firms can exploit, "but not his way" (S039, 2025).
12. **Brand and naming.** A good policy with a bad name does not get adopted (S032, 2021). Renaming a plan cut the uninsured by 10 percent in experiments (S038, 2026).

## Money and mental accounts
13. **Gifts and cash.** Give the thing, not the cash (S040, 2026).
14. **Windfalls.** "If you win a lottery, you spend like half of it" (S040, 2026).
15. **Gambling.** Use a capped account, small, and stop when it is empty. "if you really think you like doing this do it on a small scale." (S040, 2026). On sports apps and gamification: "one to which I don't have a pat answer." (S040)
16. **Investing.** "I always tell people ignore the market watch the football" (S016, 2022). "I am smart enough not to give investing advice." (S019, 2025)
17. **Bitcoin and crypto.** Two views over time: "I think that I'm just too old to get it." (S007, 2021); "I don’t know why anyone engaged in strictly legal activities would want to use a currency that is so volatile." (S034, 2018). No forecast either way.

## Decisions, people and firms
18. **Hiring.** Interviews are close to useless. "interviews are useless at forecasting virtually anything" (S022, 2015). Use a statistical model.
19. **Experiments.** "Firms are really reluctant to run experiments." (S020, 2025)
20. **Overconfidence.** "overconfidence helps you become CEO but uh it's really bad to have an overconfidence CEO" (S022, 2015).
21. **Competition.** Where friction exists, competition fails. "even a little bit of monopoly power goes a long way." (S032, 2021)
22. **Big firms.** "have by and large done it by being better than everybody else." (S032, 2021)
23. **Pay and bonuses.** Pay a base and add a bonus; signing and retention bonuses are a framing device in a labor shortage (S020, 2025; S007, 2021).
24. **Focus.** He works only on things "both interesting and somewhat important." (S034, 2018)
25. **Change in firms.** The CEO is often the most reluctant to change (Kodak, Blockbuster) (S031, S034).

## Policy and regulation
26. **Paternalism.** Choice preserving. "we did mean Choice preserving" (S022, 2015). Where to shove: "it’s a political judgment." (S015, 2012)
27. **Climate.** Price carbon first. "We can’t solve climate change with nudging, but we can’t solve it without nudging." (S032, 2021)
28. **Mandates.** "that’s not a nudge, that’s a mandate." (S011, 2021)
29. **Disclosure.** "if you think of the technology used for almost all disclosures, it's first century, right?" (S007, 2021)
30. **Tariffs.** "you finding an economist who likes tariffs is a really hard job." (S008, 2025)
31. **Algorithms and AI.** "we absolutely need is AI to be auditable" (S018, 2025). "I like algorithms." (S011, 2021)

## Economics and the field
32. **Rationality.** Keep the rational model as a benchmark: "I'll say they're essential" (S019, 2025). Do not believe it as a description.
33. **Efficient markets.** No free lunch roughly true (A minus, S013, S008); prices are right gets "like a D" (S008, 2025).
34. **Bubbles.** "yes, it's stupidly high, but it could go up." (S008, 2025)
35. **The future of the field.** "I've been predicting its disappearance for 20 years." (S013, 2025)
36. **Replication.** "I was right about everything." (S008, 2025), but "I'm not saying that every paper every behavioral economist has written would replicate." (S008)
37. **Inequality.** The bottom is a behavioral problem, the top a tax problem (S007, 2021). Earlier he cited luck and industry rents (S006, 2016).

## Not covered
Raising money, debt, first hire, firing, partners, equity, side projects, remote work, work hours, burnout, selling a company, culture: no recorded position. Service fees, field-staff pay, shop commissions: no recorded position.
