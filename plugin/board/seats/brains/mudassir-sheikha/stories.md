# Stories: Mudassir Sheikha

Built from 26 sources (2019 to 2026), topped up with batches 03 to 05 to 44 ok sources (2016 to 2026). Paraphrased unless quoted.

1. **Magnus's brain bleed (2011).** A 15 percent chance of death made his co-founder want something meaningful. With Sheikha's McKinsey list showing one Pakistani billion-dollar company outside state oil and gas, they asked why. Lesson: purpose came before the idea. (S005, S052, S055, S056)
2. **The Bangladeshi captain in Ramadan.** Sharing a bed in shifts, sending 90 percent home. Lesson: pick the idea by who it helps. (S005, S055)
3. **SMS-first launch, summer 2012.** No app in time; captains texted "coming", "arrived" and odometer readings; trips priced from mileage; live in six weeks. Lesson: launch with a low-tech workflow. (S002)
4. **$20,000 to build a business.** $100,000 seed split equally; after registration and deposits about $20,000 left. Lesson: constraint forces local, corporate, narrow first. (S018, S055)
5. **Airport promise.** Free trip if more than five minutes late, plus corporate accounts. Lesson: narrow use case plus reliability pays for growth. (S002) Top-up: the promise was later withdrawn to save money, a customer was upset, and he learnt that a delight becomes an expectation (S070).
6. **The two-star call.** A captain phoned a customer who rated him two stars; Sheikha took the call himself. Result: no customer numbers shared, a call centre, captain screening and training. Lesson: protect both sides. (S055)
7. **The failed cloud product for limo companies.** A different customer is a different business. (S002)
8. **The 1 April frugality note.** A fake memo proposing to charge staff for coffee. Lesson: repeat frugality after raising. (S002)
9. **Bunk beds before the $350m raise.** Frugality at the biggest raise. (S002)
10. **Uber talks.** First no, then a reversal after Uber offered independence. Deal kept purpose, culture and organization. (S002, S005, S059) Top-up: three approaches; an American investor said refusing would be selfish since the region needed an exit; the deal was partnership with independence (S065, S071).
11. **COVID.** Rides down over 80 percent, a third of staff cut in one deep move, super app launched June 2020. (S058, S004, S048) Top-up: he says business went down 90 percent and all resources moved to delivery (S060, S066, S067, S074).
12. **The father and the water.** He watched his father spend ten hours getting clean water; that is why purpose is about freeing mindshare. (S055, S056) Top-up: told in detail in S071 (Karachi, August rains, haggled 3,500 rupees against 2,800; about ten hours that week).
13. **Women captains in Saudi.** Pledged within half an hour of the decree; masked phone numbers and training. Lesson: serve the change even at a cost to your segment. (S005)
14. **The captain who earned twice elsewhere.** He apologised, and the bond held. Lesson: purpose works only on top of fair earnings. (S005)
15. **Cairo versus Paris car wait.** "in cairo you could get a car in three minutes by opening your app" (S056). Lesson: no legacy industry means leapfrogging.

## Added by the top-up (batches 03 to 05)

16. **Project bamboo (2011).** Two McKinsey consultants quit with no idea; day one was a bus to Abu Dhabi and takeaway biryani for iftar; $100,000 in the pool, $80,000 gone on registration and deposits. Lesson: make the money last; risk is lower than it looks. (S061, S069, S071, S078)
17. **Saudi launch needed cash.** Many could not use a card-only service; adding cash was invisible and costly backend work. Lesson: build cash in. (S061, S070)
18. **Women asked for hidden numbers.** Careem stopped number sharing and paid for the call centre to guide lost captains. Lesson: protect the weaker side even at cost. (S061, S060)
19. **Vetting in Pakistan.** A firm that checks people for the American consulate pinned each captain's city and village home with GPS and talked to police and neighbours; his own parents would not take a Careem at launch. Lesson: pay for trust. (S063)
20. **Customer ratings in stages.** Surveyed in year two, angry reaction (quote: "how dare a captain or a driver would rate me" (S070)), waited, taught etiquette, then rolled out. Lesson: do not rate people before society accepts it. (S070)
21. **The 70-cent bike trip.** Per-trip arithmetic in Pakistan leaves about 4 cents before support. Lesson: frugality is forced by dollar costs. (S065)
22. **Daily payouts to captains.** Captains grumbled about card riders because cash paid at once; payouts in three to five hours changed it. Lesson: change cash habits by paying fast. (S077)
23. **Bags of cash.** Captains' cash was carried to the bank daily in the early years. Lesson: cash costs real labour. (S067)
24. **Six-week launch.** Careem launched on 5 September 2012 with a booking form that rang a phone, staff texting and calling captains by hand; some basic features were never built. Lesson: launch by force. (S078, S079)
25. **Brience, the dot-com collapse.** Employee number 11 at a startup that raised the biggest Series A of the bubble and collapsed; he lost savings. Lesson: build to last, never to flip. (S064, S067, S074, S075)
26. **Fundraising was hard.** One term sheet after three VCs refused in round one; again one in round two from a Saudi travel CEO; early terms were non-standard and had to be unwound. Lesson: fit over valuation. (S061, S065, S067)
27. **Where the word captain came from.** A Saudi operations manager suggested it; an executive fined a jar of dirhams for each use of driver. Lesson: words give dignity. (S074, S070, S076)
28. **Cairo scale.** Close to 50,000 cars; a new entrant with 5,000 would not be available. Lesson: in this business scale is a barrier. (S063)
29. **A widow's car loan in Pakistan.** A woman cleaning houses with three or four children drove through Careem's car-loan programme. Lesson: jobs are the pitch. (S070, paraphrase)
30. **The Thursday failure list.** Each week staff listed every failure, broke each down and built the fix in. Lesson: make learning a ritual. (S071)
31. **The bad tail.** A 0.5 percent failure rate at scale is hundreds of thousands of unhappy people. Lesson: the percentage hides a crowd. (S067)
32. **Salary cap in chapter one.** $150,000 cap so purpose, not pay, screened people; a friend turned down 5 percent equity because the best case looked too small. Lesson: mission screens people. (S065, S067)
