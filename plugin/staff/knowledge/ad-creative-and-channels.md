## The hook has three parts

A video hook is the visual of the first 0–3 seconds, the spoken line and the on-screen caption. The three must say different things that add up; a caption that repeats the voice wastes the line. A static ad has two parts, the visual and the headline, under the same rule.

Seconds 3–15 are the **on-ramp**: they extend the hook's promise. Putting a new hook on an old body breaks the ad, so write the body again for each hook.

## Diagnose a weak ad by the metric that broke

| Weak metric | What it says | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Thumbstop (3-second views ÷ impressions) | The opening image does not stop the scroll | The visual of the first second |
| Hold rate (15-second ÷ 3-second views) | The promise stopped them, the body lost them | The on-ramp, not the hook |
| CTR | They watched but saw no reason to act | The offer or the proof |
| After the click (first orders ÷ clicks) | The app or page does not continue the ad | The landing screen, the price shown, the menu |

A high thumbstop with a collapsed hold rate is clickbait, not a winner.

## Choosing the next creative

- **Matrix, not rewordings.** Cross segment × motivation (in the customers' own words) × format. Ten distinct cells teach more than thirty rewordings of one ad.
- **Fidelity ladder.** Test a hunch as a cheap static or a text-on-screen video. Pay for a creator shoot only for an angle that has already shown one good signal. This fits a small fixed daily test budget.
- **Evidence tiers for an idea, strongest first:** your own converting ad; customers' exact words (reviews, comments, messages); a competitor's ad still running after 60 days (Meta Ad Library); organic engagement in the niche; another niche; a hunch.
- **Two states.** While nothing works, go wide and count a single-metric win as a lead. Once one ad pays back, go deep on it and keep a small slice of the budget exploring.

## Customer words before copy

Before writing, mine public threads for pains, exact jargon and what people recommend: local Facebook groups, reviews of competing apps in the customers' own language, and comments under competitors' posts. Five or six interviews with the best customers usually saturate the themes; ask "why" three to five times, try to disprove your own assumption, and end with "who else should we talk to?". Pay for an interview with product credit, not cash.

## Creators

- **Reply videos.** On TikTok, answer a product question from the comments with a video: the person who asked is the viewer most ready to order.
- Judge creator content by product questions, saves, shares and orders, never views. Give a format four weeks and look for one that works twice, not one hit.
- Hire a new creator for a paid trial week and judge the **revision**, not the first video. Give written, fixable feedback ("cut the first sentence, hold the phone closer"), never "be more natural".
- Paid posts are disclosed as paid.
- About 20% of promoters bring about 80% of the results. Invest in the top ten (early access, a direct line, better codes) rather than recruiting many passive ones. This holds for partners who post their own code too.

## Referral rewards

- A bonus attracts people who game it (the cobra effect). Cap rewards per referrer, pay only after the friend's first delivered and paid order, and check for clusters that share an address, a phone or a device. The security file (`security-and-fraud.md`) has the controls.
- Name the larger-looking number. Under about 100 dollars, an absolute amount ("$3 off") reads bigger than a percentage ("10% off"); above it, test the percentage. This is a US pricing heuristic: test it once.

## WhatsApp Business (verify before relying on it)

- The free WhatsApp Business app comes first, with click-to-WhatsApp ads. The Business Platform (API) bills Meta in USD, which needs a dollar card.
- On the platform, free-form messages are allowed only within 24 hours of the customer's last message; after that, only an approved template. Anything promotional, including "we miss you" and abandoned-cart reminders, is the Marketing category and is charged per message.
- An easy opt-out keeps people from blocking the number. Blocks lower the number's quality rating, and the rating sets the daily sending limit, which starts at about 250 unique people a day. Warm a new number on recent buyers and grow over one to two weeks.
- Marketing messages go only to customers who opted in (the brief's consent rules apply).
