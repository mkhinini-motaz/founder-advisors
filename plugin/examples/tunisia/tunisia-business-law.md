# Tunisia: company law, work status, data, consumer and IP

General information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Check the official text and a licensed professional before relying on it.

**As of 30 Sept 2026.** Items marked **(verify)** come from general practice or a single secondary source. Confirm them against the official text (JORT, jurisitetunisie.com) before relying on them.

## Choosing a legal form

| Form | Who | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| Auto-entrepreneur | One Tunisian individual; industry, trade, services, crafts | Turnover up to **75,000 TND a year**. Income tax replaced by a single contribution of **0.5% of turnover**. Social contribution **7.5%** on two-thirds of the relevant minimum wage. Declared electronically each quarter, within 15 days of its end. The single contribution is paid by the National Employment Fund in the **first year**. (décret-loi 2020-33) [1][2] |
| Sole trader (personne physique) | One person, full personal liability | Real regime or flat-rate (forfait) regime. **(verify forfait limits)** |
| SUARL | One associate, limited liability | Minimum capital **1,000 TND**: 50% paid in at creation, the rest within 5 years. [3] |
| SARL | 2 to 50 associates, limited liability | Minimum capital **1,000 TND**. [3] Transfers of shares to outsiders usually need the other associates' approval (agrément). **(verify in the Code des sociétés commerciales, loi 2000-93)** |
| SA | Share company | Higher minimum capital, and a board or directors. **(verify)** Needed mainly for outside investors or a stock market listing. |

**Creating a company:** name reservation (24–72 h), statutes, capital deposit, then registration at the national business register (RNE, loi 2018-52), 3–5 working days when the file is complete. Allow **7–15 days** in all. Almost everything is online through the "Entreprendre en Tunisie" platform. Costs run about 150–250 TND for RNE and registration plus 500–1,500 TND for a lawyer or accountant. The matricule fiscal is issued at registration. [3][4]

**Structural rules worth knowing before signing a shareholder or partner deal:**
- A clause that gives one associate all the profits, or frees one from all losses (clause léonine), is void. **(verify article)** A contract with a non-associate (a fee, a commission or a revenue share) avoids the company-law question but must be written carefully.
- Vesting, buyback and leaver clauses belong in a shareholders' agreement (pacte d'associés) that sits beside the statutes. How far a Tunisian court enforces them is a question for an avocat.
- Work a founder creates (code, designs, a brand) is the founder's personal property under copyright law (below) until it is assigned or contributed in kind (apport en nature). A licence keeps ownership with its creator; an assignment moves it to the company, where its creditors can reach it.

## Startup Act (loi 2018-20)

**Label criteria:** at most 8 years since creation; at most 100 employees; total assets and turnover each at most 15M TND; more than two-thirds of the capital held by individuals, SICARs, venture or seed funds, or similar investment vehicles; a business model with a strong innovative dimension, especially technological, and strong growth potential. [5][6] The label is requested through the official Startup Tunisia portal (Smart Capital) and granted by a committee. **(verify current procedure)**

**Benefits:** [5]
- **Exemption from IS** during the label (up to 8 years).
- The State pays **employer and employee social contributions** for a set period (sources say up to 3 years).
- A **founder stipend** during the first year (sources cite up to 1,000 TND a month), for at most three full-time founder-associates.
- A one-year, renewable **startup leave** for founders employed in the public or private sector, with the right to return.
- Tax relief and capital-gains exemption for people who invest in labelled startups.
- Foreign-currency facilities. **(verify)**

**Startup Act 2.0** (label up to 10 years, a stock-option regime with tax deferred to sale, a phased exit from the tax benefits, faster permits for foreign hires) was **before Parliament in 2026 and not adopted** as of the source. [7]

**Fit:** the committee judges innovation. Whether a delivery or e-commerce marketplace qualifies is **unknown**. The label is free to request, so the cheapest test is to apply.

## Work status: employees, riders, contractors

- **Loi 2025-9 (21 May 2025, JORT n° 61 of 23 May 2025):** [8]
  - The **open-ended contract (CDI) is the default**. Fixed-term contracts (CDD) are allowed only for a temporary increase in activity, replacing an absent employee, or seasonal work.
  - **Labour subcontracting (sous-traitance de main-d'œuvre) is prohibited.** Guarding and cleaning are presumed to be labour subcontracting.
  - Penalties (Code du travail arts. 28–29, as amended): **10,000 TND** for an individual, **20,000 TND for a company**, and 10,000 TND personally for a director involved in recruiting the workers. A repeat offence brings **3–6 months' prison**. [16]
  - The only exception (art. 30): a service contract for work that needs specialised professional knowledge, is not the client's main activity, and whose workers stay outside the client's direct control. Rider delivery on a platform that dispatches them fails all three. [16]
  - The client pays for the contractor's failures: when the contractor's guarantee falls short, the client covers its unpaid wages and social contributions (art. 30 quater), and the workers and the CNSS can claim directly from the client (art. 30 quinquies). [16]
  - Unknown: the transitional rule for workers already supplied this way, and whether a paid rider recruiter (apporteur) needs a placement authorisation. Ask an avocat.
  - An employee who keeps working after a CDD ends is on a CDI automatically.
  - **Consequence for platforms:** riders supplied by a labour intermediary that pays them (the 3PL model; 95% of Glovo's riders in 2023 came through seven such intermediaries, per Inkyfada [18]) is now high-risk. Contract directly.
- **Independent riders:** the auto-entrepreneur regime is the only formal independent status today. Requalification into employment turns on subordination. The signs to avoid are fixed shifts imposed, sanctions or penalties, exclusivity, no freedom to refuse an order, and pay set by time instead of by task. Keep riders free to log in and out, to refuse, and to work for others. **(have an avocat review the rider contract)**
- **Platform-work bill:** in July 2026 the Ministry of Social Affairs said a bill on the platform economy (and one on telework) was nearly finished, aligned with the ILO standards on decent work and social protection. [9][10] From **September 2026**, delivery-platform workers come under **labour-inspection control** through a digital platform built with the ILO, with planned data exchange with the RNE and CNSS. [11] Expect duties on social protection, insurance and transparency. None is law yet.
- **Context:** the UGTT counts about 60,000 platform workers, 98% without social protection. [12] Riders struck in July and August 2026 for a 3 TND minimum per delivery within 3 km, 80 TND for an 8-hour day, per-order pay instead of bundling, free log-in and log-out instead of slots, social coverage, accident insurance and an appeal against deactivation. [13]

## Personal data (INPDP)

Organic law 2004-63 (procedure: décret 2007-3004; forms on inpdp.tn): [14][17]
- Declare to the INPDP **before** processing personal data (art. 7). No objection within one month counts as acceptance.
- **Hosting or processing outside Tunisia is a transfer.** Art. 50 bans transfers that could harm public security or vital interests, and art. 51 requires adequate protection at the destination. Art. 52 requires INPDP authorisation **in all cases**, decided within one month. **Silence is not acceptance for transfers:** wait for the written authorisation. Cloud hosting abroad, push notifications, map services and foreign SMS gateways all count.
- Penalties (art. 90): processing without a declaration, or transferring without authorisation, carries one year's prison and a 5,000 TND fine. **(verify exact wording)**
- A child's data needs the guardian's consent and the family judge's authorisation (art. 28), hence "18 and over" in customer terms.
- Never copy a database holding real people's data abroad before the authorisation exists.
- Consent needs a **written trace** (art. 27). Marketing needs a **separate opt-in** (art. 30).
- **Retention limits must be real and enforced** (art. 45): purge jobs, not intentions.
- **Security audit:** ANSI is now the **ANCS** (décret-loi 2023-17). Companies that process their users' personal data over telecom networks must be audited by an ANCS-certified auditor. The frequency is not stated on the ANCS site; past practice was annual. [17]
- **A new data-protection law** (organic bill 095/2025, 132 articles, GDPR-style) was before a parliamentary committee in Feb 2026. It had not been adopted as of 30 Sept 2026.

## Selling online and consumers

- **E-commerce law 2000-83**, arts. 25–35: before confirmation, show the seller's identity, the price and **every fee**; send an order recap and an acceptance notification. There is a **withdrawal right of 10 working days from receipt** (art. 30), which does not apply to perishable food but does apply to household goods. Non-conforming or late goods can be returned. [14] Loi 2000-83 also makes electronic signatures valid.
- **Distribution law 2009-69:** art. 4 al. 2 makes an online distributor notify the commerce ministry, with a copy of its hosting contract, within one month of opening and after every site change. Arts. 14–17 govern franchise contracts: read them before drafting a franchise contract.
- **Consumer protection law 92-117** covers information, fair pricing and misleading advertising. **(verify specifics)**
- **Competition law 2015-36** covers abuse of dominance and anti-competitive agreements. **(verify)** The regional precedent: on 31 July 2025 Morocco's competition council settled with Glovo, **capping merchant commission at 30% and striking every exclusivity clause**. [13]

## Intellectual property

- **Trademarks** are registered with INNORPI (loi 2001-36). Register the brand before the public launch, in the classes that match the business (for example: 9 software, 35 retail and marketplace, 39 transport and delivery, 43 food services). **(verify fees and timing)**
- **Software** is protected by copyright (loi 94-36) without registration. The author owns it unless it is assigned in writing. Code or design by a freelancer, a friend or an employee needs a **written assignment**.

## Money and payments

- **Cash:** the 5,000 TND ceiling was repealed from 1 January 2026. See `tunisia-tax.md`.
- **Online payment providers:** Konnect, Flouci, D17 (La Poste), ClicToPay (SMT). Card use online is low. See `tunisia-market.md`.
- **Foreign exchange:** residents cannot pay foreign shops for goods. A "technology card" allows about 1,000 TND a year of digital services abroad. LF 2026 lets residents open foreign-currency accounts without prior central bank approval. [15]
